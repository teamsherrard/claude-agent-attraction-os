# Deck and Document Craft — part of the aa-offer-kit-design skill (the one-pager, the call deck, the welcome pack)

Read in full before building the call deck, the one-pager or the welcome pack. The deck mechanics are
the ones proven in the sister suite's presentation skill; the document mechanics are the ones its guide
designer uses. The audience here is an agent weighing a move, never a buyer or a seller. The words are
chapter 7 of the Week 3 Kit — these pages say how they are laid out, never what they say.

## PART A — THE DECK (16:9, presented live or shared after a call)

### Right-size, then structure
- **The kit's ten slides plus a contact slide:** THE CALL DECK, SLIDE BY SLIDE gives ten `Slide N — …:`
  lines — one slide each, in that order — and the contact slide closes it (11 slides). Never pad; never
  split a kit line into two slides unless it genuinely holds two ideas (then both carry the kit's words).
- **The order is the kit's:** Who I am → The problem → What changed for me → The method → The program →
  What you get → Proof → Your first 30 days → The model → Your next step. Do not reorder; do not add a
  feature slide; do not move the model forward.
- **Slide 2 is THEM.** The problem in the agent's words, as the kit wrote it — set large, quoted. Slide 1
  (Who I am) is one line from the member's story, never a résumé.
- **Slide 9 is the model, and it carries no figure.** It says what the kit says and nothing more: *"We
  talk through the model on the call, with the documents."* No split, cap, stock, tier, or dollar figure —
  compensation is "on the call", in those words.
- **Slide 10 is an ask with next steps**, never a bare "Thank you": the kit's line — why now, the booking
  line (or "message me on Instagram", exactly as the kit says), Day 1 in one line.

### Build in waves — a deck must not degrade or die mid-render
- **Wave 1:** slides 1–5 (slide 1 doubles as the cover: the member's name and mark, the headshot, the
  kit's one line), locking the master template (title treatment, margins, accent, footer, page number).
  **Then** slides 6–10 and the contact slide.
- **THE NO-DRIFT TEST after every wave:** put the newest slide beside slide 2 — same title position,
  margins, footer, accent, and level of craft? Late slides coming out thinner or plainer get fixed
  before continuing. The last slide must be indistinguishable in build quality from the first.
- **THE TURN NEVER ENDS AT A WAVE** — a wave that passes is followed by the next wave in the same
  response. "Say go and I'll continue" is banned.
- Render each slide as its own frame, in order, clearly labelled ("Slide 1 — Who I am", "Slide 2 — The
  problem", …); keep key content inside a safe margin (~80–100px at 1920×1080).

### Concrete specs (the difference between agency work and a slide generator)
- **Type scale at 1920×1080:** slide titles ~64–88px bold; divider titles ~96–120px; a big statement
  ~120–200px; body and support ~28–36px; bullets ~28–32px; eyebrows, labels, and the footer ~16–20px
  (often uppercase, tracked). Two sizes minimum between a headline and its support; generous line
  spacing (~1.3–1.5 on body).
- **Grid and margins:** a 12-column grid, ~80–100px outer margins, one consistent gutter; a strong
  left or centre alignment axis; nothing floating.
- **Compose to FILL the frame, balanced.** One idea per slide, but the layout uses the whole canvas —
  content paired with the member's cut-out, the offer stack object, a photo panel, a designed diagram,
  or balanced columns. Content crammed into the top third with an empty bottom half is the #1 cheap
  tell. "Whitespace" means breathing room around a full composition, not a blank lower slide.
- **Section dividers for rhythm** — at most three, between the story (slides 1–3), the program (4–6),
  the proof and the path (7–8) and the close (9–10); a divider carries a kit lead-in as its title ("The
  method", "What you get"), never a line of your own — full-bleed or accent-ground slides that reset
  attention.
- **Deliberate layout variety:** slide 1 the member (the cut-out, the kit's one line) · slide 2 the
  problem (the agent's words, large, quoted) · slide 3 the turning point (a photo or the cut-out with the
  kit's line) · slide 4 the method as a designed one-line chain · slide 5 the program as a strip (name ·
  type · cadence) · slide 6 the stack slide (the transparent object from the offer stack piece, the kit's
  outcomes beside it) · slide 7 proof as a clean grid of the kit's lines · slide 8 the four weeks as a
  roadmap · slide 9 the model (plain text, calm, one accent, no figure) · slide 10 the ask with next
  steps · the contact slide. No two consecutive slides share a layout; never "headline + three text
  cards" twice.
- **Real visuals only:** the member's headshot or cut-out, the offer stack object, the product mockup,
  real photos the member dropped (a scrim behind any text over them), the toolkit's devices, clean
  icons in one style. Never a stock person, never a generated face, never a fabricated office or crowd,
  never one photo repeated to fill slides.
- **One cohesive template:** the title treatment, margins, accent, and the footer (the member's mark,
  left · page number, right) on every non-cover slide.

### Numbers on a call deck (there are almost none — and that is correct)
- A number appears ONLY when the kit carries it (chapter 2's `Proof:` lines and slide 7, as written,
  `(demo)` tags kept), labelled as the member's own with the date the kit gives it. No projection,
  no "what you could earn", no comp-plan figure, no illustrative rev-share scenario on a slide — those
  belong to the private call.
- If a real number earns a visual, it is a clean, honest one: bars start at zero, time windows honest,
  values labelled on the chart, no 3D, no tilted pies, no dual axes. A proof grid of 2–4 real figures
  is the most a deck here ever carries.
- **THE SKEPTICAL-AGENT TEST:** the prospect will check a number on their phone mid-call. Every figure
  must survive that check or carry its source — otherwise it comes off the slide.

### Speaker talking points (deliver alongside the deck)
After the slides, give the member brief talking points for each slide as copyable text — 2–4 lines of
what to actually SAY, copied from the kit: chapter 3's THE WORDS TO USE (the 30-second explanation, the
DM, the caption, the video intro), chapter 2's free line and up-front cost line, the proof lines as
written — never a line of your own. The deck supports the member speaking; it is not the script. No
talking point ever says a compensation number out loud on a first call; the money line is slide 9's:
*"We talk through the model on the call, with the documents."*

### Cheap-deck tells (fix before presenting)
Text walls and bullet soup · tiny type and timid hierarchy · ten near-identical text slides · the
default-template look · low contrast or text on a busy photo with no scrim · clip-art or mismatched
icons · edge-to-edge slides with no breathing room · a bare "Thank you" close · a model slide that
reads like a comp plan · a feature dump where the kit wrote outcomes.

## PART B — THE DOCUMENTS (US Letter portrait — the 1-pager, the welcome pack)

### The page contract
- **US Letter portrait, 2550×3300 px at 300dpi** (A4 via tweak when the kit's `What to build:` line says
  A4). Every page its own clearly labelled frame, in order; content inside a safe margin (~0.5–0.75 in);
  nothing touching an edge; nothing sitting outside the frame to be clipped when it becomes a PDF page.
- **One cohesive template:** consistent margins, a clear type scale (section headers large, body
  ~11–12pt, an eyebrow above headers), one accent, page numbers on multi-page pieces, a small running
  footer (the member's name or the piece's title) — this consistency is what reads premium.
- **Body ≥ 11pt, contrast that passes on a phone** — an agent reads the 1-pager on a phone, from a DM.
- **Strong hierarchy, generous whitespace:** one dominant idea per page, copy broken into short
  sections, bullets, and pull-quotes — never a wall of text. A page that reads as a Word document fails
  the craft bar.
- **LAYOUT QUOTA on multi-page pieces:** at least three visibly different page layouts across the
  welcome pack (a full-width opener, a two-column page, a table page, a checklist page) and never the
  same layout on more than two consecutive pages — same grid, same type scale, different rooms.
- **Nothing overlaps; nothing is cut off.** Every element gets its own space; text never runs under a
  photo or off the page.

### The 1-pager's anatomy (fills the body `aa-brand-kit-design` reserved)
Header third = the cover's five answers, compressed: the member's name (or the organization lockup)
and role line · the positioning line · the who-you-help row · the proof chip if real · the member's
cut-out or the treated culture photo. Body two-thirds = chapter 7's THE JOIN MY TEAM ONE-PAGER band, in
its order: the `Headline:` line as the headline (never the band's name) · the `Subhead:` · `The promise:`
· "What you get" — the five bullets as designed rows, free items first, the brokerage line last · "How it
works" — the three steps numbered · the offer stack strip (the transparent object, small, when it
exists) · `Proof:` (the consented lines, or the kit's one honest line) · `Why me:` · the `Call to
action:` line in FULL plain text, exactly as the kit holds it (a booking link, or "message me on
Instagram · comment [KEYWORD]"). Bottom edge = the `Compliance:` strip + the required disclaimer
verbatim. One page, always; every word the kit's.

### The welcome pack's anatomy (what a new partner receives on day one — 4–6 pages, the kit's words)
1. **Cover** — the program name (chapter 2's `Program:` name) with "Day 1" as its eyebrow, the member's
   mark, the date line, a designed name slot the member fills per partner ("For: ______").
2. **The welcome letter** — chapter 7's `Welcome letter:`, verbatim, set as a letter in the member's
   voice with the headshot in the brand's frame.
3. **Day 1** — the `Day 1 checklist:` (five lines) as a checklist with real checkbox squares, each line's
   bold lead-in kept as the kit writes it.
4. **The first lesson and the calendar** — `First lesson:` as the page's one idea; `Calendar:` as a
   designed strip (the weekly call · the first lesson · the day-30 self-check), link slots left
   blank for the member to fill.
5. **Your first 30 days** — slide 8's four weeks (THE CALL DECK, SLIDE BY SLIDE) as four designed rows;
   omitted when the kit's slide 8 is honestly empty — never invented.
6. **Who to call, and the closing page** — the member's contact in full as the kit's FOOTER carries it;
   the broker, upline, or admin contacts ONLY when the member supplies them (a designed empty row
   otherwise — never an invented name); the compliance strip.
No page carries a split, cap, stock, or income figure, and no line about money at all — every word on
the pack is the kit's.
