# Brand Directions — part of the aa-style-sheet-design skill

Read in full before designing.

## THE DIRECTION ENGINE — BUILD ANY AESTHETIC

There is no single "house style". Across a cohort of leaders the right brand could be premium,
minimal, dark, light, neutral, colourful, earthy, or editorial — 100+ valid directions. Your job is to
READ the member's direction from their inputs, then commit the WHOLE sheet to that one aesthetic. Two
different leaders must produce two visibly different sheets.

**1. Detect the direction.** Infer it, in priority order, from: (a) the Design Package brief's feel
words and palette (the Brain already captured these with the member — they win), (b) uploaded
inspiration or a palette image, (c) pasted colour codes, (d) the feel pick in intake, (e) the voice
and brand words in the Book ("calm", "direct", "warm", "systems", "premium"), (f) the primary type of
agent they attract (a brand aimed at new agents reads warmer and clearer; one aimed at top producers
reads more premium and restrained). If signals conflict, the member's own uploads win. If there is
truly nothing, pick a tasteful default that fits their voice and say which direction you chose.

**2. Commit fully.** Drive EVERYTHING from it together — background darkness or lightness, the
palette, textures and finishes, type personality, accent shapes, headshot treatment, mood.
Half-committing is what makes a sheet look unfinished.

**3. Honour custom input verbatim.** If the member gives exact codes, specific fonts, or clear
inspiration, follow THAT even if it matches no recipe. Blend two directions when asked.

**4. Stay distinct from the brokerage.** Whatever direction wins, hold its lead and accent colours
beside the brokerage palette named in the brief. Same hue family at a glance = shift it (hue, depth,
or temperature) before you build. A leader's sheet that reads as the brokerage's brand has failed its
one job.

**FONT RULE — only ever use and name fonts the design tool ACTUALLY has.** Every font must be a real,
reliably available face (the names in these recipes are chosen for that — stick to them or equally
available equivalents). Never spec a paid or unavailable font (no Gotham, Canela, Addington, MADE
Outer Sans): silently substituting while printing the fancy name poisons the member's permanent brand
spec. The font name printed MUST be the font rendered, and it must cover the brand language's full
character set. Favour widely available faces so the brand reproduces easily in Canva, Google Docs,
Riverside, and every video tool.

Use these proven directions as reference recipes (palette logic · type · texture · accents · mood):

- **Calm Premium (Navy & Brass / Black & Gold)** — deep navy, charcoal, or black grounds with gold,
  champagne, or brass and warm cream. High-contrast serif display (Playfair Display, DM Serif Display,
  Cormorant, Fraunces) + clean sans (Montserrat, Inter). Soft metallic or paper texture; thin gold
  rules; generous dark space. Mood: established, trusted, the standard-bearer. Reads authority first.
- **Bold Modern** — one or two vivid hues (cobalt, coral, orange, teal, lime) on white or a dark
  ground. Strong display sans (Anton, Archivo Black, Bebas Neue, Kanit) + clean sans. Geometric
  patterns, bold blocks, dynamic angles. Mood: energetic, memorable, the builder. Reads aspiration first.
- **Warm Approachable (Neutral & Organic)** — sand, taupe, camel, mushroom, mauve, off-white, soft ink.
  Elegant serif (Cormorant, EB Garamond, Quattrocento, Lora) + a delicate script accent + quiet sans.
  Paper, linen, arch, and stone textures; organic shapes. Mood: editorial, boutique, calm, the mentor.
  Reads relatability first.
- **Dark Confident** — near-black grounds with one electric accent (acid green, electric blue, violet,
  orange). Condensed display (Oswald, Chakra Petch) + clean sans. Grain, halftone, sharp geometry.
  Mood: bold, forward, high-energy, "different".
- **Earthy Grounded** — forest, olive, sage, eucalyptus with cream and a metallic. Serif + clean sans.
  Paper, foliage, stone; arch shapes. Mood: grounded, steady, premium-natural.
- **Light & Airy** — blush, dusty rose, sage, powder blue, cream. Light serif or rounded sans + a
  flowing script. Watercolour washes, soft gradients, negative space. Mood: friendly, warm, welcoming.
- **Coastal** — sky, teal, seafoam, sand, white. Airy sans + light serif. Wave lines, gentle gradients.
  Mood: fresh, relaxed, open. For members whose organization lives by the water.
- **Modern Minimal** — mostly light or monochrome with ONE confident accent. Clean geometric sans
  (Plus Jakarta Sans, Barlow, Inter). Flat fields, whitespace, almost no texture, one accent shape.
  Mood: current, clean, design-literate, the systems person.
- **Dark Premium Jewel-tone** — deep emerald, sapphire, plum, or burgundy grounds with gold, silver, or
  rose-gold. Serif display + sans. Velvet, marble, metallic-gradient textures. Mood: opulent, dramatic.
- **Hometown / Modern Farmhouse** — warm white or cream grounds with charcoal, wood-brown, and one
  muted accent (sage, denim, clay). Sturdy serif or slab (Quattrocento, Bitter, Zilla Slab) + a
  friendly hand-script accent (Caveat, Kalam) + clean sans. Wood-grain, kraft paper, window-pane line
  frames. Mood: welcoming, hometown-trusted. Strong for leaders whose organization is rooted in a
  suburban or small-town market.
- **Terracotta / Desert Modern** — terracotta, clay, rust, burnt orange with sand, cream, and a deep
  brown or cactus-green anchor. Warm serif (Lora, Fraunces, Cormorant) + clean sans. Stucco texture,
  sun-arc and horizon motifs, rounded arches. Mood: warm, grounded, modern-Southwest.
- **Editorial Black & White** — pure black + warm white only (optionally one hairline metallic).
  High-fashion serif display (Playfair Display, DM Serif Display) at oversized scale + a light sans.
  No colour and no texture — contrast, scale, and whitespace ARE the design. Mood: gallery,
  top-of-market, the quiet authority.

## TEXTURES, GRADIENTS & FINISHES (render them for real — they make it look paid-for)

Flat colour alone reads cheap on a premium brand. Bring in ONE or two finishes that match the
direction, rendered as real artwork in the palette — never a label that says "texture":

- **Metallics** — gold foil, champagne, brushed gold, rose gold, silver/pewter, as a swatch and a thin
  accent; shown as a soft light-to-deep gradient so they read as foil, labelled like a real spec.
- **Stone & natural** — marble veining, concrete, stone, linen/paper grain, wood — for premium,
  neutral, and earthy brands.
- **Soft / organic** — watercolour washes, duotone photo fields, grain overlays, blurred colour blobs.
- **Gradients** — a tasteful brand-colour gradient (light→deep, or two adjacent brand hues). Smooth,
  on-palette, never a rainbow.
- **Geometric / pattern** — diagonals, grids, dots, halftone, chevrons, or a tiled motif — for modern,
  bold, and confident brands.

Keep finishes consistent; one cohesive finish system beats a pile of effects.

**THE PERSON-NOT-PLATFORM TEST (textures must smell like a leader, not a product):** every texture
and pattern must read as craft, place, and people — paper, linen, stone, wood, grain, a skyline
silhouette, contour lines, a hand-drawn rule. Circuit-board grids, dot matrices, pixel motifs, and
dashboard graph-paper read "software company", and the brand is a person agents will follow. Even a
modern, systems-minded member gets a grid softened toward drafting paper — and offered as a TWEAK so
they can compare two texture reads.
