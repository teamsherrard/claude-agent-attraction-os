# Deck and Document Craft — part of the aa-offer-assets-design skill

Read in full before building the opportunity deck, the welcome pack, or the comparison sheet. The deck
mechanics are the ones proven in the sister suite's presentation skill; the document mechanics are the
ones its guide designer uses. The audience here is an agent weighing a move, never a buyer or a seller.

## PART A — THE DECK (16:9, presented live or shared after a call)

### Right-size, then structure
- **8–12 slides total:** cover · the 6–8 content slides the Conversion brief titles · the close +
  contact. Fewer, stronger slides beat a padded deck; split a slide only when it holds two ideas; never
  pad to hit a number.
- **The order is the brief's.** The Conversion system wrote it through the prospect's lens: the mirror
  → the three things that matter most → the bridge in three layers (the member's support · the upline
  and community · the brokerage model, LAST, in plain English, "walked through on the call") → the story
  → close with confidence. Do not reorder; do not add a feature slide; do not move the model forward.
- **Open on THEM.** Slide 2 is their current state in their words ("you said…"). An "about me" slide,
  if the brief has one, caps at ONE, after the three things — a deck that opens with the member's
  résumé loses the agent before the bridge arrives.
- **The model is one slide, and it is the last content slide before the story.** It says what the
  brief says and nothing more: the vehicle in plain English, generically, with the line *"the numbers
  are real and I'll walk you through every one of them on a call."* No split, cap, stock, tier, or
  dollar figure — compensation is "on the call", in those words.
- **The close is an ask with next steps**, never a bare "Thank you": the transition question, what
  happens next this week, the booking line.

### Build in waves — a deck must not degrade or die mid-render
- **Wave 1:** the cover + the first 4–5 slides, locking the master template (title treatment, margins,
  accent, footer, page number). **Then** the rest in waves of ~5.
- **THE NO-DRIFT TEST after every wave:** put the newest slide beside slide 2 — same title position,
  margins, footer, accent, and level of craft? Late slides coming out thinner or plainer get fixed
  before continuing. The last slide must be indistinguishable in build quality from the first.
- **THE TURN NEVER ENDS AT A WAVE** — a wave that passes is followed by the next wave in the same
  response. "Say go and I'll continue" is banned.
- Render each slide as its own frame, in order, clearly labelled ("Slide 1 — Cover", "Slide 2 — Where
  you are", …); keep key content inside a safe margin (~80–100px at 1920×1080).

### Concrete specs (the difference between agency work and a slide generator)
- **Type scale at 1920×1080:** slide titles ~64–88px bold; divider titles ~96–120px; a big statement
  ~120–200px; body and support ~28–36px; bullets ~28–32px; eyebrows, labels, and the footer ~16–20px
  (often uppercase, tracked). Two sizes minimum between a headline and its support; generous line
  spacing (~1.3–1.5 on body).
- **Grid and margins:** a 12-column grid, ~80–100px outer margins, one consistent gutter; a strong
  left or centre alignment axis; nothing floating.
- **Compose to FILL the frame, balanced.** One idea per slide, but the layout uses the whole canvas —
  content paired with the member's cut-out, the offer stack object, a photo panel, a designed diagram,
  or balanced columns. Content crammed into the top third with an empty bottom half is the #1 cheap
  tell. "Whitespace" means breathing room around a full composition, not a blank lower slide.
- **Section dividers for rhythm** at the three bridge layers ("Your support" · "Your community" ·
  "The vehicle") — full-bleed or accent-ground slides that reset attention.
- **Deliberate layout variety:** title · mirror (their words, large, quoted) · the three things as three
  designed blocks (pain → what I give → outcome → proof line) · a big-statement slide · the first-30-days
  roadmap (four milestones) · the offer stack slide (the transparent object from `aa-offer-stack-design`) · the
  story slide (a photo or the member's cut-out with one pulled line) · the model slide (plain text,
  calm, one accent) · a quote slide only with a real, consented agent quote · the close with contact. No
  two consecutive content slides share a layout; never "headline + three text cards" twice.
- **Real visuals only:** the member's headshot or cut-out, the offer stack object, the product mockup,
  real photos the member dropped (a scrim behind any text over them), the toolkit's devices, clean
  icons in one style. Never a stock person, never a generated face, never a fabricated office or crowd,
  never one photo repeated to fill slides.
- **One cohesive template:** the title treatment, margins, accent, and the footer (the member's mark,
  left · page number, right) on every non-cover slide.

### Numbers on an opportunity deck (there are almost none — and that is correct)
- A number appears ONLY when the brief carries it from the member's own materials, labelled as theirs
  and with its source and date ("my production, 2025"; "our group, as of [Month YYYY]"). No projection,
  no "what you could earn", no comp-plan figure, no illustrative rev-share scenario on a slide — those
  belong to the private call.
- If a real number earns a visual, it is a clean, honest one: bars start at zero, time windows honest,
  values labelled on the chart, no 3D, no tilted pies, no dual axes. A proof grid of 2–4 real figures
  is the most a deck here ever carries.
- **THE SKEPTICAL-AGENT TEST:** the prospect will check a number on their phone mid-call. Every figure
  must survive that check or carry its source — otherwise it comes off the slide.

### Speaker talking points (deliver alongside the deck)
After the slides, give the member brief talking points for each slide as copyable text — 2–4 bullets of
what to actually SAY, drawn from the Conversion brief's "Say it like this" lines where it has them. The
deck supports the member speaking; it is not the script. No talking point ever says a compensation
number out loud on a first call; the money line is the model slide's: *"I'll walk you through every one
of them on a call."*

### Cheap-deck tells (fix before presenting)
Text walls and bullet soup · tiny type and timid hierarchy · ten near-identical text slides · the
default-template look · low contrast or text on a busy photo with no scrim · clip-art or mismatched
icons · edge-to-edge slides with no breathing room · a bare "Thank you" close · a model slide that
reads like a comp plan · a feature dump where the brief wanted three things.

## PART B — THE DOCUMENTS (US Letter portrait — the 1-pager, the welcome pack, the comparison sheet)

### The page contract
- **US Letter portrait, 2550×3300 px at 300dpi** (A4 via tweak when the Book's locale says so); the
  comparison sheet may run landscape (3300×2550) when five columns need the width. Every page its own
  clearly labelled frame, in order; content inside a safe margin (~0.5–0.75 in); nothing touching an
  edge; nothing sitting outside the frame to be clipped when it becomes a PDF page.
- **One cohesive template:** consistent margins, a clear type scale (section headers large, body
  ~11–12pt, an eyebrow above headers), one accent, page numbers on multi-page pieces, a small running
  footer (the member's name or the piece's title) — this consistency is what reads premium.
- **Body ≥ 11pt, contrast that passes on a phone** — an agent reads the 1-pager and the comparison
  sheet on a phone, from a DM.
- **Strong hierarchy, generous whitespace:** one dominant idea per page, copy broken into short
  sections, bullets, and pull-quotes — never a wall of text. A page that reads as a Word document fails
  the craft bar.
- **LAYOUT QUOTA on multi-page pieces:** at least three visibly different page layouts across the
  welcome pack (a full-width opener, a two-column page, a table page, a checklist page) and never the
  same layout on more than two consecutive pages — same grid, same type scale, different rooms.
- **Nothing overlaps; nothing is cut off.** Every element gets its own space; text never runs under a
  photo or off the page.

### The 1-pager's anatomy (fills the body `aa-brand-kit-design` reserved)
Header third = the cover's five answers, compressed: the member's name (or the organization lockup)
and role line · the positioning line · the who-you-help row · the proof chip if real · the member's
cut-out or the treated culture photo. Body two-thirds = the Conversion brief: the UVP one-liner as the
headline · the three outcome blocks (pain → what I give → outcome) · "What partnering looks like" (the
first 30 days, four lines) · the offer stack strip (the transparent object, small, when it exists) ·
the proof line · the one-line story · the next step: **"Book a call with me"** + the booking link in
FULL plain text + the comment keyword where the Brain coined one. Bottom edge = the compliance strip +
the required disclaimer verbatim. One page, always.

### The welcome pack's anatomy (what a new partner receives on day one — 4–8 pages)
1. **Cover** — "Welcome to [organization]" (or "Welcome, partner" when there is no organization name),
   the member's mark, the date line, a designed name slot the member fills per partner ("For: ______").
2. **Day one** — the "what you get day one" list from the Partner Offer as a checklist with real
   checkbox squares, each item one bold lead-in + one outcome line; "and everything my brokerage
   provides — your onboarding walkthrough covers it."
3. **Your first 30 days** — the week-by-week table from the Partner Offer (| Week | You | Me | Done
   when |), rendered as four designed rows.
4. **What I teach you first** — the three lessons (| Lesson | After this you can | What we build
   together |).
5. **Where everything lives** — the weekly call (day, time, link slot), the group, the training library,
   the digital product with its honest status line — only what exists today, from the Book's How You
   Operate and Your Offer chapters.
6. **Who to call** — the member's contact in full; the broker, upline, or admin contacts ONLY when the
   member supplies them (a designed empty row otherwise — never an invented name).
7. **The closing page** — one warm paragraph in the member's voice and the compliance strip.
No page carries a split, cap, stock, or income figure: *"your compensation details are in your brokerage
agreement and the call we had"* is the only line that touches money.

### The comparison sheet's anatomy (model TYPES, one page — the leave-behind)
- **The columns are model TYPES**, never brokerage names: cloud brokerage with revenue share ·
  franchise split (with or without a cap) · flat-fee / 100% · independent or local brokerage, and the
  local team — the member's own model type first or last, never in the middle, labelled "mine" in one
  quiet word. Include only the types the Model Positioning Sheet or the prospect's situation calls for
  (three or four columns read; five is the ceiling).
- **The rows are the same five blocks for every column** — *how it works* (structure: compensation
  shape, additional income if any, equity if any, support, operations — never numbers) · *who it tends
  to fit* (by career stage, production, and goals only — never a protected characteristic) · *what to
  love* (credit where due, exactly as Mike grants it) · *the honest trade-off* (one or two, stated as
  trade-offs, not flaws; the member's own model's trade-off said straight) · *the question to ask*
  (one, model-agnostic).
- **Every cell is the same length.** A longer or bolder column for the member's model reads as a
  pitch; the sheet's honesty is its whole value.
- **Numbers: structure only.** No splits, caps, fees, tiers, stock values, or income — for any column,
  including the member's own, unless their compliance policy explicitly allows a public figure for
  THEIR plan and it is cited to their brokerage's document with its date. Other models' numbers never.
- **The header** — "How the main brokerage models work — compared honestly" · "by [Name]" · **"Last
  updated [Month YYYY] — brokerage plans change; verify anything current with the brokerage itself."**
- **The footer** — the two questions every agent should ask anyone, including the member (*"Show me the
  training I'd get, today."* · *"Show me an agent like me you've helped."*), the booking line, the
  compliance strip + the required disclaimer verbatim.
- **Never:** a ranking, a score, a star, a tick-and-cross grid, a "winner", a named brokerage other
  than the member's own, a former brokerage, a claim about another brokerage made by the member.
