## The framework — the transcript's wording is the truth

**Listen → Validate → Reframe → Invite** (bonus: the objection framework; `/46`). "Objections are not rejection —
they're a request for more clarity." Stay calm, listen, reframe; don't fight, don't get defensive. Welcome it:
*"I get that — I used to feel that way too — but … — would you be open to seeing …?"*

| Step (vault) | What it is, in Mike's words | The tell that it was skipped |
|---|---|---|
| **Listen** | Hear the objection without reacting. Do not interrupt — "this will ruin everything." Let them vent, nod, show genuine curiosity. "Tell me more about why you feel that way." | The member answered before the prospect finished |
| **Validate** | Make them feel understood; acknowledge the concern as normal. "A lot of great agents feel that way." Then the ninja line: "a lot of the top agents who joined us felt that way too" (future pace, assume the close). | The member went straight to the counter-argument |
| **Reframe** | Shift the lens; offer a new perspective that reduces the fear. Use data, stories, analogies, your own experience. | The member repeated the pitch instead of changing the angle |
| **Invite** | Open the door — not pressure. "Would you be open to exploring what that looks like?" Warm, inviting, welcoming. | The call ended on the reframe with no question |

**Every lesson also carries "the additional questions I ask."** They sit between Validate and Reframe: the question
that finds the real fear. That is what the handlers below call **the question to ask**.

## The seven archetypes and the hidden fear (`/46`)

| # | Archetype | Hidden fear (Mike's words) | Typically heard from |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | I'm happy where I am | change feels risky | anyone comfortable who still took the call |
| A2 | I don't have time | disruption of the business — already overwhelmed, burnt out | producing agents, team leaders |
| A3 | Compensation structure | short-term expenses outweigh long-term gain | 100% / flat-fee agents, capped agents |
| A4 | Support and leads | they won't succeed without their current safety net | agents on a team, lead-fed agents |
| A5 | Recruiting isn't for me | sees attraction as pressure or MLM, not leadership | nearly everyone, at least once |
| A6 | Brand recognition | losing credibility with clients | top producers, agents with a brand, broker-owners |
| A7 | Fear and uncertainty | lack of belief in themselves or in the unknown | new agents, big-decision moments |

"Most agents will throw one to three of these at you before committing." Once you know the archetype you know the
fear; address the fear and the objection disappears. **The archetype assignments below are the build's, made from
the lesson's hidden-fear definitions — `/46` does not assign the fifteen one by one.** Primary first, secondary after
the dot.

## The fifteen

Shape of every entry: archetype · what it probably means · root fear · the question to ask · Mike's reframe ·
invite · the mistake to avoid · proof / story slot · brokerage slot. The coach fills the two slots from
`identity/proof.md`, `identity/story-bank.md`, and `identity/brokerage-model.md` — never from these notes.

### 1. "I'm happy where I am" — `/48`
- **Archetype:** A1 · A7
- **What it probably means:** comfortable, not fulfilled. They booked the call, so something is missing — usually they
  are not hitting the goal they told you about.
- **Root fear:** change feels risky; comfort is safer than the unknown.
- **The question to ask:** *"If you were already getting everything you needed, would you even be open to this
  conversation?"* · *"If you're not hitting your goals there, what makes you believe things are going to change?"*
  · *"Based on the vision you shared with me, is your brokerage even the right vehicle to get you there?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "A lot of great agents feel comfortable until something forces them to look around" — and
  successful agents get comfortable staying uncomfortable. Keep what you like; add the thing you told me you want
  (their pain point, their exit strategy, their time freedom). If nothing changes, nothing changes.
- **Invite:** *"If I could show you a way to keep exactly what you like and add [the thing they named], would you
  want to see how that works?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** arguing that they are not really happy. Talking about rev share when they said production.
- **Proof / story slot:** an agent of their type who was "fine" and then grew — their own words from `proof.md`.
- **Brokerage slot:** none needed.

### 2. "It seems like a pyramid scheme" — `/49`
- **Archetype:** A5 · A7
- **What it probably means:** somebody explained it badly, or they are looking at the shape of the org chart instead
  of where the money comes from. Mike calls this one of his favourites because it is the easiest to clear.
- **Root fear:** being pulled into something illegitimate; looking foolish.
- **The question to ask:** *"Can I walk you through exactly where the money comes from, with the real mechanics?"*
  (This one is answered by the math, not by more questions.)
- **Mike's reframe:** "I hear you — a lot of people explain it improperly. Honestly, I used to think that too, until
  somebody properly explained it, and it changed everything." Revenue share is paid from the **company's** dollar,
  not the agent's — the company reinvesting in the people who help it grow. You are paid only when agents **close
  deals**, never for recruiting. At a franchise, the company dollar flows up to the franchise owner; at a cloud
  brokerage with no offices and lower overhead, it flows back to agents. A pyramid scheme pays you to sign people
  up (and is illegal); here, "if I bring you on and you don't close, I get nothing — I just spent all that time with
  you. I have to help you perform."
- **Invite:** *"Let me show you exactly how it works with the actual mechanics, so you can see for yourself."*
- **Mistake to avoid:** making them feel silly for asking. Getting defensive. Skipping the walk-through. Quoting
  anyone's earnings — the walk-through is **mechanics** (where the dollar flows), never a projection; this is
  private-call material (`identity/compliance.md`).
- **Proof / story slot:** the member's own "I thought that too" moment from `story-bank.md`, if they have one.
- **Brokerage slot:** the member's plan mechanics in plain words (from `brokerage-model.md`).

### 3. "I don't want to recruit" — `/50`
- **Archetype:** A5
- **What it probably means:** other agents forced recruiting on them and left a bad taste. They want to sell.
- **Root fear:** being turned into a salesperson for a brokerage; pressure; MLM.
- **The question to ask:** *"At your current brokerage, when somebody reaches out asking which brokerage they should
  go to, what do you tell them?"* (their brokerage) · *"Do you get compensated for that?"* (no) · *"And when they
  bring somebody in, what happens?"* (nothing).
- **Mike's reframe:** "Totally get it — recruiting doesn't excite you; that's normal. Most agents don't want to
  recruit either. The good news is you don't have to. Sell as usual — and if other agents are attracted to what you
  are already doing, you have the option to benefit from it. It's attraction, not chasing." Every agent already
  does this; they just don't call it that. How do you build a team? You recruit. A brokerage? You recruit. Here you
  are compensated for the same thing without the risk, liability, and "adult daycare."
- **Invite:** *"If you could simply do your business as usual and, over time, build a retirement plan from agents
  who joined because of your success — would you want to see what that looks like?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** selling rev share hard to someone who just said they don't want to recruit. Quoting what
  "my partners" earn — Mike's figures in this lesson are his story and never go in a member's handler. Citing a
  brokerage-wide statistic (the framework lesson's "88% aren't recruiting") unless the Brain holds a dated source
  for the member's own brokerage.
- **Proof / story slot:** an agent in the member's organization who joined to produce and never recruited.
- **Brokerage slot:** how the member's plan pays on production, in one line.

### 4. "I need to think about it" — bonus lesson
- **Archetype:** A7
- **What it probably means:** one or two questions are not fully answered. "If you were 100% certain there'd be
  nothing to think about" — so there is a question; find it. Often: a spouse.
- **Root fear:** making the wrong big decision; explaining it badly to someone at home.
- **The question to ask:** *"That's fair — it's an important decision. What specifically do you feel you need to
  think through?"* Then probe until the actual question surfaces.
- **Mike's reframe:** "Thoughts equal questions; questions need answers. My job is to figure out what they are."
  If it is a spouse: "You're still learning the model — we just went through it, it has layers, and it took me
  months. Explaining it second-hand is hard. I talk with husbands and wives all the time; let's get on a call
  together and I'll walk them through how this specifically impacts your family." The family framing: one active
  income stream versus a residual that can continue for the family.
- **Invite:** *"If we could address those right now and give you clarity, would you be open to exploring this
  further?"* · for the spouse: *"Want to pick a time the three of us can talk for twenty minutes?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** letting them leave without naming the question. Pressure. Stating a brokerage benefit as
  fact for a brokerage you have not verified (the lesson's health-benefit savings figures are Mike's brokerage,
  his estimate — never repeat them as the member's).
- **Proof / story slot:** a couple or family the member helped decide, if in `story-bank.md`.
- **Brokerage slot:** what the member's brokerage offers a family — benefits, inheritance of residual, stock — only
  what `brokerage-model.md` confirms.

### 5. "I've already been talking to another agent" — `/52`
- **Archetype:** A7 · A4
- **What it probably means:** they are sponsor shopping, which is right — "most sellers interview multiple agents."
  Usually it is down to one other person.
- **Root fear:** choosing the wrong partner; at many cloud brokerages changing sponsor later means leaving for
  months and coming back.
- **The question to ask:** *"What do you like about what they have to offer?"* · *"Have they shown you proof of
  what they have to offer?"* ("This is how I win a lot — most can't show it.")
- **Mike's reframe:** "Thanks for sharing that — I appreciate the honesty, and I love that you're talking to other
  agents; it shows how serious you are. Here's the thing: who you partner with is everything. Agents don't join
  brokerages, they join people. The question is who can actually guide you and provide the most value." Then
  show, don't tell: the training, the proof, the people.
- **Invite:** *"Would you be open to hearing how I specifically support my agents, so you can decide which leader
  is the right fit?"* If the other sponsor genuinely offers something the member does not and the brokerage allows
  co-sponsorship: *"Let's co-sponsor — you get the best of both."*
- **Mistake to avoid:** one negative word about the other sponsor (cardinal rule two). Competing by comparison
  instead of by proof. Telling them what they want to hear.
- **Proof / story slot:** the member's strongest shown proof — their training, their interviews, agents who applied it.
- **Brokerage slot:** the brokerage's sponsor-change rule and whether co-sponsorship exists.

### 6. "This other agent said X about you" — `/53`
- **Archetype:** A7 · A6
- **What it probably means:** someone competing for the same agent tried to tear the member down. The prospect is
  checking whether it is true.
- **Root fear:** trusting the wrong person; being embarrassed later.
- **The question to ask:** *"If somebody's strategy is to tear others down and talk negatively, what does that say
  about them?"* · *"Don't you believe that if somebody is confident in what they offer, they wouldn't have to tear
  anyone else down?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "I respect that you're doing your homework — that's smart. Opinions vary. What I'd suggest is
  look at the results, the leadership, and the alignment with your goals. That speaks louder than gossip." And the
  stance: "On this call I haven't said a negative word about anyone. I'm confident in the value I provide, so I'm
  going to focus on how I can help you."
- **Invite:** *"Would you be open to seeing for yourself how it worked for other agents, so you can decide on facts,
  not hearsay?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** defending point by point. Retaliating or naming the person. Mike's "if you don't see the
  value, don't join" is confidence, not a dare — the member's version stays warm.
- **Proof / story slot:** results the prospect can verify themselves (interviews, agents they can talk to).
- **Brokerage slot:** none.

### 7. "My brokerage / my team gives me leads" — `/54`
- **Archetype:** A4
- **What it probably means:** a team agent whose team lead feeds them leads at a split. The safety net is real;
  so is the dependence.
- **Root fear:** losing the pipeline; not knowing how to generate their own.
- **The question to ask:** *"What percent of your deals actually come from those leads — and at what split?"* ·
  *"Do you want to be dependent on someone else to operate your business forever?"* · *"Why do you believe all
  successful agents learn to generate their own leads?"* · the future-pace: *"What happens to your business if your
  team lead stops, sells, or can't work anymore?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "That's a real benefit you're enjoying right now — many agents value that **at first**." (Plant
  "at first.") The math in their numbers: ten deals on a 50% split is five deals' income; ten deals you generated
  yourself is double. You already know how to close and follow up; the one missing skill is lead generation — "and
  that's what I'll show you how to do."
- **Invite:** *"What if I showed you how to generate your own pipeline so you're not dependent on anyone's leads,
  and you keep more of your income — would that be worth the conversation?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** dismissing the leads or the team lead (cardinal rules). Promising to double their business
  — frame it as what the member will **teach and show**, never a guaranteed outcome.
- **Proof / story slot:** an agent who went from fed leads to their own pipeline.
- **Brokerage slot:** none.

### 8. "Your brokerage has no offices" — `/55`
- **Archetype:** A6 · A4
- **What it probably means:** they picture meeting clients at an office and worry how it looks.
- **Root fear:** losing credibility with clients; losing a place to work and belong.
- **The question to ask:** *"If the lack of an office actually meant more money in your pocket and more freedom,
  would you want to explore how that works?"* · *"How often do your clients ask to meet at the office?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "That's an important factor for you — I get it. Many agents are used to a physical office, but
  the industry has shifted; most agents rarely go in." Mike's own story: his previous independent brokerage had
  the most beautiful office in the city — he was the only one who used it, to record video, and every client wanted
  the coffee shop, the restaurant, their own home. The money saved on brick-and-mortar goes back to agents in rev
  share, stock, and technology; co-working spaces and professional settings cover the rest.
- **Invite:** *"Would you be open to a partnership where you get the local office through someone in our
  organization?"* — the two practical fixes: a partner in the member's organization who has an office locally, or
  co-sponsorship with a local team that does.
- **Mistake to avoid:** mocking offices or the agents who use them. Asserting the member's brokerage has an office
  program (the lesson's co-working access is Mike's brokerage; verify the member's).
- **Proof / story slot:** the member's own "where I actually meet clients" story.
- **Brokerage slot:** office / co-working access the member's brokerage actually provides; local partners with space.

### 9. "I don't like your brokerage's branding" — `/56`
- **Archetype:** A6
- **What it probably means:** a top producer, an agent with a brand, or a broker-owner for whom brand is identity.
  Less common now than it was, but it still comes.
- **Root fear:** their brand gets swallowed by a corporate look they didn't choose.
- **The question to ask:** *"If I showed you how to elevate your personal brand far beyond any brokerage name,
  would you be open to seeing that?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "Thanks for sharing that — branding matters to you, I hear you. Some people worry the
  corporate brand doesn't fit their style, but here's the key: at a cloud brokerage, **you** are the brand. Your
  name, your team, your marketing — that's what clients care about, not a company logo." Independent cloud
  brokerages give far more brand flexibility than a franchise with strict rules; the one real rule is the local
  board's requirement on brokerage-logo size next to yours, which "doesn't mean much."
- **Invite:** *"Can I show you a few agents in our group whose brands look nothing like the brokerage's?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** defending the logo. Naming a franchise brand to contrast with (cardinal rule one — the
  lesson's balloon reference stays out of the member's mouth).
- **Proof / story slot:** the member's own brand (from `brand-visual.md`) and branded agents in the organization.
- **Brokerage slot:** the brokerage's branding policy and the local board's logo rule (from `compliance.md`).

### 10. "I'm already doing well at my current brokerage" — `/57`
- **Archetype:** A1 · A3
- **What it probably means:** a successful agent, loyal, usually the most loyal — but on the call for a reason.
- **Root fear:** disrupting what works; also, quietly, no exit strategy.
- **The question to ask:** *"Are you building long-term wealth and residual income, or just more closings?"* ·
  *"If you're one of the top agents in your brokerage, how do you plan on getting to the next level?"* (if you're
  the average of the five around you and you're at the top, who pulls you up?) · *"You mentioned you want to do
  better — correct? What made you agree to this call?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "That's great to hear — I love that you're doing well. Successful agents like you are usually
  the most loyal, and I respect that. Success today is great; positioning for tomorrow is what creates legacy." As
  income goes up, expenses go up; many top producers have no exit strategy and are tired of closing fifty deals a
  year to maintain the lifestyle. And: successful agents attract agents — the member can feature them (interviews,
  the channel), give them exposure, and coach the agents who come through them, without disrupting production.
- **Invite:** *"If I could show you how to add real wealth-building to your current success without disrupting
  what you're already doing, would you be open to that?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** pitching production training to a top producer. Flattery without a question. Any income
  figure. "Challenge the ego" gently — a question, never a dig.
- **Proof / story slot:** a top producer in the organization who kept producing and built an organization.
- **Brokerage slot:** how the member's plan rewards a top producer's production (awards, stock) — only what the
  Brain confirms.

### 11. "I have closings I need to finish first" — `/58`
- **Archetype:** A2
- **What it probably means:** they want to come. The timing is the question, not the decision.
- **Root fear:** disrupting clients mid-transaction; losing commission on deals under contract.
- **The question to ask:** *"When is your last closing?"* · *"Have you read the termination section of your
  independent contractor agreement?"* · *"What if I helped you build a proactive plan so the transition is the least
  disruptive possible?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "Of course your current clients come first — that's the sign of a true professional. We can
  time your transition so nothing gets interrupted; many agents move right after closings with zero disruption."
  Often a solo agent can bring clients over by terminating and relisting the same day; team agents usually can't;
  always check the ICA — if it keeps a cut of in-flight commissions, plan for the last closing instead. Then "the
  one that works like magic": work together behind the scenes for one to three months before the move — strategy,
  momentum, record the launch video — so the week they arrive, they already feel like they've been here.
- **Invite:** *"Would you be open to planning the move together now, so we maximize the benefits the day those
  deals close?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** pushing them to come now. Giving a legal opinion on their contract (point them at the
  ICA; it is their read and their broker's). Promising what the new brokerage will do with their listings before
  the Brokerage Model Expert has the transfer steps. Any written "switching transition plan" waits for the
  inducement-rules review in `compliance.md` (`cv-follow-up` holds that asset behind the gate).
- **Proof / story slot:** an agent the member transitioned without disruption.
- **Brokerage slot:** the brokerage's exact transfer steps (license, listings, MLS, email, signage).

### 12. "My brokerage is cheaper — I keep 100%" — `/59`
- **Archetype:** A3
- **What it probably means:** a flat-fee or 100% agent doing the math on the split and not on the business.
- **Root fear:** paying more for the same thing; short-term cost.
- **The question to ask:** *"Would you rather keep 100% of what you earn today, or build a bigger business with
  residual income and ownership?"* · *"Would you rather 100% of your current deals, or training that helps you
  double them?"* · *"How do you plan on doubling at your current brokerage?"* · *"If you're paying almost nothing
  in, how does the brokerage fund training, tools, and support?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "That's appealing — keeping more of your commission is good. But it doesn't matter if your
  split is 100% if you're not growing." The value has to be worth more than the split — "it's worth 50%, 100%." A
  brokerage running on a flat fee can't fund much, so agents pay for coaching, courses, and events out of pocket.
  Some models let a producing agent earn back more than the split (brokerage slot).
- **Invite:** *"If I could show you how you could net more, not just save on splits, would you want to see that
  breakdown?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** arguing splits. Quoting another brokerage's cap or split (cardinal rule one — and never
  assume the member's numbers; `brokerage-model.md` holds them). The lesson's "tripled their business" stories are
  Mike's — the member uses only their own `proof.md`.
- **Proof / story slot:** an agent who moved from a 100% shop and grew — in their words.
- **Brokerage slot:** the member's split, cap, and any production-award mechanics — exact, from the Brain.

### 13. "I've already capped at my brokerage" — `/60`
- **Archetype:** A3
- **What it probably means:** a high producer, often from a franchise cap structure, riding 100% until the cap
  resets. They earned it and want to keep it.
- **Root fear:** giving up the capped months; paying a split again.
- **The question to ask:** *"Does capping build wealth, or does it reset every year?"* · *"When does your cap
  reset?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "You're clearly producing at a high level — that's an achievement to be proud of. Here's the
  thing: in a cloud model, capping is just the start — you also get stock, rev share, and more." Two paths: if the
  member's brokerage honours a cap earned elsewhere, come now at the equivalent terms; if not, plan the move for the
  reset date and build the strategy proactively so day one launches "full tilt."
- **Invite:** *"Would you be open to seeing how to turn your production into wealth, not just capped
  commissions?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** assuming a cap-honouring program exists (the lesson's is Mike's brokerage; "forgive me — you
  should know the answer for yours"). Disparaging profit share or the franchise.
- **Proof / story slot:** a capped producer who moved and what changed, in their words.
- **Brokerage slot:** cap-honouring / deferral program yes or no; the cap and reset mechanics.

### 14. "I just switched brokerages" — `/61`
- **Archetype:** A1 · A6
- **What it probably means:** they like what the member offers but just paid for signs, cards, and a rebrand.
  Usually within the last few months.
- **Root fear:** looking flaky; wasting the money they just spent.
- **The question to ask:** *"Do you think it would work better to build at a brokerage that helps you reach your
  long-term goals, instead of having to switch again later?"* · *"Wouldn't it be better to make the change before
  you've spent months branding yourself there and people recognise you as being there?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "I hear you — you just made a move, and nobody wants to bounce around. But sometimes agents
  switch for a short-term fix and realise later it wasn't the long-term solution. The key isn't how long you've
  been somewhere; it's whether you're in the best place for your future." It is far easier to switch now, before
  the content, the brand, and the systems are entrenched — nobody will notice. If they are not ready: "No problem —
  give it a shot. Let's stay in touch; I'll check in in a couple of months." Stay in relationship; "they all
  eventually switch — it's now or later."
- **Invite:** *"If I could show you a way that makes this your last move ever, would you at least want to see it?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** pushing. A negative word about the brokerage they just joined.
- **Proof / story slot:** an agent who moved twice in a year and why the second move stuck.
- **Brokerage slot:** none.

### 15. "I love my broker" — `/62`
- **Archetype:** A1 · A7
- **What it probably means:** loyalty, and a real relationship. Mike's own story: still close with his past broker,
  who supported the move.
- **Root fear:** betraying someone who has been good to them.
- **The question to ask:** *"When it's time for your broker to retire or sell, do you believe they'll stop because
  of their relationship with you?"*
- **Mike's reframe:** "That's awesome — I love my past broker too. I respect loyalty; it says a lot about your
  character. Loyalty is great as long as it isn't costing your future. You can love and respect your broker and
  still make the best move for your family, your income, and your legacy." A broker who loves you back keeps the
  door open — Mike's did: "we knew you'd make a decision like this one day; we were waiting for when."
- **Invite:** *"If I could show you how to honour your loyalty while still setting yourself up for more, would you
  be open to seeing how that works?"*
- **Mistake to avoid:** one negative word about their broker (cardinal rule two). The lesson's stories of brokers
  sending ugly emails after an agent leaves are Mike's private perspective — they never go in a handler or a call.
- **Proof / story slot:** the member's own relationship with their former broker (never named: "my former
  broker at an independent").
- **Brokerage slot:** none.

## Brokerage-specific objections — the slot the Brokerage Model Expert fills

The launching doc's examples: *"[Brokerage] isn't for me"* · *"The split isn't worth it"* · *"I'm already at a cloud
brokerage"* · *"I don't want to leave my brand."* These are handled on the same four steps, but the reframe depends
