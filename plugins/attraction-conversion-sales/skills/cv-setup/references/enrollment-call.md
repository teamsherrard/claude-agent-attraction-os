# The Enrollment Call — how Step 1 of the sitting builds the member's script

*Read by `cv-setup` at Step 1 (and on "update my call script"). The method is Mike's sequence of events, exactly
as lesson 42 states it (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/conversion-doctrine.md` §1–§3, §10). The questions on every
step come from `references/question-funnel.md`; the pre-handled objections from `references/objection-playbook.md`
(the playbook itself is Step 3 — the script carries the three or four this member's type throws most, in short
form). Not a script to read; a script to know. Every line is built from the member's Brain so it passes the
any-agent test.*

## What to read (silent — nothing is asked)
`brain.md` first · `identity/voice.md`, `voice-samples.md`, `voice-print.md` (the script sounds like them or it is
useless) · `identity/journey.md` incl. `## Why join me` (the 60-second story) · `identity/offer.md` (the Partner

· `identity/avatars.md` (the primary type — the script is written for them; a secondary type gets a short variant

## The spine — both lengths share it (minutes shown 60 / 30)
The member sees these step names, with the plain gloss beside each the first time it appears: **Discovery ·
Diagnosis · Fit · Positioning · Questions (their questions and objections) · Next Step.** Each step in the document
carries **Say it like this:** and the line, in the member's voice.

**OPEN · rapport (0–5 / 0–2).** Energy; looking forward to it; one specific thing from their booking answers or
their content; *"How's your business been this year — what's working, what isn't?"* Rapport comes before any question.

**1 · DISCOVERY — current state (5–15 / 2–7).** The discovery questions for the primary type (the funnel): what
piqued your interest · what do you love about where you are · what's frustrating you most · what's missing. Let
every answer finish. The member talks about 30% of the call.

**2 · DIAGNOSIS — go deeper (15–22 / 7–11).** What it's costing them · what they've tried · *"if nothing changes,
twelve months from now?"* · *"what would [their goal] mean for you and your family?"* Mirror their vision back in
their words. This opens the loop.

**3 · FIT — the three things that matter most (22–27 / 11–14).** *"Based on what you told me, there are three
things that matter most here: [pain 1], [pain 2], [goal]."* Chosen live from what they said, never from a list. The
member's own moment from `journey.md`: *"I was there — [the story's hook]."*

**4 · POSITIONING — the bridge, through their goals (27–40 / 14–21).** Three layers, only as far as their pain
goes: **the member's support** (the program by name — "[name] is a [type]: [cadence]" — the framework's steps, the
90-day outcome, the first 30 days as a partner) · **the partner and the community** (as it is today — real and small
beats big and promised; the 3-way partner named from `operations.md`, or "my partner") · **the brokerage's model,
in plain English** from `brokerage-model.md` — production pain → the training and the member's system, "and
everything [brokerage] provides — I'll walk you through it"; exit or time-freedom pain → residual income as the
vehicle, every number from the member's own materials and labeled. Proof: one real line from `proof.md`; none yet →
*"you'd be early, and here's what I'll do for you in week one."*

**5 · QUESTIONS — their questions and objections (40–50 / 21–26).** *"What questions do you have? What would stop
you?"* The three or four objections this type throws most, pre-handled on Listen → Validate → Reframe → Invite in
the member's voice (short form; the full handlers are the playbook), then the commitment questions: *"If we can
solve [what they named], would you be ready to go all in?"*

**6 · NEXT STEP — assume the close, make it foolproof (50–60 / 26–30).** Energy up; future-pace: *"This is going to
be an exciting new chapter. The number one thing people say is they wish they'd come earlier. When it comes to a
transition date, what feels least disruptive? Time's never perfect — what works, so we can put a plan in place and
you launch with momentum?"* Then exactly what happens next — Day 1 as the offer defines it (the welcome call, what is
handed over, the first lesson, the group and the weekly call when the member runs one, the first date on the
calendar) — with confidence and clarity: *"What's the best email to send the steps to? You'll get one email from me
today with how to join and what happens after."* Never "let me know what you think."

## The three endings (`/42`) — every script carries all three
- **Ready →** the NEXT-STEPS EMAIL, as a structure the member personalizes (never a verbatim template — Mike's own
  text was shown on screen, not read aloud): a first paragraph in their words (`[what they said that showed you
  listened]`) · the model video link · the page with everything the member provides · `JOIN STEPS — [brokerage]` as
  a labeled block: the exact link, the exact button, "when it asks who introduced you, put [member's name]", "this
  doesn't notify your current broker", "solo or team" — filled from the brokerage's onboarding document, or left
  clearly marked for the member. Then the WELCOME EMAIL structure (the personal welcome · the walkthrough video · the
  community link · the training links · "questions come to me first, then [support]") and the line that books the
  week-one strategy call.
- **Hesitant →** *"I'd love you to meet [partner] — [one true edification line] — they can answer anything about the
  model. Does [day] at [time, time zone] work?"* The edification line stacks real, verifiable things, never an income
  figure (doctrine §5). No partner on file → "my partner", and the line stays.
- **Not ready →** *"No pressure at all — this is about the right time for you. Let's connect in a couple of weeks and
  see how things are going."* The resources email still goes out today, to everyone, ready or not; only the join
  steps are held for the ready.

## The money rule (doctrine §10) — in the script's own words, once
Compensation is **answered, never led with.** The booking answers tell the member the brokerage, so the splits, caps
and fees are known before the call — the math is broken down only when THAT is the pain. Production pain → revenue
share is not mentioned unless they ask. Residual income is a vehicle for an exit, never the pitch. Every number is
the member's brokerage's, labeled; no income claims, no projections, nothing about anyone's earnings. The script
says this in one short box at the top of Positioning: **"If they ask about money: answer it plainly from your
model, then back to their goal."**

## The 30-minute version and the card
After the full call, **THE 30-MINUTE CALL** as its own section: the same spine at the shorter minutes, the two or
three questions per step that matter most, the same three endings. Then **THE CARD** — one page: the six step names
with the one question each and the one line that moves to the next step — for the member's second screen. They run
the call with the card open, not the script.

## Page one — YOUR WEEK, then the psychology (doctrine §1, in the member's words)
The document opens, before the call itself, with two short bands. Plain words; no file names, no lesson numbers.

**YOUR WEEK** — six lines, exactly these (the time in line 4 is the member's Morning Call Prep hour; `later` or
`declined` → "Monday morning, once your Morning Call Prep is on, …"):
1. Ten minutes a day with Stacy — say "train me", here or by voice in this project.
2. Before every partner call, say "prep my call".
3. After every call, say "debrief my call".
4. Monday morning your follow-ups for the week arrive, each with a reason.
5. On the 1st, the agents who've gone quiet — with a reason to reach out, or "leave it".
6. The numbers you watch: calls held, 3-ways, joins.

**HOW TO USE THIS** — the first band of the call, opening with four lines on the psychology of enrollment, then the
three how-to lines. The four, as printed (the member's "you", never a quote, never a lesson):
- You aren't selling anyone a move — you're enrolling them: helping an agent say yes to their own future. Influence
  pulls people in; pressure pushes them away.
- Three tells that you've slipped into pressure: you talked over them; the call became about your goal instead of
  theirs; you felt a flicker of desperation. Any one of them — stop, ask a question, and let the whole answer finish.
- Enroll with certainty. When they're ready, let them hear that you believe in them: "Based on everything you've told
  me, I know you're going to do well here — this is the exact training to go to first."
- The agent decides. Give them room: "This is about the right time for you — let's connect in a week or two and see
  how things are going." Everyone comes when it's their time; your job is to be the obvious call when they do.
Then the three how-to lines: know it, don't read it; the card on your second screen; the 30% rule.

## Gates and rules
- The script is private-call material; the two email structures inside it are public → the first line of
  `identity/compliance.md`, `Status:` — `unset` → the two blocks are built and stamped *"hold until your compliance
  basics are set"*, the brokerage's wording kept as one bracket; the rest renders. `set` → apply, one reminder at the
  close. `confirmed` → apply.
- Cardinal rules on every line: nothing against another brokerage, sponsor or person; former brokerages unnamed
  ("a franchise", "an independent"). No income claims anywhere. Zero fabrication — no borrowed stories, no join step
  the brokerage's document does not state. Never a protected characteristic.
- The quality bar: the delete test · the any-agent test (a line any leader could say is rewritten with this member's
  story, market, proof) · the so-what test · no hedging · no filler headings. Banned words and the recruiter register
  per `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/how-we-speak.md` §7 — "enroll", "partner", never "recruit".
- Stamp `Used-where` on each placed story (the one line of `story-bank.md` this plugin may write).
