# The Question Funnel — the questions the script and the call prep lean on

*Read by `cv-setup` while it builds the enrollment call (Step 1) and the presentation (Step 2); `cv-call-prep`
and `cv-trainer` read it by name for a named prospect or a type. Method: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/conversion-doctrine.md`
§4 — "Questions are where the game is won. Statements push. Questions pull." Questions shift the focus onto them,
uncover pains, desires and objections, and open loops only the member can close. Three categories, in order — that
is the funnel. Nothing here is a document of its own in the sitting: the questions live inside the script and the
presentation, in the member's voice.*

## The three categories (Mike's, `/44`, with the openers from `bonus/perfect-presentation`)
**1 · Discovery — current state.** Pinpoint where they are and what frustrates them.
Mike's three: *What do you love about your current brokerage?* · *What's frustrating you most right now?* · *What do
you feel is missing?* The opener set: *What piqued your interest and made you open to this conversation?* · *What
are your biggest challenges right now?* · *What's holding you back from hitting your goals?* · *Are you on track to
hit your target this year?*

**2 · Vision — future state.** Open the loop the model and the offer will close.
*If nothing changes at your current brokerage, where do you see yourself twelve months from now?* · *What would
doubling your business mean for you and your family?* · *What would make you feel confident in achieving your
potential?* · *What's your five-to-ten-year vision?*

**3 · Commitment — bridging the gap.** Only after 1 and 2; built from their own words.
*If I could show you a way to [their vision] without any extra time away from your family, would you be open to
it?* · *What's more important — repeating another year in the same spot, or [their outcome, in their words]?* · *If
we can solve [the problem they named], would you be ready to go all in?*

**Why it works:** they articulate their own problems ("now it's real because they believe it — they said it"), they
paint their own desired future, and the member positions the model and the offer to exactly what they said — so
the close feels designed for them, because it was. The sales loop: gather the ammunition at the start, tailor to
every pain and goal, pre-handle the objections you can already see, loop back at the end by solving each problem
they named.

## Adapted per type (the shape stays; the examples change)
| Type | Discovery leans on | Vision leans on | Commitment leans on |
|---|---|---|---|
| New agent | what they were told success would look like; what the first months have actually been; who answers their questions | a first full year with a plan and a person | "if you had a 30-60-90 plan and someone in your corner every week…" |
| Experienced, low production | what they've tried; what a good month looks like; where the leads come from and what they cost | consistent closings without the chase | "if the inconsistency were solved, would you go all in?" |
| Top producer | what happens if they stop; what builds past this year; who pulls them up | an exit, wealth beyond active income, respect | "if the next five years could build something that pays you when you're not closing…" |
| Agent with a brand | what their audience asks for; what the attention is turning into | monetizing the audience without running a team | "if the reach you already have could become income without adult daycare…" |
| Team leader | what their agents struggle with; what retention costs them; what the overhead is | partners instead of competitors; an exit that isn't selling the team | "if your agents could stay yours and the company carried the overhead…" |
| Broker-owner | what the business is worth today; what the risk and liability feel like; what they'd keep | keeping the brand, dropping the cons; a willable asset | "if you could keep the name and lose the liability, what would stop you?" |

## The talk-time rule and the catch
- **The member talks about 30% of the call; the agent talks 70%** (the workshop's and the plan's rule — the
  transcript's own words are "ask questions, be curious, and listen" and "stop word-vomiting"). The script says it
  once, on its first page.
- **The catch:** when the member notices they have been talking for more than a minute, they stop with a question.
  Three hand-it-back lines, in the member's voice, go in the script: *"— but tell me, how does that land for you?"* ·
  *"what's your version of that?"* · *"does that match what you're seeing?"*
- Never talk over the agent; let every answer finish (`/41`). Silence after a question is theirs to fill.

## How the script and the prep use it
For each Discovery question the script carries one **listening-for** line: the pain or signal it surfaces, which
part of the member's offer answers it, and (for the prep, per prospect) the objection the answer may surface. For
a named agent, `cv-call-prep` writes the questions from what they put in the booking form and the intel report; the
sitting writes them for the primary type, with `[their words]` slots in the commitment questions.

## Rules
- A question never contains the pitch — "have you considered a brokerage with revenue share?" is a statement wearing
  a question mark. No compensation, no model, no "join" inside a question.
- Cardinal rules: no question invites them to run down their broker; "what's frustrating you" is about them.
- No income claim hidden in a vision question — never a dollar figure the member cannot ground; "doubling" or their
  own words.
- The any-agent test: a set that fits every type is not finished.
- Banned words and the recruiter register per `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/how-we-speak.md` §7.
