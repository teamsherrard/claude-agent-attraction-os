## 0. The stance in one paragraph

You are not recruiting; you are enrolling. Enrollment is guiding somebody to say yes to their own future
(`10-presentation-delivery/41`). The whole method is one move, repeated: find out where this agent is today,
find out where they want to be, and position your leadership, your brokerage's model, and your value proposition
as the bridge between the two (`bonus/bridging-the-gap`). Questions do the work — statements push, questions pull
(`10-presentation-delivery/44`). Compensation is answered honestly when asked and never led with. Nothing is ever
said against another brokerage or another person (`03-model-positioning/13`). When it is done right the call ends
with the agent asking "what are the next steps?" — the number one question Mike hears at the end of a call
(`/41`). If they are not ready, that is fine: "everyone's coming, it's a matter of when" (`/42`).

## 1. The psychology of enrollment — influence vs. pressure (`10-presentation-delivery/41`)

**Enrollment, not recruiting.** "You're not running out recruiting, you're enrolling. Enrollment is guiding
somebody to say yes to their own future." Helping the agent see what is possible by working with you, never
forcing them to move. **Influence inspires; pressure repels.** Done right, they enroll themselves.

**What pressure looks like (and does):**
- Talks over people. "Talking over somebody is the worst thing you can do." Let everybody finish everything they
  have to say, every time — even the ones who cut you off. Remove your ego; a reaction is a defence mechanism.
- Focuses only on your own goal (to attract). It feels manipulative and leads to regret and churn — like pushing a
  buyer into the wrong home for this month's commission. Push someone to join, over-promise, under-deliver, and
  they leave.
- Comes from desperation and a lack of confidence in what you offer.

**What being magnetic looks like:** builds trust and curiosity; pulls people in. Ask questions, be curious, be
interested, be empathetic, listen. Align with THEIR goals — "who cares what you want? It's all about them." Most
agents just want to produce; some will want to attract. Lead with influence and they recruit themselves, and both
of you are excited.

**The journey of a call, in Mike's words:** uncover their pain points → mirror back their vision → help them see
the gap between where they are and where they want to be → show them the bridge (your leadership, the brokerage
model, your value proposition) → give them space to decide. "Pressure breaks trust. Influence builds it."

**Space to decide, said out loud:** "This is all about finding the best time for you — let's connect in a week or
so, or next month, and see how things are going." Mike never pushes a join date. He has no drip campaign: "if you
suck at agent calls and you don't have a good value proposition" a drip won't save it; if you are good, they want
to join.

**Enroll with certainty.** Give them confidence that this will work for them and that you believe in them: *"I'm
super excited to see you become our next success story. Based on everything you said, I know you're going to
crush it. This is the exact training you should go to first."* Talk like that so they believe you believe in them.

## 2. The sequence of events — EXACTLY as lesson 42 states it (`10-presentation-delivery/42`)

This is the locked spine. Every call skill, script, and prep sheet follows it in this order.

1. **The call is booked.** One hour in the beginning "until you master the craft," then 30 minutes once you can
   hold your conversion rate. The booking form collects the ammunition before the meeting (§8).
2. **Start the meeting by building rapport and asking qualifying questions** — to identify their current state
   and open the loop: their pain points and struggles, why they agreed to this call, what they are intrigued by,
   what they are struggling with. Rapport comes first, before any question (`bonus/perfect-presentation`:
   "Hey, thanks for making the call today, I've been looking forward to chatting. How's your week been? How's your
   business been this year — what's working, what isn't?").
3. **Tailor the model and your value proposition to exactly what their goals and desires are** — their desired
   state, "not what you care about. Nobody cares what you care about. If you care about revenue share and they
   care about production, don't talk about revenue share — talk about production."
4. **Handle all of their objections.** "You're going to get some, and that's okay." (§6 and the Brain doctrine §14.)
5. **Get them excited about the opportunity to partner with you.** At the end, when they are ready: be hyped,
   energetic, future-pace, talk about how excited you are to finally work together — so they are not alone, they
   have support, they have training.
6. **Then one of three endings** (Mike's "three options"):

   **Ending A — Ready.** Send the next-steps email: the first paragraph personalized ("so people can see I actually
   listened"), then the copy-and-paste part — a video breakdown of the model, the funnel with everything you provide,
   then the exact steps to join with you named as sponsor, bullet-pointed and foolproof (the exact link, the exact
   button, "when it asks who influenced you to join, put my name," "this does not notify your current broker,"
   "joining as a solo agent or a team"). Map the steps to YOUR brokerage — they differ. Once they join: the welcome
   email (personal welcome, the video walkthrough of everything you offer, the private community link, the training
   links, "questions go to your sponsor first, then this support email") — and book a strategy call to build their
   first-week plan around their goals. "Everything I promise is given the day they join, 100% of the time, because
   this email goes to every person who joins that day."

   **Ending B — Hesitant.** The agent needs more help, more proof, or has unanswered questions → "bring in the heavy
   artillery, your upline": book a 3-way call with your strongest upline partner (§5). Find the one who is the best
   communicator and understands the model — not necessarily your sponsor.

   **Ending C — Not ready.** "This is going to happen and it's okay. Everybody has their own timeline." People have
   joined one to four years after the first conversation. Nurture with real reasons (§7): positive changes at the
   brokerage, an agent similar to them joining ("they reminded me of you"), their life milestones (a personal video
   message), and every time you add something to your value proposition that solves their pain.

   **The resources email goes to EVERYONE after the call, ready or not** — "they have everything I have so they can
   sit and think about it." Only the join-steps section is held for the ready.

**Endings → the locked pipeline vocabulary:** A → `Joined` (then `Onboarded` after the welcome and strategy
call) · B → `3-way` · C → stays `Call held` with a dated next move. `Parked` is for fit or timing the member has
decided to stop working actively — never for "not ready yet," because "it's not a matter of if, it's a matter of
when."

## 4. Questions control conversations (`10-presentation-delivery/44`)

"Questions are where the game is won. Statements push. Questions pull." Questions shift the focus onto them,
uncover pains, desires, and objections, and create open loops only you can close. Three categories, used in order:

- **Discovery (current state):** What do you love about your current brokerage? What's frustrating you most
  right now? What do you feel is missing?
- **Vision (future state):** If nothing changes at your current brokerage, where do you see yourself 12 months
  from now? What would doubling your business mean for you and your family? What would make you feel confident in
  achieving your potential?
- **Commitment (bridging the gap):** If I could show you a way to [their vision] without any extra time away from
  your family, would you be open to it? What's more important — repeating another year in the same spot, or
  doubling your income and spending more time with your family? If we can solve [their stated problem], would you
  be ready to go all in with us?

**Why it works:** they articulate their own problems ("now it's real because they believe it — they said it"),
they paint their own desired future, you position the model and the value proposition to exactly what they said
they care about, and by the end they feel "this is the right move for me," not "I was sold."

**The sales loop** (`bonus/bridging-the-gap`): gather the ammunition at the start, tailor the model and the UVP to
every pain and goal, pre-handle the objections you can already see, and loop back at the end by solving each
problem they named — so the close feels designed for them, because it was. (Mike's 8 Mile image: say what they
were going to say before they say it.)

**The talk-time rule.** The member talks about **30%** of the call; the agent talks 70%. *This number is the
workshop's and the master plan's rule, not a figure in a transcript — the transcript's wording is "ask questions,
be curious … and listen" (`/41`) and "stop word-vomiting all over the prospect" (`bonus/bridging-the-gap`).*

**Mirror the questions to the six types** (`shared/persona-doctrine.md` §9): new agents need a plan and a person;
experienced low-production agents need a system and a reason; top producers need scale, wealth, exit, and respect;
agents with a brand need a way to monetize their audience; team leaders need retention and an exit from "adult
daycare"; broker-owners need to keep the brand and drop the risk. The questions stay the same shape; the
examples change.

## 5. The 3-way call (`10-presentation-delivery/43`, with `02-prospect-targeting/19` and `/42`)

**What it is:** you, your prospect, and somebody from your upline who understands the model, has more credibility
and a more recognized brand, and can get the prospect over the hump. Mike's own phrasing, quoted: *"in the
beginning, leverage 3-way calls"* — "do not try and do this all yourself … drop the ego. The only thing that
matters is that the agent actually joins you." In his first 30 days he put all 30 agents on 3-way calls with his
sponsor, took notes, and studied how every objection was handled — 30 hours of live practice before flying solo.

**Pick the right partner:** the best communicator in your upline who understands the model — "it might not be
your sponsor" (`02-prospect-targeting/19`, `/42`). If your sponsor is a production killer who can't explain the model, let them sell.

**Edification — "nobody talks about this; it increases show-up rates and conversion rates, the two most important
metrics."** Edification is building someone up so the prospect knows why they are worth listening to. The bad
introduction: "This is my sponsor Mike, he has a YouTube channel and is active on social media — Mike, I'll let
you take it from here." The good introduction stacks real, verifiable accolades, then says what the partner is
there to do: "He's going to be able to answer any and every question you have about [brokerage] and our group so
you can understand the opportunity and how we can help you." Without it the partner has to boast about themselves,
"which ruins the entire call." (Every accolade in an edification is true, verifiable, and never an income figure.)

**The sequence — before, during, after:**
- **Before:** edify your partner to the prospect and get the prospect to commit to the 3-way. Send ONE calendar
  invite with the Zoom link to both. **Always write the time zone** — in every message, text, DM, and invite
  ("10 a.m. MDT / 12 p.m. ET"); Mike made this mistake "tons of times." Check that BOTH accepted; if not, you
  confirm with the prospect yourself.
- **During:** **edify the guest first** — even a brand-new agent has something to be made to feel special about;
  it sets the whole call on the right track. **Then edify your partner.** Then **stop talking.** Do not jump in,
  do not take the call down a random path, do not speak unless the guest or your partner asks you a question —
  "I've seen it ruin calls with top producers, team leaders, broker-owners." Sit back, take notes, and study how
  it is done. **Pay attention on camera** — no texting, scrolling, or checking notifications; the guest and the
  partner can see you. Let your partner present the model and the value proposition and handle the objections.
- **After:** **you** send the next steps if they commit; **you** follow up if they don't. "This is your prospect.
  Own the outcome." Never assume the partner will.

**The weekly model call** (`02-prospect-targeting/19`): for four years Mike hosted a one-hour "model explained + my value proposition"
call every Tuesday evening that his agents could invite prospects to — one presenter who controls the narrative,
knows the handlers, and has the brand. A member with an upline partner who runs one of these points prospects to
it the same way.

## 6. Objections are a request for clarity (`11-objection-handling/46`)

"Objections are not rejection, they're simply a request for more clarity." Guaranteed to come; most agents throw
one to three before committing. Answer with confidence and clarity — shaky and nervous gives them no confidence
to move. Treat it as a game or an obstacle course: the harder, the more fun to win.

**The seven archetypes and the hidden fear** (the full handler list is `attraction-doctrine.md` §14):
happy where I am → change feels risky · no time → disruption of the business · compensation → short-term expense
outweighs long-term gain · support and leads → won't succeed without the safety net · recruiting isn't for me →
attraction = pressure or MLM, not leadership · brand and credibility → losing credibility with clients · fear and
uncertainty → lack of belief in themselves or the unknown.

"Once you know the archetype, you know their hidden fear. Address the real fear and the objection disappears."
The locked shape: **Listen → Validate → Reframe → Invite** — "Hey, I get that. I used to feel that way too, but …"
Never fight, never get defensive, never get confrontational; welcome it. Pre-handle what the booking form and the
discovery questions already revealed (§4 sales loop). `cv-objection-coach` owns practice; `memory/objections.md`
logs what THIS member heard and what worked.

## 7. Follow-up with a reason — never "just checking in" (`12-simple-tech-stack/85`, `/84`, `10-presentation-delivery/42`)

"The fortune's in the follow-up." Most agents won't join after the first conversation until you get good at
bridging the gap. Follow-up "should feel like leadership, not chasing."

**Principles:** always leave value — "if you're just checking in, they're like 'go away, you're annoying'"; share
updates that benefit or inspire THEM; personalize to what matters most to them; multiple channels (text, email,
DM, video message); **consistency matters more than frequency.** No drip campaign, no CRM auto-sequence — "it's
templated, impersonal, and it doesn't work."

**The triggers (reasons to reach out):** positive changes to the brokerage structure (a new plan, a stock update,
a rev-share change — "if you care about residual income, this just happened") · new tools, technology, or training
rolled out · a big agent, team, or brokerage joining ("this reminded me of you") · major recognition or wins in
your group · a company event coming up (invite them; Mike has paid the way for top producers, team leaders, and
broker-owners to see the culture first-hand) · industry shifts that make the model more attractive (the NAR
settlement and liability, interest rates) · their own milestones — a wedding, a baby, a trip, an anniversary — a
personal video message (`10-presentation-delivery/42`) · anything NEW in your value proposition that solves the pain they told you about
(`10-presentation-delivery/42`: "all those agents who wanted AI training — now you can get it free").

**The simple plan:** Week 1 — the recap with every resource, thanks for the time, something personal so they know
you listened (always). Weeks 2–4 — value-driven updates. Monthly — a success story or an industry update.
Quarterly — an invitation to an event, webinar, or mastermind. **Adjust to their level of interest** — "there is
no perfect plan because it doesn't exist; it depends on them." The outcome: when their pain increases or their
timing shifts, you are the first call, because everyone else was "annoying the shit out of them" and you led
with value.

**Social media nurtures in the background** (`/84`): value content, recognition of your agents, culture
(behind the scenes, events, team wins — "their broker is doing nothing"), leadership (your story, lessons,
mindset), lifestyle (freedom and flexibility, never framed as income). Instagram daily (stories, reels, DMs);
YouTube for the conversion and the depth; a Facebook group if you have one; the email list. "They're always
watching" — when the timing is right they already feel they know you, which is why a 30-minute call converts.

**Owner skills:** `cv-follow-up` (the touches), `cv-reactivation` (quiet prospects, the 30-day agent), both draft-only.

## 8. The simple tech stack (`12-simple-tech-stack/83`, `bonus/calendly`)

"This is going to disappoint the wrong people." Don't overcomplicate attraction: the right tools let you
communicate, follow up, and deliver value quickly; a lean stack reduces overwhelm and increases action; simplicity
duplicates — your agents can copy it.

- **G Suite** — professional email, shared drive for scripts and resources; the two emails you need (the
  next-steps/invite email and the welcome email) saved as templates; personalize the first paragraph, send.
- **Zoom (paid)** — attraction calls, events, team meetings; recorded to repurpose for training and onboarding;
  raise the attendee limit as you grow.
- **Loom** — quick personalized video follow-ups: next steps, a resource walkthrough, the answer you didn't have on
  the call — "scalable while feeling personal."
- **An AI assistant** — Mike's lesson names ChatGPT; in this OS that job is Claude: scripts, handlers, follow-ups,
  guides and checklists for your agents.
- **Calendly** (`bonus/calendly`, `10-presentation-delivery/42`) — the booking page everything links to. 30-minute one-on-one Zoom,
  time zones auto-detected and shown, invitees may add guests (team leaders bring partners), and FIVE required
  questions: (1) *Do you acknowledge this is a discussion about joining my group at [brokerage]? Please don't book
  if you're already there or have chosen a sponsor* — a "no" means the meeting is deleted with a one-line note;
  (2) *Which brokerage are you with?* (3) *Where are you located?* (4) *Are you a new agent, experienced agent,
  team leader, or broker-owner?* (multi-line — context matters); (5) *Please share anything that will help me
  prepare, including why you're interested* — "people write novels." Reminders at 24 hours, 1 hour, and 10 minutes.
  Availability as flexible as humanly possible in the beginning (Mike: 10–2 once scaling, far wider in year one),
  "convenient for them, not you."
- **A Google Sheet** numbered 1 to 100 — where every agent stands, so you follow up one by one, personally. That
  is the whole CRM. (This OS keeps the same idea in the Brain's pipeline and Top-50; the member's real CRM, if
  any, stays the system of record.)

Owner skills for the OPS: `sales-system-setup`, `sales-booking-page`, `sales-show-up`, `sales-setter`,
`sales-call-block`, `sales-scorecard`.

## 9. Before the call — Agent Intel and starting conversations (Launching doc; Week 5 breakdown; `02-prospect-targeting/18–19`)

**Don't recruit. Start conversations.** Outreach is **relevant · personalized · selfless · value-driven · low
pressure** (Week 5 breakdown; Launching doc Week 5 Part 3). *These five words are the course documents' wording;
the transcripts say the same thing as "agents attract agents — this is a relationship business" (`02-prospect-targeting/19`).*

**The Agent Intel report** (Launching doc `/agent-intel`): from a name, brokerage, website, Instagram, LinkedIn,
YouTube, bio, and previous messages, a one-page brief — who they are · what they seem to care about · current
business model · potential brokerage frustrations · growth opportunities · things you have in common · likely
objections · best conversation angle · what NOT to say · suggested opening message. "The agent goes into the
conversation knowing something about the person instead of sending 'Hey John, have you ever considered [brokerage]?'"
Why it works is `02-prospect-targeting/18`: agents leave problems, not companies — financial structure, poor leadership, poor support,
poor tech, limited growth, culture — so the report looks for the problem, never the company.

**The NEVER list for outreach** (Launching doc `/start-conversation`, enforced by read-back in every outreach
skill): never pitch immediately · never send giant paragraphs · never use corporate recruiting language · never
lead with compensation · never sound AI-generated · never pretend personalization · never force a Zoom.
Instead: **context → curiosity → conversation → discovery → next step.** "That's the IP. Claude is merely
executing it."

**Invite early, never force, never present by text** (`02-prospect-targeting/19`): "your goal is to get them on a private Zoom call as
quickly as possible — say as little as you have to. Don't try to recruit by email or explain the model through
text; it's not going to work." The invitation is earned by the conversation; the model is explained only on the
call. No premature "want to learn about my brokerage?" (Launching doc).

**Channels × relationship state** (Launching doc): Instagram DM · text · email · Facebook · LinkedIn · voice note ·
referral introduction — for cold · acquaintance · friend · former colleague · past conversation · inbound lead.
Owner: `cv-conversation-starter`; comment-to-DM replies: `cv-dm-flow`.

## 10. The cardinal rules and the money (`03-model-positioning/13`; `10-presentation-delivery/42`, `/44`, `bonus/perfect-presentation`)

1. **Never talk badly about another brokerage.** Give credit — "Real is an incredible brokerage, I applaud them for
   that — and here's what I can do for you, specific to your goals." If an agent is comparing you to another
   sponsor, never a negative word; ask whether they were shown proof.
2. **Never talk badly about another person.** Word gets around. "Be the better man or woman."

**Compensation is answered, never led with.** The booking form tells you their brokerage, so you know the model,
splits, caps, and fees before the call and can "break down the math" when THAT is their pain (`10-presentation-delivery/42`). But if
production is their struggle, "I won't even mention revenue share — only if they ask, or only if the conversation
goes there" (`bonus/perfect-presentation`). Residual income is "a vehicle" for an exit strategy, never the
pitch (`10-presentation-delivery/44`). On the call: honest, plain, from `identity/brokerage-model.md`, every number from the member's
brokerage materials. In content: never. **No income claims anywhere** — no "you'll make $X," no rev-share earnings
projections, no stock-value projections; every illustrative number labeled (`shared/compliance-doctrine.md` §2).

**Never invent production numbers, stats, quotes, or proof.** An intel report cites what it found and when; a prep
sheet uses the Brain's proof or says "none yet — you'd be early." Targeting is by career stage, production,
business model, and mindset — never a protected characteristic.
