# Persona Doctrine — the six types of agent

*Built from `02-prospect-targeting/18–26` (Mike's prospect-targeting module) and the workshop
`aa-ideal-agent-profile` method. Read by `attraction-persona-map`, `attraction-top-50`, `attraction-story-bank`,
`attraction-prospect-radar`, and every content skill that speaks to an avatar. Cite as `module/lesson`.*

*Say "type of agent" to the member — never "persona", "avatar", or "tier" out loud. Targeting is by career
stage, production, business model, and mindset — never age, family status, or any protected characteristic.
A type is a TYPE, never a location: at a cloud brokerage the member can partner with an agent anywhere they are
licensed to attract (`compliance.md` → recruiting scope).*

---

## 1. The method (how the Brain picks who the member attracts)

1. **The mirror principle** (`04-value-proposition/27`: you are most qualified to help the person you used to be;
   `02-prospect-targeting/21`: "you attract the type of person that you are"). The member's ideal agent is
   usually living the member's hardest stretch right now, two or three years behind them on the same road. A
   newer member with little production history attracts by shared vision and stage instead.
2. **One primary type, at most two secondary.** Never "all of the above" — a profile that fits everyone attracts
   no one. Mike's progression: new agents first (volume, reps, the foundation), then work up to top producers,
   team leaders, and brokers (`02-prospect-targeting/23`).
3. **The relatability test, in order:** would this agent recognize themselves in the member's journey? Would the
   member's "what worked" strategies solve this agent's biggest problem right now? Could the member credibly
   help them — mentor if a few years ahead, build alongside if at the same stage (never "out-earn them")? Any
   "no" and that type is not the primary.
4. **Readiness sorts within the type**, never across types: Ready now · Worth staying close to · Just keep
   posting · Not the right fit (fit and timing, never disrespect, never a brokerage name).
5. **Lean on the upline where the member lacks the proof or the seniority** — "we, not me"
   (`02-prospect-targeting/21`); top producers, team leaders, and brokers usually need the upline on the call
   (`/23`, `/25`, `/26`).

## 2. New agents (`02-prospect-targeting/21`) — the lowest-hanging fruit

**Who they are.** 0–2 years, few moving parts (no listings, no signs), often digital natives looking for
guidance on social media, AI, and the modern way; growth-oriented, seeking mentorship, coaching, and fast wins;
community-focused — they value culture, collaboration, and leadership over the brokerage brand.
**What Mike has found.** Unrealistic expectations (encourage them anyway); care more about a local office and
local support; uneducated on the model (expect left-field questions; more educating to do); do far more
research than busy agents and compare brokerages side by side.
**Pains.** Zero income → need leads · craving mentorship and guidance · don't want outdated tech · no direction
("here's exactly what to do in your first 30/60/90 days") · afraid of being just a number.
**Triggers.** They are starting, or a few months in with nothing closed, surrounded by low performers.
**Objections to expect.** "I need a local office" (clients don't care; nobody went to the nicest office in Mike's
city) · "I need local support" (you still get it; a local co-sponsor or partner where the brokerage allows) ·
"I love my broker" (what happens when they retire or sell?) · "I don't want to recruit" (you don't have to).
**How to position.** Mentorship closer than a broker can give (the broker is doing compliance, risk, accounting
for everyone) · five-to-seven layers of support, not one · a path to success that mirrors where they said they
want to go · a circle of winners · **show proof**: pull up the actual training on the call; success stories of
agents just like them — if you have none, your upline's ("we") · financial potential is the least important
lever; Mike never shows his rev share on a call.
**How to spot them (observable).** Newly licensed or in licensing class · "just got my license" posts · asking
in groups where to start · no closings yet or one or two from family · watching brokerage-comparison videos ·
comments on new-agent content.
**First touch.** Answer the question they actually asked, invite them to one short call, never explain the model
by DM (`/19`). They are the volume and the reps.

## 3. Experienced agents with low production (`02-prospect-targeting/22`)

**Who they are.** Three to eight-plus years in, not closing consistently, not at six figures; know how the
industry and brokerages work; sick of chasing, burnt out, frustrated; open to change but **skeptical of failed
promises** — usually three to five brokerages in.
**What Mike has found.** They want to know exactly how **you** will help, not the brokerage; they hope this move
is the last; the most excuses and finger-pointing (market, broker, team leader, economy); riddled with
self-doubt; often the most technology-challenged.
**Pains.** Inconsistent closings, unpredictable income · no clear process, terrible time management, no leverage ·
technology gap · low confidence, constantly comparing themselves to peers who became top producers · distracted
("kings and queens of shiny-penny syndrome").
**Triggers.** The comparison moment; another year of the same; a lead bill with no closings.
**Objections.** "Switching didn't work before" (the root cause was the activities, not the brokerage — here is the
exact 30/60/90 plan) · "How are you different?" (proof of agents like them, turned around) · "I need a clear
plan" (show the training and how you personally help) · "I don't want to recruit."
**How to position.** Be the turnaround plan: mentorship, systems, accountability · **empathy, not shame** — they
feel embarrassed about the last five years; everyone has a turnaround point · path to success painted clearly ·
proof specific to struggling agents who came back · financial potential matters more here than for new agents —
many are past the halfway mark of their careers and starting to think about an exit.
**How to spot them.** Licensed years ago, few recent closings · "busy but broke" posts · multiple brokerage
logos in their history · buying leads or a coach that didn't stick · asking for "a system" · comparing
themselves to a classmate who made it.
**First touch.** Lead with the shared wall (the member's hardest stretch); one question about what they've
tried; invite to a call framed as a plan, not a pitch.

## 4. Top producers (`02-prospect-targeting/23`)

**Who they are.** Mike's line: at least ~$250k a year, usually more (relative to market); confident, respected,
proud; loyal to the brokerage where they succeeded; entrepreneurial, thinking about expansion.
**What Mike has found.** Ego (earned); slow to move — active listings, buyers under contract, new-construction
possessions; **math first** ("if the numbers don't make sense there's no point"); the member usually needs the
upline on the call until they have their own track record.
**Pains.** Plateau — $500k and looking for the next level · too much on their shoulders, business "hanging by a
thread", burning out · only active income · lack of recognition from the broker · geographically restricted —
a brand followed from outside the market with no way to capitalize beyond referrals · the biggest person in
their room (nobody to grow toward) · technology gaps (built it the traditional way) · thinking long-term in
their late forties and fifties.
**Objections.** "I'm already successful" (you can't grow if you're the best in your room) · "I don't want to
disrupt my momentum" (a proactive transition plan, months ahead, behind the scenes) · "I have everything I need"
(residual income, equity, impact) · "I don't want to recruit" (agents already ask you where to go; you get
nothing for it).
**How to position.** Add value: a strategy for scaling, wealth creation, and an exit — with the upline who can
show it · case studies of top producers who moved · **genuine respect** for what they built, then how to expand
beyond it · proof aligned to their gap (often social media or AI training) · financial potential via the upline.
**How to spot them.** Awards and rankings in their bio · a recognized local brand · a following from outside their
market · posts about team hires, burnout, or "what's next" · ten-plus years in.
**First touch.** Collaborate first (Mike's own sponsor did); a relationship, not a pitch; bring the upline to the
numbers conversation.

## 5. Influencers (`02-prospect-targeting/24`) — Mike dislikes the word; say "agents with a brand"

**Who they are.** Strong presence on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, or a podcast; an engaged following of
consumers and agents, local to international; respected as creators and modern marketers; often no
monetization beyond closings.
**What Mike has found.** Usually earn less than you'd think; ego from production and from the following; don't
yet see what the brand could become in the right vehicle; worried about the brokerage brand overshadowing
theirs (Mike made his own brand the focal point and kept the brokerage logo where compliance requires it).
**Pains.** Leaving money on the table — influence in the wrong vehicle (Mike: ~$300k to $3.2M in the 12 months
after his move, his figures) · brand spans beyond the market but income doesn't · no duplication, entirely
dependent on their own effort · no exit (you can't sell an Instagram account) · impact left on the table.
**Objections.** "I don't want to push the brokerage brand" (you won't have to; the narrative is partner with YOU)
· "I'll lose speaking and coaching opportunities at traditional brokerages" (it's how you do it; Mike's programs
are brokerage-agnostic and scaled after the move) · "I don't have time to manage people" (the member and upline
take the calls, onboarding, and support) · "I don't want to recruit."
**How to position.** A monetization strategy for the audience without adult daycare · success stories of other
creators who moved · "the man or woman of the town, or of the world?" (challenge the ego, gently) · freedom and
legacy — a movement that scales beyond the time they put in.
**How to spot them.** Follower counts and engagement from agents, not just consumers · DMs from agents asking
"how do you…" · speaking slots, a podcast, a program · no visible organization behind the brand.
**First touch.** Collaborate (a guest spot, an interview); never a DM pitch.

## 6. Team leaders (`02-prospect-targeting/25`)

**Who they are.** Established leaders with 5–25-plus agents (Mike has moved teams of 100); producing solo
volume while bogged down by overhead, support, and admin; "kings and queens of adult daycare"; exhausted,
looking for scalability, support, and retention.
**What Mike has found.** Ego; slower still (staff, systems, their own CRM, multiple agents' contracts); math
first; this is where corporate leadership joins the conversation; most are **less profitable and less sellable
than they think**, with no exit.
**Pains.** Overhead (salaries, office, marketing) · retention — the 80/20 rule, and the 1% who take everything
you taught and become your competition · no broker support (the team leader does all the training) · scaling
costs money, time, and stress · geographically restricted · no exit · no time freedom — often 60–80% of the
team's production is the leader.
**Objections.** "I don't want to disrupt my team's momentum" (a proactive 3–4-month transition plan with
corporate) · "We'll lose our brand and family feel" (keep the local team; now agents who aren't a fit can be
sponsored instead of turned away) · "We'll lose money" (sometimes, year one — "two steps back to take twenty
forward"; the brokerage can sometimes get creative) · "We're already successful" (no exit, no leverage, and
five-to-seven layers above you with a financial interest in your success).
**How to position.** Bridge the gap: other teams that scaled and added residual income beyond the local market
with less stress · **retention**: your best talent can leave the team and stay sponsored by you — partners
instead of competitors · proof: the training their whole team gets, so the leader isn't the only trainer ·
success stories of teams · financial potential (via the upline) · company support that removes overhead.
**How to spot them.** Team branding and a roster · hiring posts · "looking for an admin / ISA" · visible burnout
or "scaling" language · their name on most of the team's deals.
**First touch.** Peer-to-peer, through the member's or the upline's team-leader success stories; corporate
joins by the second conversation.

## 7. Broker-owners (`02-prospect-targeting/26`) — "the big one"

**Who they are.** Owners of independent brokerages or franchise offices; 10–200-plus agents; highly
entrepreneurial, proud of what they built; carry all the overhead and liability; deep emotional attachment to
ownership and reputation; ambitious but exhausted by operations.
**Compliance first.** Franchise owners are in a franchise agreement with a **look period**; talking to them about
moving outside it is tortious interference — "you can get sued and you probably will" (`/26`). Independent
owners have no such window, but the member confirms with their brokerage before any such conversation
(`compliance-doctrine.md` §6).
**What Mike has found.** The biggest ego; the slowest to move (all the staff, systems, agents, transactions);
math first and complete clarity required; **most brokers are broke** (his example: a 33-agent brokerage losing
six figures); no clear exit — they won't sell for what they think; all the risk and liability.
**Pains.** Overhead (salaries, rent, insurance, compliance, marketing, legal, E&O) · retention and churn ·
competitive disadvantage against cloud models · scaling limits · no exit · isolation — carrying leadership alone.
**Objections.** "I'll lose my brand and control" (keep the name — "[Brokerage] brokered by …" where the model
allows; keep the local office) · "My agents won't come" (the member explains the opportunity to the agents
after the owner is on board; the ones who don't come usually aren't the ones to keep) · "I already own a
brokerage" (ownership isn't wealth; look at the P&L) · "We'll lose money" (two steps back…).
**How to position.** Collaboration and support — no longer alone · modernize: amplify the pros of ownership
(brand, ownership, agent-based compensation), remove the cons (risk, liability, geography, adult daycare, no
exit) · growth and retention stories of brokerages that moved · proof of the training all their agents get ·
financial potential via the upline · company support and the broker/staff infrastructure they stop paying for.
**How to spot them.** "Broker/Owner" in the title · office openings and hiring · recruiting posts of their own ·
public frustration with costs or churn · a franchise renewal on the horizon (ask, never assume).
**First touch.** Relationship only, with the upline and corporate ready; the sequence is owner first (several
conversations, corporate, the numbers), then their agents.

## 8. Where they gather — what the vault says, and what it doesn't

The vault is **thin here**, and this file does not invent. What Mike says: new agents research heavily and
compare brokerages online (`/21`); agents with a brand live on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, podcasts (`/24`);
Mike's own prospects come through YouTube, then book a call (`/01`, `/19`); company events are where big
decisions get made (`06-content-framework/38`); team leaders and broker-owners are less on Reels and more on
LinkedIn (cohort doc). Generic rooms the workshop method names: Facebook groups by agent type, the YouTube
channels and podcasts agents follow, masterminds, conferences, local association events, brokerage trainings.

**Doctrine:** `avatars.md` lists *types* of places; `attraction-prospect-radar` researches the *actual* rooms,
events, and channels in the member's market and cites them in `prospect-intel.md`. Never list real people as
targets; never scrape a group's members.

## 9. Quick-reference table

| Type | Mike's one line | Primary pain | The lever | Upline needed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New agent | lowest-hanging fruit; the reps | no income, no direction | mentorship + a 30/60/90 plan + proof | no (use "we") |
| Experienced, low production | sick of chasing, skeptical of promises | inconsistent closings, no process | the turnaround plan, empathy, proof of comebacks | sometimes |
| Top producer | math first; the biggest person in their room | plateau, only active income, no exit | scale + wealth + exit, respect | yes |
| Agent with a brand | influence in the wrong vehicle | brand reach ≠ income | monetize the audience without adult daycare | sometimes |
| Team leader | adult daycare, overhead, churn | retention and no exit | partners instead of competitors; company support | yes + corporate |
| Broker-owner | all the risk, less profit than it looks | overhead, liability, isolation | keep the brand, drop the cons; look-period rule first | yes + corporate |
