## 1. First principle: master your own model, then understand the others (`03-model-positioning/14`)

"99% of people at any brokerage can't explain their model properly" (`01-foundation-mindset/08`); agents join
the person who finally answers their questions with clarity. So: study your own brokerage model rigorously,
then study competing models — surface level (compensation: splits, caps, fees; rev-share tiers; stock; support)
and a layer deeper (training quality, opportunity and growth, financial health, leadership, operations such as
commission payout, onboarding, offboarding). Every model has a competitive advantage you can position
positively; the cardinal rules forbid positioning on anyone else's weakness.

**Two guardrails from the same lesson:**
- Never project a stock's future value — "you can't talk about something like that, especially if it's
  publicly traded; it's SEC-regulated." Early cloud brokerages' stock stories will not repeat.
- Never make a claim about another brokerage yourself. If an agent who left it told you something, you may say
  "an agent who came over from there said…" — never "this is the fact."

## 2. Cloud brokerage with revenue share (eXp, REAL, LPT, Epique, and others — the member's Brain says which)

**Mechanics, generically (`/14`, `/15`, `/16`, `11-objection-handling/49, /55, /60`; verify current):**
- **Split and cap.** The agent pays a split until an annual cap, then keeps 100% for the rest of the anniversary
  year. Mike's example (`/14`): "80/20 with a $16,000 cap" vs "85/15 with a $12,000 cap" — both reach the cap at
  the same ~$80,000 GCI, so "the cap is the exact same." Per Mike; verify current.
- **Production awards.** Some plans return the cap in company stock when the agent hits a production threshold
  after capping (Mike: "if you do 20 deals after that, you get your cap back for free in company stock" —
  `/15`); blended with rev share, top producers "can make more than 100% of their commission" (`/16`, `/23`).
- **Revenue share.** Funded from the **company's** share of commission, never from the agent's; paid only when
  agents in your organization close deals, never for recruiting (`11-objection-handling/49`). Organized in
  **tiers** (Mike: seven at eXp, five at most others — `/14`); the "Christmas tree" shape means the bottom tiers
  become the largest over time. Per-tier annual maximums exist and differ by brokerage (Mike's example: a first
  tier paying up to ~$3,200 vs up to $5,000 elsewhere — `/14`, per Mike; verify current). Tiers typically
  "unlock" with frontline agent counts (not in the vault in detail — confirm from the brokerage's materials).
- **Willable.** At least at eXp and REAL, per Mike, revenue share is a willable asset: a family member can
  license within a window and inherit it (`/16`). Verify per brokerage.
- **Stock / equity.** Awards for milestones (first deal, capping, attracting), plus a stock-purchase plan at a
  discount — the vault names these only in passing; the member's materials are the source.
- **Sponsorship.** One sponsor, chosen at joining; changing sponsors usually means leaving the brokerage for
  6–12 months and coming back (`01-foundation-mindset/05`, `11-objection-handling/52`) — which is why "choose the
  right sponsor" is a real conversation. **Co-sponsorship** exists at eXp and REAL per Mike (`04-value-proposition/28`,
  `11-objection-handling/52`): two sponsors share; used to give a prospect a local partner or a specific UVP.
- **Cap honoring / deferral.** Mike (`11-objection-handling/60`): eXp honors a cap proven at another brokerage
  until that cap's reset date. Unsure about the others — ask.
- **Offices.** No company offices; eXp agents get shared-office (Regus) access per Mike (`/55`); a local
  co-sponsor or downline partner with an office covers the rest.
- **Support layers.** "Five to seven layers of people above you who are financially incentivized to help you
  win" (`/15`) vs one broker at a traditional brokerage.
- **Teams and brokerages inside the model.** A team keeps its brand; a brokerage can join as "[Name], brokered
  by [cloud brokerage]" and keep its name and local office (`02-prospect-targeting/25–26`). Agents who leave a
  team can stay sponsored by its leader — the retention argument.

**How Mike positions it (positive only):** residual income that compounds; equity; more than one income stream;
global or international reach; brand flexibility (independent, not franchise — `11-objection-handling/56`);
training beyond the brokerage comes from the sponsor.

**Competing cloud models (`/14`):** it is "a bit of a dance" — more tiers vs higher first-tier pay; who the
biggest tier rewards; which incentivizes helping partners grow. Whatever the member's brokerage, there is a
positive talking point; never a negative one about the other.

## 3. Franchise split brokerages (`03-model-positioning/15`, `11-objection-handling/60`)

Think the national franchise brands (Mike names several; this file doesn't need to). "The easiest conversations"
— win with math, because "you cannot argue ego and emotion with math."
- **Mechanics (per Mike; verify current):** a split, with or without a cap; higher fees; franchise fees that
  continue after a cap (Mike's KW example: ~70/30 with a ~$15–16k cap, then 100% less the franchise fee — `/60`);
  one income stream; some have **profit share** rather than revenue share (`/60`).
- **Pros Mike grants:** a local office, local market share and familiarity.
- **Cons Mike names:** one stream of income; higher fees; geographically restricted; no exit or retirement plan;
  no ownership ("renting your business"); often old-school training; one layer of support.
- **The math conversation:** no cap → run the last 12 months at their split vs yours (Mike's example: a 90/10
  no-cap agent doing 50 deals would have made ~$111,000 more under an 80/20-with-cap plan — his numbers, his
  example). With a cap → still run it. Then the real questions: are you in the right vehicle for your long-term
  goals? As your brand grows, can you be compensated for it where you are?
- **Franchise owners:** the look-period rule (`02-prospect-targeting/26`) — see `compliance-doctrine.md` §6.

## 4. Flat-fee / 100% brokerages (`03-model-positioning/16`, `11-objection-handling/59`)

"A little more difficult": the agent keeps 100% for a flat fee, so short-term math favors staying.
- **Mechanics:** flat monthly and/or per-transaction fees; no split; usually a local office.
- **Pro Mike grants:** more money today on the same deals.
- **Cons Mike names:** one income stream; geographically restricted; no exit; no ownership; often old-school;
  one layer of support; and — the lever — a brokerage running on flat fees can't fund much training, so the
  agent pays for coaching elsewhere.
- **How to win:** discover their purpose and why (family, protection, a willable income); current state vs desired
  state ("100% of 10 deals, or a split with a cap on 20 and beyond?"); the value has to be worth more than the
  split; "you can make more than 100% here" only where the member's plan supports it (production awards + rev
  share). Mike brought over a 60-plus-agent flat-fee brokerage this way.

## 5. Independent / local brokerage and local team (`02-prospect-targeting/25–26`; the member who IS one)

The OS serves members building a local team or local brokerage too, not only a cloud downline. The vault
describes these from the attractor's side; the mechanics below are the generic shape, not a plan.
- **Local team (inside any brokerage):** the leader provides leads, systems, training, admin, sometimes an
  office; agents pay a team split on top of (or inside) the brokerage split. The leader's economics: overhead
  (salaries, marketing, office), retention risk (the top 1% leave to compete), and production concentration
  (often 60–80% is the leader). The attraction offer for a team leader is leads, systems, and a real plan —
  "what you give" (`offer.md`), and compliance on recruiting scope still applies.
- **Independent brokerage:** the owner carries overhead, compliance, E&O, legal, insurance, and liability; keeps
  the brand and ownership; compensation from agents. The retention problem is the same. If a member is
  building one, their model explanation is their own fee schedule — cited from their own documents.
- **Not in the vault:** a lesson on building a local team or brokerage as the *end goal*. The Brain reads the
  member's materials and says so rather than inventing a standard.

## 6. The questions every member must be able to answer (the Model Expert's Q&A bank)

From the objection lessons and `/14`: What is the split and cap, and what happens after the cap? What are the
fees, all of them? How is revenue share funded, and when is it paid? How many tiers, and how does a tier open?
What happens to my rev share if I leave, or if I die? What stock do I get, and when? Can I change sponsors?
Is there co-sponsorship? Is my cap honored if I just capped elsewhere? Where do I meet clients without an
office? Can my team keep its name? Can my brokerage keep its name? Who supports me — the broker, my sponsor,
the upline — and for what? How is onboarding, offboarding, commission payout?

Every answer in `identity/brokerage-model.md` carries the source document and date, or says "ask the brokerage."
