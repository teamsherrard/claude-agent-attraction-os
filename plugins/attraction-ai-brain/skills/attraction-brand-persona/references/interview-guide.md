# Interview Guide — Leader Identity (Attraction Brain, Conversation 1)

*Mike's thirteen questions of Setup Conversation 1 ("who you are as a leader"), one per turn, with no
follow-ups except two — Q10's prompts when the brokerage story comes back thin, and the one on Q12 when the
story comes back as a one-line summary — and the common answers. Inside Setup, `attraction-brain-setup` asks
the questions itself; this guide is read when YOU ask the first question (a standalone run), and its rules on
answers bind the writer either way. The questions are the owner's (2026-11-02); do not add to them, bundle
them, or reorder them. Follow `shared/ask-once-default.md`: one plain question per turn inside the Brain setup,
every question a handoff, every question skippable; "I don't know" earns a consult only on the questions
`attraction-brain-setup`'s Principles list (the WHY, the sponsor, the strategy, the 12-month number) — no
other list.*

## How to run it

- Thirteen turns, never a card of four. Each turn is one question with a plain "your turn" at the end. The
  two allowed follow-ups (Q10, Q12) go in the *next* turn and then the next question follows; never two
  questions in one message, never a follow-up on a fact question, never "anything to add?" after a chip.
- Warm, like a colleague who has done this before. Never marketing jargon. "Short, messy answers are
  perfect" is said once and meant.
- Accept shorthand as typed: brokerage names, city abbreviations, "6 yrs". Clean it up in the file, not
  in the conversation. Keep the typed answer verbatim somewhere (the story and the leader moment go to the
  story bank; the voice is written from how they typed).
- If an answer is already in the Brain (bridged from a Realtor Brain, imported from Materials, said
  earlier), drop the question and say in one line what you used.
- Never ask where they can attract agents, and never ask which type of agent they want. Both are the
  owner's hard rules (`shared/attraction-doctrine.md` §9b).

## Who are YOU

**Q1. What's your name?**
*As they want it to appear. "Taylor" is enough to continue; the full name is captured when it comes.*

**Q2. What brokerage are you with?**
*Any of: a cloud brokerage (eXp, REAL, LPT, Epique, another), a franchise, an independent, a local team, or
"none yet". Record it as they say it. Never assume eXp.*

**Q3. What's your local market?**
*City plus state or province. A FACT for `profile.md`. It is never followed by "and where can you attract?" —
at a cloud brokerage they attract anywhere.*

**Q4. What category of agent are you — solo, a team leader, or a broker owner?**
*"On a team" is a real answer too; record it.*

**Q5. How long have you been a licensed real estate agent?**
*Years, or the year licensed. Both go in the file.*

**Q6. What did you do before real estate?**
*A sentence, not a résumé: "former high-school teacher", "sold cars for nine years", "engineer who quit a day
after being named top employee". It is often the most relatable line in the whole Brain; keep the specific
detail. "I've been a lot of things" is recorded as given — no follow-up; the writer keeps the one that
shaped how they work with people if the answer names it.*

**Q7. Why did you decide to get into real estate? What's your big WHY — creating wealth for your family, helping and impacting others, something else?**
*Mike's exercise (`01-foundation-mindset/10`): who are you doing this for, and what are the specific scenes
you replay on the days you want to quit? Capture the people and the scenes. If the answer is "money" or
"freedom", take what they give — no follow-up; the second spoken answer at the end of Setup ("talk about
your WHY") deepens it, and both are kept. Never copy Mike's list (the parents' trip, the car) into a
member's file.*

**Q8. Why do you want to build a revenue share organization?**
*Their words. "The rev share" is a real answer; the human reason goes under it ("income that isn't tied to
the next closing", "something that doesn't reset every January"). Numbers never go in this file.*

## Your brokerage

**Q9. When did you join your current brokerage?**
*Month and year. If they founded it, record the founding month. If they are "between brokerages", record
that honestly.*

**Q10. What's the story behind you choosing that brokerage?**
*The bare question — it no longer packs five asks. The one allowed follow-up, in the next turn, only when
the answer comes back thin or as the brochure ("the technology, the culture, the training"): "Things to
touch on if they apply: why you chose it over other cloud-based brokerages, whether you ignored it at first
or had a bad taste about it, what changed your perspective, and what you now understand that most agents
still misunderstand. Your turn." Then take what they give. The "what you now understand that most agents
misunderstand" part is their best content angle — keep it in their words.*

**Q11. Why did you select your sponsor specifically? Did they bring certain value to the table — have they built the style of business you're looking to build?**
*First name is enough. The value the sponsor brings is recorded as the member describes it; it becomes the
upline layer the offer blends in (Week 2). "I didn't really choose — they were the one who called" is a
real answer and is written as that.*

### The common answers at the brokerage questions and what you do

| They say | You do |
|---|---|
| "Honestly, the rev share." | Real answer. Write the human reason under it: "income that isn't tied to the next closing", "something that doesn't reset every January". Numbers never go in this file. If they push for numbers: *"Numbers stay for your private call. The file holds the reason."* |
| "I left [named brokerage] because they…" | Keep the wall, drop the name: "a franchise", "an independent", "a 100% shop". If they insist on naming it: one line — the story is the wall they hit, not the company, and never talking badly about another brokerage or person is what makes them look like a leader (`03-model-positioning/13`). Then write it that way. |
| "My old broker was a friend and I still left." | Gold. That is Mike's own story (his broker was his best friend; he still switched when the timing was right, `01-foundation-mindset/01`), and it answers the "I love my broker" objection later. Capture the human tension, warmly. |
| "I thought it was a pyramid scheme." | Also Mike's story; capture what changed their mind (someone explained the model properly; a peer they respected was already there). It becomes an objection answer in the story bank. |
| "I'm at a local brokerage / I own it." | The question still works: why *this* one, why *build* here. Brokerage-agnostic means the answer shape is the same. |
| Brochure language | The Q10 prompts, once (the one allowed follow-up, above), then take what they give. |

## Your journey

**Q12. Describe your story. This one should take the longest, and talking it out loud works well here. How did you get started? Were there turning points where things started to click and you began to succeed — what happened, what did your business look like after, and what strategy helped?**
*Ask for scenes. Follow-up if the answer is a summary ("year two was rough"): "Give me the picture — what
were you looking at the day it was worst?" One follow-up, then move on. The strategy that helped is the
seed of what they can teach (Conversation 2 confirms it). Invite voice input in one phrase ("talking it out
loud works well here"); a typed answer is just as good and is kept verbatim as a voice sample.*
*Newer agents: year one has the whole story in miniature. "I'm still in the hardest stretch" is a valid
answer and a very relatable one.*

**Q13. Tell me about the moment you realized that just selling houses and chasing the next deal for the rest of your life wasn't enough — and why you believe revenue share is the key to what you want in life, and why other agents should care.**
*The leader moment is an identity shift (`01-foundation-mindset/06`): from caring only about their own
production to wanting other people's transformation. It might be a burnout, an agent who asked for help, a
cap that did not move, or a conversation. "Haven't fully decided" is recorded as the current state. The
"why other agents should care" half is their first public line — keep it, number-free.*

### The common answers at the journey questions and what you do

| They say | You do |
|---|---|
| "I don't really have a story." | Everyone who has sold a house has one. Ask for the first deal and the worst month. If they are brand new: the before-story, the decision to get licensed, and what they are building now are the story. |
| "The hardest stretch is right now." | Record it as the present tense. The "who relates to this" line practically writes itself: agents in the same stretch. |
| "I haven't decided to build an organization." | Fine and common. Record "deciding" and the reason they are in the room. No push. |
| "My why is to make a lot of money." | Write what they say — no follow-up; the second spoken answer deepens it. Never invent a nobler reason. |
| "Can you just write something good?" | The WHY is on `attraction-brain-setup`'s consult list, so consult: propose 2–3 WHY lines built only from what they have told you (the before-story, the family they mentioned, the agent they helped), say why each fits, recommend one, let them choose. Mark it "(drafted — confirm)". |

## Drafted, never asked (the member corrects later, never composes)

- The **"Who relates to this:"** line under each turning point (six types; career stage, production, model,
  mindset only; never a place). These seed `attraction-persona-map`'s DERIVE mode.
- The **one line out loud** — "why are you there?" — from Q10, Q11 and Q13 (the seed line in `positioning.md`).
- **What they want to be known for** (drafted — confirm), from the story's "what strategy helped".
- The **vision line**: a future big enough that every agent's goals fit inside it (`01-foundation-mindset/06`).

## Never in any file from this skill

Former brokerage names · splits, caps, stock, tiers, rev-share numbers · anything negative about another
brokerage or person · invented facts, numbers, agents, or quotes · age, family status, or any protected
characteristic as a targeting line · a place as where they attract · a type of agent the member "picked" ·
the words unlock, supercharge, game-changer, revolutionary, secret weapon, leverage (as a verb), beat.
