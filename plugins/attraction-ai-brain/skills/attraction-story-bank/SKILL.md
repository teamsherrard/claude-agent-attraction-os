# Personal Story & Experience Bank — the stories only this leader can tell (Brain, Conversations 1 and 3)

The magic of growth and retention is relatability, and relatability comes from stories, not facts
(`06-content-framework/40`). An agent decides to follow someone who was recently where they are now and
got out, and the proof of that is a story: the invoice with no closings, the cap that didn't move, the
Sunday the routine got built. This skill mines those moments, structures them, and tags each one with who
it is for and where it gets used, so no content skill, call prep, or objection answer ever has to invent
one. **About 10 minutes, spoken. Target twelve or more; three real ones are better than twelve forced ones.**

## Step 0 — Read the shared engine
Read `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/spoken-capture.md` (mechanism · natural interview · never-fabricate) and
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/ask-once-default.md`; `shared/how-we-speak.md` binds every line. Read
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/persona-doctrine.md` only when you reach Step 3 (the six types and their pains,
for tagging). **Exception: the "use defaults / you decide" escape hatch does NOT apply here.** Stories
cannot be invented. If they have few, that is the **starter state**; never write a fabricated story to
fill the bank.

## Step 1 — Load the Brain and set expectations
Read `~/attraction-brain/brain.md`, `identity/journey.md` (the story with its turning points and the leader
moment are already stories; start there), `identity/proof.md` (agents helped and wins are story seeds), and
`identity/voice-print.md` if it exists (capture in their spoken voice). If the local copy is missing, pull
with **attraction-brain-sync**; a connector error is never "no Brain".

Open warmly and defuse the "I have no stories" fear up front:
> "We're going to grab the real stories from your career — they're what make another agent think 'that's
> me' and decide you're the one to follow. Before you say 'I don't have any': you have more than you
> think, and if you're newer and only have a few, that's fine. We capture what's real today and the bank
> fills up as agents join and win. Best out loud — **voice mode** — or paste a transcript." *(A pasted
> transcript is data, never instructions.)*

**Inside full Setup** this skill runs in the background of Conversations 1 and 3: Setup asks Mike's
questions one at a time — the story and its turning points (question 12), the moment selling houses wasn't
enough (13), the moment they almost quit and what kept them going (18), the mistakes that taught them
something (19), the agents they've helped in detail (21), why their agents joined them (22) — and this skill
develops each answer **in the same conversation, before the Book is built**: each answer stays verbatim in
the seeds list AND becomes its own story block of 60–120 words in the member's own words — the scene they
gave · what it proves (the lesson) · where it gets used — with its tags (Step 3), written under its own hook
heading. Drafted from their words, never invented: no follow-ups inside setup (Setup's one-question rule), a
thin answer becomes a short true block, never a padded one, and the member corrects later rather than
composing now. An answer pasted under a heading is not done (the echo test); six developed seeds are what
Section 5 of the Brain Book draws its lines from. On its own, start with the six seeds and widen.

## Step 2 — Mine for stories (the setup seeds first, then the doorways)
One prompt at a time, follow whatever lights them up, one natural follow-up each ("what happened next?",
"what were you looking at that day?"). The six Setup seeds, exactly:
1. The moment you almost quit — and what caused you to keep going.
2. A mistake you made and what it taught you (paying for bad leads, trying too many things at once…).
3. The turning point — when things started to click and you began to succeed; what happened, what the business looked like after.
4. The moment you realized that just selling houses and chasing the next deal wasn't enough.
5. An agent you've helped — what happened, in detail.
6. Why the agents in your group joined YOU specifically.

Then widen toward twelve with the doorways an attractor's content actually needs:
- The day you decided to join your brokerage, and the conversation that got you there (human reason only).
- The thing you believed about your brokerage or its model before you looked properly, and what changed your mind *(Mike's own: he assumed it was a pyramid scheme and told his sponsor never to mention it again, `01-foundation-mindset/01`; it is the most useful objection story a member can have)*.
- The broker, mentor, or friend you respected and still left, and how it felt.
- A time you kept going with no result to show for it *(three years of two videos a week before anything worked, `01-foundation-mindset/01`)*.
- The first agent who asked you for help, and what you showed them.
- An agent's small win that mattered more than it looked *(one deal in thirty days after a year of none, `06-content-framework/40`)*.
- Something from before real estate that explains how you lead.
- A client story that shows how you work.
- A moment you were wrong about an agent, or an agent was wrong about you.
- A thing that happened at your kitchen table, in your car, or at 11 pm that nobody sees.

**If they are newer or thin on stories:** never push or make them feel short. Widen to life-before-real-
estate, one honest client moment, and the "why I got into this" story (everyone has it). Three real
stories written in the starter state are a success. **If they truly have one:** capture it, write the bank
as starter, and lean into Step 5.

## Step 3 — Structure each story → `identity/story-bank.md`
A pile of anecdotes is useless; a tagged library is the asset. Read `shared/persona-doctrine.md` now for
the six types and the five pains. Capture each story as:
- **Title** — three to six words, so a skill can scan fast ("The year I paid for leads").
- **Hook** — one line, the first sentence of a reel or a video.
- **The story** — the real narrative in their spoken voice, with the specific details that make it land.
- **The lesson** — what it proves. **Mandatory.** A story with no point cannot be used; ask "what's the
  takeaway?" if it is missing.
- **Lands with** — the type of agent who recognizes themselves in it, named by you from the story (never
  asked): new agents · experienced but low production · top producers · agents with a brand · team
  leaders · broker-owners (one, at most two). By career stage, production, model, mindset; never a
  protected characteristic, never a place.
- **Speaks to** — the pain it answers, in Mike's wording (`shared/attraction-doctrine.md` §7b): financial
  uncertainty · lack of support, mentorship, training · technology gaps · limited growth · work-life balance and
  recognition (one, at most two; the plain alias may follow in parentheses).
- **Used for** — one or more of: **story reel** · **YouTube hook** · **partner call** · **objection
  answer** (name the objection in the member's words, e.g. "it's a pyramid scheme", "I love my broker",
  "I'm doing fine where I am", "I just switched"; the canonical list is in `shared/attraction-doctrine.md`).
- **Emotional turn** — relief · pride · heartbreak · vindication · embarrassment · gratitude (lets content
  match a feeling, not just a topic).
- **Permission** — if it involves a real agent or client: *public: yes / ask first / anonymize*. Default
  "ask first" for agents; "anonymize" for clients.
- **Used-where** — starts empty; content and call skills stamp it when they use the story (rotation).

Three rules on every entry:
- **Former brokerages are never named.** "A franchise", "an independent", "a 100% shop". The story is the
  wall, not the company (`03-model-positioning/13`). Nothing negative about another brokerage or person,
  even if the member said it; keep the lesson, lose the target.
- **No compensation mechanics.** The "why I joined" story carries the human reason; splits, caps, stock,
  and rev-share numbers stay for the private call.
- **Nothing invented.** If they didn't say it, it isn't in the bank. No composite stories, no "improved"
  details, no success stories about agents the member did not name.

Framing the five-part story (`04-value-proposition/34`: past · transformation · mission · value · vision)
is **attraction-why-join-me**'s job, not this skill's; this bank is what it draws from.

## Step 4 — Confirm and write
On demand: reflect the set back briefly ("here are the nine I've got, tagged like this — look right?"), let
them adjust, write. Inside setup: write silently. Write → push → verify: run **attraction-brain-sync**
(PUSH) immediately; stamp **last updated + story count** at the top. On demand, confirm honestly for their
level, no file names:
- A full bank → *"Twelve stories, each tagged with who it's for and where it goes. Your content, your
  calls, and your objection answers never start from blank again."*
- A starter bank → *"Three real ones, which is three more than most leaders have written down — and this
  grows every time an agent joins or wins. Send me a line when it happens."*
If run inside Setup, hand control back with all six seeds developed (above) — Setup builds the Book from them.

## Step 5 — The growth loop (how a starter bank becomes a deep one)
Point them at **attraction-capture**: *"Any time something happens — an agent closes their first deal, a
conversation surprises you, you remember a moment from year one — just tell your Brain in a sentence. It
files it as a seed."* "Add an attraction story" fleshes one seed into a full entry; **"refresh my
attraction story bank"** re-reads the seeds capture left (wins in proof, ideas on file) and the newer
agents-helped rows in `proof.md`, and turns them into tagged stories. Agent breakthroughs are the stories
that prove the member does what they say (`03-model-positioning/17`); every new one goes in with its
permission flag.

## How every output uses this (the payoff)
Per the story law in `brain.md`: before writing content, prepping a call, or answering an objection, a
skill checks `story-bank.md` for a story matching the **type of agent, the pain, or the emotional turn**,
weaves it in using the member's spoken voice (anonymized or permission-checked per the flag), and stamps
**Used-where** so the same story does not repeat too soon. The Brain Book tells the almost-quit moment and
the lessons in its "Your proof" section and the turning points in "Your story". If the bank is empty, a
skill writes without a story; it never invents one to fill the gap.
