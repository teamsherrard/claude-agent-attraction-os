# Interview guide — UVP Builder (Week 2)

One stop, 2–4 questions, only the gaps. Everything from setup's Phase 4 (Stops 8–9) and Phase 2 is
already in the Brain and is read back, never re-asked. This should feel like ten minutes with a coach
who has already read the file, not a questionnaire.

## What is usually already answered (drop the question)
| Need | Where it lives | Ask only if |
|---|---|---|
| What worked in production, and whether they can teach it | `strategy.md` (teach-it flags) | the flags are all "not yet" or the section is a placeholder |
| The first thing they'd teach a new agent | Stop 8 Q32 in `strategy.md` / `offer.md` seeds | the answer was "not sure" |
| What the brokerage gives every agent | `brokerage-model.md`, else Stop 9 Q34 seeds | both are "not sure yet" |
| What the upline provides | Stop 9 Q35 seeds, materials | "nothing I know of" and no materials |

| Proof | `proof.md` | there is a result the member mentioned in passing that isn't recorded |

## The stop (pick the 2–4 that are actually gaps)

**Q-A · What you already have** — *"Here's what I've got on what your brokerage and your upline already give
an agent on day one: [the list, generic names]. What am I missing, and what would you personally hand an
agent in their first week?"* Listen for: a weekly call, an onboarding doc, templates, a group, a mentor
program, access to the member. Capture in their words.

**Q-B · The one outcome** — *"For [the one-line target], what's the ONE result you can honestly help them
get in their first 90 days — something you've produced yourself?"* Push past "more deals": *"what does
that look like on a Tuesday — three buyers from open houses? their first listing from their sphere?"*

**Q-C · The first lesson** (only if Stop 8 Q32 was blank) — *"If a partner joined tomorrow, what would you
sit down and show them in the first hour? The actual sheet, the actual message, the actual setup."*

**Q-D · Proof not yet written down** — *"Any agent you've helped, even informally, with a result you can
point to? Or your own result with this method — the number you'd say out loud?"* "None yet" is a real
answer and goes in as an open item.

End the stop: *"**Your turn** — short, messy answers are perfect."*

## Consultant moves for "I don't know"
Never write "TBD". Offer 2–3 concrete options built from THEIR data, say why each fits in one line, write
the exact wording, recommend one. Examples of the shape (never reuse the words):
- Thin "you" layer, strong upline: *"Your offer this quarter is 'I get you plugged into [upline's weekly
  calls and onboarding] in week one and I personally walk you through the first [strategy] setup' — small,
  true, and it's what you'd actually do."*
- Strong strategy, no proof: *"Lead with your own result — '[the number they said]' — and say you're taking
  your first three partners through it for free to build the case studies. That's exactly how Mike
  started."*
- Several strategies: pick the one with "Teach it: yes" that maps to the avatar's #1 pain; the rest are
  lessons 2 and 3.

## Vague answers and the follow-up
| They say | Ask |
|---|---|
| "Training and support" | *"Which training, and what does an agent do differently after it?"* |
| "I'll be there for them" | *"What does that look like on a calendar — a weekly slot, a first-week 1:1, a group?"* |
| "Our brokerage has everything" | *"Name three things, in plain words, that an agent uses in their first month."* |
| "I'm good at marketing" | *"Which one thing produced the most business — and could you show someone the exact setup?"* |

Maximum two follow-ups. Then build with what's there and mark open items.

## Never ask
Splits, caps, fees, stock, rev share, income. Anything the Brain answers. A second stop of questions
before the draft exists. "What's your offer?" — the member is here because they don't know yet.
