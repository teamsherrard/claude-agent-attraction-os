# Agent Attraction Brain — Persona Map

The one question every later skill depends on: *who am I actually trying to reach?* The biggest
mistake in agent attraction is trying to attract anyone with a license. This skill ends that. It
picks ONE primary type of agent from the six Mike teaches, builds a real portrait of that agent,
adds up to two secondary avatars if the member wants them, and writes it all to the Brain.

Mike's rule for choosing (`04-value-proposition/27`): *you're most qualified to help the person you
used to be.* The avatar comes from the member's own experience, skills, and hardest stretch, never
from which type sounds attractive.

**Say "type of agent" and "Agent Avatar" to the member. Never "persona", "tier", or "segment".**

## The six types (the card you work from)

Every agent is one of these, sometimes two. Citations are the lessons the facts come from.

| Type | Who they are | What hurts right now | What they're looking for | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **New agents** (0–2 yrs) | Often digital natives; growth-oriented; want to feel part of something; have time, so they research heavily | Zero income, no leads, no direction, no clue where to start; fear of being "another number" | Mentorship, a clear first 30/60/90 days, community, modern tools, local support | `02-prospect-targeting/21` |
| **Experienced, low production** (3–8+ yrs, not consistently closing) | Understand the business; have switched brokerages 3–5 times; skeptical of promises; tired | Inconsistent income, no process, poor time management, technology gap, low confidence, comparing themselves to peers | A turnaround plan and someone who will actually work with them; empathy without shame | `02-prospect-targeting/22` |
| **Top producers** (Mike's line: ~$250K+/yr, market-relative) | Confident, respected, loyal to where they succeeded, entrepreneurial; slower to move (listings, contracts) | Plateau, everything on their shoulders, active income only, under-recognized, brand bigger than their market can pay for | The math, a smooth transition plan, people ahead of them, a way to get paid for a brand beyond their market | `02-prospect-targeting/23` |
| **Influencers** (audience-first agents) | Strong presence on IG / TikTok / YouTube / podcasts; followed by consumers AND agents; brand-protective | Leaving money on the table, income still tied to their own effort, no exit, geographic cap on a brand that isn't geographic | A monetization path that doesn't dilute their brand, no "adult daycare", bigger stage | `02-prospect-targeting/24` |
| **Team leaders** (5–25+ agents) | Produce themselves AND carry training, leads, culture; often 60–80% of the team's volume | Overhead, churn of their best agents into competitors, no broker support, no time, no exit | Retention of their talent, scaling without more cost, a transition plan, corporate support | `02-prospect-targeting/25` |
| **Broker-owners** (independent or franchise, 10–200+ agents) | Proud, attached to their brand, carry all risk and liability; most are less profitable than they look | Overhead, churn, competing against cloud models, geographic limits, no real exit, isolation | Keep the brand, drop the risk, five-to-seven layers of support, a clear numbers case | `02-prospect-targeting/26` |

**The niche-to-type map** (`04-value-proposition/27`): new agents value foundational skills, mindset,
productivity, lead generation · struggling experienced agents want to rebuild momentum and course-correct
(systems) · top producers want branding, reach beyond their own effort, expansion · influencers want monetization of the brand
and residual income · teams and brokerages want retention, growth, leadership, culture. A member whose
"what worked" is open houses and a Sunday follow-up routine maps to new or low-production agents; a member
who built a YouTube channel maps to influencers and producers who want a brand.

**Where each type lives in the week** is what the doctrine adds; `where they gather` in the written avatar
must name types of rooms (the brokerage training room, the new-agent Facebook group, the local
association's event, the YouTube channel they binge), never real people.

**Franchise broker-owners** carry a legal caution (`02-prospect-targeting/26`): approaching an owner under a
franchise agreement outside their "look period" is tortious interference. If broker-owners become an
avatar, the avatar file carries that line and the prospect radar enforces it.

## The method (how you decide who fits)

**The mirror principle.** Agents are attracted to someone who was recently where they are now and got
out. The member's primary avatar is usually living the member's **hardest-stretch beat** today, two or
three years behind them on the same road. A newer member with little production history attracts by
shared vision instead: agents who want to build the same thing, at the same stage.

**The relatability test, in order:**
1. Would this agent recognize themselves in the member's journey?
2. Would the member's "what worked" strategies solve this agent's biggest problem right now?
3. Could the member credibly help this agent — mentor them if they're a few years ahead, build alongside
   them if they're at the same stage (never "out-earn them")?
If any answer is no, that type is not the primary.

**Who's already around them** (Stop 4, Q13) is evidence, not the decision: a sphere full of new agents
makes new agents the easy first 90 days, but the primary is still the mirror.

**The two-type trap.** If the member wants "everyone", explain the mirror in two sentences and deliver one
primary plus at most two secondary anyway. An avatar that fits everyone attracts no one.

## Stop 4 · Your niche (the questions, exactly)

Orient first: *"Next: who you're building for. Here are the six types of agent Mike teaches — most people
were one of them, and most of us are best at helping the one we used to be."* Show the six types in one
line each. Then the four questions, together, with chips where the interface renders them (multi-select
where it fits, always "or tell me in your own words"):

11. *"Which type of agent do you understand best, because you were one?"*
12. *"Which type is living your 'hardest stretch' right now?"* (name their beat back to them from `journey.md`)
13. *"Who's already around you most? Roughly count the agents in your sphere by type."*
14. *"Geography: local only, your whole state/province, anywhere in the country, or specific states?"*

**Your turn** — tap or type. Anything the Brain already answers (a Realtor Brain bridge usually answers
part of 11 and 13) is folded in and its question dropped.

Geography is stored as the member's **reach** (for the radar and the Top-50), never inside the portrait: a
type of agent lives anywhere the member's brokerage operates, and the written avatar carries no city.

## Between the stops — propose the primary (consultant, not scribe)

From the answers plus the Brain, propose the primary type. Say WHY in one plain line built from their
data, name the type you rejected and why, and give the one-line target in this exact shape:

> **[Career stage] agents doing [production range], at [brokerage type], who [the specific frustration]
> and want [the specific outcome].**

Brokerage type is franchise, independent, cloud, team — never a brokerage's name. Production range
describes the type, labeled as a profile, never presented as data.

If they're unsure or disagree: give 2–3 options with the why for each, recommend one, their choice is final.

## Stop 5 · Your primary Agent Avatar (the questions, exactly)

Confirm the primary in one line, then:

15. *"In their words, what's their biggest problem right now?"*
16. *"What have they already tried that didn't work?"*
17. *"What would they need to hear from you to believe you can help?"*
18. *"Do you want a second (and third) avatar, or keep it to one for the first 90 days?"*

**Your turn.** Default on 18 when unsure: one avatar for 90 days, and say so — *"One is faster. We add the
second the day your content starts pulling a different kind of agent."* If they want a secondary, ask 15–17
again for that type in ONE stop (never a third stop per avatar; a secondary gets the short form).

## Rules

**The quality bar (check before anything is shown or written)**
- The delete test: a sentence that could go without losing information goes.
- The any-agent test: a line any agent at any brokerage could have written isn't finished — rewrite it with
  this member's journey, strength, or market.
- The so-what test: every fact is followed by what to do about it.
- No hedging. No filler headings. Develop, never transcribe: the echo test — if the avatar just restates
  the member's answers in order, it isn't built yet.

**Hard rules**
- One primary. Secondary avatars are optional and short.
- Pain points come from this type's real day-to-day (doctrine + lessons), matched to what the member has
  actually done. Never invent a strength to solve a pain with. Newer members get "we're building this
  together" honesty, never fake seniority.
- No production numbers or market stats presented as fact unless the member gave them or they are cited
  ("per [source], as of [month]"). The profile's ranges describe a type.
- Targeting by brokerage NAME, or compensation anywhere in the avatar: one line, no lecture — *"I target by
  brokerage type, never by name, and the numbers stay for your private call"* — then build it that way.
- Cardinal rules (`03-model-positioning/13`): never talk badly about another brokerage; never talk badly
  about another person. "Not the right fit" is about fit and timing, never disrespect.
- Brokerage-agnostic: eXp, REAL, LPT, Epique, any cloud brokerage, a local team, or a local brokerage.
  Read the Brain for which; never assume.
- Attraction, not recruiting: the avatar is built so the member leads with story, skills, and support.
- Banned words: unlock, supercharge, game-changer, revolutionary, secret weapon, leverage (as a verb).
- Demo mode (the member explicitly asked for a fictional brain): same structure, fictional member, every
  number "(illustrative — demo)", per `shared/brain-book-spec.md`.
- This skill writes `identity/avatars.md` and nothing else. It never writes `top-50.md`, `offer.md`, or
  `prospect-intel.md`; it points the member to the skills that do.
