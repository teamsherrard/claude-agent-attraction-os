# Simple Tech Stack (Week 5)

Mike's deliberately minimal tooling for attraction and the follow-up philosophy that goes with it. No CRM, no drip campaigns: G Suite, Zoom, Loom, ChatGPT, and Calendly cover almost everything, social media does the quiet nurturing, and follow-up is personal, value-driven, and triggered by real events rather than a schedule. Simple is what your agents can copy.

### 83 — Tech Stack
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/2db8ab926ed14672b669b93def81caf0 · 7 min*
**In one line:** You do not need a CRM or drip campaigns; G Suite, Zoom, Loom, ChatGPT, and Calendly cover 90% of attraction, and simple is what duplicates.
**Mike's points:**
- Don't overcomplicate attraction. The right tools let you communicate, follow up, and deliver value quickly; a lean stack reduces overwhelm and increases action.
- On drip campaigns: Mike says everyone he has heard of relying on them is not growing. People lean on them because they are weak on the call and on the value proposition. Do an effective call, send the right email, put out the right content, and they join after one call.
- Minimum stack: G Suite (professional email, shared Drive for documents, scripts, and resources; turn the invite email and the welcome email into templates and personalize the first message). Zoom (paid account so there is no 45-minute limit; raise the attendee limit as you grow, Mike went to 1,000; record calls to repurpose as training and onboarding). Loom (quick personalized video follow-ups, walkthroughs of resources, answers to questions you didn't have on the call; scalable but personal). ChatGPT (scripts, objection handlers, follow-up messages, guides, checklists, training outlines, email nurturing). Calendly for bookings, covered in its own video.
- These cover 90% of what you need. Master the basics before adding complexity, and don't let tech become an excuse for inaction. Simplicity allows duplication because your agents can replicate it.
- Tracking: Mike started with a Google Sheet numbered 1 to 100 because he wanted 100 agents fast. After each call he noted where they were in the process and followed up personally, one by one. Once the foundations were right, very few needed following up with.
**Say it like Mike:** "Don't overcomplicate attraction. You do not need dozens of tools." · "Master the basics before adding complexity." · "Simple scales, make it simple. Don't over complicate it."
**Answers these questions:**
- What tools do I need for agent attraction?
- Do I need a CRM?
- Should I set up a drip campaign for prospects?
- How did Mike track his prospects?
- Which Zoom plan do I need?
**Do / don't:** Do template the two emails and personalize the first paragraph. Don't buy a CRM or drip system to cover for weak calls.

### 84 — Social Media to Nurture
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/4d8a3ee907944e2f83dd12f33d89869f · 7 min*
**In one line:** Prospects who don't join today keep watching; social media nurtures silently in the background so when the timing is right you are the obvious choice.
**Mike's points:**
- Not every prospect joins right away. Most take months; some joined Mike two or three years after the first conversation. Social keeps you top of mind, builds value and credibility, and when they're ready you are the obvious choice.
- Goal: stay present, add value, build trust, show leadership and results without being pushy, and build the relationship digitally before the next conversation.
- Five content types: value content (free prompts, lead magnets, courses that keep them in your world while the real stuff is what they get when they join); recognition content (celebrating your agents; prospects notice and want that); culture content (behind the scenes, events, masterminds, team wins; creates FOMO because their broker does nothing fun); leadership content (personal story, lessons, mindset); lifestyle content (freedom, trips, not chasing the next deal).
- Platforms: Instagram is the foundation for daily visibility (stories, reels, DMs). YouTube is where the major conversion comes from, long-form authority that built Mike's entire organization. Facebook groups for community nurturing on your niche. Email list to repurpose your best content; Mike sends value two to three times a week, every week, for years.
- How: serve, don't sell. Post a mix of value and recognition; use DMs to deepen conversations and send resources (story replies keep the conversation going); share wins of agents who partnered with you (he has playlists with hundreds of these); invite prospects to events, webinars, and masterminds.
- Payoff: a thousand people watch your story and fifty interact; they are always watching. When the timing is right they already feel they know you, which is why a 30-minute Zoom converts.
**Say it like Mike:** "Social media keeps you top of mind until the timing's right." · "serve, don't sell" · "They're always watching."
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent didn't join. How do I stay in touch without being annoying?
- What should I post to nurture prospects?
- Which platform matters most for nurturing versus converting?
- Should I follow my prospects on Instagram?
**Do / don't:** Do make sure prospects follow you and you follow them. Don't pitch in the DMs.

### 85 — Simple Follow Up
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/a83a36e5c86c4a4b97bbf64392d8bcc7 · 9 min*
**In one line:** The fortune is in the follow-up, but it should feel like leadership, not chasing: always lead with value, triggered by real updates, personalized to what they told you.
**Mike's points:**
- Most agents won't join after the first conversation until you get good at bridging the gap. Mike says he reached 98% joining after a 30-minute meeting in his second and third year.
- Principles: always leave value, never "just checking in"; share updates that benefit or inspire them; personalize to what matters to them; use multiple channels (text, email, DM, video message); consistency matters more than frequency.
- Follow-up triggers Mike uses: positive changes to your brokerage structure (his eXp examples: the revenue share update and the co-sponsorship announcement); new tools and technology (his example: CRM of choice, AI training); big agents, teams, or brokerages joining ("this reminded me of you"); major wins in your group; company events coming up; industry shifts that make your model more attractive (his example: the NAR lawsuit made independent brokers nervous about liability, while a cloud-based brokerage carries the risk).
- Events: invite your prospects. Mike's group has paid flights, hotels, and experiences for prospects who are top producers, team leaders, broker owners, or have a big audience, so they see the culture firsthand.
- Value-driven messages: "an agent just like you hit this milestone"; "we just rolled out this tool, it solves exactly what you mentioned"; "with this industry change, a lot of agents are rethinking their brokerage, curious how you're approaching it."
- Simple plan: week one, a recap with all your resources, a thank-you, and something personal. Weeks two to four, value-driven updates. Monthly, a success story or industry update. Quarterly, an invitation to an event, webinar, or mastermind. Adjust to their level of interest; there is no perfect plan.
- Outcome: other sponsors annoy them with calls and texts; you lead with value, so when their pain increases you are their first call.
**Say it like Mike:** "Follow-up should feel like leadership, not chasing" · "Always leave a value, never just like checking in" · "consistency matters more than frequency"
**Answers these questions:**
- How often should I follow up with a prospect?
- What do I say when I follow up?
- What are good reasons to reach back out?
- Should I pay for a prospect to come to the company event?
- What is the week-by-week follow-up plan?
**Do / don't:** Do tie every touch to something that benefits them. Don't "just check in."
