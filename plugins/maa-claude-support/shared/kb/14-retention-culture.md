### 68 — Pillars of Culture
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/87dffa3e3b6a488eb860fe1fabfe9207 · 4 min*
**In one line:** Culture retains what attracting brings in; six pillars make a "must stay" culture that no split can compete with.
**Mike's points:**
- Attracting gets agents in the door; culture makes them stay. If they leave, every call, video, and follow-up that brought them in is wasted money.
- Agents don't just want a brokerage; they want belonging, recognition, and growth. A strong culture lowers turnover, boosts productivity (which means more revenue share), and creates loyalty.
- Six pillars: (1) recognition: acknowledge wins big and small; Mike opens every mastermind call with wins. (2) The right tools: technology and resources agents will actually use, not fluff. (3) Shared experiences: bonding moments through events, calls, and masterminds. (4) Strategic environment: location, systems, and setup that make working easier, so everyone knows exactly where the training is and where to start. (5) Fun and relief: balance hard work with celebration. (6) Team connection: real relationships and peer support; brotherhood, sisterhood, family.
- Why it works: agents stay where they feel valued; recognition builds confidence; growth and tools remove frustration (usually inconsistent income); community and fun create loyalty (the agents who come to his events are ride or die); connection makes it feel like something bigger.
- Culture is a competitive advantage no split can compete with. His agents could get a better split or cap elsewhere but never the value and culture. Nobody wants to leave a family.
**Say it like Mike:** "Culture retains what recruiting attracts." · "Agents stay where they feel valued and supported." · "Nobody wants to leave a family. That's what we need to create."
**Answers these questions:**
- What are the six pillars of culture?
- Why do agents leave even when the split is good?
- How do I start building culture with a small group?
- What's the first thing I should add to my calls?
**Do / don't:** Do open every call with wins. Don't rely on split and cap to keep agents.

### 69 — Creating a community
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/1d9232a300cc4f6f8965c85875d401b6 · 7 min*
**In one line:** A group chat is not a community; build a central hub with live calls, peer sharing, recognition, and rhythm, and host it on Skool, never a Facebook group.
**Mike's points:**
- Community extends beyond you. His group has micro-communities run by leaders he developed (his examples: Denver, Seattle, Arizona, Toronto, New Brunswick) doing local meetups, boats, lunches.
- Why: retention is the name of the game. Agents stay where they feel supported and connected; community gives accountability, shared learning, and emotional connection. Recruiting without community is churn.
- What it looks like: a central hub for communication and resources (referrals, updates, wins, new training, everyone contributing, not just the leader); regular live calls, masterminds, and office hours (his group runs one live call a day plus a support team); peer-to-peer collaboration (his agents interview each other on YouTube and collaborate on Instagram); recognition of wins.
- Platform: Skool. He has tried Facebook, Kajabi, Circle, Teachable, and others; Skool is the best for community in his opinion because it has courses, discussions, and built-in leaderboards with gamification that make recognizing engaged people easy. He hosts courses on Circle for automations and branding, but community on Skool. Facebook groups have the worst engagement, can't integrate training, and people are there to doom scroll; Skool is a focused platform.
- Keys: onboard every new agent into the community on day one; post consistently (training, updates, encouragement, featured guests pinned); recognize members publicly (he prefers the live mastermind calls for emotion); encourage peer-to-peer sharing so you're not the only voice (Mike posts less than almost anyone; members share scripts, posts, and content); create rhythm with weekly calls, monthly contests, quarterly events and challenges.
- Benefits: agents never feel alone; your influence scales across hundreds or thousands; when agents see other agents attracting they think "so can I"; loyalty money can't buy.
**Say it like Mike:** "recruiting without community is churn" · "Facebook has the worst engagement." · "a community and culture of sharing value, not hoarding it"
**Answers these questions:**
- What platform should I host my community on?
- Is a Facebook group or a group chat enough?
- How do I get members posting instead of just me?
- What rhythm should the community run on?
**Do / don't:** Do put every new agent in the community on day one. Don't build on a Facebook group.

### 70 — Creating culture
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/e4ace02700b84e5496943d222dada749 · 12 min*
**In one line:** Culture is by design, not accident; shared experiences, events you pay for, and traditions turn agents into advocates who attract for you.
**Mike's points:**
- Culture is the retention engine and turns agents into advocates who talk about the group constantly. Agents leave brokerages when they feel they've outgrown them; make sure they don't leave you for the same reason.
- Core ingredients: attend company events together ("big decisions are made at big events"; they see someone on stage who looks like them; the company spends millions, so use it), host local gatherings (satellite groups doing lunches, boats, lunch-and-learns), and create unforgettable experiences: team dinners, sporting events, adventures, retreats. His examples: helicopters, pontoon boats, lake houses, five yachts for a day.
- Pay for your agents. He and his sponsor have made events free for their agents since day one. In his first year, when he says he made $87,000 in revenue share, they were doing $5,000 dinners for the whole group.
- The $15,000 dinner: ten people, two leaders deciding between him and someone else. They joined, and Mike says they've been worth roughly $200-300K a year each for three years. At the time that dinner was about a quarter of his annual revenue share. Start small: a nice dinner for your current people is about $1,000 and pays for itself.
- Bring a videographer to everything. You post it, the agents post it, their followers get FOMO, more of your agents come to the next one, and it becomes recruiting content: "this is the community you're joining."
- Practical: start a tradition and lock it in (ongoing calls, a monthly local dinner, a quarterly retreat); recognize big wins and small steps; collaboration over competition (friendly trolling, everyone sharing strategies); involve families and partners (spouses at events gain appreciation and support the agent more); make culture part of your recruiting.
- Impact: agents are loyal to people, not logos; attraction gets easier; agents bring others.
**Say it like Mike:** "Culture is by design, not accident." · "Big decisions are made at big events" · "they're loyal to people, not logos"
**Answers these questions:**
- How do I build culture with a small group and a small budget?
- Should I charge agents for dinners and events?
- Why does Mike spend so much on experiences?
- What should I film and share from events?
**Do / don't:** Do document every experience and share it. Don't charge your agents for the dinner.

### 71 — Supporting Without Babysitting
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/6fda150ef4054840a188a8ae919cbab3 · 8 min*
**In one line:** Empower, don't enable: save the people swimming toward the boat, record the answer once, and make agents earn your time by plugging in.
**Mike's points:**
- Your role is to empower, not enable: clarity, tools, and accountability, not micromanagement. Babysitting drains leaders and creates dependency.
- His mistake: he took every call, email, text, and Zoom in the beginning. The people constantly asking for his time weren't on the calls and had zero progress in the training; they were complaining and making excuses. Everybody wants your time; not everybody earns it.
- The quote his group lives by: save the people swimming toward the boat, not the ones swimming away from it.
- The scale math: five minutes feels like nothing to the agent, but a hundred agents times five minutes is 500 minutes answering questions that are already in the training or one Google or ChatGPT search away.
- He has never missed a call with an agent who plugs in and tries. Show up, plug in, and he'll do whatever it takes.
- What support means: pre-record as much as possible so answers scale; a clear 30/60/90-day action plan; systems, scripts, and resources on day one; regular touch points; recognition when they take action; an open door, but responsibility stays with them.
- Traps: answering the same question over and over (his fix: any question asked more than once becomes a Loom video added to the training in Skool, so the reply is "it's in Skool"); doing tasks for agents instead of teaching; and skipping reminders. Keep saying old things to new people; if your top leaders are sick of hearing it, you're doing it right.
- How: document answers, guides, and FAQs; use group platforms so answers scale; ask "did you go through the training?"; celebrate initiative, not dependence. The goal is agents who could run their business if you disappeared.
**Say it like Mike:** "Everybody wants your time. Not everybody earns it." · "save the people swimming toward the boat, not the one swimming away from it" · "That's being a real leader. Not being a cheerleader."
**Answers these questions:**
- My agents keep asking me the same questions. What do I do?
- How do I decide who gets my time?
- Am I being harsh if I don't answer every text?
- How do I support agents at scale?
**Do / don't:** Do turn repeated questions into a recorded answer in your training. Don't do the task for them.

### 72 — Exit Interviews & Retention Metrics to Watch
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/5506df2a7c4a4537a4fc2441d9042053*
**In one line:** Recruiting fills the front door, retention keeps the back door closed; measure turnover, tenure, and engagement, and learn from every departure without burning the bridge.
**Mike's points:**
- Most attractors focus on the front door while the back door is wide open and lose as many as they gain. Without data you're guessing why agents stay or leave.
- Exit interviews: do them with every agent until about 100 to 250; after that, your frontline, and have your leaders do theirs. Keep it open, curious, non-defensive.
- When an agent leaves, Mike sends a congratulations-on-the-next-chapter message, offers help anytime, and leaves the door open ("we'd love to welcome you back into the family one day"). He says the majority come back because he didn't burn the bridge.
- Key questions: what made you decide to leave? What did you value most here? What do you feel was missing? What could we have done differently to support you? Use the answers as feedback, not criticism.
- Metrics, as Mike states them: agent turnover, goal under 20% a year (he cites an industry average of 25 to 30%); average length of service, strong organizations three-plus years; a quarterly satisfaction survey; engagement (call attendance, community activity, event participation; the back office shows deals since joining, and he says about 75% have done none); referral rate; net promoter score.
- He says he is, to his understanding, the only person in eXp's top 50 who has never lost a big team, brokerage, or influencer.
- Turn insight into action: look for patterns across interviews; share metrics transparently with your leadership team (his weekly leaders call, around 50 leaders) and ask for the "fix it forever" solution; change onboarding, culture, or systems based on the feedback. Retention is dozens of small improvements made consistently; five and a half years in he is still building retention assets.
**Say it like Mike:** "if recruiting fills the front door, retention keeps the back door closed" · "what gets measured gets managed" · "retention isn't one big fix. It's dozens of small improvements made consistently."
**Answers these questions:**
- What retention metrics should I track?
- What should I say when an agent leaves?
- What questions go in an exit interview?
- What is a good turnover rate?
- Who runs exit interviews once the group is big?
**Do / don't:** Do keep the door open when they leave. Don't get defensive in the exit interview.
