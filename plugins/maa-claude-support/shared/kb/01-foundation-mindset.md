# Foundation & Recruitment Mindset (Week 1)

This module sets the mindset before any tactic. Mike walks through his own story (three years of YouTube with no results, then the switch to a cloud-based brokerage that made the content pay off), defines attraction versus recruiting, explains why residual income beats active income, and tells members how to set realistic goals, find their why, and guarantee a return on the program by doing the work. His frame throughout: this is a three-year commitment, there is no shortcut, and you attract the kind of person you are.

### 1 — What You Can Learn From It
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/56f6e440f25b428cbdb9c50f611be107 · 13 min*
**In one line:** Mike's own journey proves the model: three years of consistent content with no results, then the right vehicle, then everything compounded.
**Mike's points:**
- Quit an engineering job, got into real estate in 2017, door knocked three hours a day for six months and posted on Instagram every day for 1,000 days because he was broke and those were free.
- Launched YouTube properly in 2018, two videos a week, and was a one-man show for three years: recording, editing, thumbnails, uploading. No excuses about needing a team first.
- His early mistake: mixing local production content with "what works for me" content on one channel, so engagement was terrible.
- He said no to his sponsor at first; he thought the brokerage was an MLM. The sponsor kept collaborating instead of disappearing, and Mike eventually asked to have the model explained properly.
- When he joined (his example: eXp; for you, any cloud-based brokerage) he had only 1,700 subscribers and zero deals from YouTube. Then 30 agents joined in the first 30 days, 357 by end of year one, 1,000 by end of year two.
- Two videos drove it: "why I joined" (so nobody could make assumptions) and a 90-day review.
- Five years as the number one personal attractor at his brokerage without reaching out to a single agent; about 600 agents a year book from his YouTube channel (his numbers).
- Timing is everything; people join on their own timeline. Joshua Smith joined him seven years after they first collaborated.
**Say it like Mike:** "I was consistent for three years without seeing any results. Most people give up after three months." · "If it doesn't duplicate, it doesn't matter." · "So many people are looking for the shortcut to success, which does not exist."
**Answers these questions:** How long until agent attraction works? Do I need a team or editor before I start YouTube? What if an agent says no to me? Did Mike start with a big audience? Why does Mike keep saying three years?
**Do / don't:** Do commit to one platform for years and keep posting through silence. Don't take a no as final; keep collaborating. Don't treat attraction as a selfish revenue-share play.

### 2 — Attraction vs. Recruiting
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/0987b179b2664d6caf3997f0b428aa14 · 6 min*
**In one line:** Recruiting is reaching out; attraction is agents coming to you, and attraction wins on culture, conversion, retention, and leverage.
**Mike's points:**
- Recruiting = cold calling, DMs, lunch and learns, paid ads (he notes that at his brokerage you cannot run ads to a brokerage explained). It is active prospecting, and he has never done it.
- Attraction = YouTube, short form, personal brand, virtual events and webinars. Short form builds awareness; YouTube is usually where the conversion happens.
- "You attract the type of person that you are." Recruiting pulls in a melting pot of personalities and values; attraction brings people who already like you and your mission, so culture and retention are easier.
- Until you have a recognized brand, recruiting is "selling your soul" the way prospecting is in production. With attraction, people arrive already knowing your value.
- Better conversion and show-up rates, the same way a known luxury agent rarely competes against a lineup.
- Recruiting is linear: time in equals output. Attraction is exponential: starts low, hits an inflection point, then takes off (his third year of YouTube).
- Agent attraction is built like a listing business.
**Say it like Mike:** "Your vibe attracts your tribe." · "Recruiting is reaching out to people directly." · "Yes, it's going to be a three-year commitment, but after those three years, you'll change your life forever."
**Answers these questions:** What's the difference between attracting and recruiting? Should I DM agents? Why is Mike's culture so tight if everyone is remote? Why do attracted agents convert better? Can I run ads for my brokerage?
**Do / don't:** Do build through content and brand. Don't cold-prospect agents; you will attract people who do not fit you.

### 3 — Guarantee Success
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/7cd416ed45d24c8885a3c083360f3721 · 10 min*
**In one line:** The return on this program is a choice: winners take ownership, execute immediately, and commit to the process; broke agents binge and blame.
**Mike's points:**
- What broke agents do: binge training without executing, want overnight success, want it without sacrifice, blame the program, play the victim, treat an investment as an expense, and convince themselves they are the special case it won't work for.
- What winners do: take complete ownership ("not everything is your fault, but make everything your problem"), execute immediately, commit to a proven process until it works, stay disciplined, consistent, and embrace delayed gratification.
- Extend your time horizon: if you expect results in three months you will be disappointed; expect three years and you are almost guaranteed to win.
- Focus on one thing. "The man who chases two rabbits catches none." He became the top attractor by doing one platform well.
- He has invested upwards of a million dollars in himself (his figure), never asked for a refund even on terrible programs, and always hunts for one new thing to implement; one strategy can be the whole return.
- His examples (his numbers): $5,000 took him from $103K to $205K; $21,000 on branding took him from $25K to $300K; $25,000 on YouTube took him to $3.2M. Friends who bought the same trainings are where they were years ago.
- Commit to at least a five-times return on anything you invest in; that is on you, not the program.
**Say it like Mike:** "Not everything is going to be your fault, but you have to make everything your problem." · "The man who chases two rabbits catches none." · "Guaranteeing the return on this training or any training is a choice that winners make."
**Answers these questions:** How do I get the most out of this program? Why isn't this working for me yet? Should I ask for a refund? How many things should I focus on? What's the right timeline to expect?
**Do / don't:** Do execute on each lesson as you go and pick one platform. Don't binge all the videos first. Don't tell yourself your market or situation is the exception.

### 5 — New Era of Cloud Based
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/9dcb51757cec484cb5cf92182e11e2c2 · 6 min*
**In one line:** Agents are now sponsor shopping, not just brokerage shopping, so your goal is to be the sponsor they choose, with a value proposition you actually deliver.
**Mike's points:**
- With many cloud-based options (he names eXp, Real, LPT, Epic), agents compare sponsors inside and across brokerages.
- What agents look at in a brokerage: timing (is it in their market), opportunity, support, training, compensation. "Early" is relative: a brokerage going from 80,000 to 120,000 can be earlier than one going 5,000 to 20,000.
- What agents look at in a sponsor: track record, reputation, training beyond the brokerage, support beyond the brokerage (strategy, mentorship, coaching, attraction), culture and community, and a brand they are proud to be associated with.
- Most of his agents say they would join him regardless of brokerage; the brokerage is just the vehicle.
- The magic happens when agents want to partner with you so badly the brokerage becomes irrelevant; position yourself as a no-brainer.
- The value proposition has to actually work and you have to actually deliver it. He gets weekly emails from agents who were promised things by a sponsor and got nothing; switching sponsors is nearly impossible without leaving for 6 to 12 months.
- Genuinely care about people, not the money; revenue share is the byproduct.
**Say it like Mike:** "Agents are now not only brokerage shopping, but they're also sponsor shopping." · "The magic happens when agents want to partner with you so bad that the brokerage becomes irrelevant." · "When an agent joins you, they're putting themselves and their family's future in your hands."
**Answers these questions:** What are agents looking for in a sponsor? Is it too late to be early at my brokerage? Why do agents pick one sponsor over another? What if I promise more than I can deliver?
**Do / don't:** Do build value above and beyond the brokerage and deliver every piece of it. Don't tell agents what they want to hear to get the signature.

### 6 — From Agent to Leader
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/489cc9d92d954997a8427211fecb7b6e · 9 min*
**In one line:** The size of your business is the size of your leadership; attraction is a selfless, lead-by-example identity shift, not another production play.
**Mike's points:**
- Production is mostly about you; attraction is real leadership: building influence, serving others, developing talent, long-term growth, changing lives.
- Selfish sponsors plateau and lose more agents than they gain; he tracks it and sees the cycle repeat.
- Compelling vision: paint a vision so big that everyone in your group can hit their goals inside it, or they will feel they have to outgrow you.
- Absolute certainty: in a bad market, never talk negative, never complain, never use fear; give agents confidence and strategies to take market share.
- Authentic congruence: never tell agents to do something you don't do yourself at the highest level. He executes more consistently than any of his agents.
- Courage and high standards: hold agents accountable, call people out during challenges, raise their standards in every area of life.
- John Maxwell laws he cites: magnetism (you are who you attract), solid ground (trust), addition (serve), influence (the true measure).
- Build a culture that lasts beyond you; his Wolfpack would continue without him because it is built on core values and standards.
**Say it like Mike:** "The size of your business is the size of your leadership." · "I never have told my agents to do something that I don't do myself at the highest level." · "Leaders bring certainty with whatever they can control."
**Answers these questions:** How is attraction different from production mentally? What should I say to my agents about a tough market? Why aren't my agents doing what I tell them? What kind of vision do I need?
**Do / don't:** Do walk the walk before you ask anyone else to. Don't vent doom and gloom to your group. Don't compare or talk negatively about others.

### 7 — Power of Residual Income
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/16c2137a11e24b9ead7af217db66f0ee · 6 min*
**In one line:** Active income resets to zero every January 1; revenue share starts where you left off and compounds, which is the path to time freedom.
**Mike's points:**
- Production matters first; without revenue there is nothing to share. He is not discounting it.
- Active income problems: one deal, one paycheck; back to zero every January 1 regardless of awards; income swings with the market; time traded for money; no exit plan; "agents never retire, they just expire."
- Residual income: one attracted agent can turn into hundreds (he has agents who now have 400 in their group); like selling one house that sells 400 more without you.
- Think compounding interest: January 1 starts where December 31 ended, and it compounds every year.
- Spread across many markets and price points, so market swings barely dent it.
- His approximate path (his numbers): year one 357 agents and about $87,000 in revenue share; then about $450,000; about $820,000; then $1.4 million in year four.
- Roughly doubling each year with less of his time required, the opposite of production.
**Say it like Mike:** "Agents never retire, they just expire." · "With revenue share, you start January 1st where you left off the day before." · "You can create money freedom, but you cannot create time freedom."
**Answers these questions:** Why bother with revenue share if I'm busy producing? How does revenue share compound? What did Mike's income look like year by year? Does the market hurt revenue share?
**Do / don't:** Do keep producing while you build. Don't expect year one to look like year four; the front line carries it early.

### 8 — Setting Goals
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/197045dd1c5d4767a26f6292edd396f0 · 11 min*
**In one line:** Set realistic three-year expectations, measure the actions you control, audit monthly, and value the intangible wins.
**Mike's points:**
- Unrealistic expectations guarantee failure: people see his 357 first-year agents and forget he had posted for three years before.
- Short-term sacrifice (three years) or long-term sacrifice (30 to 40 years chasing the next deal). Commit until it works, "not if, but when."
- Do not compare your chapter one to someone else's chapter 20; only compare to who you were last week.
- Income is a direct reflection of standards and habits. Ask what someone earning your goal would do, then do that.
- Two goal types: outcome (e.g., 30 frontline agents in 12 months) which you cannot fully control, and action (e.g., 2 long-form and 5 short-form videos a week, every week) which you can.
- He cites one leader at his brokerage who averaged about two attracted agents a month and built a huge group; big numbers are not required, consistency is.
- Monthly audit: did you attract the agents, convert the ones you spoke with, stay consistent? If no, why? Own it.
- Intangibles count: better on camera, more confident explaining the model and on calls. Most people at any brokerage cannot explain their model properly.
- Work in quarters (he references The 12 Week Year) and map agents plus committed actions.
**Say it like Mike:** "You're making right now the exact amount of money you deserve based on your standards and habits." · "Don't compare your chapter one to somebody else's chapter 20." · "You cannot control the uncontrollable, so stop trying."
**Answers these questions:** How many agents should I aim for in year one? What should my weekly content goal be? How do I review my progress? I'm posting but no agents yet; is it working?
**Do / don't:** Do set action goals you can guarantee. Don't watch what other attractors are doing.

### 9 — 1 Agent Can Change Your Life
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/583d8fba300e4189aa4da6aef66ef3c4 · 6 min*
**In one line:** One agent can change everything, but you earn that agent by hitting singles consistently and proving yourself, not by chasing one whale.
**Mike's points:**
- He is approximately 70% of his sponsor's entire revenue share group (his figure); one disciplined executor changed his sponsor's life. But he joined three years into that sponsor's journey, and his own first major attractor (Joshua Smith) came four years in.
- Strong leaders don't follow weak people: complainers, excuse makers, inconsistent people.
- Many strong agents watch before they join. Several DM'd him early, waited to see his first year (357) and second (1,000), then came over.
- People are watching even when they don't engage: a story gets a thousand views while the reel gets a hundred.
- It is a numbers game; after enough agents, the odds favor at least one incredible one. Don't put all your eggs in one basket.
- The mistake: spending every waking moment on one big team leader or influencer who may never join, and who often does less than expected if they do.
- Hit enough singles and you win the game and set yourself up for home runs. "Repetition is the mother of mastery."
**Say it like Mike:** "You were one agent away from a completely different life." · "Strong leaders don't follow weak people." · "If you hit enough singles, not only will you win the game, but you will position yourself to hit more home runs."
**Answers these questions:** Should I focus on landing one big influencer? Why did that top producer not join after our call? Are people even seeing my content? When will I attract a big player?
**Do / don't:** Do lead by example so watchers see it's legit. Don't bet your year on one person.

### 10 — Clarity on Your Why
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/d6b142e3450c4661999d7705eacd1db6 · 6 min*
**In one line:** This journey is hard, people will talk badly about you, and you will want to quit; a why bigger than yourself is what keeps you going.
**Mike's points:**
- It will not be easy and will not happen overnight. He feels like giving up about once a year and never does because he replays his why.
- People will talk badly about you and make up lies, almost always people who have never met you. That is jealousy and ego from people who are losing.
- You must execute when you don't feel like it; that separates winners from "fair weather agents."
- Show up with passion even when exhausted; if you bring an argument or no sleep into a mastermind call, that energy transfers to hundreds of people.
- His why: take his parents to Hawaii (their honeymoon they could never afford to revisit), retire them, buy his mom her dream car, take his dad on his dream fishing trip, help agents build financial freedom. Every item has come true.
- Make the purpose bigger than you; disappointing yourself is easy, disappointing people you promised hits differently.
- Write the vision down: who you are doing this for, why the sacrifice is worth it.
**Say it like Mike:** "The only thing that guarantees that you never lived the life that you dreamed of is if you quit." · "Anything worth doing, anything worth achieving, anything big, substantial, life-changing is not easy." · "Never, ever give up."
**Answers these questions:** How do I stay motivated when nothing is working? What do I do about agents gossiping about me? Do I still post when I'm sick or exhausted? What should my why look like?
**Do / don't:** Do write your vision and replay it on hard days. Don't transfer bad energy to your group. Don't respond to haters by tearing anyone down.
