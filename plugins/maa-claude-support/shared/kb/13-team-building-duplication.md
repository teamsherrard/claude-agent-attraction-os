### 63 — Duplication 101
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/3be09f87821848aa94a0ab25b151a585 · 8 min*
**In one line:** If it doesn't duplicate, it doesn't matter: you attracting is linear growth, your agents attracting is exponential growth.
**Mike's points:**
- Adding agents personally is linear; your agents adding agents is exponential. Leaders create leaders. Mike says he could step away now and the business keeps growing because he has trained 20-plus people to do exactly what he does.
- A real business is one you can step away from. Duplication turns revenue share into a real business, not a side hustle.
- Helping your agents attract: teach them the same frameworks (fastest route is enrolling them in this program), provide scripts, objection handlers, and systems, model leadership because agents duplicate what they see, and build a culture where attraction is celebrated (first agent, 10, 100, and up).
- Agents won't put effort into something they don't believe is worth it. Explain what's in it for them and how you will support them with three-way calls so it doesn't disrupt production. Mike's method: one call every Tuesday evening for four years explaining the model and value proposition. Production-focused agents (88% of his group, in his numbers) just invite someone; he presents and the guest joins whoever invited them.
- You are serving people. An agent at a traditional brokerage has one income stream, no ownership, no residual income, no exit strategy.
- His story: an agent in his city with about $20,000 a month in revenue share could spend the last three months of her father's life at his bedside without producing.
- The compounding math as he gives it: five agents each attract three, you have 20, then 60, 180, 360, 1,080 at six levels; a thousand agents can mean six-figure months in residual income.
- In the beginning you do the heavy lifting; Mike personally paced growth until he had 157 frontline agents. One agent can change your life: one agent makes up 70% of his sponsor's group.
**Say it like Mike:** "If it doesn't duplicate, it doesn't matter." · "A real business is one that you could step away from and it continues to operate." · "you're actually serving people by bringing them into this opportunity"
**Answers these questions:**
- Why isn't attracting agents myself enough?
- How do I get my agents to attract without disrupting their production?
- What do I tell an agent who asks what's in it for them?
- How does the compounding math work?
**Do / don't:** Do make it as easy as "invite them to my call." Don't expect agents to attract without knowing what's in it for them.

### 64 — Creating an Onboarding Experience
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/765a6b7b062a441daa7005f2b7c44ff9 · 6 min*
**In one line:** The first 30 days define the relationship; onboarding is your retention and duplication strategy.
**Mike's points:**
- Most sponsors do what most agents do after a closing: disappear. The new agent gets lost, confused, overwhelmed, sees no success, feels the promises were a lie, and leaves. Agents decide within weeks whether they made the right move; it's buyer's remorse.
- Goals: connection (welcome, invite to the mastermind calls), clarity (exactly what to do in the first 30 to 90 days, "start here"), confidence (tools, scripts, and systems day one; Mike gives access to the private community, all past calls, and every training program the day the license goes active), quick wins (first lead, first meeting booked), and reinforced value, support, and leadership (his community has a whole mindset section because mindset is the number one reason agents fail).
- Five-part process: (1) welcome and culture integration: personal welcome video or call while you're small, a welcome email with every link in sequence and a welcome video; (2) technical setup: CRM, email marketing, branding templates, the brokerage's transaction and e-signature tools; (3) training, resources, and mentorship: the brokerage mentor, an accountability partner, and a quick check-in text every week for the first 90 days; (4) quick wins and recognition: help them to the first deal or first agent conversation fast, celebrate with a quick video, call, or text; (5) keys to magnetic onboarding: make them say "I've never experienced this before."
- Most brokerages have terrible onboarding. Mike says eXp's is not great, so he built his own. Over-communicate early, pin video walkthroughs to the top, give a step-by-step roadmap that is clear not overwhelming, assign a point of contact, create immediate results, and recognize them publicly on calls.
- Checklists for all of this are provided as downloads.
**Say it like Mike:** "the first 30 days define the relationship" · "Make them say, I've never experienced this before." · "over communicate early, don't let them feel lost"
**Answers these questions:**
- What should happen in an agent's first 30 days?
- What do I include in my onboarding?
- My brokerage's onboarding is bad. What do I do?
- How often should I check in with a new agent?
**Do / don't:** Do give access to everything on day one with a clear "start here." Don't go quiet after they join.

### 65 — Plugging in
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/30861f4997d94b5681a53c681b7570a2 · 6 min*
**In one line:** Attracting gets them in, plugging in keeps them in; isolated agents go silent, then leave.
**Mike's points:**
- If agents don't experience your value quickly they disengage, go silent, stop showing up to calls and training, and leave. Mike tracks it and has the numbers. Community and support are the glue.
- Most agents leave because they don't know what they have access to. The agents he has seen leave did zero deals and never accessed the training; you can't save those people.
- Explain why to plug in, with references and proof: all the top producers go to the mastermind calls looking for one thing a week to apply; every success story on his YouTube channel followed the exact process in the training. Success leaves clues.
- What plugging in looks like: community access (group chats, masterminds, events; his group has a private community, daily mastermind calls, and a group chat), easy-to-access training, playbooks, and scripts with a support team behind them, mentorship ("don't ever feel bad about hitting me up"), public recognition, and an engagement rhythm (start with one weekly call like he did in 2020).
- Risks: isolation leads to attrition; post-call excitement fades when reality hits (his Mike Tyson "punched in the face" reference) and they drift back to old habits; much higher chance of leaving if they don't feel connected in the first 90 days; recruitment without integration is wasted.
- Retention formula: plug in, produce, stay. Mike cites a stat he saw that about 90% won't leave once they've closed around four deals or attracted three agents. So push producers to their first three agents and non-producers to their first four deals. Math makes everything easier.
**Say it like Mike:** "Attracting gets them in, but plugging keeps them in." · "Most agents leave because they don't even know what they have access to." · "recruitment without integration is wasted time and energy"
**Answers these questions:**
- Why do new agents go quiet and then leave?
- How do I get agents to actually use the training?
- What is the retention formula?
- How many deals or agents before someone is likely to stay?
**Do / don't:** Do explain why to plug in, with proof. Don't assume they know what they have access to.

### 66 — Teaching Others to Attract
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/5f2e2abd4c5441bd85a55b7ade5d6040 · 7 min*
**In one line:** The true measure of leadership is how many leaders you create; model it first, show them what's in it for them, make them learn before they talk to anyone, and make sure they plug their agents in.
**Mike's points:**
- Leadership is measured by leaders created, not followers gathered. Mike says the agents he has helped become financially free matter more to him than his audience.
- Your role: execute first (he's the most consistent on YouTube in his group), then empower them to duplicate.
- What's in it for them: residual income, influence and leadership positioning, legacy and impact, financial stability. Production season runs roughly April to October, and the slowest production months are the busiest attraction months, so revenue share smooths the income curve.
- The framework: share your scripts, systems, and processes; teach them to lead with value, not pressure; and get them to learn first. The worst thing he sees: an agent excited about revenue share who doesn't understand the model, three-way calls, edification, or the value proposition goes out recruiting, can't answer questions, and burns bridges. Coach them on proper introductions.
- Make sure they plug their recruits in. Once your agents bring in their own "onesie twosies" you weren't involved; you just see new names in the back office. Mike sent two emails: one to the new agent, one to the sponsor saying this is a huge responsibility, plug them in, get them through onboarding.
- Compounding: Mike says he no longer personally sponsors agents and gives away 600-plus a year to partners whose complementary skill sets give the agent more value. Lean into the ones who prove they're serious: showing up, going through the training, executing.
**Say it like Mike:** "The true measure of leadership is by how many leaders you create, not by how many followers you gather." · "the slowest months of production are the busiest months of attraction" · "Walk the walk, execute at the level that you've never done before to show them what's possible"
**Answers these questions:**
- How do I teach my agents to attract?
- What do I say when an agent asks why they should bother attracting?
- My agent is excited about revenue share but doesn't understand the model. What do I do?
- How do I decide which agents to invest my time in?
**Do / don't:** Do make them learn the model and edification before they talk to anyone. Don't let an agent recruit untrained.

### 67 — Systems to Support Your Team Without Burnout (VA's, etc.)
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/8bd5378b069d46a89dc0476b60c64c64*
**In one line:** Leaders who do everything themselves hit a ceiling and burn out; systems plus a VA give every agent the same experience and give you your time back.
**Mike's points:**
- In the beginning it was all on Mike: agent calls, fires, everything, and he burned out. Your time and energy are what's most valuable; protect them at all costs.
- Core systems every leader needs: (1) onboarding, step by step: first what they need at the brokerage to operate (transaction platform, e-signature, brokerage support), then your group's value proposition with "if you're at this level, go here; if this is your pain point, start there"; (2) an organized training system; (3) communication: a platform for the group chat, community, and weekly calls (he uses Skool and likes how easy the calendar, events, and training are to find); (4) a recognition system; (5) an attraction system, rinse and repeat so anyone can follow it.
- Virtual assistants: scheduling onboarding calls and follow-ups, updating resource libraries (he records a Loom, the VA uploads it with titles and descriptions), social posts and marketing tasks, data tracking and reporting (he tracks group data every month against every other group). Mike offers to help find a VA through the link in the lesson.
- Automate repetitive tasks; spend your energy on leadership, vision, and high-value work: content, agent calls, three-way calls, personal calls.
- Benefits: a consistent agent experience whoever onboards them (everything promised, day one, no questions asked), more time to lead and attract, less decision fatigue, stronger retention because everyone has the support team's email instead of waiting on Mike, and a business that runs on systems.
- Start small: template every repeatable email; add VAs as you scale.
**Say it like Mike:** "leaders who try to do everything, ah, themselves will end up hitting a ceiling" · "what gets measured, get managed" · "a business that runs on systems, not just you"
**Answers these questions:**
- How do I support a growing group without burning out?
- What should a VA do for me?
- What systems does every leader need?
- Which community platform does Mike use?
- What do I do first if I can't afford a VA yet?
**Do / don't:** Do template anything you do twice. Don't be the only support channel.
