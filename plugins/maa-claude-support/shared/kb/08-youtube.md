# YouTube (Week 4)

YouTube is Mike's king platform for agent attraction: intent-based, evergreen, compounding, and the channel that made him the top attractor at his brokerage every year. This module tells his journey (157 views after year one, 1,700 subscribers after three years, zero deals, then everything changed), the three content types that attract (niche value, agent interviews, model breakdowns), how to build niche content and never run out of ideas, how to run interviews where the agent is the star, how to do fair model breakdowns, why titles and thumbnails matter most, the warm casual call to action that turns views into booked calls, and how to build a binge-worthy channel.

### 91 — YouTube
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/49fc23d0479344e7a4156b254c4f6abf · 7 min*
**In one line:** YouTube is king for attraction because it is intent-based and evergreen: videos rank for years, now surface in AI answers, and compound like revenue share.
**Mike's points:**
- The only platform where content works 24/7. Videos rank on Google and YouTube search for years, and now his and his agents' videos get recommended by AI when agents search for the best sponsor.
- Deep trust: agents spend far more time with you (his videos average 20 minutes; a 10-minute video is 10 times a 60-second short).
- Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook are feed-based and "interest" spaces; YouTube is search and learning. Agents are actively looking for answers.
- A library of evidence: last year's videos compound into next year, the way revenue share does. Short form always starts from scratch.
- Mindset: an attraction machine, not just posting. Every video is an extension of your value proposition, quality, and leadership. Focus on leadership, value, and credibility, not perfection; none of his 1,000 videos are perfect.
- Give as much tactical value as possible; he gives away more free than creators charge $3,000 for. Give enough to take action, not the exact full outcome.
- Outcome: agents reach out already trusting you, often thankful because you poured into them more than their broker or paid coaches. That is why he went from 60-minute calls to 30-minute calls with no model explained needed.
- Pre-frames your value proposition: if this much is free, what happens when I join?
**Say it like Mike:** "YouTube is king when it comes to agent attraction." · "It's the only platform where content works for you 24-7." · "Agents come to you, ready to join."
**Answers these questions:** Why YouTube over Instagram or TikTok? Do old videos keep working? How much should I give away free? Why are Mike's calls only 30 minutes?
**Do / don't:** Do treat every video as part of a funnel. Don't post fluff and then say "buy my course."

### 92 — My YouTube Journey
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/bf19028f00c34b8a9ae05b4d124e9540 · 10 min*
**In one line:** Mike's channel started with a 2013 GoPro at midnight, 157 views in year one, and 1,700 subscribers after three years; consistency and the right vehicle did the rest.
**Mike's points:**
- Year one (2017): petrified of the camera, recording at his old office at midnight so nobody saw, licensing books as a tripod, GoPro built-in mic, oversized suit. 157 views after the year.
- Year two: 500 sq ft condo, iPhone, bad lighting, learned to look at the camera. 700 subscribers.
- The comment: an agent from the biggest team in his city, using his real name, told him he shouldn't be licensed. He chose to screenshot it and show what's possible; he now shows it on stage. That person later asked for a ride in the Lamborghini. Never write negative comments on anyone's content.
- Year three: $500 camera, $100 mic, still editing and thumbnails himself, 4 a.m. sessions. 1,700 subscribers when he joined his brokerage in 2020.
- Then he went all in: hired an editor and a designer, recorded at home, ended 2020 at 3,000 subscribers. Two videos changed everything: "why I joined" (30 agents in 30 days) and a three-month review (the next hundred). He updates those titles yearly.
- 2021 to 2025: improved confidence, studio, and equipment every year, built a content team, hit 100,000 subscribers by 2024, averages about 600 agents a year booking in (his numbers). Now records in one take with no script.
- Lessons no one taught him: three years to 1,700 subscribers with zero results and no coach; the brokerage was the vehicle that let the channel pay off; he did two to three videos a week himself while scaling production because it was a priority.
- Don't compare your chapter one to his chapter 20. It starts with one terrible video.
**Say it like Mike:** "It took me three years to get to 1,700 subscribers." · "You will always make time for your priorities." · "It starts with one terrible video."
**Answers these questions:** How did Mike start on YouTube? What gear did he use early? How do I handle hateful comments? How long until a channel works? What were his first big videos?
**Do / don't:** Do post the first video with whatever you have. Don't wait for a team, a studio, or confidence.

### 93 — YouTube Content That Attracts (draft)
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/0ecc12408e4146618a5027f95fbd7fb0 · 10 min*
Transcript is marked draft; content below reflects it as recorded.
**In one line:** Rotate three content types: niche value (attracts interest), agent interviews (build trust), and model breakdowns (create clarity and action).
**Mike's points:**
- Value-driven content tied to your value proposition: teach what you know to prove you walk the walk. His playlists: 42 AI videos, 36 YouTube-for-realtors, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook ads, Google ads; hundreds in his niche.
- It attracts agents aligned with your strengths (his: agents who want to scale the modern way, not door knock) and pulls without pitching.
- Agent interviews: the most powerful and the one nobody does properly. Over 100 interviews of agents who used his value proposition; they show he has never missed a call or message with an agent in his group.
- Relatability by design: every age, race, gender, language, personality, and market. If you don't see yourself in him, you'll see yourself in someone in his group, and everyone wins when you partner with them.
- Model breakdowns: pull back the curtain, simplify what most overcomplicate (he says 90-plus percent can't explain their model; that's why he turned his brokerage down for three weeks). Examples: model explained, co-sponsorship explained, the truth for new agents, how to choose a sponsor, myths and misconceptions.
- Transparency: he openly talks about his brokerage's pitfalls and built a value proposition that solves them.
- The ninja secret: he sets ego aside and features agents with smaller audiences who got results, giving them his bigger platform. Most creators chase big names for clout. It started with one interview; now it's weekly because the group has new winners every week.
**Say it like Mike:** "Value-driven content attracts interest. Interviews with other agents that you've helped succeed build trust. Model breakdowns create clarity and action." · "When you make complex things simple, agents want to join you." · "I use my bigger platform to give my agents a bigger platform."
**Answers these questions:** What should I post on YouTube for attraction? Why interviews instead of just teaching? Should I interview big influencers? What's a model breakdown video?
**Do / don't:** Do rotate all three types. Don't feature big names for clout; feature your own agents.

### 94 — Your Niche
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/cc5e1a26e08145f4af4f2b2dfe93ca25 · 10 min*
**In one line:** Build niche content around who you attract and what you can teach, never run out of ideas, hook in 10 seconds, two videos a week, and let the data tell you what to do more of.
**Mike's points:**
- Start with who you're attracting. Most of his content is for new to mid-level agents (under about $150K a year), though top producers use it too.
- Buckets: tactical training (scripts, social, lead gen), leadership insights (mindset, general education), recognition stories (wins and case studies woven in), personal brand. Test every video: would this help an agent see me as a leader worth following?
- Never run out of topics: every challenge an agent or prospect mentions is a video; use the prompts; type your topic into YouTube search and auto-suggest and make a better version of the videos that already got views; keep a running idea list (he uses Monday, used to use Google Docs).
- Hook in the first 10 seconds with a clear pain point. The hook is the only part he writes out; the rest is bullet points.
- Length: as long as it needs to be to get the outcome, as short as it can be without fluff. Usually 10 to 15 minutes; his run 20-plus.
- Always include a call to action on YouTube (not every short). Book a call for all your training free, or download an asset to build your email list.
- Two videos a week: his team tracked roughly four times the growth of one a week (his number).
- The magic casual call to action: never "click below to join [brokerage]"; the guard goes up. Instead, "if you want all my training, coaching, mentorship, and mastermind calls completely free, book a private Zoom."
- First 100 videos: the only goal is getting better each video. Repurpose the best into shorts; ask your community for feedback.
- Track impressions, click-through rate, and average view duration. Study outperformers and replicate; turn common questions into videos. Done is better than perfect.
**Say it like Mike:** "Hook viewers in the first 10 seconds with a clear pain point." · "Your video should be as long as it needs to be." · "Done is better than perfect."
**Answers these questions:** What should my first YouTube videos be about? How long should a video be? How many per week? What's the call to action? What metrics matter?
**Do / don't:** Do research the search results before recording. Don't name the brokerage in your call to action.

### 95 — Interviews
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/eee1f6aca35b452d881d28f9b3f07a38 · 10 min*
**In one line:** Niche content attracts, interviews convert: spotlight your own agents' results, make them the hero, never interrupt, and close with the casual call to action.
**Mike's points:**
- Social proof beats leader promises. He has close to a thousand photo and video testimonials and over 100 long-form interviews (his numbers), which makes "does it really work?" hard to deny. Prospects think: if it worked for them, it can work for me.
- Who: agents in your group who closed a first deal quickly, capped or hit icon or elite status, turned a struggling business around (these do incredibly well), top producers who scaled, team leaders, broker owners. His channel is production-focused; he does not interview about attraction.
- How he finds them: a quarterly email to all agents listing milestones; now his leaders send him the up-and-comers.
- Structure: backstory (business before joining and before your value proposition), what made them move, their experience and results since, advice for others. Record the intro last so you can hook with what you learned.
- Casual call to action: "if you want Suman Kim's support and mine for free, book a private Zoom." Since you're all partners, they still get you.
- Tips: make the story the hero, keep it casual, ask open-ended questions, let emotion in (people have cried), focus on freedom, income, support. Record on Zoom or Riverside; repurpose clips.
- Be a good interviewer: never interrupt, never make it about you. He knows more about social media than his guests but lets them shine, then adds layers. No written questions; he listens in real time and guides toward the outcome.
- One interview can attract multiple agents; some have brought tens.
**Say it like Mike:** "Your niche content is going to be what attracts people to your channel. The interviews are going to be what convert." · "The person you're interviewing is the star of the show." · "Never interrupt. Don't make it about you."
**Answers these questions:** Who should I interview? How do I structure an interview? What's the call to action on an interview? How do I find agents to feature? Should I script questions?
**Do / don't:** Do record the intro after the interview. Don't talk over your guest.

### 96 — Model Breakdowns
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/aa0834e62b734cecb6760b38e78060bf · 6 min*
**In one line:** Fair, clear model breakdowns turn you from recruiter into educator and make you the first call when an agent is ready; comparison videos work but he doesn't recommend them.
**Mike's points:**
- Most agents at any brokerage are bad at explaining the model, so clarity wins. Agents want clarity, not hype; they want to know they can feed their family.
- Transparency builds authority: he points out what isn't great at his brokerage and shows the resources he built to overcome it.
- Structure: overview and history, compensation (splits, caps, fees), tools and tech (strengths and gaps, filled by your value proposition), support and training, opportunities beyond closings (revenue share, equity, leadership, speaking), culture and community, ending with your value proposition.
- More breakdown angles: myths and misconceptions, best fit for new agents, team leaders, brokers, and "do not join [brokerage] if..." (people run from pain more than toward pleasure; his version has the most views of any model video he has).
- Remake these videos every year as things change to keep ranking and authority.
- Comparison videos: he did one and the other brokerage's CEO threatened to sue. His compliance team said it was accurate, but he took it down because he doesn't need drama. He does not recommend them. If you do one anyway: days of research, facts side by side, objective tone, strengths and weaknesses of each, outcomes agents care about, and never talk badly about another brokerage or sponsor (the cardinal rules).
- Outcome: agents trust you as a fair, knowledgeable leader and book when ready.
**Say it like Mike:** "Agents want clarity, not hype when considering a move." · "When agents trust your breakdown, they trust you as a leader." · "Truthfully, I don't really recommend doing them."
**Answers these questions:** How do I make a brokerage explained video? Should I do a versus video? What sections go in a model breakdown? How often should I redo it? Can I mention my brokerage's weaknesses?
**Do / don't:** Do keep it fair and end with your value proposition. Don't make comparison videos unless you accept the risk and have all the facts.

### 97 — Titles & Thumbnails
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/ef841d146020425b8cd3a2f4209432e3 · 7 min*
**In one line:** Titles and thumbnails are the most overlooked and most important parts of a video; if nobody clicks, nobody watches, so make them bold, simple, and emotional.
**Mike's points:**
- Gorgeous videos that cost thousands get 17 views because nobody clicked. Click-through rate is the most important metric after watch time, and watch time needs the click first.
- Psychology of a click: curiosity (pain to avoid or pleasure to gain), emotion (speak to pain points, desires, or strong opinions), clarity (know what the video is in seconds). Bold and emotional beats complex and clever.
- His thumbnails: high contrast, branded, his face in all of them, big emotion, simple.
- Titles that attract speak to pain and desire: the five mistakes keeping agents stuck, the truth about [brokerage] no one tells you, why most agents can't generate leads on social media, how to close a million in commission free from YouTube.
- Thumbnail rules (he says data-based): three to five words max in large bold text, different words than the title (same text wastes half your chance), facial expressions, contrasting colors (purple background, white or yellow text), visuals that support but don't repeat the title, and a "this is me, I need to watch this" feeling.
- His team now makes thumbnails with AI; he did his own for three years.
- Review click-through rate monthly, aim for 6 to 10% (his benchmark), ignore the first-day spike from supporters, and change title and thumbnail if it's low.
- YouTube lets you test three thumbnails; his team makes three per video and lets YouTube pick.
- Track which titles and visuals consistently win to find your format.
**Say it like Mike:** "Great content means nothing if nobody clicks." · "Bold and emotional always beats complex and clever." · "Use three to five words max in large bold text."
**Answers these questions:** Why are my videos getting no views? What makes a good thumbnail? Should the thumbnail text match the title? What click-through rate is good? Can I test thumbnails?
**Do / don't:** Do test three thumbnails and review monthly. Don't write a sentence on your thumbnail.

### 98 — Turning Views into Calls
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/7416861bcb3d4cd59f56bdc0b60013ab · 6 min*
**In one line:** Views don't build your business, calls to action do: invite, don't sell, put the calendar link first in the description, and sprinkle two warm calls to action in every video.
**Mike's points:**
- People with bigger followings attract fewer agents than he does because his calls to action are better. If you don't tell them exactly what to do, nothing happens.
- The description is your sales assistant working 24/7. Convenience sells: don't make them Google your brokerage and hunt for your contact info.
- Invite, don't sell. Selling pushes away; inviting pulls. Example: "If you'd like my exact playbook, click the link in the description or the pinned comment." "If you want mentorship and support to apply this, feel free to book a call."
- The call to action is the very first link in the description, above the fold, so no "see more" click is needed. Include the calendar link (follow the Calendly tutorial), secondary links to your assets, keywords for SEO, clean formatting.
- Warm phrases: "if you want to see how our agents are getting results just like this," "if this resonates and you want to take your business to the next level, I'd love to hear your goals," "everything I shared today is just the surface," "I can't wait to hear more about your story."
- Placement: hook in the first 15 to 30 seconds, then a call to action within the first minute (usually a free download), then a second around a third to halfway through inviting them to book. Two per video; it doesn't feel like it because they're warm.
- Priority one is the click; priority two is the booking.
**Say it like Mike:** "Content builds awareness, but call to actions create action." · "Invite, don't sell." · "Your description is your sales assistant and it works for you 24-7."
**Answers these questions:** How do I get people to book from my videos? Where does the call to action go? What should my description look like? How many calls to action per video? How do I ask without sounding pushy?
**Do / don't:** Do put the booking link first and ask warmly twice. Don't bury the link or sound like a recruiter.

### 99 — Bingeworthy Content
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/c43ca0be4524465a88319bbfe99846c0 · 7 min*
**In one line:** Trust is built through repetition, so organize your channel into playlists and point every video to the next so prospects binge their way from curious to committed.
**Mike's points:**
- Most agents don't join after one video; they tell him they've binged for days, weeks, or months (spouses are sick of his voice). The more time they spend with you, the faster they move.
- Volume matters, which is another reason not to be a perfectionist. It starts with one video.
- Content pillars become playlists: value and training (your value proposition), mindset, success stories (new and experienced), his journey, culture. Playlists make each pillar easy to binge.
- YouTube rewards channels people stay on and punishes channels people leave.
- At the end of every video, point to the next logical video with an end screen (co-sponsorship explained points to full model explained; a breakdown of an agent's $100K month points to the interview with that agent).
- Link related and top videos in descriptions; use cards and end screens; build series playlists for your brokerage and your value proposition. Never let someone leave without watching one more.
- Small details: consistent branding, matching title and thumbnail style, playlists on the channel homepage (his order: brokerage explained, success stories, value proposition), a short verbal call to action, repurpose clips.
- Outcome: nurture on autopilot. Prospects self-educate, objections get handled, which is how his calls dropped from an hour to 30 minutes with higher conversion. After one year you're in a different place; after three you won't worry about agents coming again.
**Say it like Mike:** "Trust is built through repetition." · "Never let people leave your channel without watching at least one more video." · "After three years, you will never have to worry about agents coming to you again."
**Answers these questions:** How should I organize my channel? What goes on my homepage? What's an end screen for? How do prospects go from watching to joining? How many videos do I need?
**Do / don't:** Do build playlists and end every video with the next one. Don't let your channel be a random pile of uploads.
