# Market Analysis & Prospect Targeting (Week 1)

This module covers why agents move (they leave problems, not companies) and how to position yourself as the person they move to. Mike's controversial opener is that videos don't attract agents, agents attract agents: the goal is a private Zoom call, not a forwarded "model explained" video. He then breaks down six personas (new agents, experienced low producers, top producers, influencers, team leaders, brokerages) with who they are, what he has personally found, their pain points, the objections to expect, and how to position yourself, always with proof and, early on, your upline.

Lessons: 18 Understanding Movement Patterns · 19 Videos Don't Attract · 21 New Agents · 22 Experienced Low Production · 23 Top Producers · 24 Influencers · 25 Team Leaders · 26 Brokerages

### 18 — Understanding Movement Patterns
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/2a2a7733e3614893b2866a62fb5a7bec · 10 min*
**In one line:** Agents leave problems, not companies; solve the five data-backed pain points with the model, your brand, your training, and your culture.
**Mike's points:**
- Why agents leave (he says this is data, not his assumptions): financial structure (limited earning potential, bad splits), poor leadership, poor support (broker never responds), poor tech, limited growth, culture misalignment (toxic, no recognition, no belonging).
- The five critical pain points: financial uncertainty, lack of mentorship and training, outdated training, no work-life balance (the hamster wheel), limited recognition.
- Lean on the model for superior training, multiple income streams, long-term vision and a willable exit, and leverage to earn more than 100% of commission.
- Personal brand: become a role model through execution; prove what's possible so watchers get hope. Some people took one to seven years to come over after watching him.
- "The more attractive you become, the more agents you attract." Attractive means mindset, perspective, work ethic, discipline, results, not appearance. Would you join yourself?
- Fill the gaps of the brokerage training. His example: his brokerage had decent traditional training but weak social media, AI, and branding training, so he built Social Agent Academy and the AI Agent Accelerator and gives them to his agents free. Always have something new on the horizon.
- Getting agents eventually becomes easy; keeping them is where the game is won. Do not create inner circles, because that creates an outer circle and people leave.
- Reinvest in your agents the way you reinvested 20 to 30% of commission into production; revenue share is not 100% profit.
**Say it like Mike:** "Agents leave problems, not companies." · "Would you be attracted to yourself and would you join yourself?" · "Getting agents eventually becomes easy, but keeping them is where the game is won."
**Answers these questions:** Why do agents actually switch brokerages? What pain points should my value proposition hit? What does Mike mean by "becoming attractive"? Is an inner circle in my group a problem? Should I reinvest revenue share?
**Do / don't:** Do build training that fills your brokerage's gaps. Don't create a visible inner circle. Don't treat revenue share as pure profit.

### 19 — Videos Don't Attract
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/3dcac54b561d4ac183b8e4d92108357b · 7 min*
**In one line:** Videos don't attract agents, agents attract agents: get them on a private Zoom, say as little as you have to, and present the model tailored to their pain points.
**Mike's points:**
- Early sponsors at his brokerage grew by sending a "model explained" video, and that worked ten years ago with no competing cloud brokerages or sponsors. They still advise it today, and it no longer works.
- What happens when you send the video: they don't watch it, or they watch it and have a million questions the video can't answer, and they don't join. He calls this the lazy route.
- The right way: a private one-on-one Zoom. Ask what they are looking for, why they took the call, why they aren't hitting their goals. Then present the model tailored to them and blend in your value proposition, answer every question, handle every objection.
- Don't try to explain the model by text, DM, or email. "That's stupid."
- Over 98% of agents he talked to after a 30-minute call joined (his number). If still unsure, a three-way call with the best model-explainer in your upline, which may not be your sponsor.
- His sponsor converted his first 30 agents while Mike studied every objection; then one-hour calls with every agent for a year (157 frontline agents, 157 hours), then 30-minute calls once people had binged his content.
- Ninja strategy: for four years he hosted a weekly Tuesday "model explained plus my value proposition" call his agents could invite prospects to, so he controlled the narrative. He never missed one.
**Say it like Mike:** "Videos don't attract agents. Agents attract agents." · "Your goal is to get them on a private zoom call as quickly as possible. Say as little as you have to." · "This is the lazy route. Do not do this if you want to build a successful business long-term."
**Answers these questions:** Can I just send prospects my upline's explainer video? How do I run the first call? What if I can't close them myself? Should I explain the model over DM? How do I help my agents convert their prospects?
**Do / don't:** Do book the Zoom and ask questions first. Don't recruit by text or by forwarding a video.

### 21 — New Agents
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/6de21fe41f874ac8b0d53c129b677b29 · 13 min*
**In one line:** New agents are the lowest-hanging fruit and where you get your reps: give them a plan, mentorship, community, and proof.
**Mike's points:**
- Who they are: mostly digital natives wanting guidance on social media, AI, and modern business; growth oriented; seeking mentorship and quick wins; community focused; they value culture and leadership over the brokerage brand.
- What he has found: unrealistic expectations; they care about a local office and local support; uneducated on caps and splits; they research heavily and compare brokerages because they have time.
- Pain points: zero income (they want leads), lack of support, outdated tech, no direction, fear of being just a number.
- Quick objections: "I need a local office" (clients don't care; his old boutique brokerage had the nicest office in the city and nobody went). "I need local support" (co-sponsor with someone local, or partner them with a strong agent in your group). "I love my broker" (your broker loves you because you benefit them; what happens when they retire or sell?). "I don't want to recruit" (you don't need to; focus on production).
- Positioning: mentorship (you pour into them while the broker runs adult daycare; five to seven layers of upline support), a 30/60/90-day path mirroring their goals, culture (surround them with winners, not low performers), and proof.
- Show proof by pulling up the actual training on the call. If you have no success stories yet, use "we, not me" and your upline's stories. He has a whole playlist of new-agent success stories.
- He never shows his revenue share on calls; value should carry it, though a top upline's income can inspire.
**Say it like Mike:** "New agents are going to be the most volume, and these are honestly going to be where you get the repetitions in." · "You're a product of your environment. Who are you surrounded with and how often?" · "Use we, not me."
**Answers these questions:** Who should I focus on first? How do I handle "I need a local office"? What do new agents care about? What if I have no success stories yet? Should I show my revenue share?
**Do / don't:** Do give a 30/60/90 plan and show the training live. Don't make promises you have not built.

### 22 — Experienced Low Production
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/1710868bb5dd4e65bf7e5c1aeafce592 · 8 min*
**In one line:** Experienced but struggling agents are skeptical from failed promises; be the turnaround plan, with empathy and proof.
**Mike's points:**
- Who they are: three to eight-plus years licensed, not consistently closing, burnt out, frustrated, open to change but skeptical. Usually three to five brokerages already.
- What he has found: they want to know how YOU will help, not the brokerage; they hope yours is the last switch; they have the most excuses and blame the market, broker, government; riddled with self-doubt; often the most technologically challenged.
- Pain points: unpredictable income, no clear process, terrible time management, no leverage, technology gap, low confidence, constant comparison to peers who became top producers, shiny penny syndrome.
- "Switching didn't work before": show proof of agents who felt the same and turned it around; get to the root cause, which was activities, not the brokerage; give the exact 30/60/90 plan.
- "How are you different?": proof from you or your upline; "I pour into people."
- "I don't want to recruit": focus entirely on production; attraction happens by osmosis as you build.
- Positioning: be the turnaround plan with mentorship, systems, and accountability; empathize, they are often ashamed; everyone has a turnaround point (he has turned 10 to 15-year strugglers into top producers).
- Financial potential matters more here because many are past the halfway point of their career and thinking about an exit.
**Say it like Mike:** "They want to know exactly how you are going to help them, not the brokerage." · "It wasn't a matter of the brokerage not working before you switched, but you didn't have the right plan." · "Everybody has a turnaround point."
**Answers these questions:** How do I talk to an agent who has switched brokerages three times? What are their real pain points? How do I answer "how are you different"? Should I lead with money?
**Do / don't:** Do empathize and give a daily plan. Don't make them feel stupid for lost years.

### 23 — Top Producers
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/678573937dbd459594257f8caf53f03f · 10 min*
**In one line:** Top producers need the math to make sense and a path past their ceiling; respect the ego, show proof, and lean on your upline.
**Mike's points:**
- His definition: at least $250,000 a year. Confident, respected, loyal to the brokerage where they succeeded, entrepreneurial, thinking expansion.
- What he has found: ego (earned), slower to move because of active listings and contracts, focused on the math; you will likely need your upline until you are a big success yourself.
- Pain points: plateau (making $500K, want $750K or $1M), carrying all the weight, active income only, lack of recognition, geographically restricted brand (only referrals capture it today), biggest person in the room so no one to grow from, technology gap, long-term thinking in their late 40s and 50s.
- "I'm already successful": you can't grow if you're the best in your circle; surround yourself with people making double; make more than 100% of commission through cap-back awards, stock, and revenue share.
- "I don't want to disrupt my momentum": a proactive transition plan, working behind the scenes for one to three months so everything is ready on day one.
- "I have everything I need": residual income, equity, impact. "I don't want to recruit": agents already ask you for advice and you get nothing for it; here you are compensated.
- Positioning: add value through upline future-pacing, case studies of top producers who moved, empathy (genuinely admire their success), proof of AI and social training that can add a quarter or half million in production.
**Say it like Mike:** "If the math doesn't make sense, there's no point in them wasting any more time with the discussion." · "You can't grow if you're already the best." · "With top producers, leverage your upline."
**Answers these questions:** How do I approach a top producer? How do I answer "I'm already successful"? What if they have deals in progress? Who counts as a top producer? Should I run the numbers myself?
**Do / don't:** Do bring upline partners who out-earn them. Don't pretend to go toe to toe on numbers you don't have.

### 24 — Influencers
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/756c1ff8086448d3be7e569345c9b6be · 10 min*
**In one line:** Agents with a following are leaving money on the table in the wrong vehicle; show them a monetization and legacy path where their brand stays the focal point.
**Mike's points:**
- He hates the word "influencer" (overused by people with a bit of a following). Who they are: strong presence on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, or podcasts, with local, national, even international followers, respected as creators, but no monetization beyond closings.
- What he has found: most make far less than you'd think; double ego (production and following); they don't see what the brand could become in the right vehicle; they worry about the brokerage brand covering their own.
- His own example: he joined with 1,700 subscribers, disliked the old brokerage logo, kept it for compliance on signs and cards, and made his own brand the focal point.
- Pain points: leaving money on the table (his income went from just over $300K to $3.2M in 12 months after switching, his number); not maximizing leverage; business entirely dependent on them (stop posting, income stops); no exit strategy (you can't sell an Instagram); missing impact.
- "I don't want to ruin my brand": you don't push the brokerage brand; the narrative is that people can now partner with you from anywhere; independent cloud brokerages give brand flexibility.
- "I'll lose speaking and coaching opportunities": his programs grew when he went global; he keeps them brokerage agnostic; forgoing a franchise office talk is worth it.
- "I don't want to manage people": you take the calls, convert, onboard, train for them, as he did early on.
- Positioning: monetization strategy, success stories, "man or woman of the town, or of the world?", freedom and legacy, videos that go make videos for you.
**Say it like Mike:** "Influence in the wrong vehicle, heavily reduces income potential." · "Once they stop creating, their business dies." · "It's not what you do, it's how you do it."
**Answers these questions:** How do I approach an agent with a big following? Will the brokerage brand bury their brand? What if they run a coaching program? What if they don't want to manage anyone?
**Do / don't:** Do challenge the ego gently with the global vision. Don't push the brokerage brand on them.

### 25 — Team Leaders
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/317100591e8741e79fc04efe2950b39d · 9 min*
**In one line:** Team leaders are exhausted by adult daycare and losing their best people; the model lets talent stay partners instead of becoming competition.
**Mike's points:**
- Who they are: leaders with 5 to 25-plus agents (he has brought over 100-plus teams), producing but bogged down by overhead, training, lead gen, and culture.
- What he has found: ego; slow to move (staff, CRM, many agents with contracts); focused on the math. This is where he brings in corporate leadership to run numbers and pull strings.
- Most teams are not as profitable or sellable as you think and have no exit strategy.
- Pain points: overhead (salaries, office, marketing), retention (80/20: most do nothing, the 1% who crush it leave and become competition), lack of broker support, scaling costs more money, time, stress, geographic restriction, no time freedom. Some leaders are 60 to 80% of the team's production; that's not leverage.
- "I don't want to disrupt momentum": proactive three to four month plan with corporate help. "I'll lose my brand and family feel": keep the local team; now agents who aren't a fit can still be sponsored. "We'll lose money": sometimes year one, two steps back to take 20 forward; show case studies. "We're already successful": no exit, no leverage; now five to seven layers of support with a financial interest in you.
- Positioning: bridge the gap, retention story, proof that all their agents get the training too, success stories of teams, financial potential (he out-earns most team leaders' split income with zero overhead), company support.
**Say it like Mike:** "They are the kings and queens of adult daycare." · "They stay your partner and you get to benefit long-term from their success instead of competing against it." · "Sometimes you need to take two steps back to take 20 steps forward."
**Answers these questions:** How do I attract a team? What's the retention argument for a team leader? What if they will lose money in year one? Who runs the numbers for a team?
**Do / don't:** Do involve corporate and plan the transition around closings. Don't promise there is no short-term cost.

### 26 — Brokerages
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/0f63c5f5673149d2a58e947c93545031 · 12 min*
**In one line:** Broker owners carry all the risk and most are broke; a cloud brokerage keeps their brand and ownership while removing overhead, liability, and isolation.
**Mike's points:**
- Warning first: franchise offices have a "look period" near the end of their agreement. Approaching them outside it is tortious interference and you can get sued. Don't.
- Who they are: independent or franchise owners, 10 to 200-plus agents, all overhead and liability, deep emotional attachment, ambitious but exhausted.
- What he has found: the biggest ego of all (it's theirs), the most moving parts (staff, systems, training, agents), and absolute need for number clarity. Most brokers are broke; one he spoke with had about 33 to 35 agents and was losing six figures a year. They won't sell for what they think.
- Pain points: overhead (salaries, rent, insurance, compliance, legal, E&O), churn of top agents, competitive disadvantage against cloud brokerages (three income streams, equity, better splits, training), scaling limits, no exit, isolation.
- "I'll lose my brand and control": they keep it (his example: "Starlight Realty brokered by eXp Realty"); keep the local office. "My agents won't come": you help explain it to every agent after the broker is on board; those who won't come, you don't want. "I already own a brokerage": ownership doesn't equal wealth; look at the P&L. "We'll lose money": two steps back, 20 forward.
- The pitch: keep the pros (brand, ownership, compensation from agents) and lose the cons (risk, liability, geographic limits, no exit, adult daycare).
- Positioning: collaboration and support (five to six layers), growth examples, retention (agents build a brokerage within a brokerage under you), proof of training, success stories via upline and corporate, financial potential, company support (his example: eight brokers and 1,500 employees in California doing the job for you).
**Say it like Mike:** "Most brokers are broke." · "Ownership doesn't equal wealth." · "A brokerage, I promise you, will not move if they don't have full immense clarity of the numbers."
**Answers these questions:** Can I approach a franchise office? Do broker owners keep their name? What if their agents refuse to move? What's the order of conversations when bringing a brokerage over?
**Do / don't:** Do check the look period before any franchise conversation. Don't assume a broker is rich. Don't talk down another brokerage; position the pros and cons.
