# Content Framework (Week 3)

Four short lessons that define the content buckets every platform strategy draws from. Value-based content dissects your niche into as many tactical pieces as possible so you cast a wide net; behind-the-scenes and leadership content shows your culture and transformation (team wins, events, new training); personal brand content shows who you really are (family, values, struggles, passions), not flash; and storytelling content, the success stories of your own agents across every demographic, is what Mike calls his secret sauce and credits with over $5 million in revenue share.

Lessons: 37 Value Based Content · 38 BTS & Leadership · 39 Personal Brand · 40 Storytelling

### 37 — Value Based Content
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/4ec9ecb76872431b9a0070284dc367e2 · 4 min*
**In one line:** Dissect your niche into as many specific, simple, tactical pieces of content as you can so that whatever agents search, they find you.
**Mike's points:**
- Value content is content based on your niche. His niche is social media and AI, so his channel teaches YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and Google ads, and AI tools for real estate agents.
- The goal is to cast a wide net around your niche. He has made 40-plus videos on each of YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok (not combined), so it is nearly impossible to search those topics without finding him.
- You still start with one video.
- You now have an unfair advantage: AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity) for ideation and research. For his first four years he did hundreds of hours of competitor and keyword research manually and spent over $100,000 on mentorship to figure it out (his numbers). So no excuses.
- Examples of his value videos: how a camera-shy introverted realtor gets consistent leads on YouTube (a 35-minute breakdown), Instagram with ManyChat for leads, how agents close $100K-plus from Instagram, AI for the perfect Facebook ad, AI tools to identify sellers. All in-depth, tactical, easy to follow.
- The program includes prompts to help build content batches from your niche and value proposition.
**Say it like Mike:** "The goal is to cast a wide net around your niche, ensuring that no matter what people search for, they will always find you." · "You have to start with one video." · "If I could do this from zero manually by myself, you can do this with a strategy with AI."
**Answers these questions:** What should my value content be about? How many videos on one topic is too many? Can I use AI to plan my content? What do Mike's value videos look like?
**Do / don't:** Do break your niche into dozens of specific tactical topics. Don't stay at the surface with one general video per subject.

### 38 — BTS & Leadership
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/3aa6365fb7e1422fbc47949e1bff95da · 3 min*
**In one line:** Behind-the-scenes content shows your leadership and the transformation you create; team wins, new training, agent results, and events, documented so people see the culture rather than hear about it.
**Mike's points:**
- BTS content does incredibly well. It demonstrates leadership and transformation.
- Team wins on mastermind calls: agents screenshot the 100 to 300 people on the call, post it to their stories, and tag him; he reposts 20 or 30 of them and it creates massive FOMO. Encourage your agents to share their biggest takeaway and tag you.
- Show new training you are creating, agents applying it and getting results, events you attend, and experiences you create.
- Use the company's events: the brokerage spends millions to put them on, so you just need to get your agents there; big decisions get made at big events. His group also runs its own local events, separate from corporate.
- He documents every event in three-to-five-minute recap videos so prospects see how differently his group operates, like one unit, like a family. Later lessons walk through those videos.
**Say it like Mike:** "You just got to get your agents there, encourage them to grow and attend because big decisions are made of big events." · "It's not just us saying, oh, it's so incredible to be here. You're seeing it."
**Answers these questions:** What counts as behind-the-scenes content? How do I get my agents to share our calls? Should I go to brokerage events? How do I show culture without bragging?
**Do / don't:** Do document calls and events and repost your agents' tags. Don't just tell people your culture is great.
**Related:** 07-instagram/89, 15-advanced-scaling/74, 14-retention-culture/70

### 39 — Personal Brand
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/ee6883d781de4c50ae1612fe3cf690e9 · 5 min*
**In one line:** Personal brand content is authenticity, not flash: lifestyle, habits, discipline, struggles and how you overcame them, passions, values, family, faith.
**Mike's points:**
- Your brand is built on authenticity, not clout and vanity. People need to know who you really are and why to trust you.
- Content buckets: lifestyle, daily habits, discipline, challenges and how you overcame them (relatable), passions, hobbies, values, family, faith.
- His pinned examples: surprising his mom with her dream SUV for Christmas; his parents' first first-class trip to Hawaii, where they honeymooned decades ago and could never afford to return.
- Cars appear, but blended with meaning: at 25 with $2,500 in the bank he wrote down a penthouse, a Lamborghini, and a specific purple-wrapped SUV, all by 30; he achieved it three months before 30 and celebrated with family.
- He picked up a 14-year-old with terminal brain cancer at school in a Lamborghini and took him and his mom for ice cream; he does this often. He uses the cars to give others experiences he never had growing up.
- Document the journey: a difficult year, a retreat with Tony Robbins, time with Ed Mylett who spoke at his first event. Talk about struggles and how you overcame them so people see you are not a robot.
- People assume things from the purple cars; the content shows the heart behind them.
**Say it like Mike:** "Who I am as a man defines me." · "Not just showing all the flash and all the clout and all the vanity and all that nonsense." · "I'm very transparent and very open and make sure that I'm relatable at all times."
**Answers these questions:** What personal content should I post? Is it okay to show my car or house? How personal is too personal? How do I make struggles into content?
**Do / don't:** Do share meaning, family, and setbacks alongside wins. Don't post flash with no story behind it.
**Related:** 04-value-proposition/29, 04-value-proposition/34, 07-instagram/88

### 40 — Storytelling
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/de724348e58141f8bc6639d1e917f8ca · 6 min*
**In one line:** Share your agents' breakthroughs across every age, race, gender, market, and personality by design so everyone finds someone to relate to; no success story is too small.
**Mike's points:**
- Facts tell, stories sell. He calls this his secret-sauce ninja strategy and credits it with over $5 million in revenue share in five years (his number).
- Storytelling content is proof you do what you say: your agents who applied your value proposition and transformed.
- By design his success stories cover every age, race, gender, ethnicity, language, market size, personality, introverts and extroverts, new agents, team leaders.
- Why: relatability. A thirty-something white guy in purple Lamborghinis won't relate to everyone, and that's fine; a brand should appeal to the right people, but a revenue share group should eventually appeal to everyone. If you don't relate to him, you'll relate to one of his partners, and they're all his partners.
- Contrast with egotistical creators who only feature big names for a feather in the cap. Nobody cares.
- His example: a young agent struggling at her previous brokerage joined, closed one deal in her first 30 days, and has broken six figures and tripled production every year since.
- It doesn't have to be $87 million in year one. 71% of the industry closed zero deals last year (his figure), so "one deal in 30 days" is the relatable story.
- There is no success story too big or too small to share.
**Say it like Mike:** "Facts tell, stories sell." · "If you don't relate to me, you might relate to a middle-aged white guy that has a family." · "There is no success story that's too big or too small from your group to share and inspire others."
**Answers these questions:** What is Mike's "secret sauce"? Who should I feature in success stories? My agents' wins are small; are they worth sharing? Why not interview famous coaches?
**Do / don't:** Do feature your own agents across every demographic. Don't chase big-name guests for clout.
