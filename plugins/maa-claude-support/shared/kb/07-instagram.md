# Instagram (Week 3)

Instagram is the digital first impression and recruiting stage: YouTube books the calls, but prospects check your Instagram to see who you really are before connecting. Mike covers the mindset (post like a leader, not a salesperson; no "brokerage-ing" over everyone), profile optimization (bio formula, one link to your funnel, highlights as a mini website), Reels (reach, five pillars, three to five a week), Stories (depth, daily, culture and personal, polls), and a simple weekly posting routine that your agents can duplicate.

Lessons: 86 Instagram · 87 Profile Optimization · 88 Reels · 89 Stories · 90 Posting Routine

### 86 — Instagram
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/11888e7156fe430795529901ae623f97 · 5 min*
**In one line:** Instagram is your recruiting stage and first credibility test; every post should echo leadership, culture, and value, not desperation.
**Mike's points:**
- YouTube is king for booked calls, but it has no two-way dialogue beyond comments. Instagram is where agents check you out and where you can chat.
- On YouTube you give straight value; on Instagram you show your personal life so people connect.
- Attraction comes from intention. Don't post for the sake of posting; every post fits a bucket and answers "would a prospect see me as a leader worth joining from this post?"
- Content should echo leadership, not desperation. He describes people who are "eXp-ing over everybody," where all they care about is recruiting; it's a massive turnoff, "a massive ick."
- Mindset: post like a leader, not a salesperson; share value that educates and inspires; recognize agents publicly to show culture; highlight mentorship; balance professionalism with authenticity (family, passions, hobbies).
- Consistency builds authority; inconsistent leaders are not attractive.
- What your content should echo: I'm a leader with a following; agents succeed in my world; there is culture and recognition here; this is a family, a movement, a tribe; ambitious agents belong here.
- Outcome: attracts curious agents without chasing, builds local then global credibility, creates FOMO, positions you as a trusted guide.
**Say it like Mike:** "Do not view Instagram as just posting. View it as your recruiting stage." · "Your content should echo leadership, not desperation." · "Inconsistent leaders are not attractive."
**Answers these questions:** How does Instagram fit with YouTube? What should I avoid posting? How do I know if a post is on-brand? Why do some sponsors' feeds feel like an ick?
**Do / don't:** Do ask what outcome each post achieves. Don't make your feed a recruiting pitch.

### 87 — Profile Optimization
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/64e643d0f06f4a77a9b167e537ee7755 · 5 min*
**In one line:** Treat your profile as a recruiting landing page: a clear bio (who you help, credibility, call to action), one link to your funnel, and highlights that work as a mini website.
**Mike's points:**
- Most agents check your profile before reaching out; a poorly optimized one loses opportunities. A ChatGPT prompt in the assets helps with all of this.
- Bio formula: who you are and how you help (his first line: helping agents scale using social media and AI), credibility (his: 100K-plus YouTube subscribers, number one attractor at his brokerage), and a call to action (free resources) tied to your niche. Short, clear, outcome driven.
- Link strategy: one link to your recruiting funnel. Some say never use a link-in-bio tool; he recommends one early because it gives multiple options to move people down the funnel.
- Include free resources tied to your value proposition (guides, prompts, checklists, a free course, a content calendar, a webinar), an option to book a call or apply to partner, and make the link action oriented ("get my free..."), not just a website.
- Think outcome-based, back to features versus benefits.
- Highlights: About You (story, mission, vision), Agent Wins (recognition, success stories), Culture (events, masterminds, behind the scenes), Free Value (tips, guide snippets), Partnership (how agents work with you). They should feel like a mini recruiting website.
- Outcome: profile views become conversations; the profile becomes your free funnel. Optimize YouTube and TikTok the same way.
- His team's brand launch service handles all of this if you prefer done-for-you.
**Say it like Mike:** "Your recruiting landing page, like that's what you need to view your Instagram as." · "Make the link action oriented." · "Your profile builds instant credibility. If it's optimized, agents immediately understand who you are and what you offer."
**Answers these questions:** What should my Instagram bio say? Should I use Linktree or one link? What highlights should I have? What goes in my link in bio?
**Do / don't:** Do put a lead magnet and a partner call in the link. Don't leave a vague bio with no call to action.

### 88 — Reels
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/a978fc43b7a04d36ad8cdcaf110ca029 · 6 min*
**In one line:** Reels are reach and brand awareness: rotate five pillars (value, culture and recognition, personal, leadership, and opinion) and work up to three to five a week.
**Mike's points:**
- Reels reach followers and non-followers; video builds trust faster than static posts; consistency compounds authority. Static posts still matter for recognition.
- Leadership reels: your story, vision, lessons, mistakes you made and what you learned, core values, mindset, discipline. Being on camera consistently makes people feel they know you before meeting you.
- Value reels: teach what you know, the tip of the iceberg, with your full value proposition below the water. Scripts, prospecting and attraction tips, social media and branding advice, tools and systems, leadership frameworks. Chunk your value proposition into bite-sized tastes.
- Culture and recognition reels: agents hitting milestones, events, dinners, retreats, masterminds, shout-outs, video testimonials, behind the scenes of calls. Creates FOMO.
- Personal reels: family, fitness, travel, hobbies, day in the life, values. His: travel, cars, watches, snowboarding, mountain biking, anything adrenaline. The post about buying his mom her dream car converted far higher than value content.
- Other reels that attract: opinion pieces and industry rants (green-screen takes), myth busting, future-focused AI and tech trends, Q&A, collaborations.
- Posting strategy: rotate the five pillars, aim for three to five reels weekly, static images are fine in between, work up to at least five.
- Prompts in the program help with bio optimization and video ideation; you still guide them.
**Say it like Mike:** "Reels are pushed to both followers and non-followers." · "Video builds trust way faster than static posts." · "People join people, not just leaders."
**Answers these questions:** How many reels a week? What should my reels be about? How much of my training should I give away in reels? Do personal reels actually help attraction?
**Do / don't:** Do mix all five pillars every week. Don't post only value with no personal or culture content.

### 89 — Stories
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/f43b345f7ab845faa6b130a0eb10688f · 10 min*
**In one line:** Reels are width, Stories are depth: post one to five every day, unpolished, mixing value, culture, recognition, and personal life, and watch who's watching.
**Mike's points:**
- Stories build connection and trust; they feel more authentic than reels because they disappear and are shot on the go. Prospects watch daily even when they don't engage. Everyone he talks to has been watching.
- Personal brand in stories: routines, workouts, reading, travel for coaching, screenshots of live mastermind calls (proof you do what you say), progress and wins, in-the-moment thoughts and book takeaways.
- Value in stories: quick tips and scripts that don't need a reel, screenshots of tools and templates, teasers of new things for your group, polls and quizzes (goals, what's holding them back, favorite tools), reposting your YouTube thumbnails with a link so your Instagram audience sees your channel.
- Culture and recognition (his favorite and most frequent): milestones like a first hundred agents (they give a trophy), events, dinners, retreats, Zoom calls, testimonials, shout-outs for capping or icon status. Always tag agents so they reshare to a new audience. Culture content creates FOMO.
- Personal side: family, hobbies, travel. His parents' golden retriever Maggie appears so often that followers message him when she's missing for a week.
- The patio beer test: would this prospect want to grab a drink with you? If no, they probably won't join you.
- Other high-impact stories: ask me anything (questions become content), sneak peeks of upcoming events, agents sharing the 200-plus-agent mastermind call screenshots.
- Strategy: don't go a day without stories; one to five daily; don't worry about production quality; include at least one interactive element (a poll between two logo options, for example).
**Say it like Mike:** "Reels is going to be the awareness, stories is going to be the connection." · "Would this be a person that you would want to go for a patio beer or a cocktail with?" · "Do not go a day without posting stories."
**Answers these questions:** How often should I post stories? What goes in stories versus reels? Does anyone actually watch my stories? How do I use polls? Should I tag my agents?
**Do / don't:** Do post daily and tag people for reshares. Don't polish stories or skip days.

### 90 — Posting Routine
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/5eed0e1e3b4e4f16a2be6698caf8b9d4 · 7 min*
**In one line:** Consistency is everything, so run a simple, repeatable weekly routine: plan, batch record, light edit, post to every short-form platform, stories daily.
**Mike's points:**
- Consistency is what most agents struggle with and why he has won; he has never missed a week since joining his brokerage. Without structure you drift.
- Sample week: Monday brainstorm three to five reel topics (value, recognition, culture, personal); Tuesday record them (one to two hours max); Wednesday edit or outsource (CapCut, Captions, Submagic have AI now); then post or schedule. Keep captions simple with a call to action.
- Keep editing simple. The sensory-overload Hormozi-style editing of 2021 to 2023 doesn't work anymore; people want raw, genuine, authentic.
- Repost each Instagram short to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Facebook Reels: one video covers all short-form platforms.
- His own routine is bi-weekly on Saturdays: plan long-form one Saturday, record it the next; plan short-form the next, record it the next, covering a whole month.
- Daily stories: be present every day with one to five. Coffee runs, insights, a tip, a shout-out, behind the scenes of prospecting or events, end-of-day reflection.
- Sample calendar: Monday value reel, Tuesday recognition, Wednesday leadership insight, Thursday culture or event recap, Friday personal or opinion rant.
- Use the ideation prompts early, but put your own spin on it; about 50% of his ideas now come from AI and 50% always from his own experience and the questions he gets coaching agents.
- Teach the routine to your agents: their combined posting is like 100 times his views and drives traffic back to the group, and everyone wins through the tier structure.
**Say it like Mike:** "Consistency is everything." · "Simple, sustainable, scalable, that's the name of the game." · "Reels build authority, stories build intimacy."
**Answers these questions:** What does a weekly posting routine look like? How long should recording take? Do I need fancy editing? Can I reuse reels on TikTok and Shorts? How does Mike plan his month?
**Do / don't:** Do batch, keep edits raw, and cross-post everywhere. Don't rely on AI for every idea forever.
