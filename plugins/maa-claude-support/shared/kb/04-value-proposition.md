# Crafting Your Value Proposition (Week 2)

This module turns the member from "someone at a brokerage" into a sponsor agents choose. Mike starts with the niche (you are most qualified to help the person you used to be), opens up his own value proposition as a target to work toward, explains why brand is everything and why leaders invest in themselves first, sets his free-versus-paid rule, teaches features versus benefits (agents buy outcomes), lays out the anatomy of an irresistible offer, and ends with crafting the personal story that becomes your "why join me" statement.

Lessons: 27 Identifying Your Niche · 28 My Value Proposition · 29 Your Brand is Everything · 30 Investing In Yourself · 31 Free vs. Paid · 32 Features vs. Benefits · 33 Building Your Irresistible Offer · 34 Crafting Your Personal Story

### 27 — Identifying Your Niche
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/9ee5fcbb4e4d4fa9b9c0b959fd82ca08 · 10 min*
**In one line:** Your niche comes from what you have actually done: your experience, your skills, your hero's journey, and the agent persona it fits.
**Mike's points:**
- Winning = deep understanding of the model plus your unique value proposition on top of it. That starts with a niche.
- You are most qualified to help the person you used to be. The failure pattern: buy someone's course, regurgitate it, never done it yourself, can't support anyone.
- Three lists (worksheets): what you have real experience in (social media, YouTube, AI, ads, luxury, open houses, cold calling, door knocking, relocation, investing), what skills go with it (prospecting, conversion, nurture, CRM, content, video, ads), and what challenges you overcame (struggling to top producer, disorganized to clear, burnt out to systems).
- Anything works if you do the work: he has agents winning with open houses, cold calling, and investing; three agents in his group attract purely by teaching CRMs.
- His example: door knocking, 1,000 days of Instagram, Facebook ads that sold a $1.1M house in three days, then YouTube for leverage; niche became social media with heavy YouTube, personal branding, and now AI. When he joined he had three terrible separate programs (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube); it evolved every year.
- Match the niche to a persona: new agents (foundations, mindset, lead gen, the biggest net), struggling experienced (course correction, systems), top producers (brand, leverage, expansion), influencers (monetization), teams and brokerages (retention, growth, culture).
- You attract who you are; a featured team attracts more teams, a creator attracts creators.
- Solve one or more of the five core pain points: financial uncertainty, lack of support, technology gaps, limited growth, work-life balance.
**Say it like Mike:** "You're most qualified to help the person that you used to be." · "Anything works if you do the work." · "A clear niche will lead to a clear value proposition."
**Answers these questions:** What should my niche be? Do I have to teach social media like Mike? What if my strength is cold calling or open houses? Which type of agent should my niche serve? What are the five pain points?
**Do / don't:** Do build from your real experience. Don't pick a "sexy" topic you have never executed.

### 28 — My Value Proposition
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/812d256bb8b147ac8ff188133f163196 · 6 min*
**In one line:** Mike's full stack, free to everyone in his group, is the model: build resources your agents can offer to their own prospects so they only have to focus on conversations.
**Mike's points:**
- Social Agent Academy Pro: his top social training on every platform (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Google and YouTube ads, branding, lead conversion, systems), step-by-step screen recordings, updated every year.
- AI Agent Accelerator: his AI training, positioned as three times more leads, content in 80% less time, 10-plus hours a week saved (his framing).
- This agent attraction program itself, so his agents can attract with it too.
- Wolfpack community (private, on Skool): streamlined onboarding (because his brokerage's onboarding is chaotic), mindset training, and traditional prospecting training built by agents in his group who excel at it, because he does not teach what he does not do at the highest level.
- Five to six live mastermind calls a week and a full-time support team.
- The point: every agent in his group gets to use his value proposition to attract, so they don't spend five years and a million dollars building their own. Build resources your agents can give away.
- Creates co-sponsorship opportunities for agents at his brokerage who are not in his downline. Once built, it's built; it doesn't take more of his time.
- Designed intentionally to solve all five pain points: financial (Academy), support (team and calls), tech (AI Accelerator), growth (proven plans), work-life balance (this program).
- He did not arrive with any of this; he built it on the journey.
**Say it like Mike:** "Anything I create, everybody in my group gets free access to it." · "Once it's built, it's built." · "I didn't start with this. I built it on the journey."
**Answers these questions:** What exactly is in Mike's value proposition? What do my agents get to offer their prospects? What is the Wolfpack? Do I need to build all of this before I start?
**Do / don't:** Do design your offer so your agents can attract with it. Don't try to clone the whole stack on day one.

### 29 — Your Brand is Everything
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/236213abe400482bb886b655baabaa2e · 5 min*
**In one line:** You are your brand; a strong personal brand gets you more agents, cheaper, faster, with less resistance, who stay longer and refer more.
**Mike's points:**
- Six outcomes of a strong brand: more (agents come to you), cheaper (free organic content), move faster (they join quicker), less resistant (fewer objections because you've proven it), buy again (they stay, retention), prefer more (they refer and duplicate).
- Like a strong listing agent, agents with a strong brand don't get interviewed against a lineup; prospects just want them.
- Brand differentiates you from every other sponsor, attracts agents aligned with your values and style, and positions you as the go-to leader.
- Common mistakes: only promoting the brokerage, inconsistent messaging across platforms, no value proposition (doesn't answer "why you"), copying others, being transaction-focused instead of leadership and culture.
- What good looks like: consistent across digital, physical, and social; clear leadership positioning (story, values, vision); a content strategy that educates, inspires, and attracts; professional identity (logo, colors, fonts are vehicles, not the brand); built on authenticity.
- Everything he puts out is branded so you always know it's him. His example of a built brand: Suman, "the real estate ninja," with separate production and attraction brands.
- His team offers done-for-you brand building (link in the Loom description).
**Say it like Mike:** "You are your brand." · "People join people, not companies." · "These are vehicles to communicate your brand."
**Answers these questions:** Why does personal brand matter so much for attraction? What are the common branding mistakes? Is my logo my brand? Can Mike's team build my brand?
**Do / don't:** Do keep one consistent look and message everywhere. Don't promote the brokerage instead of yourself.

### 30 — Investing In Yourself
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/2afd975d3c23415f9b9eb13fcf47dff7 · 5 min*
**In one line:** Leaders go first: your ceiling becomes your agents' ceiling, and what you learn becomes their shortcut.
**Mike's points:**
- It baffles him when agents won't make a reasonable investment in their brand; that is why they struggle to become leaders.
- Leadership starts with personal growth. He has invested upwards of a million dollars in coaching and development (his figure), and there has never been a time in five and a half years he was not in a program or working with a coach.
- Agents model what you do, not what you say. If you tell them to get better and you aren't, what does that say?
- You can only execute on what you know; expand your knowledge and your agents get better results too.
- Most agents can't afford high-level coaching; you bridge the gap. His example: he spent $311,000 on personal development in one year (his number) and every week compiles the greatest lessons and shares them free with his group.
- This creates loyalty: they stay because you gave them growth they couldn't otherwise access. Learn once, apply one to many.
- The best leaders are the best students; humility matters. If money is tight, start with books; share lessons on mastermind calls and in content.
- Growth is an attraction tool; agents are drawn to leaders who stay ahead of the curve.
**Say it like Mike:** "Leaders go first." · "Your ceiling becomes your agent's ceiling." · "Your growth becomes their shortcut to success."
**Answers these questions:** Should I spend money on coaching or my brand? How does my own learning help my agents? What if I can't afford expensive programs? Why does Mike share his coaching lessons?
**Do / don't:** Do document and share what you learn with your group. Don't ask agents to grow while you stop.

### 31 — Free vs. Paid
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/6145714825444bf399a828053120ace6 · 7 min*
**In one line:** If it doesn't require your time, give it to your agents free; if it requires human capital, discount it; be transparent either way.
**Mike's points:**
- His rule: anything pre-recorded or evergreen (Social Agent Academy, AI Agent Accelerator, this program) is free for his agents and for agents he co-sponsors. Anything requiring his time or a team (video editors, designers, funnel builders, the Brand Launch program) gets a discount for his group.
- The programs sell for $997 each to outsiders; his group gets them free. Buying outside gets the course but not access to him; one-on-one time is reserved for his group.
- The math he shares: roughly 3,000 agents times three programs at $997 is about $12 million of real value given away (his numbers), and that is actual cost people pay, not inflated "worth $30,000" marketing.
- That is how the offer becomes a no-brainer, and what to work toward at any brokerage.
- Where other sponsors get a bad reputation: advertising "we do funnels" then charging $5,000 to $10,000 without saying so, or charging $250 a month for mastermind calls, or $500 a month to $5,000 for AI training.
- Be upfront: if there is a cost, say so; if he created it, it's free on day one.
- Over-deliver on value and you grow faster and bigger than everyone else.
**Say it like Mike:** "If it doesn't require my time, I make it free for any of my agents." · "If you have a service, then discount." · "If you over deliver on value, you're going to grow and you're going to grow way faster and way bigger than everybody else."
**Answers these questions:** Should I charge my agents for my training? What should be free and what should be paid? What if I have a done-for-you service? How does Mike handle agents who want to buy his course without joining?
**Do / don't:** Do give evergreen content away free to your group. Don't hide costs until after someone joins.

### 32 — Features vs. Benefits
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/1607e50c57cf49cba2340cc9637c0349 · 7 min*
**In one line:** Agents don't buy features, they buy outcomes; rewrite every message as pain point, feature, benefit, outcome, at a fifth-grade reading level.
**Mike's points:**
- He paid hundreds of thousands to learn this and calls it one of the greatest lessons he can share; most will nod and never apply it.
- Features are what you provide (CRM, training, support). Outcomes are what they want (more deals, less stress, financial freedom). Wrong messaging, not lack of knowledge, is why funnels and content don't convert.
- Weak: "social media training." Strong: proven strategies that helped hundreds of agents like you, introverts and extroverts, any market, close six figures in four months with zero video experience.
- Weak: "free training calls." Strong: every week learn strategies from top producers doing $50M-plus that you can implement today.
- Formula: identify the pain point (inconsistent income), show the feature (the Academy), translate to the benefit (know exactly how to attract clients free every week), paint the outcome (consistent closings with ideal clients).
- Speak their language, not jargon. His team runs everything through ChatGPT with "rewrite at fifth grade reading level." He says that one habit could pay for the whole course.
- Always connect back to growth, freedom, and security. Use stories and proof to make outcomes believable; he deliberately showcases camera-shy, introverted agents because most agents think social media is for extroverts.
- Study outcome-based funnels like Tony Robbins'; strugglers only talk features.
**Say it like Mike:** "Agents don't buy features. They buy outcomes." · "Rewrite this in fifth grade reading level." · "Speak their language, not industry jargon."
**Answers these questions:** Why isn't my funnel or content converting? How do I describe my training? What reading level should my messaging be? How do I make my outcomes believable?
**Do / don't:** Do lead with the outcome and prove it with stories. Don't list the trinkets inside your program.

### 33 — Building Your Irresistible Offer
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/eaac8f51e0e84c5b8a69959bbdbc9e1d · 8 min*
**In one line:** Package your value so it answers "why you, and why now"; start with something small today and let it grow every year.
**Mike's points:**
- Value proposition is who you are and what makes you different; the irresistible offer is the packaging agents can't ignore. It must be clear, outcome driven, impossible to say no to.
- Anatomy: core promise (the result), unique mechanism (why your system is different: extra money, proprietary tech, strategy), proof (success stories, case studies), support (coaching, systems, culture), scarcity (why now, not someday).
- No proof yet? Help agents for free, as he did in the beginning, to earn case studies.
- Position the offer as the shortcut agents crave; there are no shortcuts, but a proven path is shorter than a distracted one.
- His example line: join me and learn the exact strategies agents like you use to close $100K-plus from social media, plus daily mastermind calls, a full support team, like-minded accountability, things people pay thousands for, and the only way to get him personally.
- Common mistakes: too generic ("free tools, free training," so does everyone), too feature focused, no urgency, no proof.
- His journey: joined April 20, 2020 with three basic, terrible tutorials and one weekly call; 2021 a second call; 2022 Academy 2.0 and a leaders' call; 2023 3.0; 2024 shared his coaching lessons; 2025 Academy Pro, the AI Accelerator, and this program.
- You don't need a pre-recorded program. Some of his agents attract with a weekly cold-calling script role-play Zoom.
**Say it like Mike:** "Why should I join you over everyone else and why should I do it now?" · "Offers must be clear, outcome driven, and impossible to say no to." · "Just get started somewhere, anywhere today. Now, do not wait."
**Answers these questions:** What goes into an irresistible offer? I have no case studies; what do I do? Does my offer have to be a course? How did Mike's offer start? Why aren't agents acting now?
**Do / don't:** Do launch a simple version now and improve it yearly. Don't wait until it's a polished "monster."

### 34 — Crafting Your Personal Story
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/b7d717e196ce4d51b036292929ec5ee5 · 5 min*
**In one line:** Facts tell, stories sell; your past, transformation, mission, value, and vision become the "why join me" statement that attracts and retains.
**Mike's points:**
- Story is both a growth play (people connect and relate) and a retention play (it's easier to leave a service than a friend).
- Agents connect with people, not brokerages. His example of story done right: Gary Vee, whose lemonade stand to Wine Library arc makes you feel he's speaking directly to you.
- Five parts: your past (where you started, the struggle), your transformation (what you learned and overcame), your mission (why you care about agents winning, not a selfish prophecy), your value (what they get by joining), your vision (where you're taking the organization and how they benefit, not just "I want 10,000 agents").
- His example: new agent, new city, no money, no sphere; took massive action with social media, became a top producer; trained over 7,000 agents; value is the exact blueprint; vision is to help 1,000 agents make a million dollars.
- In the beginning he helped 20 to 30 agents for free to get case studies; those stories built everything after.
- Your differentiator: what makes you different from every other leader at your brokerage, what pain points you solve, what outcomes they can expect, how you are uniquely equipped.
- Template: "I know what it's like to [pain]. That's why I built [thing]. Now I help agents [outcome]. When you join me you don't just get the power of this brokerage, you also get [unique value]."
- This is why "come join me, let's talk about [brokerage]" fails; it has no story.
**Say it like Mike:** "Facts tell, stories sell." · "Nobody could be a better version of you than you." · "Agents don't connect with brokerages at all. They connect with people."
**Answers these questions:** How do I write my why-join-me statement? What should my story include? Why does my story matter for retention? What if my story feels ordinary?
**Do / don't:** Do fill in all five parts and weave it into every message. Don't make the vision about your agent count.
