# Advanced Strategies for Scaling (Week 6)

Attraction channels beyond your own content: an email list you own, live and virtual events where you are the one at the front of the room, evergreen webinars for when you are big enough, and strategic partnerships with the lenders and vendors who hear agents' frustrations first. Every event ends the same way: the "iceberg" close, where the free training is the tip and partnering with you is everything below the water.

**Lessons**
- 73 — Building an email list
- 74 — Live Events
- 75 — Virtual Events

### 73 — Building an email list
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/ae633a6d28244a0eb787682257d29f42 · 7 min*
**In one line:** Your email list is the only audience you own; build it by trading real assets for a name and email, then nurture with value every week.
**Mike's points:**
- Platforms change, accounts get restricted or removed. He has seen people with big audiences at cloud-based brokerages lose a channel or an Instagram. Email is the only audience you own, and it has the highest return of any marketing platform. Mike's list is the size of his YouTube channel; if YouTube went away he would rebuild from it.
- It keeps you top of mind when they're not ready. People email him asking where the weekly email was.
- Build it: YouTube descriptions with lead magnets, free mini courses, custom GPTs, checklists, scripts, and prompts ("click the link in the description"); ManyChat automations on Instagram ("comment PROMPT"); webinars and trainings that require an email to attend; landing pages with simple opt-ins from stories. Use a proper funnel and email sequence, not an unprofessional ticketing-style sign-up, or it won't convert.
- Give the information away. He gives free YouTube and AI courses that others charge thousands for, because information is no longer novel to anyone who can prompt; people pay for proximity, intimacy, access, support, and accountability.
- Nurture: a weekly newsletter (his two to three videos a week, often plus one pure-value email); success stories of agents in your organization (from his interviews, anyone who comes in joins under the agent interviewed); leadership insights and personal lessons; and a warm call to action: "if you'd like to chat about partnering with me and getting my training for free, book a call."
- Payoff: compounding. Every piece of content can grow the list, every subscriber is a potential future partner because most agents become unhappy at some point, and the list grows in value year after year.
**Say it like Mike:** "My email list is the size of my YouTube channel. My YouTube channel goes away. I rebuild it." · "People pay for proximity, intimacy, access, support, accountability" · "every new subscriber is a potential future partner"
**Answers these questions:**
- Why do I need an email list if I have YouTube and Instagram?
- What do I give away to get emails?
- What do I send to the list?
- How do I turn the list into booked calls?
**Do / don't:** Do give the asset away free in exchange for an email. Don't collect sign-ups without a funnel and a sequence behind them.

### 74 — Live Events
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/c4d95bd551e1476c9af029aeb343433e · 7 min*
**In one line:** Be the one at the front of the room: give real value on a hot topic, then close with the iceberg; the event is the tip, partnering is everything below.
**Mike's points:**
- In-person builds influence faster. The one at the front of the room controls the audience and everyone comes to talk to you. Events position you as leader and expert; free value builds reciprocity; the group creates social proof. Get all your agents to come and bring a guest.
- Formula: pick a hot topic agents care about (his: social media, YouTube, lead channels, AI); market it locally with personal invites, social posts, your email list, and get every agent and speaker to share it on their stories; deliver free, high-value training they can use immediately; keep it interactive with Q&A and networking; end with a light transition to your offer.
- Where people go wrong: the tip-of-the-iceberg tease with nothing tangible, then "if you want to know how, come join me at my brokerage." Give real value. If you over-deliver, they'll want to join because there are layers below.
- Invite script: "We're hosting a free YouTube training for local agents this week, want me to save you a seat?"
- The close without selling: what I shared today is just the tip of the iceberg; if you want the exact strategies, mentorship, and systems behind this, I'm happy to share how you can get access entirely for free and get my help to execute. Above the water: the free strategies. Below: systems, mentorship, community, playbooks, accountability.
- Benefits: instant credibility; agents get results from the free value and wonder what joining gives; scalable and it duplicates when your agents host their own.
- Example: Mike and Suman Kim packed about 200 agents at the Austin board, an hour of networking after, then a catered private venue for their own agents with a branded banner everyone shared.
**Say it like Mike:** "The one at the front of the room controls the audience" · "nothing builds trust faster than face-to-face" · "If you over-deliver on value, they'll want to join you because there's layers below the iceberg."
**Answers these questions:**
- How do I run a live event to attract agents?
- What topic should I pick?
- How do I pitch at the end without being salesy?
- How do I fill the room?
**Do / don't:** Do give real how-to value. Don't end with a brokerage pitch.

### 75 — Virtual Events
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/6728fb69203c48a2bce7daa5d3f2a24c · 5 min*
**In one line:** The live-event formula on Zoom, free to run, across markets; follow-up matters more because they are meeting you for the first time.
**Mike's points:**
- Why: scale influence from home; reach dozens or hundreds of agents across state, province, and country lines at once (cloud-based brokerages make that possible); position as a thought leader; easy to promote; low cost, high leverage: a Zoom room and a clear topic. Mike did everything with virtual events at the beginning.
- Free to start by having your agents share it; Meta ads only later when you want bigger reach. Don't overcomplicate or overthink it, just execute.
- Framework: pick a pain point most agents care about (lead gen, social, YouTube, scaling, AI); a simple registration page or funnel plus a calendar invite; deliver free, actionable, tangible strategies, not fluff; keep it interactive (he drives chat engagement: "drop it in the chat if you're feeling this"); invite the ones who want the full playbook to partner with you.
- Invite script: "I'm running a free virtual training next week on [topic], want me to send you the link?" Post multiple times, especially stories; have your other speakers (top agents in your market or your upline) share it; get your agents to invite their prospects.
- Iceberg close, same as live: everything today is the tip; the systems, mentorship, and playbooks come free by partnering; book a call.
- Benefits: reach beyond your local market (his ads run across North America); easy for agents to duplicate because their followers are everywhere too; positions you as the educator, not the recruiter; a pipeline of curious agents.
- Follow-up: more important than after a live event. These people aren't local and are hearing about you for the first time, so they need more nurturing. Check in, ask what they thought, make sure they know they can book a call, and get your agents to follow up with their guests.
**Say it like Mike:** "low cost, high leverage, just your zoom room and a clear topic" · "I just want you to get started, not overcomplicate this, not overthink it and just execute." · "they're going to require more nurturing, more love"
**Answers these questions:**
- How do I run a virtual event to attract agents?
- Do I need to run ads?
- How is the follow-up different from a live event?
- What do I say at the end?
**Do / don't:** Do follow up the next week with everyone who attended. Don't wait until you can afford ads.

### 76 — Evergreen Webinars
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/8021c1be12da414ab5abc5f8c0568521 · 5 min*
**In one line:** A pre-recorded webinar running on ads around the clock; powerful, but skip it until you are past 250 agents with the skill and the team to run it.
**Mike's points:**
- The gate: under 250 agents, skip this video. You pay for ads every day, and if you aren't great at webinars, communication, and pitching, with a team to build the systems, it will be overwhelming.
- What it is: a live training, recorded and automated to run on demand. Same message delivered the same way every time; your calendar fills while you focus on leadership.
- Four steps: record a training on a hot topic aligned with your value proposition; refine it so it delivers value while positioning your offer (it has to be dialed in because you pay whether people click or not); automate delivery with a webinar platform or funnel; drive traffic through Instagram ManyChat ("comment LEADS"), your email list, and YouTube descriptions.
- His tools: Zoom or Loom to record, EasyWebinar to host, GoHighLevel for integrations and emails. He pays someone to build it because it isn't the best use of his time.
- Invite: "I put together a free training on [topic]." Focus on value, not recruiting; the webinar does the positioning.
- Iceberg close: what you learned today is the tip; the full system, mentorship, and community is what you get when you partner.
- Why he doesn't recommend it for most people: holding a strong message for 60-plus minutes and pitching in an inviting way is a different skill from a 10-minute YouTube video, and webinars convert far better once you have a credible brand people recognize. Do the hard foundation work first.
**Say it like Mike:** "I don't recommend doing this in the beginning." · "Once you get over 250 agents, you could start to explore something like this" · "it's the ultimate leverage tool, but it requires a really radically different skillset"
**Answers these questions:**
- Should I set up an evergreen webinar?
- When am I ready for one?
- What tools does Mike use for it?
- How is it different from a virtual event?
**Do / don't:** Do build the brand and the live-event skill first. Don't start here under 250 agents.

### 77 — Strategic Partnerships
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/bd7bd05cd7db414eb93585e78b750388 · 3 min*
**In one line:** Lenders, title reps, inspectors, and coaches hear agents' frustrations first; position yourself as the person who helps struggling agents close more deals, and they will send them to you.
**Mike's points:**
- Nobody does this, and Mike says his group has attracted a ton of agents this way. Borrow trust to expand your reach; one introduction from a trusted partner goes a long way.
- Who: lenders and mortgage brokers (daily conversations with agents, so they know who's struggling), title and escrow officers, home inspectors, appraisers, real estate coaches, anyone who interacts with many agents across brokerages.
- How: make it a win-win. Educate them on what you do and who you help; position yourself as helping agents succeed, not recruiting. The ask: "I've been helping a ton of agents with this; if you run into anyone struggling with production, support, training, or income, send them my way, I'd love to help, and it's free." When those agents close more deals, the partner wins too because they work those deals.
- Give them resources to pass along: guides, invites to your live and virtual events (his partners send event invites to their own databases).
- Thank and recognize them for every introduction, make them feel part of your mission, and always give credit.
- Benefits: scale beyond your own reach; warm introductions instead of cold outreach; faster trust through third-party credibility; a consistent pipeline of agent conversations without prospecting.
- Action: make a list of industry partners you trust and start the conversations.
**Say it like Mike:** "These partners often hear about agents' frustrations before anybody else" · "position as helping agents succeed, not just recruiting" · "Always give credit where it's due."
**Answers these questions:**
- Who should I partner with to find agents?
- How do I ask a lender to refer agents to me?
- What do I give partners to share?
- Why would a partner send me agents?
**Do / don't:** Do frame it as helping struggling agents. Don't ask partners to "send recruits."
