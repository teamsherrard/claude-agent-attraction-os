# Objection Handling (Week 5)

Mike's playbook for the objections you will hear on attraction calls. Lesson 46 gives the seven buckets every objection falls into and the hidden fear behind each; lessons 48 to 62 handle the specific objections one at a time, always in the same shape: listen, validate, reframe, then ask the questions that get the agent thinking differently. Objections are not rejection, they are a request for more clarity. The two cardinal rules apply throughout: don't talk shit about other brokerages, don't talk shit about other agents.

### 46 — 7 Objection Archetypes
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/3695a36d1a7f4e7a9cd1ee7f6c1b41aa · 5 min*
**In one line:** Every objection fits one of seven buckets; find the bucket, find the hidden fear, address the fear, and the objection disappears.
**Mike's points:**
- Objections are not rejection, they are a request for more clarity. You will get them, guaranteed. Answer with confidence and clarity; if you get shaky, they will not have confidence to move forward.
- Treat it like a game or an obstacle course. Mike found the harder calls more fun because the win was bigger.
- The seven buckets and the hidden fear: (1) I'm happy where I am: change feels risky. (2) I don't have time: disruption of business, they are already overwhelmed. (3) Compensation structure: short-term expenses outweigh long-term gain, common with agents on 100% commission looking at a split. (4) Support and leads: they won't succeed without their current safety net, usually agents on a team being fed leads. (5) Recruiting isn't for me: they see attraction as pressure or MLM, not leadership. (6) Brand recognition: losing credibility with clients. (7) Fear and uncertainty: lack of belief in themselves or the unknown.
- On recruiting: you already recruit. When an agent asks where to go, you tell them your brokerage 100% of the time. Now you can prove financially why it is better for them, with multiple income streams and your support.
- On brand: clients do not care what brokerage you are at; most could not tell you six months after closing. The agent is the brand.
- Most agents throw one to three objections before committing. Stay calm, listen, reframe. Don't fight, don't get defensive. "I get that, I used to feel that way too, but..."
**Say it like Mike:** "objections are not rejection, they're simply a request for more clarity" · "Once you know the archetype, you know, their hidden fear." · "objections aren't the end, they're the doorway"
**Answers these questions:**
- What are the seven objection archetypes?
- What is the real fear behind "I'm happy where I am"?
- How many objections should I expect on a call?
- How do I stay calm when they push back?
**Do / don't:** Do validate before you reframe. Don't get defensive or confrontational.

### 48 — Happy Where I Am
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/67b745b2efe94710a1ba5fe5990254dd · 3 min*
**In one line:** They are comfortable, not where they want to be; if they were getting everything they needed they would not be on this call.
**Mike's points:**
- Listen and validate: "you're feeling good about where you're at, that makes sense; a lot of agents feel comfortable until something forces them to look around."
- Reframe: if you were already getting everything you needed, would you even be open to this conversation? If I could show you a way to keep what you like and add [their pain point], would you want to see how that works?
- Questions Mike has used hundreds of times: if you aren't hitting your goals there, what makes you believe things are going to change? If nothing changes, nothing changes. Based on the vision you shared, is your brokerage even the right vehicle to get you there?
- Tie it to what they told you: they want to retire in five or ten years or want time freedom, but they only have active income. Time freedom comes from passive or residual income they do not currently have.
- Everyone is happy where they are, but what if you are missing a new level? Successful agents get comfortable staying uncomfortable. Then show examples of agents who did it.
- Mike calls this one easy to handle if you ask the questions.
**Say it like Mike:** "if nothing changes, nothing changes, right?" · "all successful agents get comfortable staying uncomfortable" · "is your brokerage even the right vehicle to get you there?"
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent says they're happy where they are. What do I say?
- How do I challenge "happy" without being pushy?
- What questions expose the gap between comfortable and goals?
**Do / don't:** Do point back at the vision they already shared. Don't argue that they are unhappy.

### 49 — Pyramid Scheme
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/ecb358a839204efe8f26a2002b75611b · 2 min*
**In one line:** Revenue share pays from the company dollar when agents close deals, not for recruiting; walk the math instead of making them feel silly.
**Mike's points:**
- Validate: "I hear you, a lot of people explain it improperly; honestly I used to think that too." Reframe: then somebody properly explained it and it changed everything.
- Revenue share is paid through the company dollar, not the agent. It is the company reinvesting in the ones who help it grow. You only get paid when people close deals, not when they recruit.
- At a traditional franchise brokerage, the company dollar stays with the broker or flows up to the franchise owners. At a cloud-based brokerage it comes back to the agents because of reduced overhead and no offices.
- Pyramid schemes are illegal, and the bad reputation comes from being paid to recruit (people buying potions, powders, products). Here, if Mike brings someone on and they close nothing, he gets nothing and has lost the time he spent. He has to help them succeed first.
- Show it with real numbers and examples so you can prove it is not.
**Say it like Mike:** "Revenue share is paid through the company dollar, not the agent." · "You only get paid when people close deals, not when they recruit." · "if I bring you on, but you don't close any deals, I don't get anything"
**Answers these questions:**
- How do I answer "this is a pyramid scheme"?
- How is revenue share different from an MLM?
- Where does the revenue share money come from?
**Do / don't:** Do walk through the math with real numbers. Don't make them feel silly for asking.

### 50 — I don't want to recruit
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/2f0ca08c6c164d5194377444cf10f579 · 2 min*
**In one line:** You don't have to; sell as usual, and if agents are attracted to what you are doing, you have the option to benefit.
**Mike's points:**
- Validate: recruiting doesn't excite you, that's normal, most agents don't want to recruit either. They have probably had other agents force it down their throat.
- The good news is you don't have to. Sell as usual; if other agents are attracted to what you are doing, you have the option to benefit. It is attraction, not chasing.
- Questions: at your current brokerage, when somebody asks which brokerage they should go to, what do you tell them? (Theirs.) Do you get compensated for that? (No.) Mike says his partners built residual income doing that same thing.
- Every agent already does this; they just don't call it recruiting. How do you build a team? Recruit. How do you build a brokerage? Recruit. Most agents eventually want one of those.
- Here you get compensated on a global scale without the risk, liability, headache, and adult daycare of running a team or brokerage.
**Say it like Mike:** "Recruiting doesn't excite you. That's normal. Most agents don't want to recruit either." · "It's attraction, not chasing." · "Every agent does this. They just don't call it that."
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent says they don't want to recruit. What do I say?
- Do agents have to recruit to benefit from revenue share?
- How do I show them they already recruit?
**Do / don't:** Do normalize it and show they already do it. Don't sell them on recruiting.

### 52 — Talking to Another Agent
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/c319b57d9c164ab2a9d315d89bdb4c17 · 5 min*
**In one line:** Agents are sponsor shopping, and rightly so; validate it, never knock the other sponsor, and ask whether they have shown proof.
**Mike's points:**
- People are not just brokerage shopping, they are sponsor shopping, like sellers interviewing multiple agents. Thank them for their honesty; say you love that they are talking to other agents.
- Reframe: who you partner with is everything. Agents don't join brokerages, they join people. Who do you believe can actually guide you and provide the most value? Would you be open to hearing how I specifically support my agents so you can decide which leader is the right fit?
- The two cardinal rules apply: don't talk shit about other brokerages, don't talk shit about other agents. Never talk negatively about other sponsors.
- Questions: what do you like about what they have to offer? Have they shown you proof of what they have to offer? Many sponsors say what the agent wants to hear but cannot prove it. Mike can pull up the training and the interviews of agents who applied it.
- Why it matters: at most cloud-based brokerages, switching sponsor later means leaving the brokerage for six to twelve months and coming back. Choosing right the first time matters.
- If the other agent really provides something incredible and your brokerage has co-sponsorship (eXp does, in Mike's example), offer to co-sponsor: the best of both worlds.
**Say it like Mike:** "Cardinal rule number one. Don't talk shit about other brokerages. Cardinal rule number two. Don't talk shit about other agents." · "Who you partner with is everything." · "Have they shown you what they have to offer? Because a lot of times they can't."
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent is talking to another sponsor. How do I compete?
- Can I say anything about the other sponsor?
- What is co-sponsorship and when do I offer it?
- Why does choosing a sponsor matter so much?
**Do / don't:** Do ask for proof and show yours. Don't say a word against the other sponsor.

### 53 — Agents Talking Shit
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/2a33b8ea18a44156ba194308f432449d · 4 min*
**In one line:** When another agent tears you down, do not fight it; redirect to results, leadership, and alignment, and ask what tearing others down says about them.
**Mike's points:**
- You will hear "this agent said this about you" far too often; Mike calls it jealousy and says it comes from people not confident in their own value. He has never spoken negatively about anyone on a call.
- His stance on calls: if you don't see the value in what I provide compared to others, don't join. He says of the few who joined other groups, almost all reached back out weeks later saying they didn't get what was promised.
- A common one aimed at him: "you don't sell homes anymore." His answer: results speak; he has trained thousands of agents.
- The response: I respect that you're doing your homework. Opinions vary, but look at the results, leadership, and alignment with your goals; that speaks louder than gossip. Would you be open to seeing how it worked for other agents so you can decide on facts, not hearsay?
- The two questions he wins with: if somebody's strategy is to tear others down, what does that say about them? And don't you believe that if somebody is confident in what they offer, they wouldn't have to tear others down?
**Say it like Mike:** "I don't have to talk negatively about anybody. And I never have, never will." · "That speaks louder than gossip, right?" · "if somebody is confident in what they offer, they wouldn't have to tear others down"
**Answers these questions:**
- Another sponsor is bad-mouthing me to a prospect. What do I say?
- Should I defend myself or respond in kind?
- How do I turn gossip back into a question about the other agent?
**Do / don't:** Do redirect to facts and proof. Don't retaliate or name the other person negatively.

### 54 — Brokerage Gives Leads
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/6eb0fcabcf634b269fe45071b4eada38 · 3 min*
**In one line:** Leads at a 50% split mean half the deals you think you closed; show them how to own their lead flow instead of renting it.
**Mike's points:**
- Usually a team gives the leads, not the brokerage. Validate: that's a big benefit; many agents value that at first (plant the seed that "at first" has a second act).
- Break it down: what percent of your deals actually come from those leads, and at what split? Closing 10 deals on a 50% split is the same money as five deals. What if I showed you how to close 10 or more with leads you own and keep all the commission?
- Questions: do you want to be dependent on someone else to operate your business forever? Right now you are an employee, not a business owner. Why do you believe all successful agents learn to generate their own leads?
- Future-pace: what happens if the team leader gets sick, injured, or passes away? You don't have the skill to generate leads; your business disappears. Does that feel comfortable?
- They already know how to close and follow up; the one missing piece is lead generation, and that is what you are going to show them.
**Say it like Mike:** "Do you want to be dependent on someone else to operate your business forever?" · "right now, the truth is, you're just an employee, right?" · "All that you're missing is this one component, which is generating your own leads."
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent gets leads from their team. Why would they leave?
- How do I show the real cost of team leads?
- What questions shift them from employee to owner thinking?
**Do / don't:** Do run the split math with their numbers. Don't dismiss the value of the leads they get now.

### 55 — Your Brokerage Has No Offices
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/80f0581e54434e7b8e71509c8e436cbf · 4 min*
**In one line:** Nobody goes to the office anyway; the money saved goes back to agents, and if they truly need one, co-sponsor with a partner who has one.
**Mike's points:**
- Validate: that's an important factor for you, many agents are used to physical offices. Reframe: the industry has shifted, most agents rarely go in, and the brick-and-mortar savings go back to agents as revenue share, stock, and technology. You can still use co-working spaces or meet clients in professional settings.
- Mike's own experience: his previous local independent brokerage had the best office in the city with a full studio, over a hundred agents, and nobody went. He only went there to record because it was a better background than his 500-square-foot condo. Clients wanted coffee shops, restaurants, or their own home, wherever was convenient for them.
- The agents who did hang out at the office were mostly the lowest producers convincing themselves they were working. You are the average of the five people you surround yourself with.
- At eXp, Mike's example, agents get access to Regus co-working spaces; he uses them when meeting partners in other cities.
- The resolution in almost every case: partner them with someone in your organization who has a local office, or co-sponsor. His example: a Florida agent who wanted luxury, team training, and a local office; he co-sponsored with someone there who had the office, and everyone won.
**Say it like Mike:** "Most agents today rarely go into the office." · "every client wanted to meet up at a coffee shop, at a restaurant, at a bar, at a cocktail lounge, at their house" · "Would you be open to discussing a potential partnership with somebody who has one?"
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent wants a physical office. What do I say?
- How do I handle "where will I meet clients?"
- What if they really do need an office?
**Do / don't:** Do offer a partner or co-sponsorship with an office. Don't pretend the office never matters to anyone.

### 57 — I'm Doing Well at My Current Brokerage
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/50b27393a98f437695f6c7bc83488681*
**In one line:** Success today is great; ask whether they are building long-term wealth or just more closings, and why they are on the call if everything is fine.
**Mike's points:**
- Empathize: that's great, successful agents like you are usually the most loyal and I respect that. Reframe: are you building long-term wealth and residual income, or just more closings? Positioning for tomorrow is what creates legacy. If I could add real wealth building without disrupting what you are doing, would you be open to it?
- Two questions Mike leaned into: if you're one of the top agents in your brokerage, how do you plan on going to the next level? You are the average of the five people around you; if you are at the top, everyone is below you and pulling you back. You need an environment with people far more successful than you.
- "You mentioned you wanted to do better, correct?" Nobody gets on the call to make less money. Find out why they agreed to the call and what is holding them back.
- The long-term play: as income goes up, expenses go up. Mike knows top producers who are broke, doing 50 deals a year with no exit strategy, burnt out from having to repeat it to maintain their lifestyle.
- What's in it for them: successful agents attract the most agents, because other agents want to partner with top producers. Mike will feature them on his YouTube channel, coach and mentor the agents who come through them, and they benefit without disrupting their production.
- Mike says this one comes down to a little bit of challenging their ego.
**Say it like Mike:** "are you building long-term wealth and residual income, or just more closings?" · "I know a lot of agents that are top producers that are broke." · "if you're one of the top agents in your brokerage, how do you plan on going to the next level?"
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent is a top producer and happy. Why would they move?
- How do I talk to a successful agent without insulting their success?
- What is the "average of five" argument?
**Do / don't:** Do ask why they took the call. Don't argue they are not successful.

### 58 — I Have Closings I Need to Finish
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/04158b50bf43439592cb9e58e211c61a · 4 min*
**In one line:** Clients come first; time the transition so nothing is interrupted, check their ICA, and build a proactive plan together before they move.
**Mike's points:**
- Validate: of course your current clients come first, that's the sign of a true professional. Reframe: we can time your transition so nothing gets interrupted; many agents move right after closings with zero disruption. Would you be open to planning the move so you maximize the benefits once the deal closes?
- Did you know you can usually bring your clients with you? Check the ICA (Independent Contractor Agreement); termination stipulations are usually at the end. A solo agent can typically terminate listings and relist the same day with the new brokerage. On a team, usually not, because the listings belong to the team lead.
- Always check the ICA. Mike saw one office that kept a share of commission on deals under contract at transition; the fix was simply to wait for the last closing so the agent was paid in full.
- The move that works like magic: a proactive transition plan. Want to come in one, two, three months? Fine. Work together behind the scenes now: build the strategy, record a YouTube feature, and launch it the week they arrive so they start with momentum. By transition day they feel like they have been there the whole time.
- Always do what is in the agent's best interest. Don't push them to come now. There is never a perfectly non-disruptive time; find the least disruptive one.
**Say it like Mike:** "of course, your current clients come first. That's first priority, right? That's the sign of a true professional." · "We can time your transition so that nothing gets interrupted." · "Don't push them to come now."
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent has deals under contract. When should they move?
- Can an agent bring listings and clients with them?
- What is an ICA and why check it?
- How do I keep momentum while they wait to transition?
**Do / don't:** Do build the plan with them before they move. Don't rush them past their closings.

### 59 — My Brokerage Is Cheaper (100% Brokerage)
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/0057473122fe4b72ae201f517c3196bb*
**In one line:** A 100% split on a business that is not growing is the trap; the value has to be worth far more than the 15 or 20 percent.
**Mike's points:**
- Validate: keeping more of your commission is good, a lot of agents love 100% up front. Reframe: it doesn't matter if your split is 100% if you're not growing. Would you rather keep 100% of $50,000 or build a $200,000-a-year business with residual income and equity ownership? If I could show you how to net more, not just save on splits, would you want the breakdown?
- Question: would you rather 100% of your current deals, or training that helps you double your commission? And at brokerages with lead-agent or icon-style programs, Mike's example, you can make more than 100%.
- The flat-fee reality: if you pay the brokerage almost nothing, it has no income to give you training, tools, and resources, so you pay separately for coaches, courses, and masterminds.
- Mike's numbers, as he states them: eXp 80/20 and Real 85/15, both capping at $80,000 GCI. Whether it is 15 or 20 percent, your value proposition has to be worth more than that, worth 50 or 100 percent.
- Proof: agents like them who went through his training and community and tripled their business in 12 months. He has brought over whole flat-fee brokerages because they saw the value.
- This one takes more finessing than most.
**Say it like Mike:** "It doesn't matter if your split's a hundred percent, if you're not growing your income" · "the value has to overcome the split" · "Why would you only want a hundred percent? You can make more than that over here."
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent is at a 100% or flat-fee brokerage. How do I justify a split?
- What does a flat-fee brokerage not give you?
- How do I make the value worth more than the split?
**Do / don't:** Do run the net-income math, not the split math. Don't apologize for the split.

- If no deferral: ask when their cap resets. Two months? Great, start building the strategy now so they launch full tilt on day one.
- They earned the 100% and want to ride it out; that is fair. It comes down to deferral or timing plus a proactive plan.
**Say it like Mike:** "Does capping build wealth or does it reset each year?" · "In the cloud-based brokerage, capping is just a start." · "when it resets, you can have all your ducks in a row"
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent has capped and is at 100% now. Why would they move?
- What is a cap deferral program?
- When should a capped agent transition?
**Do / don't:** Do ask when the cap resets and plan to it. Don't ask them to give up earned 100% for no reason.

### 61 — I Just Switched Brokerages
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/6974898ff416463f8df7daa65f0f168b · 4 min*
**In one line:** It is easier to switch now, before anyone knows you are there, than after months of branded content; and if not now, they will switch eventually anyway.
**Mike's points:**
- The agent loves what you offer but just switched, did signs, cards, branding. Validate: nobody wants to bounce around, but agents often switch for a short-term fix and realize later it wasn't the long-term solution.
- Reframe: the key isn't how long you've been somewhere, it's whether you're in the best place for your future. If I could show you a way to make this your last move ever, would you want to see it?
- Questions that worked magic: wouldn't it be better to build at a brokerage that helps you reach your long-term goals instead of switching again later? Don't you think it's better to change before you spend time and money branding yourself there and people recognize the change?
- Nobody knows they are at the new brokerage yet, so nobody will notice another switch. Months later, after content and systems, a rebrand is far more disruptive.
- If they resist, say no problem, give it a shot, stay in touch and follow up in a couple of months. Mike says everyone he has talked to in this spot eventually switched; it is now or later. Don't push, but explain the value of now because it is true, not a sales technique.
**Say it like Mike:** "sometimes agents switch for a short term fix and realize later it wasn't the longterm solution" · "You might as well make the switch now. When nobody even knows and nobody's going to notice" · "either which way they're going to join you now, or they're going to join you someday"
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent just moved brokerages. Is it too soon to ask them to move again?
- How do I argue for switching now versus later?
- What if they want to give the new brokerage a chance?
**Do / don't:** Do explain why now is less disruptive. Don't push if they want to try it first; stay in relationship.

### 62 — I love my broker
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/82f298802cee41298a8614c9c9270671 · 4 min*
**In one line:** Loyalty is great until it costs your future; a broker who truly loves you supports your best move, and your family comes first.
**Mike's points:**
- Mike is still close with his past broker, who fully supported his move. Validate: that's awesome, I respect loyalty, it says a lot about your character.
- Reframe: loyalty is great as long as it's not costing your future. You can still love your broker while making the best move for your family, income, and legacy. If I could show you how to honor your loyalty and set yourself up for more opportunity, would you be open to it?
- The perspective shift that worked: when your broker retires or gets an offer to sell, do you believe they'll stay because of their relationship with you? Once the broker has made their decision, the relationship does not factor in.
- The test of a real relationship is what happens after you leave. Mike has seen brokers who "loved" an agent send disrespectful emails once the agent stopped benefiting them. His own broker's response was that they knew he would make this decision one day and kept the door open.
- Lean on family: you might love your broker, but you don't love them as much as your family. Family and future always come first. This one is about challenging them a little and getting them to think differently.
**Say it like Mike:** "loyalty is great as long as it's not costing your future" · "you might love your broker, but you don't love them as much as your family" · "do what's best for your family. Do what's best for your future."
**Answers these questions:**
- The agent loves their broker and feels disloyal leaving. What do I say?
- How do I challenge broker loyalty respectfully?
- What does a good broker do when an agent leaves?
**Do / don't:** Do respect the loyalty out loud, then reframe around family. Don't bad-mouth their broker.
