# Model Breakdowns & Positioning (Week 2)

This module is about knowing your brokerage model inside and out and positioning it against every competitor without ever tearing anyone down. Mike opens with his two cardinal rules (never talk badly about another brokerage, never talk badly about another sponsor), then shows how to position against other cloud-based brokerages, franchise split brokerages, and flat-fee 100% shops, always with math and the long-term vehicle question. It closes with the principle underneath all of it: agents follow people, not companies.

### 13 — 2 Cardinal Rules
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/96a40319aaf8434ba40d9fd55e8bae78 · 7 min*
**In one line:** Never talk badly about another brokerage and never talk badly about another sponsor; be the better person every time and redirect to your value.
**Mike's points:**
- These are his own rules. Most people break them, which is why they build a bad reputation and fail to grow.
- Rule 1: do not talk negatively about any other brokerage. It looks unprofessional and desperate. If you have to tear another brokerage down to prop yours up, that is terrible leadership and it means you don't know how to position the pros of your model against the cons of theirs.
- Rule 2: do not talk negatively about other sponsors. Agents sponsor shop inside your brokerage; you will compete with other sponsors there too.
- He gets talked about constantly by people who have never met him. Jealousy is the reality of growing. Be the better person every time, no matter how much it hurts.
- His script when an agent mentions another brokerage or sponsor: give credit if it's a good person ("they're great, I've heard great things"), or simply acknowledge the conversation without comment, then revert immediately to what he can do for them, his transformations, and proof.
- Word gets around. Agents go back and repeat what you said. When someone tells him another group said X about him, his turn is: you haven't heard me talk negatively about anyone, even though I could, so what does that say about them?
- He became the top attractor at his brokerage without tearing anyone down.
**Say it like Mike:** "Do not talk shit about any other brokerage." · "Be the better person ever. Every time, no matter how much it hurts." · "If they have to try and tear somebody down in order to prop themselves up versus me, that spent the entire time focusing on you."
**Answers these questions:** What do I say when a prospect is also talking to a sponsor at Real or another brokerage? Can I point out another brokerage's flaws? Someone is badmouthing me; how do I respond? Why does Mike refuse to compare himself to other sponsors?
**Do / don't:** Do give credit and redirect to your value and proof. Don't badmouth any brokerage or sponsor, even if you have heard negative things.

### 14 — Understanding Competing Models
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/65fcabc748ab4ae28ae66355647a251b · 11 min*
**In one line:** Master your own model, study the others, and you will always have an honest talking point; every brokerage has a competitive advantage you can position.
**Mike's points:**
- Knowledge is power: study your own model religiously first, then other cloud brokerage models. You can ask ChatGPT for a full breakdown with pros and cons.
- Surface-level comparisons: splits, caps, fees. His example: an 80/20 with a $16,000 cap versus 85/15 with a $12,000 cap both cap at $80,000 GCI, so ask "do you not believe you're going to cap?" The other side could say most agents never cap, so they keep more early. Both are valid; position for the agent in front of you.
- Revenue share tiers: his example that seven tiers versus five matters because groups build out like a Christmas tree and the bottom tiers get biggest; the other side can argue higher first-tier pay. His counter: a big first tier incentivizes selfish personal sponsoring; he hasn't personally sponsored anyone in two years and gives his roughly 600 yearly calendar bookings to his agents.
- Stock: be careful with "potential." You cannot promise a small brokerage's stock will do what his brokerage's did; that early run was valued as a tech stock, and brokerage stocks are now valued as brokerage stocks. Publicly traded means SEC regulated; don't sell pie in the sky.
- Deeper layers you learn from agents who left other brokerages: training quality, opportunity (international footprint), financial health, leadership, operations (commission payout, onboarding, offboarding).
- Never talk about another brokerage's leadership. Only speak positively about your own; his example is same-day access to his CEO.
- When he found weaknesses in his own brokerage (onboarding is overwhelming), he built his own streamlined onboarding and added it to his value proposition.
- Never make bold claims yourself. If an agent who left another brokerage said payouts took months, say "I've heard that from agents who came over," never "this is fact."
**Say it like Mike:** "Knowledge is always power." · "At either brokerage, you always have a talking point." · "I will never talk about the leadership at any other brokerage ever."
**Answers these questions:** How do I answer "Real has a better split"? How do I talk about stock without overpromising? Should I mention what I've heard about another brokerage's payouts? What should I study about competing models?
**Do / don't:** Do run the cap math and have a positive spin ready for your model. Don't project stock outcomes or criticize another brokerage's leadership.

### 15 — Franchise Split Models
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/c653768b71fc431a877b8f229fd47a2f · 8 min*
**In one line:** Against franchise split brokerages the math almost always wins; you cannot argue ego and emotion with math, then ask whether they are even in the right vehicle.
**Mike's points:**
- Think the big franchise names. These are the easiest conversations: archaic models often losing agents year over year while cloud brokerages grow.
- Pros of a franchise: a local office, and that is pretty much it. Cons: one stream of income, higher fees, geographically restricted, no exit or retirement plan, no ownership (you are renting your business while telling clients to buy), outdated training, one layer of support versus five to seven.
- If they have no cap, run the math; they are almost guaranteed losing money. His example: a California agent at 90/10 with no cap, 50 deals a year, ignored every 80/20 pitch until Mike explained the $16,000 cap and cap-back in stock; he would have made $111,000 more in the prior 12 months (his numbers). He joined the next day.
- If they do have a cap, still run last-12-months math; they will likely make more with you.
- The vehicle question: ask their long-term goals (wealth, retirement, generational wealth), then ask whether one stream of income can get them there. His analogy: flying eight hours doesn't land you in Miami unless the plane is pointed there.
- Talking points: leverage as their brand grows (other agents want access to them and can't get it at a franchise), better training, five to seven layers of support, global or international opportunity (the California agent speaks Portuguese and can attract agents from Portugal and Brazil).
**Say it like Mike:** "You cannot argue ego and emotion with math." · "If you're paying a monthly fee to a brokerage that you don't have ownership in, you're renting your business." · "Are they even in the right vehicle?"
**Answers these questions:** How do I win an agent who loves their big-name franchise? What if they are on a 90/10 split? What are the cons of a traditional brokerage? What is the "right vehicle" question?
**Do / don't:** Do run their last-12-months numbers against your model. Don't argue with their pride in the brand; shift to math and goals.

### 16 — Flat Fee Brokerages
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/d264e3d3d9814094a722fe163e363e0e · 6 min*
**In one line:** Against 100% flat-fee shops the short-term math won't win; shift perspective to their why, current state versus desired state, and earning more than 100%.
**Mike's points:**
- At split brokerages the math works about 99% of the time; at 100% brokerages the agent makes more in the short term, so "I don't want to pay a split" comes immediately.
- Master three things: their purpose and why, current state versus desired state, and their constraints and pain points.
- The why: if they are doing it for family, what happens if they pass away or can't work for six months? At a 100% shop income goes to zero. At the cloud brokerages he knows, revenue share is a willable asset a family member can inherit by getting licensed within a window.
- Current versus desired: would you rather keep 100% on 10 deals, or pay a split with a cap and get the training, support, and accountability to do 20 or 30?
- Pros of flat fee: local office and more money today. Cons: one stream of income, geographic restriction, no exit, no ownership, old school, one layer of support.
- More than 100%: with icon or elite programs, cap back in stock, equity, and revenue share, many of his top producers make 200% or more of their commission (his claim).
- Lean harder into personal development and mindset here. He brought over a full 100% brokerage of 62 or 63 agents from Colorado.
**Say it like Mike:** "Most agents go wrong is they can't think past the next transaction or the next year." · "You have to get them thinking differently. You have to shift their perspective." · "You just got to have the right approach."
**Answers these questions:** How do I compete with a 100% brokerage? What do I say to "I don't want to pay a split"? Is revenue share inheritable? Can agents make more than 100% of commission?
**Do / don't:** Do discover their why before any numbers. Don't try to win a flat-fee agent on next-year math alone.

### 17 — Agents Follow People, Not Companies
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/fc1e7db9208040b493d96f322f0cc552 · 6 min*
**In one line:** Agents say yes to you, not a logo; the brokerage is the platform, you are the reason they build there, and relationships are what close the back door.
**Mike's points:**
- He cites his research: 87% of agents who switch prioritize leadership, mentorship, and support. They ask: will you guide me, will you support me, do you have a clear path I can follow?
- Relationships over commission split: he brought a 60-plus agent 100% brokerage over because the relationship and value were worth more than the split. Agents compare numbers but stay for relationships.
- Your personal brand is the magnet; become the role model agents want to learn from and grow with. Even at a brokerage with weaker compensation you can win with the right relationships and value.
- Retention is the name of the game. Many groups grow 30 to 150 a month and stay flat because the back door is wide open. It is easy to leave a service; it is hard to leave a friend. His top leaders feel like brothers and sisters.
- Lead by example and prove it: he gives his group free access to his social media and AI training and has close to 200 YouTube interviews of agents who got results. He features about one agent a week, roughly 50 a year.
- Be obsessed with creating success stories; duplication means others reach your level or beyond. His example: Vinay Munro started with no license, no channel, followed his blueprint, and now has a bigger YouTube channel than he does.
**Say it like Mike:** "People are joining you for you." · "It's very easy to leave a company or a service, it's very difficult to leave a friend." · "The brokerage is the platform. However, you are the reason they choose to build there."
**Answers these questions:** Does it matter which brokerage I'm at? Why are people joining and then leaving my group? What do agents actually want from a sponsor? How do I prove my value works?
**Do / don't:** Do build real relationships and showcase success stories weekly. Don't lead with the brokerage logo.
