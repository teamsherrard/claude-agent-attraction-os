### calendly — Calendly (Presentation for AAA)
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/1ff16520d49d478c8ec11bf64a05d396 · 8 min*
**In one line:** His exact Calendly setup: a 30-minute Zoom discovery call with five required qualifying questions, reminders at 24 hours, 1 hour, and 10 minutes, and wide-open availability.
**Mike's points:**
- He has used Calendly since day one in 2020 with zero issues (about $20 a month, he thinks). The link goes everywhere: YouTube descriptions, Instagram bio.
- Event: "Agent Wolfpack Discovery Call, 30 minute Zoom one-on-one," Zoom connected, description written with energy about what the group does for realtors. Time zones auto-detected, which matters for bookings from everywhere.
- Invite form, all required: name and email (auto), guests allowed (team leaders, partners, a friend who's also considering).
- Q1: "Do you acknowledge this is a discussion about joining my group at [brokerage]? Do not book if you're already at [brokerage] or have chosen a sponsor." Yes/no. If they answer no, he deletes the meeting; people book to ask about a video or training and waste your time.
- Q2: which brokerage are you with (one line). Q3: where are you located (one line). Q4: new agent, experienced, team leader, or broker owner (multiple lines, gives you the avatar). Q5: anything to help me prepare, including why you're interested in this opportunity (multiple lines; some write paragraphs).
- Communicate with energy everywhere, including the booking form.
- Reminders: templated emails at 24 hours, 1 hour, and 10 minutes before. He would personalize them today.
- Availability: he used 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., later 10 to 2, for 8 to 10 calls a day. Make it as flexible and convenient for them as possible early on; protect evenings if you have family.
**Say it like Mike:** "The questions you ask will save you an enormous amount of time, frustration and headache from the wrong people booking in." · "Make it as convenient for them as possible."

### think-about-it — Handling the "I Need to Think About It" Objection
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/edf2ace9e7314d338e735395a024cad7*
**In one line:** "I need to think about it" is a deflection; thoughts equal unanswered questions, so validate, then ask what specifically they need to think through.
**Mike's points:**
- The script: "Got it, you want some time, that's fair, this is an important decision. But what specifically do you feel like you need to think through? Usually one or two questions aren't fully answered. If we could address those now, would you be open to exploring further?"
- Logic: if someone is 100% certain, there's nothing left to think about. So they need to think about something, and that something is a question. Ask probing questions to find it.
- Common reason: the spouse. Offer to get on a call with the spouse and explain it yourself, because the agent is still learning a multi-layer model and will explain it poorly. Empathize: it took him months to understand it.
- What to tell the spouse: a cloud-based brokerage is a security blanket for the family. At a traditional brokerage with one income stream, if you die the family gets whatever you have. At his brokerage, a family member can get licensed and inherit the residual income. He calls it "God's gift to families."
- His brokerage example: health benefits through an acquired provider that he understands save the average agent $300 to $800 a month (his figure); retirement plan, safety net, exit strategy, better coverage. Hard for a spouse to say no.
**Say it like Mike:** "Thoughts equal questions, questions need to be answered." · "What specifically do you feel like you need to think through?" · "This is God's gift to families."
**Answers these questions:** What do I say when an agent needs to think about it? What if they want to talk to their spouse first? How do I explain the family protection angle?
**Do / don't:** Do validate, then dig for the real question. Don't let "think about it" end the call.

### objection-framework — Mastering the Objection Framework
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/891e7bc918e84783a317191dc0809fac*
**In one line:** Four steps for every objection: listen, validate, reframe, invite; it keeps you calm, empathetic, and the trusted guide instead of a pushy salesperson.
**Mike's points:**
- Works on every archetype. Shifts you from salesperson to trusted guide.
- Listen: hear it without reacting, stay calm, never interrupt (interrupting ruins everything). Let them vent fully, nod, show genuine curiosity; agents want to feel heard before they hear you. "I completely understand, tell me more about why you feel that way."
- Validate: make them feel understood, acknowledge the concern as normal, never make them feel stupid. "I used to feel that way too; I'm still great friends with my broker." Then the ninja move: "a lot of the top agents that have joined us also felt that way," which future-paces and assumes the close.
- Reframe: shift the lens with data, stories, analogies, your own experience. Example for "I don't want to recruit": you don't have to; most agents here don't (he cites a figure from his brokerage). But when someone asks which brokerage to join, you say yours and get nothing; here you're compensated for the same thing and can build equity and income.
- Invite: "Would you be open to exploring what that looks like?" Not pressure, opening the door, warm and welcoming.
- The power: you stay in control without being controlling, because questions control conversations. Agents feel respected; objections become opportunities.
**Say it like Mike:** "Listen, then validate, reframe, invite." · "Validation lowers defenses and builds trust." · "The invite isn't pressure. It's opening the door."
**Answers these questions:** How do I handle objections without sounding pushy? What's the order of steps? What do I say to "I don't want to recruit"? Should I jump in when I know the answer?
**Do / don't:** Do let them finish and validate first. Don't butt in or argue the moment you hear the objection.

### perfect-presentation — The Perfect Presentation: Building Rapport and Closing Effectively
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/a982501feb3b4bd3ba17552f853c8802*
**In one line:** The call flow: build rapport, ask qualifying questions, bridge the gap with only what they care about, then assume the close and walk them through exactly what happens next.
**Mike's points:**
- Step 1, rapport: show you genuinely care, with energy. "Hey John, thanks for making the call today, I've been looking forward to chatting. How's your week been? How's business this year, what's working, what isn't?" This starts collecting ammunition.
- Step 2, qualifying questions: "What piqued your interest and made you open to this conversation? What do you like most about your current brokerage? Biggest challenges? What are you lacking? What's holding you back from your goals?" This opens the loop.
- Step 3, bridge the gap, tailored: if the struggle is retirement, explain revenue share and a structured exit plan (he frames the target as residual income covering at least double their living expenses). If the struggle is production, explain your value proposition and training and don't mention revenue share unless they ask or steer there. If the struggle is support, explain how you personally help and the multi-layer support. Use case studies of similar agents.
- Step 4, assume the close: "This is going to be an exciting new chapter. The number one thing people say is they wish they came earlier. What transition date feels least disruptive so we can plan a launch with momentum?"
- Then walk them through next steps with clarity, because people go into "paranoia mode" after deciding. Get their best email, send the exact join steps (which button, which name, which email), show them the exact welcome email they'll receive and the links to the training, community, and support team.
**Say it like Mike:** "People join people they trust." · "If their struggle is production, do not focus on revenue share." · "The number one thing people say once they come over here is that they wish they came earlier."
**Answers these questions:** How do I run the discovery call? What questions do I ask first? When do I bring up revenue share? How do I close without pressure? What do I send after they say yes?
**Do / don't:** Do tailor everything to the pain they named. Don't present the whole model to someone who only cares about production.

### bridging-the-gap — Bridging the Gap: Mastering Agent Attraction Presentations
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/8974b75e0889426b93eb035df0a11c09*
**In one line:** Agent attraction is finding their current state and desired state and positioning the model, your brand, and your value proposition as the bridge; questions control the conversation.
**Mike's points:**
- The goal of a call is that agents willingly want to join because they see you as the solution to their pain. Never pitch or push.
- Current state (almost always has pain, since they aren't hitting their goals) to desired state. You bridge the gap.
- When done well, calls end with the agent asking "how do I get started?" That is how most of his calls end now; it did not happen in the beginning. Reps and brand make it easier.
- Where most go wrong: word-vomiting information without knowing what the agent cares about. Same as listing presentations, where he won by asking sellers what they wanted first (usually communication).
- Seed questions: are you on track to hit your target this year? What's your five to ten year vision? What are you struggling with most right now?
- The sales loop: gather ammunition at the start, tailor the model and value proposition to their exact pain points and goals, pre-handle every objection during the presentation, then loop back at the end and solve every problem they named. His analogy: the final rap battle in 8 Mile, where every comeback is said before the opponent can.
- Reaffirm and provide confidence that you, your value proposition, and the brokerage will get them where they want to be.
**Say it like Mike:** "Questions control conversations, not statements." · "If you ask better questions, you get better outcomes." · "You open the loop in the beginning, you close it at the end."
**Answers these questions:** What is "bridging the gap"? What is the sales loop? Why shouldn't I explain the model right away? How do I get agents to ask to join instead of me asking them?
**Do / don't:** Do open with questions and close the loop with their own words. Don't lead with revenue share or a model dump.
