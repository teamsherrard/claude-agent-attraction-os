# Presentation & Delivery (Week 5)

How Mike runs the attraction call itself: the mindset (you are enrolling, not recruiting), the exact chain of events from the Calendly booking to the welcome email, how to run a three-way call with your upline, and the three types of questions that get the agent to sell themselves. Everything here is about making the call about them, not you.

**Lessons**
- 41 — Psychology of Enrollment
- 42 — Sequence of Events
- 43 — 3-way calls
- 44 — Strategic Questions

## The sequence of events, as Mike lays it out (lesson 42)

1. **Call booked** through Calendly. One hour in the beginning until you master the craft, then try 30 minutes and see if you keep your conversion rate. The booking form asks mandatory questions (see card 42) so you walk in prepared.
2. **Start the meeting** by building rapport and asking qualifying questions. Identify their current state, pain points, and why they agreed to the call. Open the loop.
3. **Tailor** the model and your value proposition to their goals and desired state, not to what you care about.
4. **Handle their objections.** You will get some; that is fine.
5. **Get them excited.** Be hyped, energetic, future-pace how you get to work together.
6. **Send the email** with the exact steps to get started and name you as sponsor. Part 1 (resources) goes to everyone you speak with, joining or not. Part 2 is the foolproof bullet list of how to join.
7. **Once they join: welcome email** with access to your value proposition and next steps, then **book a strategy call** to plan their first week against their goals.

Three endings: **ready** (steps 6–7), **hesitant** (book a three-way call with your strongest upline, card 43), **not ready** (nurture on triggers: positive changes at your brokerage, a similar agent joining, their life milestones, anything new you launch).

## The three-way call structure, as Mike lays it out (lesson 43)

**Before:** edify your sponsor to the prospect and get the prospect to commit to the call. Send a calendar invite with the Zoom link to both your sponsor and the agent. Always include the time zone. Check that both have accepted and follow up to confirm.

**During:** edify the guest first. Then edify your sponsor. Then do not talk unless the guest or your sponsor asks you a question. Take notes. Pay attention (no phone). Let your sponsor present the model, the value proposition, and handle objections.

**After:** if they commit, you send the next steps. If they don't, you follow up, not your sponsor. Own the outcome.

### 41 — Psychology of Enrollment
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/9f0b0be91f614758812a187ef999cae7 · 7 min*
**In one line:** You are not recruiting, you are enrolling: guiding someone to say yes to their own future so they recruit themselves.
**Mike's points:**
- Enrollment is guiding somebody to say yes to their own future. Influence inspires, pressure repels. Done right, the call ends with them asking "what are the next steps?" Mike says that is how the majority of his calls end.
- Never talk over anybody. Let them finish everything they have to say. Remove your ego; some people will cut you off and it should not show that it bothers you.
- Pressure breaks trust and creates resistance. Pushing someone to join leads to regret and churn, the same way pushing a buyer into the wrong home for a commission cheque does.
- Being magnetic: ask questions, be curious, be empathetic, listen, and align with their goals. Most agents just want to produce; some want to attract. It is all about them.
- The journey on the call: uncover their pain points, mirror back their vision, show the gap between where they are and where they want to be, show the bridge (your leadership, the brokerage model, your value proposition), then give them space to decide.
- Mike never pushes. If they are not ready, he says "cool," and suggests reconnecting in a week or next month. He has no CRM drip campaign and says he is the number one attractor without one; drip campaigns are a crutch for weak calls and a weak value proposition.
- Enroll with certainty: tell them you believe in them, that they are going to be the next success story, and which training to go to first.
**Say it like Mike:** "Enrollment is guiding somebody to say yes to their own future." · "Influence inspires, pressure repels." · "Who cares what you want? It's all about them, right?"
**Answers these questions:**
- Why does Mike say "enrolling" instead of "recruiting"?
- What do I do if the agent is not ready to join at the end of the call?
- Do I need a CRM or drip campaign to follow up?
- What is the biggest mistake agents make on attraction calls?
- How do I close the call when they are ready?
**Do / don't:** Do let them finish every sentence. Don't push for the commitment, and don't talk about what you care about.

### 42 — Sequence of Events
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/10a0927d57da4615990ea0bc69a50ae2 · 16 min*
**In one line:** The exact chain from Calendly booking to welcome email, with the real emails Mike sends, and three endings: ready, hesitant, not ready.
**Mike's points:**
- Call length: one hour until you master the craft, then 30 minutes if you can hold your conversion rate.
- Calendly mandatory questions: (1) acknowledge this is a discussion about joining my group at my brokerage; do not book if you are already there or already chose a sponsor (a "no" means Mike deletes the meeting); (2) which brokerage are you with (tells you the model and pain points); (3) where are you located; (4) new agent, experienced agent, team leader, or broker; (5) share anything that helps me prepare, including why you are interested.
- On the call: rapport, qualifying questions, tailor the model and value proposition to their desired state, handle objections, end hyped and future-pacing.
- The after-call email, two parts. Part 1: resources (his model video, his funnel, everything he provides), sent to every person he talks to whether they join or not. Part 2: foolproof bullet-point steps to join and name you as sponsor. Copy and paste everything except the first paragraph; personalize that one so they see you listened.
- Once they join: the welcome email with a welcome video, every asset, where to start, the community link, and the support email. Then book a strategy call.
- Hesitant: book a three-way call with your strongest upline, the best communicator, not necessarily your direct sponsor. Edify the guest, edify your partner, do not talk.
- Not ready: everyone is coming, it is a matter of when; some joined Mike years after the first call. Nurture triggers: positive changes at your brokerage (his eXp examples: co-sponsorship, the new revenue share system), a similar agent joining ("they reminded me of you"), their life milestones (marriage, kid, dog, trips), and anything new you launch that solves their pain point.
**Say it like Mike:** "Nobody cares what you care about." · "Do not do some random copy and paste stuff for every single person. Adjust just the first paragraph." · "It's not a matter of if, right? It's a matter of when."
**Answers these questions:**
- How long should my attraction call be?
- What questions should my Calendly booking form ask?
- What do I send after the call?
- What goes in the welcome email once they join?
**Do / don't:** Do send the resources email to everyone you talk to, joining or not. Don't lead with what you care about.

### 43 — 3-way calls
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/f1b224ce1423467c85010ab72637cbda · 12 min*
**In one line:** Bring your strongest upline onto the call, edify them properly, then shut up and take notes; the agent joining is the only thing that matters.
**Mike's points:**
- A three-way call is you, your prospect, and someone from your upline with more credibility and a more recognized brand who gets the prospect over the hump.
- Drop the ego. In his first 30 days Mike connected all 30 agents who came to him with his sponsor on three-way calls; his sponsor closed them all while Mike took notes on how he handled objections. Thirty hours of practice, then he took calls himself.
- Edification increases show-up rates and conversion rates, the two most important metrics. A bad intro: "this is my sponsor Mike, he has a YouTube channel and is active on social media." A good intro stacks the accolades so the prospect knows why to listen. His contrast: announcing Grant Cardone as "a real estate investor for a couple of decades" versus stacking the portfolio and the following.
- Before: get the commitment, send the invite with the Zoom link to both, always include the time zone (Mike made this mistake tons of times in year one), and check both have accepted; invites land in spam.
- During: edify the guest first (even a new agent has something you can make them feel special about), then edify your sponsor, then do not talk unless asked. Butting in has ruined calls with top producers and broker owners. Take notes. Put the phone down; the guest can see you scrolling.
- After: if they commit, you send the next steps. If they do not, you follow up, not your sponsor.
**Say it like Mike:** "The only thing that matters is that the agent actually joins you period." · "If your sponsor is on a call, don't talk just sit down shut up and take a lot of notes and study it" · "This is your prospect. You should do whatever it takes to get them over. This is on you."
**Answers these questions:**
- What is a three-way call and when do I use one?
- How do I introduce my sponsor on the call?
- What do I do while my sponsor is talking?
- Who follows up after the three-way call?
**Do / don't:** Do stack your sponsor's accolades and include the time zone. Don't talk unless asked, and don't touch your phone. Do own the follow-up.

### 44 — Strategic Questions
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/2ffac2002b8d4860ac55c3f589d78e98 · 5 min*
**In one line:** Statements push, questions pull: three question types (discovery, vision, commitment) get the agent to name their own problem and sell themselves.
**Mike's points:**
- Questions control conversations, shift the focus onto them, uncover pain, desires, and objections, and create open loops that only you can close. The same applies to listing presentations and buyers.
- Discovery (current state): What do you love about your current brokerage? What is frustrating you most right now? What do you feel is missing?
- Vision (future state): If nothing changes at your current brokerage, where do you see yourself 12 months from now? What would doubling your business mean for you and your family? What would make you feel confident in achieving your potential?
- Commitment (bridging the gap): If I could show you a way to double your business without any extra time away from your family, would you be open to it? What is more important: repeating another year in the same spot, or doubling your income and spending more time with family? If we can solve inconsistent income with no predictable leads, would you be ready to go all in with us?
- Why it works: they articulate their own problems, so it is real because they said it. They paint their own future; you guide them there and position the model and value proposition to exactly what they care about. They end feeling "this is the right move for me," not "I was sold."
- Do not harp on revenue share. An agent struggling to put food on the table hears residual income as recruiting, MLM, pyramid scheme. If they say they want an exit strategy, revenue share is the vehicle; bring it up only then.
**Say it like Mike:** "Statements push. Questions pull." · "Ask better questions, get better answers." · "make it all about them, make them feel seen, heard, appreciated, and understood, and ask the right qualifying questions"
**Answers these questions:**
- What questions should I ask on an agent attraction call?
- How do I find out what the agent actually cares about?
- When should I bring up revenue share?
- Why should I not just present the model?
**Do / don't:** Do run discovery, then vision, then commitment. Don't word-vomit the presentation before you know what they care about.
