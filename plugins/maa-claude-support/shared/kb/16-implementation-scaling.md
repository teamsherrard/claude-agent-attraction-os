### 78 — Performance Metrics
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/3fb8d8906c764d1891aa0ea5fe53d50b · 4 min*
**In one line:** What gets measured gets managed; track six numbers monthly, audit quarterly, and each weak number points to a specific fix.
**Mike's points:**
- Without tracking you're guessing. Metrics show where growth comes from and where you're leaking opportunity. Mike tracks monthly and audits in detail every quarter so he fixes problems before they become losses.
- Six metrics: new agent conversations (quality interactions); conversion rate from conversation to onboarding (below 50%, in Mike's words, means get better at explaining the model, the value proposition, and handling objections); agent retention; agent engagement (call attendance, community activity, culture participation); duplication rate (how many agents in your group are attracting); revenue share growth and trend lines.
- The audit: review recruiting activity, retention and culture metrics, where momentum is slowing; ask whether it's a lead-flow, conversion, or retention issue; adjust for the next period. Find your constraint and the holes in the bucket.
- The diagnosis: low conversions means not enough attraction activity (not enough valuable content) or you're not good enough at explaining, tailoring, and handling objections. High conversations but low conversions means messaging or objection handling. Good recruiting but poor retention means onboarding and a community that isn't tight-knit. Low duplication means you're not teaching agents to attract. Revenue share flatlining means no new leadership development; your group scales to your capacity, so keep growing.
- An engagement dip means people aren't plugged in; his usual fix is a new asset, resource, or challenge to pull them back.
- Outcome: calculated decisions instead of guesswork, doubling down on strengths and turning weaknesses into strengths (his Kobe Bryant reference). Tracking sheets are provided.
**Say it like Mike:** "What gets measured, gets managed." · "you can't argue ego and emotion with math" · "if your conversion rate is below 50%, you need to get better at explaining the model"
**Answers these questions:**
- What numbers should I track each month?
- My conversations are high but conversions are low. What's wrong?
- Why is my revenue share flat?
- How often should I audit?
**Do / don't:** Do track monthly and audit quarterly. Don't guess at what's broken.

### 79 — Scaling
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/3180d2f229554ced8c6874cf46a6dd5d · 5 min*
**In one line:** Sacrifice three years for the rest of your life; build on systems, community, duplication, attraction, and tracking until the business grows whether you work or not.
**Mike's points:**
- The goal: a business that grows whether you're working or not, built on systems, leadership, and duplication instead of hustle (you will burn out; he did), with predictable income that isn't dependent on one person's constant effort.
- Five foundations: systems (automated onboarding, training, communication, support); community (connection, recognition, collaboration); duplication (teaching leaders to create leaders); attraction (consistent brand content and messaging); tracking (monthly, data-driven adjustments).
- Sustainability: protect your energy and time; use VAs and automation for repetitive tasks; document everything once so you don't repeat yourself; empower agents with resources and playbooks so you're not running adult daycare; focus on mentorship, leadership, and vision; prioritize retention as much as recruiting.
- Scalability: identify and develop future leaders (on his leaders call he always asks who's crushing it, who's adding agents, who's closing more); give them frameworks, scripts, and systems (his weekly leaders call); encourage agents to run events, host calls, and lead initiatives; once you reach your first hundred agents, start your own private weekly call for your group; recognize and reward duplication (special experiences at events for top attractors).
- The flywheel: attract through content, events, and partnerships; plug them into onboarding and community immediately; teach them to attract if they're interested; track monthly; culture and retention hold it together. His production mastermind stays production; twice a year, first quarter and last quarter, he does one attraction session to see who raises their hand.
- The outcome: Mike says he could step away now, others could run the calls and masterminds, and his income wouldn't change. Three years of consistent hard work.
**Say it like Mike:** "three years, sacrifice three years for the rest of your life" · "Prioritize retention as much as recruiting" · "once you get to your first hundred agents, that's when you should be having your own personal private weekly call"
**Answers these questions:**
- What does a scalable revenue share business look like?
- When should I start my own weekly call?
- How do I find my next leaders?
- How often should I talk about attraction on my production call?
**Do / don't:** Do document once and reuse. Don't turn every production call into an attraction pitch.

### 80 — Leadership Evolution
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/d82c918b60e743d0a8afed4e45110eaf · 5 min*
**In one line:** Agents watch what you do, not what you say; grow first, lead by example in every area of life, then duplicate.
**Mike's points:**
- Your leadership brand evolves forever. You're the example agents follow; your habits, growth, and example set the standard. Mike says he has been consistent for eight years because they're watching.
- A stronger leader brand makes attraction and retention easier; as your income grows you can create better experiences, and everything grows together.
- Growth first, then duplication: masterminds, trainings, personal development events, coaches and mentors who stretch your thinking, constant reading and study. The more you grow, the more value you pass to your agents.
- Lead by example in all areas: model the work ethic you want (show up, be consistent, try harder, manage priorities); stay consistent with your own attraction and production; the first three years are a season of growth without balance, but work toward it without neglecting health, family, or values. Pour into your spouse and kids, get in shape, raise your standards.
- Role model, not recruiter: leadership is influence, not position; show what's possible instead of telling; celebrate wins tastefully and share struggles honestly (he admits his mistakes so you trust he has lived it); credibility through authenticity, not perfection. Nothing on his channel is perfect; done is better than perfect.
- Agents stay loyal to leaders they respect, not to the best splits.
- The flywheel: invest in your skills, mindset, and network; apply it to your own business; share what worked with your agents; duplicate the leaders who raise their hand; repeat, and the growth compounds.
**Say it like Mike:** "Agents don't just listen to what you say. More importantly, they watch what you do." · "Leadership is influence, not position." · "agents stay loyal to leaders they respect, not to those who pay the best splits"
**Answers these questions:**
- How do I become a leader agents want to follow?
- Should I share my mistakes with my group?
- How do I balance growth with family?
- What should I be investing in?
**Do / don't:** Do model exactly what you ask of them. Don't wait for perfect.
**Related:** 16/79, 04/30, 01/06, 13/66

### 81 — Annual Surveys
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/daf7271513b54420b82b9170e8833c84 · 6 min*
**In one line:** Ask your agents once a year what they value, what they want less of, and whether their focus is production or attraction; they will tell you what to build and who your next leaders are.
**Mike's points:**
- Feedback is your leadership advantage. Agents won't voice concerns until it's too late; exit interviews keep revealing easily solvable things. Surveys show what's working and what's missing, refine systems, culture, support, and value proposition, and identify future leaders.
- The questions: What do you like most about being part of this organization? (do more of it). What do you find not valuable or would like less of? (not "what do you hate"; if parts of a weekly call aren't valued, they unplug and drift). What would you like more of in support, training, and resources? What are your biggest pain points right now? (cater upcoming calls to them). Is your primary focus production or attraction? What would make the next 12 months your best year yet?
- The ninja trick: ask the focus question twice, for this year and next. Production-to-production and attraction-to-attraction are steady; the people who say production this year and attraction next year are shifting focus. Work closely with them and teach them to attract properly before they mess it up. Those are your emerging leaders.
- Use it: double down on what they value most; improve or eliminate what they don't; add training and resources where they want support; address pain points immediately; identify who is interested in attraction and develop them.
- Developing leaders: mentor them on attraction, duplication, and culture, or enrol them in this program; give them responsibility at events, calls, or onboarding. He has emerging leaders run some of the weekly calls in front of a couple of hundred people to build their presenting and Q&A skills internally before they go external.
- It makes planning easy: they tell you what to create this year.
**Say it like Mike:** "agents will not always voice concerns until it's too late" · "Those are the people that are shifting focus." · "They'll tell you what you need to create this year."
**Answers these questions:**
- What should I ask in an annual survey?
- How do I find the agents who want to start attracting?
- What do I do with the survey results?
- How do I develop a new leader?
**Do / don't:** Do ask the focus question for this year and next year. Don't ask "what do you hate."

### 82 — Recognition & Awards
*Loom: https://www.loom.com/share/f20c93f5497241f992ea4f164b415304 · 6 min*
**In one line:** Agents stay where they're celebrated; recognize first deals, first agents, capping, awards, birthdays, and life events, personally and publicly, and film it.
**Mike's points:**
- Recognition retains talent and inspires everyone else. Mike says they have never lost anyone they've recognized or featured on his YouTube channel. Public wins create momentum, and the right recognition strategy turns milestones into recruiting tools because you create content about it.
- Recognition points: closing their first deal; attracting their first agent; capping (his example: the $80,000 cap at eXp or Real; post a branded graphic and the agent reposts it); icon, lead agent, or other company awards; birthdays (personal, one to one); holidays (now a Loom video to the whole group for Canadian and US holidays); life events like a wedding, a baby, a trip.
- A broker never messages congratulations on a first deal; they only care about the 30-to-50-deal cash cows. That's why a simple message from you lands so hard.
- How: go beyond a private congrats and post on social; branded templates (someone on Fiverr can build them in Canva); a short video from you tagging them; share their story inside the community and on the live mastermind calls; a personal card or gift for personal milestones (his Mother's Day example: bouquets and chocolates for the leaders of the women's group, about $1,000 total); recognize on team calls and at events.
- Trophies: his sponsor Conor gives a custom branded trophy when someone hits 100 agents, presented publicly at a leaders dinner (his examples: Dimitri, and the Batista twins). Mike got his at 1,000 agents on his second anniversary. The company sends its own icon award; the group's own trophy is separate.
- A videographer and photographer cover everything, and it goes on every social platform to amplify the message.
- The ripple: valued agents stay longer, others chase the same milestones, outside agents see support their broker never gives, and the culture shifts from transactions to transformation.
**Say it like Mike:** "Anyone that's been recognized in our group, we've never lost them." · "The right recognition strategy turns milestones into recruiting tools." · "it shifts culture from transactions to transformation"
**Answers these questions:**
- What milestones should I recognize?
- How do I recognize agents once the group is big?
- Do I need trophies?
- How does recognition help attraction?
**Do / don't:** Do make it personal and public, then post it. Don't wait for only the big milestones.
