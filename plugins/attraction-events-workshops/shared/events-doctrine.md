# Events Doctrine — Mike Sherrard's event methodology for agent attraction

*The source of truth for every skill in the Events & Workshops plugin (`ev-`, Plugin 9, Week 6). Built from the
Week 6 vault lessons `15-advanced-scaling/73` (the email list), `/74` (live events), `/75` (virtual events),
`/76` (evergreen webinars), `/77` (strategic partnerships), the big-picture lessons `05-big-picture/35–36`, the
Week 6 breakdown doc, the cohort doc's "Virtual Workshop Launch Kit" line, the launching doc's Recruiting Event
Engine spec (the `/launch-event` output list), and Mike's own workshop-ops skill (the lifecycle phases, the
Trigger → Action → Outcome rule, the tag convention, the delegation principle). Cite lessons as `module/lesson`.
Where the vault is thin, §17 says so rather than inventing. Siblings: the Brain's `attraction-doctrine.md` (the
stance) and `compliance-doctrine.md` (the rules that bite); this plugin's `house-rules.md`, `brain-contract.md`,
and `ghl-workflow-table.md`.*

## 0. The stance in one paragraph

An event is the attraction stance in a room. The member teaches something agents can use on Monday, for free,
better than their own brokerage ever has — and the only "close" is a warm invitation to a conversation about
getting the rest. "Positions you as the educator, not the recruiter" (`15-advanced-scaling/75`). The event is
brokerage-neutral: a local agent mastermind, an AI or social-media workshop, a business-planning night, a
virtual training, a recorded webinar — never "an opportunity presentation." Agents follow people, not companies
(`03-model-positioning/17`), so the room is built to let them meet the person. The two cardinal rules hold on
stage exactly as they do online: never talk badly about another brokerage, never talk badly about another
person (`03-model-positioning/13`). No income claims from the front of the room, ever. Everything the plugin
produces is a draft the member delivers, posts, or sends; nothing goes out on its own.

## 1. Why events work (`15-advanced-scaling/74`, `/75`)

- **The front of the room.** "In-person creates influence faster, especially when you're the one at the front of
  the room" (`/74`). At a networking event you chase conversations; at your own event "everyone comes to talk to
  you." You are the authority, the credibility, the influence — and it builds the brand.
- **Trust.** "People make decisions based on trust and nothing builds trust faster than face-to-face" (`/74`).
  Being "belly to belly" builds the real relationship.
- **Reciprocity.** "Free value builds reciprocity" — agents "feel compelled to stay connected because you just
  probably gave them for free ten times more value than their broker has ever given them, and they're paying
  their broker" (`/74`).
- **Social proof.** "Group setting creates social proof — if others are here, there must be something valuable"
  (`/74`). That is why the member's own agents come and bring a guest, even at 50 people.
- **Content.** An event is "great content as well to be able to share on social media, which you should" (`/74`).
- **Virtual adds reach.** "Reach dozens or hundreds of agents across markets at once" — a cloud brokerage crosses
  state, province, and country lines; a local event builds the local community, "which is exceptionally
  important"; a virtual one gets "people from all over the place" (`/75`).
- **The outcome in Mike's words:** "creates conversations without recruiting, because the agents are going to ask
  how they can get access to it" (`/74`); "creates a pipeline of curious agents to follow up with after the event"
  (`/75`).

## 2. The three formats, and when each fits (`/74`, `/75`, `/76`)

| Format | Code | Fits when | Mike's line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Live local event** — workshop, mastermind, brokerage-neutral networking night, business-planning night | `ws` | the member attracts locally or builds a local team or brokerage; they have (or can borrow) a room, a co-host, and agents who will bring guests; they want the deepest relationships and local community | "This is what we do very frequently. [A partner Mike co-hosted with] does this every month" (`/74`) |
| **Virtual training** — a Zoom session on a hot topic | `vt` | the first event; a cloud brokerage reaching beyond the local market; low budget; the member's agents are spread out; the member wants to repeat often | "At the beginning I did everything with virtual events" (`/75`) — "just your Zoom room and a clear topic" |
| **Evergreen webinar** — a recorded training that runs on demand | `ew` | ONLY past the gate: an organization over 250 agents, a credible brand, a team or VA to build and run the systems, and the member is a strong presenter who can hold 60 minutes and invite without being salesy | "If you have less than 250 agents, skip this video" (`/76`) |

**Default for a first event: virtual.** It is free apart from optional ads, it needs no venue, it is easy for
the member's agents to share, and it builds the follow-up muscle the other formats rely on. **Go live when the
local community is the point** — Mike pairs the two: virtual for reach, live for depth (`/75`). **Evergreen is
last, and most members never need it**: "I don't recommend doing this in the beginning"; "it requires a really
radically different skillset"; "you're going to have to pay every day to run ads to it" (`/76`). The gate is a
kindness, not a put-down — "I don't want to waste your time."

## 3. The one formula (the same every time — `/74`, `/75`, `/76`)

"You start to see the consistency here. It's the same process every time, just slicing and dicing in different
avenues" (`/76`). Every format runs the same six beats:

1. **Pick a hot topic agents care about** (§4).
2. **Market it** — personal invites, social posts (especially stories), the email list, and get everyone to share
   (§5).
3. **Deliver free, high-value, immediately usable training** — "real stuff," not fluff (§6).
4. **Keep it interactive** — chat prompts on Zoom, Q&A, a networking hour in the room (§6).
5. **The light transition** — the tip-of-the-iceberg close and the invite to a conversation (§7).
6. **Follow up** — "the name of the game," heavier for virtual (§8).

Then measure it, debrief it, and run it again — "events become so scalable… repeats locally, the bigger the group
grows… and it creates duplication when your agents host their own" (`/74`).

## 4. The hot topic (`/74`, `/75`, `/76`)

"The topic really matters." Mike's go-to themes: **social media, YouTube, lead channels / lead generation, AI,
scaling** — "something that's really going to peak curiosity" (`/74`), "a sexy topic — lead gen, social, YouTube,
scaling, AI" (`/75`). For evergreen: "something that's going to be aligned with your value proposition — really
critical" (`/76`). The OS rule that follows: **the theme comes from what the member has actually done** — the
"What worked for them" table and the "Teach first" line in `identity/offer.md` — never from a topic they cannot
teach from experience. A brokerage-model topic is never a theme (that is private-call material).

## 5. Fill the room (`/74`, `/75`, `/77`, `/73`)

- **The invite scripts** (adapted from Mike's wording; the skills write them in the member's voice):
  - Live: *"We're hosting a free YouTube training for local agents this week — want me to save you a seat?"* /
    *"I'm putting together a mastermind on this — no cost, just sharing what's working"* (`/74`).
  - Virtual: *"I'm running a free virtual training next week on [topic] — want me to send you the link?"* /
    *"We're gathering agents on Zoom to break down exactly how to use AI in today's market to close more deals —
    want to join?"* (`/75`).
  - Evergreen: *"I put together a free training on [topic] — focus on value, not recruiting; the webinar does the
    positioning for you"* (`/76`).
- **Everyone shares.** "Critical: posting it on your social media, getting all the speakers to post it on their
  social media… get your agents to share the event and invite their prospects to it — it's going to be the
  easiest way to convert them" (`/74`, `/75`). Mike makes "a video promoting the event" plus a graphic, and his
  agents share both on their stories (`/74`). The plugin drafts the share pack; the people share.
- **Partners send it to their databases.** "Whenever we're doing an event, live or virtual, we get our partners to
  send it out to their databases as well" (`/77`). The partner line is written by the Lead Magnet plugin's
  `lm-partnerships`; the event just gives it something to pass along.
- **Ads are optional, and later.** "You can do this for free in the beginning without running ads" — "I just want
  you to get started, not overcomplicate this" (`/75`). Evergreen is the exception, where ads are the engine
  (`/76`). Anything that becomes an ad carries the Meta Employment special-ad-category note (§15).
- **The list is the asset.** Registration is an opt-in: "webinars, trainings — require email sign-up for access"
  through "a proper funnel and proper email sequences," which converts far better than a generic ticketing tool
  (`/73`). Registrants join the member's own list and stay there after the event.
- **Personal invites first.** Mike's local marketing starts with "personal invites" (`/74`). In the OS the
  personal-invite list comes from the pipeline: the AI Admin's match-back ("who in my pipeline would care
  about this event") and the Top-50.

## 6. Deliver real value — the iceberg has layers (`/74`, `/73`, `05-big-picture/36`)

"Deliver free, high-value training — real stuff. Most of the time when I speak on stage I give away for free what
most people charge thousands of dollars for in their courses. Give them something they can use immediately"
(`/74`). The failure mode, named: "where a lot of people go wrong is they give the tip of the iceberg — nothing
they can do immediately, no real tangible value. They just get them excited about a topic and then: 'if you want
to know how to do it, come join me at [brokerage]'. No. Give them real value. If you over-deliver on value, they'll
want to join you because there's layers below the iceberg" (`/74`).

Why holding back fails: "information is no longer novel… what are you sharing for free? Share it all"
(`05-big-picture/36`); "people pay for proximity, intimacy, access, support, accountability" (`/73`) — the
training is free; the member's time, community, and accountability are what partnering gives them.

**Interactive, always.** Virtual: "I'll ask them to drop something in the chat if they're feeling this way, or if
they understand this, or if they're experiencing that pain point" (`/75`). Live: "a time at the end for Q&A" and
"a time at the end for networking" (`/74`).

**How the member shows up** (`05-big-picture/36`): "If you want to attract, you have to become attractive. How do
you show up? Do you show up with energy?" — every event, however tired. "Do you radiate confidence? Not ego."
"Do you communicate with clarity and conviction? Do you share your knowledge and experience freely?" "Would you
follow yourself?" — "strong people follow strong people." The run-of-show is built to let the member be that
person: an energetic open, a clear promise, nothing withheld, a confident close.

## 7. The close without selling (`/74`, `/75`, `/76`)

"Closing the event without selling — plant the seed for partnership." Never "and by the way, come join us over at
[brokerage]" (`/74`). The transition is warm and the room already knows it earned it:

- **Live (adapted from `/74`):** *"If you liked what I shared today — that's just the tip of the iceberg. It's
  enough to get started, but there's a lot more below the surface: the exact how-to strategies, the mentorship,
  the systems behind this. I'm happy to share how you can get access to all of it for free and get my help to
  actually execute on it. Come up and talk to us, and let's have a conversation."* Mike's framing line before it:
  "you're the average of the five people you surround yourself with — you're a product of your environment — and
  this is the type of stuff we share every week on our private mastermind calls" (`/74`).
- **Virtual (adapted from `/75`):** *"Everything we covered today is just the tip of the iceberg. If you'd like
  access to the systems, mentorship, and tactical playbooks that make this work at scale, I'm happy to show you
  how to get it all for free by partnering with me — book a call."*
- **Evergreen (adapted from `/76`):** *"What you learned today is just the tip of the iceberg. If you want the full
  system, mentorship, and community, that's exactly what you get when you partner with me."*

**Above the water:** the free strategies they just got. **Below the water:** "the systems, the mentorship,
community, tactical playbooks, accountability" (`/74`) — in the OS, the member's real Partner Offer from
`identity/offer.md` (what's included, the first 30 days), named plainly, never inflated. The invite is to a
**conversation** — a booked call (the Conversion plugin's booking page and show-up sequence take it from there)
or, in the room, "come talk to us." Compensation, splits, caps, stock, and rev share are never mentioned from the
front of the room; "the webinar does the positioning for you" (`/76`), the call does the explaining.

**Two CTAs, the OS way:** the conversation is the close; a free resource (the member's live lead magnet, the
slides, a checklist) is the second ask that grows the list (`/73`). Never a third.

## 8. Follow-up is the name of the game (`/75`, `/74`, `/73`)

"Follow-up is going to be the name of the game. With in-person events you build such a deeper relationship that
the follow-up is going to be much less — you still have to do it. It's much more important with virtual events,
because these are people that aren't in your local market, that are probably just hearing about you for the
first time. They're going to require more nurturing, more love: checking in, asking them what they thought of
the event, making sure they know they can book a call with you" (`/75`). "Get your agents to follow up with their
prospects, their guests" (`/74`, `/75`).

The OS shape (the sequences are the builder's — the vault gives the intent, not the copy):
- **Attended** — day 1 the recap, the resource, the replay (virtual), one personal line, the open door; day 3 one
  more usable thing; day 7 the warm invite in Mike's register ("if you'd like to chat about partnering with me and
  getting [the real things] for free, book a call — if not, that's okay," `/73`).
- **No-show** — day 1 "we missed you" with the replay or the notes, no guilt; day 3 the one takeaway; day 7 the
  replay window closing and the open door.
- **Hot** — the agents who asked a question, stayed, replied, or came up after: a personal message within 24 hours
  naming what they asked, and the invite to a conversation.
- **Cold** — into the weekly newsletter (`/73`); the next event is the next touch.
- **The member's agents' guests** — a share pack so each agent follows up with the people they invited.

After the sequence the relationship lives on the list — "stay top of mind until they're ready… position yourself
as the trusted guide they think of when they're ready to switch" (`/73`) — and in the pipeline for the named ones.

## 9. Partnerships as a reach channel (`/77`)

"Strategic partners is a bit of a ninja strategy because nobody does it, but we've attracted a ton of agents by
doing this." Lenders, mortgage brokers, title and escrow officers, inspectors, appraisers, coaches — "anybody who
consistently interacts with multiple agents across brokerages" — hear agents' frustrations first. For events the
ask is simple and selfless: give them the invite to pass along ("a free training on [topic], no brokerage talk"),
position it as helping agents succeed, and "thank and recognize them for every introduction." The Lead Magnet
plugin owns partner outreach (`lm-partnerships`); the Events plugin hands it the event.

---

## 10. The list is the asset (`/73`)

"Your email list is the most valuable asset you will have… the only audience you own." Platforms change,
channels get removed; "my email list is the size of my YouTube channel — my YouTube channel goes away, I rebuild
it." Every event grows the list (registration = opt-in), and the list is how the event keeps paying: "weekly
newsletters of value… share success stories of agents of your organization… provide leadership insights."
Nurture is value, not nudges: "my entire nurture strategy is value." The Events plugin never holds a subscriber —
the member's list tool does; the Brain holds counts.

## 11. The lifecycle phases (from Mike's workshop-ops skill — the operating system under every event)

"The workshop changes. The operating system doesn't." **System first. Automation second. People third.** If a
process happens more than once, it gets a system; more than twice, automation; a repeated decision, a documented
SOP. Nothing relies on memory. Every recommendation names its phase.

| # | Phase | What it produces | `ev-` skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strategy | objective, audience, transformation, structure, positioning, format → outline, roadmap, success metrics, conversion goals | `ev-strategy` (format: `ev-navigator`) |
| 2 | Funnel build | registration, confirmation, thank-you pages → sections, conversion opportunities, journey gaps | `ev-registration` (→ `ds-funnel`) |
| 3 | GHL build | tags, pipelines, workflows, forms, calendars, trigger logic, email/SMS automations — **every workflow documented as Trigger → Action → Outcome** | `ev-followup`, `ev-evergreen`, `ev-registration` (the table in `shared/ghl-workflow-table.md`; the member builds it) |
| 4 | Event operations | launch and pre-launch checklists, team responsibilities, timelines, production schedules, host / moderator / coach instructions → dependencies, bottlenecks, risks, missing assets | `ev-live`, `ev-virtual`, `ev-runofshow` (host checklist) |
| 5 | Community experience | the room's design: prompts, interaction, wins, resources | `ev-runofshow`, `ev-live` (the conversation design) |
| 6 | Asset management | for every asset: Purpose · Owner · Delivery method · Distribution timing · Related email · Related post | `ev-promo` (the asset table) |
| 7 | Email & SMS systems | confirmation, reminder, attendance, recap, follow-up sequences — each with objective, trigger, timing, CTA, success metric | `ev-registration` (confirmation + reminders), `ev-followup` (recap + sequences) |
| 8 | Sales & conversion | positioning, pathways, booking, call workflows, follow-up — the pipeline (§13) | `ev-runofshow` (the close), `ev-followup` (the pipeline) → the Conversion plugin |
| 9 | Documentation | SOPs, checklists, process maps, delegation instructions — "assume hand-off to a team member" | every playbook doc; the host checklist |
| 10 | Continuous improvement | debriefs and audits: what worked, failed, can be automated, delegated, removed | `ev-analytics` |

**The Trigger → Action → Outcome rule.** Every automation the member will build in GoHighLevel (or any tool)
is written as a row — `| Trigger | Filter / Condition | Action | Outcome |` — e.g. `Form submitted | tag =
[code]-registered | send confirmation email | participant confirmed`. The plugin never builds the workflow; it
writes the table the member (or their VA) builds from. One table per sequence, in `shared/ghl-workflow-table.md`'s
shape.

**The delegation principle.** "Every system must be operable by someone who wasn't in the room. If it can't be
handed over with a checklist, document it more." Every checklist in this plugin is written for the member's VA or
a leader in their organization, not only for the member.

**The response shape** (when a skill reviews an existing event system): Objective · Current state · Recommended
system · Automation opportunities · Documentation required · Risks and bottlenecks · Next steps (prioritized,
assigned).

## 12. The event ledger — what the Brain holds per event

One block per event in `memory/events.md` (the locked shape is in `brain-contract.md`): the code, format, theme,
audience, dates, the people sharing it, and the **counts** — registrations · attended · show rate · engaged ·
conversations · calls booked · joins — plus the follow-up status, the stage moves requested, and the debrief.
**Counts only.** Attendees' names and emails never enter the Brain; they live in the member's list tool and CRM.
A named agent the member chooses to pursue becomes a Top-50 row through the Brain's capture skill with
`Source: event`, and from there the pipeline knows them.

## 13. The pipeline mapping (the seed's event pipeline → the OS's locked stages)

Mike's workshop-ops pipeline is `Registered → Attended → Engaged → Pitched → Booked → Enrolled → Retained`. The OS
has one pipeline vocabulary, locked for every plugin: `Identified → Conversation → Call booked → Call held →
3-way → Joined → Onboarded → Active · Parked`. The event words are **ledger counts and CRM tags**; the OS words are
**the stage of a named agent**. The mapping, for the agents the member names:

| Event state (counts / tags) | Means | OS stage (the named agent) |
|---|---|---|
| registered | raised a hand; on the list | `Identified` (when the member names them; Source: event) |
| attended | showed up | `Identified` — a follow-up touch is the next move |
| no-show | registered, didn't attend | wherever they already were — **never a move backwards**; the no-show sequence is the next move |
| engaged | asked a question, replied, DM'd, stayed for networking, came up after | `Conversation` |
| pitched | the invite to a conversation was made to them personally (in the room, by DM, in the follow-up) | `Conversation` (still — the invite is not a booking) |
| booked | booked the call | `Call booked` (then `Call held` · `3-way` as the Conversion plugin logs them) |
| enrolled | joined | `Joined` (then the Admin's `Onboarded` · `Active`) |
| (the seed's `lost`) | not a fit, or not now | `Parked` — the member's call, never the plugin's; fit or timing, never disrespect |

The seed's `vip` state (a paid tier) is not used: attraction events are free. The Events plugin requests every
move through the locked request shapes (`brain-contract.md`); the AI Admin applies them; before the Admin is
installed the plugin writes `memory/pipeline.md` directly in the same words.

## 14. The tag convention (the member's own GoHighLevel or CRM)

From the seed, adapted to the member's own code:

`[member-code]-[ws|vt|ew]-[nn]-[yyyy-mm]-[stage]` — lowercase, hyphens only · `member-code` = 2–5 letters set
once (`config.md → Member code`) · event type `ws` live workshop/event, `vt` virtual training, `ew` evergreen
webinar · `nn` zero-padded and never reused · `yyyy-mm` always present (reuse the same training next year without
collisions; for evergreen, the month it went live) · **never a tag without a stage.**

Stages in tags: `registered → attended → no-show → engaged → pitched → booked → enrolled → parked`.
Example: `tb-vt-01-2026-12-registered`, `tb-vt-01-2026-12-booked`. The event code without the date-stage suffix
(`tb-vt-01`) is the block heading in `memory/events.md` and the folder name in the workspace.

## 15. Compliance at events (the rules that bite here — `compliance-doctrine.md` applies in full)

- **No income claims on registration pages, in promo, or on slides** — no "six figures," no rev-share earnings,
  no "what you'd make," no lifestyle-as-income. Mike never shows his revenue share on an agent call
  (`02-prospect-targeting/21`); a stage is more public than a call.
- **Compensation stays off the stage and off the screen.** Splits, caps, stock, tiers: private call only. The
  close names mentorship, systems, community — never money.
- **The two cardinal rules on stage and from every speaker** (`03-model-positioning/13`): never a bad word about
  another brokerage or person. Every co-host and speaker is briefed on them in writing before the event.
- **Brokerage-neutral means brokerage-neutral.** The event is not branded to the brokerage; the brokerage name and
  logo appear only where `identity/compliance.md` says the local board or the brokerage requires them (a
  registration-page footer, a slide's compliance strip).
- **Recruiting scope.** A virtual event reaches agents outside the states or provinces the member may attract in
  (`compliance.md → Recruiting scope`). The teaching is for everyone; the invite to partner is scoped
  ("if you're licensed in [where]") or routed to the upline where the member may not attract.
- **The Meta Employment special-ad-category note** on anything that becomes a paid ad to attract licensed agents —
  stated in every ad hand-off; the brokerage's own ad policy applies on top.
- **Testimonials and photos**: an agent's win told from the stage, a quote on a slide, or a photo in the promo
  needs consent on file (`compliance.md → Claims to avoid`); the networking-hour photos the member posts are
  theirs to post, the people in them are not quoted.
- **AI-likeness disclosure** if a clone or AI voice appears in the promo video or the webinar.
- **The three-state gate first, always**: `identity/compliance.md`'s first line `Status:` — unset blocks every
  public piece (promo, registration page, slides, follow-up drafts) with a plain message; the private pieces
  (the brief, the run-of-show outline, the checklists, the analytics) still run.

## 17. What is thin or missing in the vault (say so; never invent)

- **Run-of-show timings.** The vault gives the beats (training → Q&A → light transition → networking) and the
  Austin example (~200 people, a networking hour after, a private after-party for the organization's agents —
  `/74`), not minute counts. The 60-minute virtual and 90-minute-plus-networking live shapes in this plugin are
  the builder's defaults, labeled as such.
- **Registration-page copy and the follow-up sequences.** The vault gives the intent ("check in, ask what they
  thought, make sure they know they can book a call" — `/75`) and the CTA register (`/73`); the copy shapes are
  the builder's, written in the member's voice.
- **The Virtual Workshop Launch Kit** (cohort bonus asset: "workshop launch checklist, promo calendar, registration
  page and slide templates, run-of-show script, follow-up sequences") is not yet produced by Team Mike. The
  reference file in `ev-virtual` is the builder's skeleton of it, to be replaced when the kit lands.
- **Benchmarks.** No show-rate, registration, or conversion benchmark exists in the vault. The plugin never quotes
  one; every verdict compares the member to their own last event.
- **Evergreen tooling.** Mike names what he uses (Zoom or Loom to record, a webinar platform to host, GoHighLevel
  for the integrations — `/76`) and says he pays someone to build it. Those are his choices, not requirements.
- **The `/launch-event` spec** (launching doc) lists outputs only — strategy, theme + positioning, promotion
  (emails, DMs, texts, posts, stories), registration (landing-page copy), presentation (outline + a Claude Design
  deck), follow-up (attended, no-show, hot lead, cold lead). It is the plugin's output list; the method is the
  vault's.
- **Paid events, VIP tiers, challenges, and bootcamps** (the seed's `ch` / `bt` types) are outside the attraction
  doctrine — the attraction event is free. A member who runs a paid program is outside this plugin's scope; say so.
