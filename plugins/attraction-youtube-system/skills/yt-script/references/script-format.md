# Script Format — the breakdown + the full script + the first Short (layout, conventions, the two CTAs)

**Applies the attraction doctrine** (`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/attraction-youtube-doctrine.md`): §8 the
structure, §9 the title and thumbnail promise the hook must match, §10 the two warm CTAs — in the shape of the CTA
law (the rewire brief, 2026-11-14), §15 what never goes public. The steps per format live in
`format-playbooks.md`; this file is how the script looks on the page.

## Keep it tight (not a 10-page essay)
- **~140 spoken words per minute. 8 to 12 minutes, every format (Mike, 2026-11-23) ≈ 1,100–1,700 words.** A
  Model Breakdown sits at the top of the range, never past it. An interview gets no script.
- As long as it needs to be for the viewer to get the outcome, as short as it can be without filler (`/94`).
- Scannable for a teleprompter: short sentences, clear sections, generous spacing, bullets for lists; easy to
  *speak*, never a stiff essay. Mike records from bullets; the hook is the only part he writes word for word — so
  every video ships in BOTH forms, in one document: **THE BREAKDOWN** (the hook verbatim, the sections as bullets)
  first, then **THE SCRIPT** (the full teleprompter script). Never one without the other; never a summary in place
  of the script.

## The breakdown (form a — the first page of the document)
```
TITLE: {final title}
THE BREAKDOWN — film from this page if you like working from bullets. The full script follows.

YOUR OPENING — say it word for word:
"{the scripted hook, verbatim — the same words as the script's YOUR OPENING band}"
THE GUIDE ASK (inside minute 1): "{the kit's spoken resource line}"
•  {SECTION 1 label} — {what it covers} · {the point} · {the move}
•  {SECTION 2 label} — …          (3–5 bullets, one a section, in the script's order)
THE CALL ASK (~a third in): "{the kit's spoken call line in the law's shape}"
PAYOFF: {one line — the promise delivered, the one thing to do this week}
NEXT VIDEO: {the next video, named}
```

## Verified facts only (never false on camera)
- Only facts with a source — and the source `(title · outlet · date · link)` goes in the footer, never inside a
  spoken line. A brokerage fact cites the member's materials or the brokerage's own page, dated. An agent's result
  cites `proof.md` with consent — no such row, no such line.
- Anything unverifiable is left out of the script — never a guess, never a flag for the member to check. Ranges
  and principles outrank invented precision.
- **Never a compensation figure, rev-share tier, stock number, or income claim** — the call carries numbers.

## Teleprompter conventions
The one filming line, on every script (Mike, 2026-11-22: name the app): *"Film on your phone in Instagram's free Edits app — it has a teleprompter built in: paste the script, press record, read."*
- Spoken words the way the member talks — contractions, their rhythm, their signature phrases (`voice-print.md`).
- `[brackets]` = delivery and extra-footage notes (not spoken). `>>` = on-screen text (not spoken). A source is
  never inside a spoken line — it goes in the footer. The legend under the title says this to the member in plain
  words, on every script.
- Never printed in a script: "hook" (the band is YOUR OPENING), "b-roll" (say "extra footage"), a bucket, a lane,
  a format name, or the word "member".
- Full sentences for parts read verbatim (the opening, the two asks, the close); bullets for list content.
- Pop-up rules (the kit's): six words or fewer, two lines at most, three seconds on screen; never over the face;
  the name card in the first three seconds; the stamp on every end card.

## The two CTAs — the law's shape, never the brokerage (doctrine §10, the rewire brief 2026-11-14)
1. **The guide, inside minute one:** the Week 2 guide, by its title — "comment GUIDE and I'll send it" or "link in
   the description or the pinned comment." The guide is the whole method; the video teaches one step of it.
2. **The call, a third to halfway in:** the person · their help · free · to run the method. Join register
   (`identity/offer.md → Join-gated package allowed: yes` or `unknown`): *"If you want my help running this — the
   lessons, the templates, the community — partner with me and it's free. Grab [the call]."* Open register (`no`):
   *"I'll walk you through it, free, no strings."* Mike's own phrasings in doctrine §10 ("all of my training,
   coaching, mentorship…") are adapted into the program's scalable assets — the recorded lessons, the templates,
   the community, the weekly group call only when the member already runs one — never a promise of the member's
   time per partner. The brokerage name raises the guard (`/94`) — never "if you want to join [brokerage], click
   below." Compensation never. Nothing recorded in the offer's `## Value stack` (its `Status:` reads `seeds`) →
   the call line names no lessons, templates or community: *"If you want my help with this, it's free."*
Both lines come from the Week 3 Kit's chapter 7 CALLS TO ACTION table (the newest `exports/week3-kit-*.txt`),
verbatim; `identity/channel.md`'s CTA line mirrors them. A light reminder at the close is fine; never a third
pitch. Both feel like invitations, which is why they work.

## Footer (every script)
```
────────────────────────────────────────────
Story used: {its name — or "none: built on your own experience"}.   Facts checked: {claim} — {title · outlet · date · link}.
Your brokerage's wording goes in the description, not in what you say.
```
The compliance check itself — cardinal rules · no compensation figures · former brokerage unnamed · nothing
promises the member's time per partner — is run before saving (`yt-script` Step 6) and is never printed. Compliance
`unset` → the one stamp line under the title, "hold until your compliance basics are set".

## Voice guardrails
Mirror `voice.md` + `voice-print.md`. No hype, no pressure, no clickbait unless the voice genuinely calls for
energy. Honor every hard-avoid, every compliance rule, and the verified-facts rule. The script must never make
the member say something false, salesy, or unlike themselves — and never a line they could not keep for ten
partners at once.
