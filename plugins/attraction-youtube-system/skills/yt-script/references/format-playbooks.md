# Format Playbooks — the four attraction formats + the interview intro and outro

Each playbook fixes the beats, the length, the hook formulas that fit (doctrine §9, numbered 1–7), what to pull
from the Brain, and the lines that never appear. The page layout is `script-format.md`; the structure every
format sits inside is doctrine §8 (hook → resource CTA → value → call CTA → payoff → next video). The vault does
not name these four formats — the cohort plan does; their content is grounded in `08-youtube/92, 93, 94, 96`
(doctrine §12, §17).

---

## 1. Why I Switched  (bucket: Situation · pillar in the log: Authority, Story angle · 8–12 min)
*The video that brought Mike his first 30 agents in 30 days (`/92`). The member's own move, told as the wall
they hit — never as the company they left.*
- **Pull:** `journey.md` (the three beats: where they started · the hardest stretch · the turning point; the
  `## Why join me` block if Week 2 wrote it) · `story-bank.md` (the moment they almost quit, the first win after)
  · `proof.md` (what changed, with real lines) · `offer.md` (what they now give agents) · `avatars.md` (who this
  is for — the agent standing where they stood).
- **Beats:** the wall (the specific frustration — one of the five pains, in their words) → what they went looking
  for (support, a path past "sell more houses," modern systems, community) → the decision and the fear around it
  → what actually changed since (habits, support, results they can state honestly) → what they would tell
  themselves a year earlier → the invite.
- **Hooks that fit:** formula 3 ("the truth about changing brokerages no one tells you"), formula 7 ("if you're
  [situation], here's what I did"), or the straight story hook ("Two years ago I almost quit real estate. Here's
  what I changed instead.").
- **Never:** the former brokerage's name ("a franchise," "an independent," "a team"); a negative word about it or
  anyone there; compensation figures, rev-share, stock, or income as the reason; the new brokerage as the pitch
  (it appears where `compliance.md` requires, in the description). The reason is the problem solved, not the
  company. Re-title yearly so it stays current (`/92`).
- **Close:** the call CTA is the natural end of the story — "if any of this sounds like where you are, I'd love
  to hear your story — book a private call, link below."

## 2. Pain Point Series  (bucket: Problem or Situation · Authority · 10–15 min)
*One pain, one avatar, one tactical answer they can use today. "Every challenge an agent names is a video" (`/94`).*
- **Pull:** `avatars.md` (the pain in their words; what they've tried) · `objections.md` (the question this
  answers; the hidden fear) · `offer.md` and `strategy.md` (the member's actual method) · `story-bank.md` (a
  story that proves the point) · `proof.md` (an agent who did it, with consent).
- **Beats:** name the pain and the agent who has it → why it keeps happening (the honest diagnosis, no blame)
  → the 3–5 moves, each with what · why · how → the story or the agent's win that shows it works → the one thing
  to do this week → the invite for help applying it.
- **Hooks that fit:** formula 1 ("why most agents can't… and how to be the exception"), formula 2 ("the N
  mistakes keeping agents stuck at…"), formula 7 ("N signs you've outgrown…", "if you're a struggling agent, do
  this for the next 90 days").
- **Give enough to get started, not the whole system** (`/91`) — the full system is what partnering gets you;
  say so warmly in the call CTA.
- **Never:** a brokerage or person blamed for the pain; income promises; a protected characteristic in who it's for.

## 3. Model Breakdown  (bucket: Model · Perspective · 12–20 min)
*Pull back the curtain, simplify what others overcomplicate, answer what they're already researching (`/96`,
`/93`). Requires `brokerage-model.md`; empty → route to `attraction-brokerage-model` first.*
- **Pull:** `brokerage-model.md` (mechanics as the member's materials state them, dated) · the Brain plugin's
  `shared/brokerage-models.md` · `memory/intel.md` (dated changes) · `objections.md` (the myths and fears agents
  raise) · `positioning.md` (positioned, not pitched) · `offer.md` (the value proposition that fills the gaps).
- **Beats (Mike's structure, `/96`):** overview and positioning (brief history, who it's built for) → how
  compensation is *structured* (the categories — splits, caps, fees — as concepts, **no figures**) → tools and
  technology (what's included; the gaps — and how the member's value proposition fills them) → support and
  training → opportunities beyond closings (rev share, equity, leadership — the concepts, what they are for, **no
  numbers**) → culture and community (what it actually feels like) → fit: who this is for, who it is not for →
  the invite to a call "where I walk you through the actual numbers for your situation."
- **Variants:** "[Model] explained ([year])" · one component explained (co-sponsorship, how rev share actually
  works — the concept) · myths and misconceptions · fit by avatar (new agents, team leaders, broker-owners) ·
  "how to choose a sponsor / before choosing a sponsor, ask these questions" · **"Do NOT join [brokerage] if…"**
  (Mike's best performer — about fit, honest, never a competitor named) · the yearly "N months in" review.
- **Hooks that fit:** formula 6 ("… explained," "should you join…," "how … actually works"), formula 7 ("do NOT
  join … if…"), formula 3 ("the truth about … no one tells you").
- **Comparisons ("[brokerage] vs [brokerage]")** — only on the member's explicit choice, built by
  `yt-model-breakdown` with Mike's warning repeated (`/96`: he took his own down; not recommended); facts only,
  dated and sourced, strengths and weaknesses of each model stated fairly, no negative word about a brokerage,
  sponsor, or person; compliance-gated.
- **Never:** splits, caps, fees, tiers, percentages, stock amounts, income or projections in the script; "#1 /
  fastest-growing" without a dated source; any disparagement; the brokerage name as the CTA. The disclosure block
  goes in the description (`yt-seo`). Remake or re-title yearly (`/96`).

## 4. Niche Breakdown  (bucket: Problem or Future · Authority · 10–20 min)
*The deep teach on the thing the member is known for — "cast a wide net around your niche so whatever they
search, they find you" (`/93`). The Future bucket when it points at leverage and the next stage.*
- **Pull:** `strategy.md` (known for) · `offer.md` (the method, the systems) · `proof.md` (results from the
  method — the member's own, labeled as theirs, or an agent's with consent) · `story-bank.md` · `avatars.md`.
- **Beats:** the outcome and who it's for → the method, step by step (the exact system the member uses — enough
  to act on today) → the case study woven in ("this is legit": an agent who used it, or the member's own numbers
  stated honestly as production facts, never income promises) → the mistakes that break it → the next stage
  (Future: what this makes possible — leverage, a team, an organization — concepts only) → the invite to get the
  full system and support by partnering.
- **Hooks that fit:** formula 4 ("the top N things to … as a real estate agent"), formula 1, formula 5 as a
  self-case-study ("how I … from YouTube" — with the facts the member can state).
- **Never:** income figures as the promise; a competitor's method run down; a stat without a source.

## 5. Interview — intro and outro  (bucket: Interview · Proof · intro 45–75 s, outro 30–45 s)
*The guest is the star. The intro is recorded LAST — after the conversation — so it hooks the best things the
member now knows are coming (`/95`). `yt-interview` owns the guest list, the question map, consent, and the
distribution ask; this playbook writes only the two spoken pieces.*
- **Pull:** `memory/interview-pipeline.md` (the guest's transformation in their words, type, consent) ·
  `proof.md` · the recording notes (the two or three moments that landed).
- **Intro beats (recorded last):** the transformation as the title promises it ("How [guest] [outcome]
  [constraint]") → who this is for ("if you're [avatar situation], this one's for you") → two or three specific
  things coming ("the exact [method], what the first 30 days actually looked like, the mistake that almost
  stopped them") → edify the guest in one honest line (`03-model-positioning/17`: recognition is retention) →
  the resource CTA in one breath → "let's get into it."
- **Outro beats:** thank the guest and name the one thing to take from them → the **joint casual CTA** (`/95`):
  "if you want to know exactly how [guest] did this and get their support and mine for free, click the link in
  the description and book a private call" → the next video (another interview on the same pain, or the model
  explained) → the end screen.
- **Hooks that fit:** formula 5 only — the hook-and-transformation title; never "tell me your story."
- **Never:** interrupting or making it about the member (the interview itself — `/95`); results the guest did
  not state; an income figure; a guest's result without recorded consent; a negative word about the guest's
  former brokerage or anyone there.

## Lines every format carries
- The resource CTA inside minute one; the call CTA a third to halfway in; the next video last (doctrine §8).
- One story from the bank where it lands, stamped Used-where; one real proof line where honest.
- The compliance footer; the disclosure block in the description, not the script.
