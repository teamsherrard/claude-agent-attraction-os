# Copywriting Knowledge Base — how to write copy a licensed agent reads, believes, and acts on

This is the standard for every word this plugin writes — the guide (the lead magnet document), the opt-in
page, the delivery DM and email, the nurture sequence, the newsletter, the partner outreach, the bios, the
Google posts. Read it before writing copy. The goal is simple: copy that a real agent — someone weighing
their next move — reads, believes, and acts on, and that sounds like the member, not like a recruiter or an
AI. If a line fails one of the principles below, rewrite it. The first half of this file is about the
**document** (the guide an agent reads to the last page); the second half is about the **page** (the opt-in
funnel, section by section). The grammar both are written in — the bands, the `── PAGE N - TITLE ──` form,
and the labelled lines the design step turns into page elements — is `shared/output-standard.md` §5.

---

## The 10 principles (non-negotiable)

1. **One job, earned with value.** The page's primary job is the opt-in — and it EARNS it by being genuinely
   useful (a real system, a real leader's experience, a real offer), never by pushing. **A thin, all-pitch
   page reads like a recruiting deck; a substantive one reads like a leader.** Every section above the
   Book-a-call area moves toward the guide by adding value, not hype; the Book-a-call area near the bottom is
   the secondary path for the agent who is already ready, and the thank-you page offers the call again after
   the download. The guide has one job too: be worth the email, and make the reader want the conversation.
2. **Outcomes beat features** (`04-value-proposition/32`). "Agents don't buy features; they buy outcomes."
   Not "weekly mastermind calls" → "every week you learn one thing from someone doing it right now, and you
   use it that week." Not "a CRM and training" → "you know exactly what to do on Monday." The formula: name
   the pain → show the feature → translate it to the benefit → paint the outcome. Always connect back to
   growth, freedom, and security.
3. **Clarity beats cleverness.** In 5 seconds the visitor must know: what this is, who it's for, and what they
   get. **Fifth-grade reading level** — Mike's rule for everything his team writes (`/32`). No industry jargon.
4. **Specific beats vague.** "Support" is noise; "a 30-minute call with me in your first week, then a group
   that answers at 9pm" is believable. Real routines, real steps, the honest trade-off, the exact question to
   ask a sponsor. A number carries its source or it isn't written.
5. **"You" beats "I."** Talk about the agent's situation, pains, and goals. The page is about *them* — the
   exceptions are the Who I Am and Journey sections and the guide's opening page, and even there, frame
   everything as "here's why that matters for you."
6. **Proof everywhere — real only.** Agents trust evidence, not claims. Weave in real agents helped, the
   organization as it is, and testimonials from `identity/proof.md` (consent on file). One specific result
   beats ten adjectives. Proof from people *like them* (camera-shy, small market, three brokerages in) lands
   hardest (`/32`). If proof is thin, the upline's proof labeled as the upline's; if none, honest experience,
   said plainly — never padded.
7. **Reduce friction.** The opt-in pop-up asks for exactly three things (first name, email, phone — nothing
   more; never "brokerage" or "production"; the ONE optional qualifier only when the member switches it on,
   and fewer fields win), and the guide is an instant download on the thank-you page. Reassure: free,
   instant, private, unsubscribe anytime. Every extra field or ounce of doubt costs opt-ins.
8. **Sound like the member.** Match `identity/voice.md` + `identity/voice-samples.md` exactly; anything
   spoken on camera follows `voice-print.md`. If their voice is calm and plain, the copy is calm and plain —
   never guru-speak. Voice mismatch kills trust faster with agents than with anyone; they've heard every pitch.
9. **Honest persuasion only.** No fake scarcity, no countdown, no hype, no guarantees, no manufactured
   urgency, no income talk. Confidence comes from specificity and proof, not exclamation marks. (This also
   keeps it compliant — house rules #5.)
10. **Attraction, not recruiting.** Agents follow people, not companies (`03-model-positioning/17`). The copy
    leads with the member's story, skills, and support — never the brokerage's brand, never the model's
    numbers. Nobody on this page is "recruited"; they read something honest and decide to talk.

## The reader is an agent weighing a move — write for it

Changing brokerages is one of the few business decisions an agent makes alone, with a pile of half-pitches and
no neutral source. So:
- **Trust is the whole game.** Agents opt in with a leader they believe is competent *and* honest about the
  trade-offs. Lead with credibility and candour, not salesiness.
- **Honesty is the differentiator.** The leader who says the real thing — the honest page, the real routine,
  the mistake they made themselves, and, when the magnet compares models, the downsides of their own — is the
  one the agent calls. That's the un-fakeable value no recruiting deck has.
- **Speak to the real pains — Mike's five (`02-prospect-targeting/18`, `04-value-proposition/27`):** financial
  uncertainty (no reliable next client) · lack of support, mentorship, training (the brokerage explains forms,
  not how to get clients) · technology gaps (paying for tools and leads that don't move the needle) · limited
  growth (no path past "sell more houses") · work-life balance and recognition (the hamster wheel, doing it
  alone). Name the ONE that fits this type of agent (`avatars.md` → "biggest problem, in their words"), then
  position the magnet (and the member) as the calm, honest way through it.
- **Name the fear under the pain.** New agents fear being just a number and choosing wrong with no
  information. Experienced agents fear another switch that changes nothing — "skeptical of failed promises,"
  three to five brokerages in (`/22`). Top producers fear disrupting momentum. Team leaders fear losing the
  team's feel. Say it in their words; they'll feel understood before anyone offers anything.
- **Lower the stakes of the opt-in.** They're not joining anything, not booking anything, and nobody will
  know — they're grabbing a guide to read before they decide. Make that feel like the obvious no-risk step.
- **Privacy is a feature.** "Will my broker find out?" is a real question. Answer it before they ask.

# PART ONE — THE DOCUMENT (the guide an agent reads to the last page)

The guide is the product. The page only promises it; the guide has to pay the promise off, page by page, in
the member's own voice, and leave the reader wanting the conversation without ever being sold. Every shape
(a system guide, a checklist, a plan or template, a questions guide, a fair comparison guide, a mini-course)
follows the same architecture — the cover, the opening page, four to eight body pages, the "what to do next"
page, the "how [first name] helps next" page, the back page — with word floors per page. The architecture and
the floors are in `skills/lm-magnet/references/magnet-guide.md`; the gate is in `lm-magnet`. This file is
how to write each part so it's genuinely good.

## Hooks for a guide (the cover's three lines — they earn the read)

The cover carries the title (the promise in the reader's words), the subtitle (who it's for and the result),
the byline, and a **hook of three lines at most**. The hook is the first thing a stranger reads after the
title; it decides whether they turn the page. Four kinds work, and every one is specific and lived:
- **The lived line** — one real moment from the member's own experience. *"I ran forty open houses before I
  noticed the sign-in sheet was the whole business."* (Former brokerages unnamed; the number only if the
  member stated it.)
- **The specific number, with its source** — *"Three buyers a month from one Sunday routine — my numbers from
  last spring, not a projection."* Only from `proof.md`; the source rides on the line or in the back-page
  "Source:" line; never an income figure.
- **The uncomfortable truth, said kindly** — *"Most agents don't have a lead problem. They have a Sunday
  problem."* Kind means it's about the work, never about a person or a brokerage.
- **The question the reader is already asking** — *"What do I actually do on Monday?"* — in their words, from
  `avatars.md` ("biggest problem, in their words") or `objections.md`.

**What fails:** *"In this guide you will learn…"* · *"Are you tired of…?"* with nothing lived behind it · a
line that could open any sponsor's guide · a hype word · a number with no source · a promise the pages don't
pay off · anything about compensation.

**The hook test (also the funnel headline's test):** cover the member's name — does the line still only make
sense coming from someone who did this? Does it name a result or a moment in the reader's words? Could you
point to the page in the guide that pays it off? Three yeses or rewrite.

## The opening page (page 1 — the context, 180 to 280 words)

Why this exists, in the member's voice. The beats, in order: **the moment** they were where the reader is
(the mirror — `04-value-proposition/27`: you are most qualified to help the person you used to be) · **what
they tried** that didn't work (the lead bill, the course, the switch that changed only the logo — a franchise,
an independent, never a name) · **what changed** (the thing they built or decided, and what it produced —
proof only as `proof.md` states it, consented, with its source) · **what the reader will have by the last
page** (the system in hand, the sheet, the questions — concrete). One real proof line if `proof.md` holds a
consented one, otherwise none — never a vague "I've helped a lot of agents." It ends with **How to use this:**
two lines — how long it takes, what to do with it (read it once, then run page N this week; screenshot the
page marked for it). No "in this guide you will learn," no table of contents, no welcome.

## The body page (one idea per page, 180 to 350 words)

Every body page carries ONE idea and follows the same beat:
1. **A plain, specific title** — a result or a step in the reader's words (*"The two-question sign-in sheet"*,
   *"What to send on Sunday night"*), never "Chapter 2" or "Step two of five."
2. **The idea in a sentence** — the one thing this page is for. It's usually the page's `Pull quote:`.
3. **The how** — the steps in order, the script word for word (`Script:`), the template rows (`Template
   rows:`), the sheet, the checklist (`Checklist:`). Handed over in full — never "download the template
   later," never "I'll show you on a call" for something a page can carry.
4. **A real example from the member's own routine** — what it looked like on a real Sunday, a real call, a
   real week; the mistake most agents make at this step and how the member made it first.
5. **The action** — `Next step:` the one thing the reader does today, small enough to do today.

**The page test:** every body page contains at least one of — a number with its `Source:`, a `Script:`,
`Template rows:`, a `Checklist:`, or a true story (the member's own, or a consented agent's). **A page that is
only prose is a failed page.** Numbers: the member's own experience needs no source line; anything else does
("(my brokerage's [document], [Month YYYY])" · "(per Mike Sherrard's lesson)" · "([source], [Month YYYY])");
no source → say it qualitatively or leave it out; never a dollar figure, split, cap, stock value, or earnings
number anywhere (house rules #5).

## Transitions between pages (the guide has to move)

The last line of a page hands to the next; the first line of the next page takes the hand. *"That's the
sheet. The next page is what you do with the names on it."* → *"You've got nine names and a Sunday night.
Here's the text, word for word."* Never "In this section," "Now that we've covered X, let's look at Y," "As
mentioned above," or a recap. A page never ends on a bullet; it ends on the `Next step:` (and, on one page
only, the mid-document `Call to action:`).

## The "what to do next" page (120 to 200 words)

The page the reader screenshots: **the whole system in five lines** (`Checklist:` — exactly five, one per
step or page, in order, each a plain instruction) · **the first step this week** (`Next step:` — the smallest
version that runs this week, as an activity: calls, doors, conversations, a sheet printed — never income) ·
**the second call to action** (`Call to action:`), in the member's voice, pointing at the last page for the
link. It reads like a leader handing you a card, not like a summary.

## The ending that earns the call ("how [first name] helps next", 120 to 200 words)

The soft close. It makes the reader want the call without being sold, because it's honest about what a guide
can't do. In order: **what they now have** (the system, in one line) · **the one thing a guide can't do** (look
at their numbers, their market, their week — "that's a conversation") · **what the member actually gives
agents who partner with them** — outcomes only, only what's in `offer.md` (the weekly call and what happens on
it, the first week, the template handed over, the group) · **the brokerage line** ("and everything
[Brokerage] provides — I walk you through that on a call") · **one real proof point** (`Proof:` — consented,
dated, or omitted) · **the third call to action with the booking link**, in Mike's register: *"If you'd like
to talk through where you're at, book a call — no pitch, I'll just answer your questions. If not, that's
okay."* Never urgency, never "join [brokerage]," never compensation, never a second link.

## The three calls to action (exactly three in every guide)

All three in the member's voice, all a conversation — book a call, or "grab 20 minutes with me" — never
compensation, never "join my brokerage," never urgency:
1. **The soft mid-document line** — on ONE body page only, the page that most needs a walkthrough (the
   numbers page, the template page, the step most agents get wrong): *"If you want me to walk you through
   this on your own numbers, grab 20 minutes with me — the link's on the last page."* No link here.
2. **The "what to do next" page's line** — the first step is theirs, the second is a conversation if they
   want one: *"Run this once. Then, if you want a second set of eyes on your week, grab 20 minutes with me —
   last page."* No link here.
3. **The closing page's line, with the booking link** — the only link in the guide (the thank-you page
   carries the same one). The canonical shape is above; the words are the member's.
Written as `Call to action:` lines so the gate can count them: three, no more, no fewer.

## The design cues are part of the writing

The labelled lines (`Pull quote:` · `Callout:` · `Checklist:` · `Template rows:` · `Script:` · `Source:` ·
`Image idea:` · `Next step:` · `Call to action:`) are not decoration added after; they're how a page gets
built. The pull quote is the sentence a reader would screenshot. The callout is the aside the member would say
in person ("Don't skip the second question — it's the one that tells you who's actually moving"). The image
idea is one plain line a designer can act on (*"the sign-in sheet with the one question circled"*, *"a phone
showing the three texts"*, *"the weekly review template, half filled in"*) — never a stock-photo brief. A label
with nothing true to say is left out. A label never carries a line in capitals (the renderer would make a
heading of it) and never a bracket token.

## The tests every page passes (run them; don't admire them)

- **The worth-an-email test.** Would a sceptical agent trade their real email for this one page, on its own?
  If the page only sets up another page, it fails.
- **The would-they-pay test.** Would an agent pay twenty dollars for this page? If not, it isn't worth giving
  away. (Information is free; the member's lived specifics are what make it worth paying for —
  `04-value-proposition/31`.)
- **The specificity test.** Point to the number with its source, the script, the template, the checklist, or
  the story on this page. Can't point? Rewrite.
- **The swap test.** Swap the member's name for another leader's at another brokerage. If the page still
  works, it's generic — bring in their routine, their mistake, their words. (The design step's any-leader test
  is the same test on the look; the words have to pass it first.)
- **The delete test.** Cut any line that could go without losing information. Then cut the line that explains
  the line.
- **The voice test.** Read it beside `voice-samples.md`. If a sentence couldn't come out of the member's mouth
  on a call, rewrite it in their cadence.

### The banned filler list
Never: *in this guide you will learn* · *in today's market* · *it's no secret that* · *at the end of the day*
· *as a real estate professional* · *whether you're a new agent or a seasoned pro* · *take your business to
the next level* · *dive in / deep dive* · *navigate* (as a verb for life) · *elevate* · *empower* · *seamless*
· *robust* · *it's important to note* · *in conclusion* · *the bottom line is* · *I'm passionate about* ·
*world-class* · *proven system* (unless the proof follows on the same page) · *limited time* · *don't miss
out* · *stop scrolling* · *opportunity call* · *I'd love to share an opportunity* · the OS-wide banned words
(unlock, supercharge, game-changer, revolutionary, secret weapon, leverage as a verb). Also: *free coaching if
you join my brokerage* — partnering with the member is the frame; a brokerage-conditional inducement is not.

### The reading-level rule
Fifth grade (`/32`): sentences under fifteen words on average, one idea per sentence, no word a brand-new
agent would have to look up, numbers as digits, contractions welcome, the member's own expressions kept. If a
paragraph runs past four lines, split it. If a sentence needs a comma to breathe twice, it's two sentences.

### Demo mode
When the session is explicitly a fictional demo (the Brain's `config.md` says `Demo brain: yes`, or the member
framed a fictional member), every production, money, ratio or count figure in the guide and the page is tagged
"(illustrative — demo)" exactly as the Brain Book spec tags it, and never carries a fabricated source. The
document is complete at every floor — a demo shows the finished thing, never a thinner one.

# PART TWO — THE PAGE (the opt-in funnel, section by section)

Eleven sections in the page's order (the funnel writer's reference has the strategy and the floors): the
Hero with the video · the Problem · the Product · Who I Am · the Journey · the Offer · Why Join Me · Proof ·
the Organization · Book a Call · the Opt-in — then the pop-up, the thank-you page, the footer. The opt-in
button repeats top, middle and bottom with the same words; the Book-a-call area is the one place on the page
that asks for a conversation.

## The hero (90% of whether they stay)

Seven pieces, phone-length (most visitors tap in from a bio link, a Reel caption, or a video description on a
phone):
- **The pre-headline** — one line above the headline: a question or a fact aimed at the type of agent, in
  their words. *"Working 50-plus hours a week on a business that never builds anything you own?"* ·
  *"Two years in, one closing, and nobody at the office has sold a house since 2019?"* Never a scarcity claim,
  never a number without a source, never a protected characteristic.
- **The headline** — the magnet's core promise in one clear, benefit-led line, for a type of agent (by stage,
  never a protected class). It passes **the hook test** (above). Proven shapes (adapt to voice — never sound
  like a template):
  - **The System formula:** *"The exact [routine] that produced [result] — step by step."* → "The exact
    open-house routine that produced three buyers a month — step by step." (the member's own system — usually
    the strongest shape, house rules #11)
  - **The Honest formula:** *"The honest [thing] for [type of agent]."* → "The honest guide to your first 90
    days — what actually happens, from someone who ran them." (a fair comparison guide uses it too: "every
    model, every trade-off, including mine")
  - **The Questions formula:** *"The [N] questions to ask any [brokerage / sponsor] before you [decide]."*
  - **The Before formula:** *"Read this before you [switch / sign / pick a sponsor]."*
  - **The Without formula:** *"How to [outcome] without [the fear]."* → "How to choose your next brokerage
    without getting sold to."
  - **The Plan formula:** *"Your first [90 days] at [a new brokerage], mapped."*
- **The subhead** — one or two lines that expand the promise, name who it's for, and remove doubt. *"The five
  steps I run every Sunday, the sheet I hand new partners, and your first week mapped. Written by a working
  agent and leader, not a recruiting department."*
- **The video block** — the member's video sits in the hero: the 2-to-3-minute video sales letter when they
  have one (their story, who they help, what the guide gives — no pitch), else the 60-second why-join-me (the
  Brain's why-join-me skill writes that script). The doc's `Video:` line carries the link, or exactly `record
  the 60-second why-join-me`; with the record note the page shows the cut-out headshot in that spot and the
  hand-off tells the member how to get the script. Never autoplays with sound; never a rival CTA.
- **The attraction line** — one bold sentence under the video that tells them why to press play, in the
  reader's words: *"Two minutes on how I help agents like you get buyers from their own Saturdays."* It names
  the outcome, not the member's credentials.
- **The button** — the canonical phrase, nothing else: **"Get the Free Guide."**
- **The trust line** — one small line under the button: who wrote this and that it's free and private.
  *"Written by a working agent and leader. Free, instant, private."*

Headline rules: specific, benefit-led, no hype words ("INSANE," "secret," "you won't believe"), no clickbait,
readable in one breath, ~6–10 words. It must match the magnet exactly (house rules #7). "[Type of agent]" in
any formula is a career stage or production level, never a protected characteristic (house rules #5). No
brokerage named in the hero other than the member's own, where compliance requires it.

## The Problem — name the pain before you offer anything

Right after the hero, name the agent's pain so they feel understood. From `identity/avatars.md` ("their
biggest problem, in their words" + "what they've already tried that didn't work") and the guide's opening
page, take their **single most acute pain** and make it concrete and honest:
- **Name it in their words** — "you're comparing splits on a spreadsheet at 11pm and every sponsor you talk
  to says the same three things"; "you switched once already and nothing changed except the logo."
- **Agitate honestly** — the real cost (another year of the same, choosing on the one number that matters
  least, a sponsor who disappears after onboarding). Real stakes, never fake doom, never a named brokerage.
- **Turn the corner** — one line: there's a better way to decide, and this guide is it (bridges into the
  Product).
Keep it tight (3–5 lines). Empathy + stakes, not a pitch — it's what makes the rest of the page land.

## The Product — what's inside, page by page, shown with the mockup

This is where the email gets earned: make the free guide feel genuinely valuable AND show it. The guide's
mockup sits left or right (the `Image idea:` line names the side). From the magnet's actual pages:
- **"Here's exactly what you'll get" — one bullet per guide page**, each a real outcome ("the exact follow-up
  sheet I hand every new partner" · "your first week, mapped Monday to Sunday" · "the one mistake that costs
  most agents the deal"), never a vague tease. The bullets must match what the guide delivers (house rules #7).
- **One line on why it's worth more than free** — it's the real thing only this leader could have written.
- **Repeat the button** here (mid-page) — some readers are already sold.
Specificity + the visual is what makes an agent hand over an email.

## Who I Am — the member in three lines, then why partner

Agents don't hand their email to a stranger — and they've met a hundred sponsors. Three lines, each framed
for the reader: **what I do** (the one line from `positioning.md` / the UVP — *"I help agents in years two to
five build a pipeline that doesn't need a lead bill"*) · **who I help** (the type of agent, by stage, in their
words) · **one real credential** (from `proof.md` / `profile.md` — years, a production milestone the member
will SHOW, agents helped with consent, an award; the upline's labeled as the upline's; never invented). The
headshot sits beside it.

Then **"Why partner with [first name]"** — six to eight short credibility bullets, each one line, each true
and sourced in the Brain: years licensed · production, only as `proof.md` allows it and framed as what the
member will show, never what an agent will earn · what they teach (the Teach-first lessons) · who they've
helped (consent on every agent named) · the weekly call they run · the templates they hand over · the group
they built. Humble-confident and human; no income claim, no "top 1%" without a dated source, no brokerage
pitch. Tone: a leader stating facts, not a résumé.

## The Journey — the story, short and true

Three beats from `journey.md`, told in the member's voice, each one or two sentences: **where I was** (the
first year, what they had and didn't) · **what I tried** (the wall — the lead bill with no closings, the cap
that never moved, the switch that changed only the logo; "a franchise," "an independent," never a name) ·
**what changed** (what they built or decided, what it produced — proof only as `proof.md` states it). It's the
mirror principle (`04-value-proposition/27`): the reader should see their own present in the member's past.
One pull quote if a line earns it. Never a brokerage named, never a person spoken against, never compensation.

## The Offer — the Partner Program (outcomes, never compensation)

This is **WHAT they give** — distinct from Who I Am (WHO they are). Read `identity/offer.md`'s `## The
program` and `## The framework` blocks when they're written, the older `## The Partner Offer` block otherwise:
- **The program's name and type** (`Program:`) — a partnership program, a mentorship program, a coaching
  program, an accelerator, a course plus community, a team system — named as the offer names it.
- **What you get when you partner with me** — each line an outcome (principle 2): the onboarding, the recurring
  call, the templates, the community as it is today — real and small beats big and promised
  (`04-value-proposition/33`).
- **The framework in one line** — the member's signature method as a chain of steps (`## The framework` →
  "One line").
- **The first 30 days in one line** — what the partner does, what the member does, done when.
- **The brokerage line** — "and everything [Brokerage] provides — I walk you through all of that on a call."
  That's the whole of the model on this page. **No splits, caps, tiers, fees, stock, or rev-share numbers**
  (house rules #5). Compensation is explained on a private call, tailored (`02-prospect-targeting/19`).
- **"A sneak peek inside [the program name]"** — six to eight one-sentence bullets of what an agent gets, from
  the Partner Offer's deliverables table: outcomes, not features (*"a weekly call where you bring one deal and
  leave with the next step"*, not *"weekly mastermind"*); only what exists today or is honestly "built with my
  first partners."
- **Free vs paid, said straight** (`04-value-proposition/31`): what the member built that needs none of their
  time is free to agents who partner with them; anything that needs their time or a team is discounted, with
  the price said up front — hidden costs after joining are how sponsors get a bad name. One honest line if it
  applies. Never "free coaching if you join [brokerage]" — the frame is partnering with the member.

## Why Join Me — the statement

One statement in the member's voice (`Statement:`), 60 to 120 words: past → transformation → mission → value
→ vision (`04-value-proposition/34`). From the `## Why join me` block in `journey.md` when it's written (the
60-second version condensed, or the one-breath version opened up), otherwise written from the offer's "why
now" and the UVP. It says why the member does this and who it's for; the brokerage is the vehicle, the member
is the reason (`03-model-positioning/17`). No compensation, no "join [brokerage]," no urgency.

## Proof — let other agents do the convincing

A standalone proof section beats proof sprinkled in passing — it's the biggest trust lever on the page. From
`identity/proof.md` (real only — never invent; consent on file):
- **2–4 short results** — first name or initials + situation + what happened ("Was two years in with one
  closing. Six months after we started working together, four under contract — Priya, Houston").
- **Hard numbers** only as `proof.md` states them — agents helped, organization size, years — each with its date.
- **Upline proof, labeled** — "our group has…" — only if `proof.md` holds it.
- **None yet?** One plain line says so (*"I'm building this with my first partners — their results will be
  here when they're theirs to share"*). The section stays; it just gets smaller. Never pad.
- **The YouTube block** — only when the member's channel exists (`identity/channel.md`, or the handles in the
  Brain): two or three videos, each the link and one line on what it shows — proof they give value every week.
  No channel → no block, never a placeholder.
- **The proof photo strip** — 8–12 real photos (the weekly call, events, agents' wins, the community) named
  as an `Image idea:`; fewer than ~6 → skip the strip. Real people only; agents' faces need the agent's OK.
Tie it straight to the reader's pain: "agents who felt exactly what you're feeling — here's how it went."

## The Organization — what it's actually like inside

The section that flips the page from "recruiting" to "real," and the strongest answer to "every sponsor says
that." From `offer.md` (what's included), `operations.md` (the weekly call, onboarding steps), `proof.md`
(organization today):
- **What we do together** — the recurring call, the group chat, the training, recognition. Only what exists
  today. A weekly script role-play call is a real thing to say; "a world-class ecosystem" is not.
- **Who's in it** — the kinds of agents by stage ("mostly agents in years one to five"); the count only if
  `proof.md` states it, with its date; never "fastest-growing" without a dated source.
- **Why that matters to you** — Mike's fifth pain: not doing it alone. "Getting agents is easy; keeping them
  is where the game is won" — the culture is the retention argument, and agents can feel it on a page.
- **Real photos** — the member adds photos of the call, events, the community at the design step. Real people only.

## Book a Call — the Partner Call area (the secondary path, no pressure)

For the agent who is already ready. It sits after the Organization and before the final opt-in, and it never
competes with the guide above it. In the member's voice:
- **What the call is** — length, where, what to bring: *"Thirty minutes on Zoom, about your business, not
  mine. Bring your last three months."* **What it isn't** — *"Not a pitch. Not a presentation. If it's not a
  fit, I'll tell you and point you somewhere better."*
- **The three questions the call answers** — the three the reader is actually asking (`objections.md`,
  `avatars.md`), each one line.
- **How it works** — the three steps to join, in plain words: grab the guide or book the call → the
  20-minute (or 30-minute, as `operations.md` says) call → onboarding Day 1 (the welcome call, what's handed
  over, the first lesson, the group, the first date on the calendar — from the Partner Offer's Day 1 sequence).
- **What we'll cover on the call** — five or six bullets: your business and your week · how the training and
  the weekly call work · what onboarding looks like · your 90-day game plan · your questions about the model,
  answered on the call (never on a page) · what happens next if it's a fit. Compensation stays "on the call."
- **The booking embed** — the booking link from `operations.md` / `profile.md`, embedded when it embeds, else
  a button: **"Book a call with me"** (the Book's primary CTA) — the only button on the page that isn't the
  opt-in's. No countdown, no "spots left," no urgency of any kind.

## The opt-in & CTA — make grabbing the guide a no-brainer

The final section, then the pop-up, then the thank-you page (house rules #4). Write all three — plus the
**FAQ**: three one-line Q&As right before the button, dissolving the last objections an agent has at the ask:
- *"Is this a recruiting pitch?"* → No — it's the guide, built from what I actually do. If you ever want to
  talk, the call's above; no pressure either way.
- *"Will anyone know I downloaded this?"* → No — the list is private, nobody's contacted on your behalf, and
  you can unsubscribe from anything any time.
- *"I'm not planning to move — is this for me?"* → Then it's the thing to read before you ever decide. No
  commitment, just the honest picture. (Adapt the third line to the magnet's reader.)
One honest line each, in the member's voice. Objections dissolve best at the moment they occur — at the ask.

- **On the page:** name the magnet + a one-line promise, a **top-3 "what you'll get" recap** (the full stack
  is up in the Product section) — these MUST match what the guide delivers (house rules #7) — and the button.
- **The pop-up** (every opt-in button opens it): a one-line headline restating the promise, **the form —
  first name, email, phone. Nothing else** unless the member switched the one optional qualifier on, friction
  reducers right by the button, and the submit button.

**Pop-up micro-copy (the hardest moment on the page — write it, don't wing it):**
- **Labels:** `First name` · `Email` · `Phone`. Plain, no asterisks, no "required."
- **The optional qualifier** — off by default (fewer fields win). When the member wants one, exactly one:
  `years licensed`, or `where are you today: new · 1 to 3 years · 3 plus`. It's a dropdown, never a text box,
  never "brokerage," never "production." The doc's `Optional qualifier:` line switches it on; the design
  step's form carries it only then.
- **The contact line under Phone** — one honest sentence on what the member will actually do with it, built
  from `identity/operations.md`'s follow-up cadence: *"I'll text or call once to make sure you got it — no
  drip, no pressure."* Expectation set = trust earned; a phone field with no reason costs opt-ins.
- **Reassurance by the button:** *"Free. Instant. Private — nobody's contacted on your behalf. Unsubscribe anytime."*
- **Headline:** restate the promise, not the mechanics — *"Your free [guide, by name] — one step away."*
- **Button:** the canonical phrase, nothing else.
- **The thank-you page** (after submit): warm confirmation + **the direct link to the guide PDF as a big
  instant-download button right on the page** ("You're in — grab your guide below"), in the member's voice —
  then **the call, offered**: one warm, optional line and the booking link (*"Want to talk through where
  you're at? Book a call — no pitch, I'll just answer your questions."*), with the "What we'll cover" list
  repeated under it. **Instant download first, NEVER email delivery — no "check your inbox" copy; the
  download never depends on booking** (house rules #4).

**CTA button copy** — ONE phrase for the opt-in, everywhere: **"Get the Free Guide."** It's this plugin's
canonical CTA — the same words on the hero, the mid-page repeat, the final opt-in, and the pop-up submit; when
the member writes a link-in-bio button or a video/social CTA that points at this page, recommend the same
phrase — so an agent meets one consistent ask at every touchpoint. Don't improvise variants per page.
- ✅ "Get the Free Guide" — the ONLY label on any opt-in button, top, middle, and bottom.
  A guide-named variant ("the Sunday routine guide") is allowed ONLY in body copy — never on the button itself.
- ✅ "Book a call with me" — the ONLY other button on the page, in the Book-a-call area (and the thank-you
  page's call line). The member's own primary CTA phrase from the Brain wins over it when they set one.
- ❌ "Submit" · "Sign up" · "Download" · "Join" · "Apply" — any opt-in button text other than the canonical phrase.
- ❌ A call button anywhere above the Book-a-call area — the opt-in comes first, always.
- ❌ "Send me the guide" or any send/email framing — delivery is an **instant download on the page**.

## Flow & structure — the page must move

The sections answer the agent's questions in the order they ask them, each building toward the opt-in, with
the call offered once near the bottom:

```
HERO (+ video)   →  "What is this, is it for me — and who's that?"   (pre-headline + promise + the video + CTA)
THE PROBLEM      →  "Ugh, that's exactly my situation."              (name + agitate the pain)
THE PRODUCT      →  "Oh — and look how useful it is."                 (page-by-page outcomes + mockup + mid CTA)
WHO I AM         →  "Can I trust this person?"                       (three lines + why partner with them)
THE JOURNEY      →  "They've been where I am."                        (where I was → what I tried → what changed)
THE OFFER        →  "What do they actually give?"                     (the program as outcomes — no comp)
WHY JOIN ME      →  "Why do they do this?"                            (the statement)
PROOF            →  "Has it worked for agents like me?"               (real results + videos + the strip)
THE ORGANIZATION →  "What's it like inside?"                          (the call, the group, who's in it)
BOOK A CALL      →  "I'm ready to talk — what does that look like?"   (the call, how it works, the embed)
THE OPT-IN       →  "Okay — what do I get and how?"                   (the magnet + the FAQ + CTA)
THANK-YOU        →  "Got it. What now?"                               (download first, then the call, offered)
```
Momentum rules: short sentences, short paragraphs (1–3 lines), one idea per block, sub-headers and bullets so
it's skimmable, generous white space, the opt-in CTA repeated 3× with the same words, the call asked for once.
Nobody reads a wall of text on a page.

## Hard "don'ts"
Hype words and fake urgency or a countdown · clickbait that the magnet doesn't pay off · "me/we"-heavy bragging ·
vague benefits · industry jargon · walls of text · invented testimonials, stats, or organization counts ·
invented or empty socials or a placeholder video block · a "follow me" button that competes with the opt-in ·
splits, caps, tiers, fees, stock, rev-share, or income anywhere public · "best brokerage" / rankings / scored
tables · anything negative about a named brokerage, sponsor, or person · a former brokerage named in a story ·
targeting by a protected characteristic · a call button anywhere above the Book-a-call area · "free coaching
if you join [brokerage]" · "check your inbox" · a copy line in capitals · a bracket token · rendering a design.
