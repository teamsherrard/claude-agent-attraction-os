# Copywriting Knowledge Base — how to write copy a licensed agent reads, believes, and acts on

This is the standard for every word this plugin writes — the magnet, the page, the delivery DM and email, the
nurture sequence, the newsletter, the partner outreach, the bios, the Google posts. Read it before writing
copy. The goal is simple: copy that a real agent — someone weighing their next move — reads, believes, and
acts on, and that sounds like the member, not like a recruiter or an AI. If a line fails one of the principles
below, rewrite it.

---

## The 10 principles (non-negotiable)

1. **One job, earned with value.** The funnel's one goal is the opt-in — but it EARNS it by being genuinely
   useful (an honest comparison, a real leader's experience, a real offer), never by pushing. **A thin,
   all-pitch page reads like a recruiting deck; a substantive one reads like a leader.** Every section moves
   toward the opt-in by adding value, not hype. The call is offered after the download, never before.
2. **Outcomes beat features** (`04-value-proposition/32`). "Agents don't buy features; they buy outcomes."
   Not "weekly mastermind calls" → "every week you learn one thing from someone doing it right now, and you
   use it that week." Not "a CRM and training" → "you know exactly what to do on Monday." The formula: name
   the pain → show the feature → translate it to the benefit → paint the outcome. Always connect back to
   growth, freedom, and security.
3. **Clarity beats cleverness.** In 5 seconds the visitor must know: what this is, who it's for, and what they
   get. **Fifth-grade reading level** — Mike's rule for everything his team writes (`/32`). No industry jargon.
4. **Specific beats vague.** "Support" is noise; "a 30-minute call with me in your first week, then a group
   that answers at 9pm" is believable. Real routines, real steps, the honest trade-off, the exact question to
   ask a sponsor.
5. **"You" beats "I."** Talk about the agent's situation, pains, and goals. The page is about *them* — the
   one exception is the About section, and even there, frame everything as "here's why that matters for you."
6. **Proof everywhere — real only.** Agents trust evidence, not claims. Weave in real agents helped, the
   organization as it is, and testimonials from `identity/proof.md` (consent on file). One specific result
   beats ten adjectives. Proof from people *like them* (camera-shy, small market, three brokerages in) lands
   hardest (`/32`). If proof is thin, the upline's proof labeled as the upline's; if none, honest experience.
7. **Reduce friction.** The opt-in pop-up asks for exactly three things (first name, email, phone — nothing
   more; never "brokerage" or "production"), and the guide is an instant download on the thank-you page.
   Reassure: free, instant, private, unsubscribe anytime. Every extra field or ounce of doubt costs opt-ins.
8. **Sound like the member.** Match `identity/voice.md` + `identity/voice-samples.md` exactly; anything
   spoken on camera follows `voice-print.md`. If their voice is calm and plain, the copy is calm and plain —
   never guru-speak. Voice mismatch kills trust faster with agents than with anyone; they've heard every pitch.
9. **Honest persuasion only.** No fake scarcity, no hype, no guarantees, no manufactured urgency, no income
   talk. Confidence comes from specificity and proof, not exclamation marks. (This also keeps it compliant —
   house rules #5.)
10. **Attraction, not recruiting.** Agents follow people, not companies (`03-model-positioning/17`). The copy
    leads with the member's story, skills, and support — never the brokerage's brand, never the model's
    numbers. Nobody on this page is "recruited"; they read something honest and decide to talk.

## The reader is an agent weighing a move — write for it

Changing brokerages is one of the few business decisions an agent makes alone, with a pile of half-pitches and
no neutral source. So:
- **Trust is the whole game.** Agents opt in with a leader they believe is competent *and* honest about the
  trade-offs. Lead with credibility and candour, not salesiness.
- **Honesty is the differentiator.** The leader who explains every model fairly — including the downsides of
  their own — is the one the agent calls. That's the un-fakeable value no recruiting deck has.
- **Speak to the real pains — Mike's five (`02-prospect-targeting/18`, `04-value-proposition/27`):** financial
  uncertainty (no reliable next client) · lack of support, mentorship, training (the brokerage explains forms,
  not how to get clients) · technology gaps (paying for tools and leads that don't move the needle) · limited
  growth (no path past "sell more houses") · work-life balance and recognition (the hamster wheel, doing it
  alone). Name the ONE that fits this type of agent (`avatars.md` → "biggest problem, in their words"), then
  position the magnet (and the member) as the calm, honest way through it.
- **Name the fear under the pain.** New agents fear being just a number and choosing wrong with no
  information. Experienced agents fear another switch that changes nothing — "skeptical of failed promises,"
  three to five brokerages in (`/22`). Top producers fear disrupting momentum. Team leaders fear losing the
  team's feel. Say it in their words; they'll feel understood before anyone offers anything.
- **Lower the stakes of the opt-in.** They're not joining anything, not booking anything, and nobody will
  know — they're grabbing a guide to read before they decide. Make that feel like the obvious no-risk step.
- **Privacy is a feature.** "Will my broker find out?" is a real question. Answer it before they ask.

## Headlines & hooks (the hero — 90% of whether they stay)

The hero headline carries the page. It states the magnet's core promise in one clear, benefit-led line, for a
type of agent (by stage, never a protected class). Proven shapes (adapt to voice — don't sound like a template):

- **The Honest formula:** *"The honest [thing] for [type of agent]."*
  → *"The Honest Brokerage Comparison Guide — every model, every trade-off, including mine."* (the locked first
  campaign — house rules #11)
- **The Questions formula:** *"The [N] questions to ask any [brokerage / sponsor] before you [decide]."*
  → "The 14 questions to ask any sponsor before you sign."
- **The Before formula:** *"Read this before you [switch / sign / pick a sponsor]."*
- **The Without formula:** *"How to [outcome] without [the fear]."*
  → "How to choose your next brokerage without getting sold to."
- **The Plan formula:** *"Your first [90 days] at [a new brokerage], mapped."*

**The subhead** (right under the headline) does three jobs in one or two lines: expand the promise, name who
it's for, and remove doubt. → Comparison guide: *"How cloud, franchise, flat-fee, and independent models
actually work — the trade-offs of each, the questions to ask, and nothing you can't check. Written by a
working agent and leader, not a recruiting department."*

Headline rules: specific, benefit-led, no hype words ("INSANE," "secret," "you won't believe"), no clickbait,
readable in one breath. **Phone-length:** most visitors tap in from a bio link or a video description on a
phone — headline ~6–10 words, subhead one to two short lines, value-stack bullets one line each. It must
match the magnet exactly (house rules #7). "[Type of agent]" in any formula is a career stage or production
level, never a protected characteristic (house rules #5). No brokerage named in a headline other than the
member's own, where compliance requires it.

## The Problem section — name the pain before you offer anything

Right after the hero, name the agent's pain so they feel understood. From `identity/avatars.md` ("their
biggest problem, in their words" + "what they've already tried that didn't work"), take their **single most
acute pain** and make it concrete and honest:
- **Name it in their words** — "you're comparing splits on a spreadsheet at 11pm and every sponsor you talk
  to says the same three things"; "you switched once already and nothing changed except the logo."
- **Agitate honestly** — the real cost (another year of the same, choosing on the one number that matters
  least, a sponsor who disappears after onboarding). Real stakes, never fake doom, never a named brokerage.
- **Turn the corner** — one line: there's a better way to decide, and this guide is it (bridges into The Guide).
Keep it tight (3–5 lines). Empathy + stakes, not a pitch — it's what makes the rest of the page land.

## The Guide section — what's inside + its value, shown with the mockup

This is where the email gets earned: make the free guide feel genuinely valuable AND show it. Pair the copy
with the **guide mockup / cover on the left or right** (note the placement — the design step renders it).
From the magnet's actual pages:
- **"Here's exactly what you'll get" — 4–7 concrete bullets**, each a real outcome from a page ("how revenue
  share is actually funded, and the one question that tells you if a plan is real" · "the trade-off of my own
  model, in writing"), never a vague tease.
- **The mockup beside it** — visual proof the guide is real and substantial.
- **One line on why it's worth more than free** — it's honest about every model, including the member's.
- **Repeat the CTA** here (mid-page) — some readers are already sold.
Specificity + the visual is what makes an agent hand over an email.

## The About section — build trust fast (this is where opt-ins are won or lost)

Agents don't hand their email to a stranger — and they've met a hundred sponsors. The About section earns it.
Three beats, all framed for the reader — and it's **the mirror principle** (`04-value-proposition/27`: you are
most qualified to help the person you used to be):

- **The mirror.** One journey beat from `journey.md` / `story-bank.md` → the agent's present-day version.
  *"Three years in, I was paying for leads that didn't close and nobody at my brokerage had sold a house in a
  decade. If that's you right now, this guide is the thing I wish someone had handed me."* Former brokerages
  are never named.
- **Why they're qualified (one credibility line).** From `proof.md` only — agents helped, years, organization
  size with its date. Never invented. If the member's own proof is thin, the upline's proof labeled as the
  upline's ("the group I'm part of has…").
- **How they work with an agent (make the scary thing safe).** The first 30 days as a partner, from
  `offer.md` — 3 steps max. Reassurance: there's a path, and the member has walked people down it.

Tone: humble-confident and human. Always close the loop back to the reader — "…which means you get [outcome]."

## The Why Partner section — the Partner Offer (outcomes, never compensation)

This is **WHAT they give** — a distinct section from the About section's **WHO they are**. Read
`identity/offer.md` and present the **Partner Offer** as outcomes:
- **What's included**, each line an outcome (principle 2): the onboarding, the recurring call, the templates,
  the community as it is today — real and small beats big and promised (`04-value-proposition/33`).
- **The unique mechanism** — what the member does differently, tied to the agent's pain.
- **The transformation** — where the agent is now → where they could be, framed as what the member will
  SHOW, never what the agent will EARN.
- **The brokerage line** — "and everything [Brokerage] provides — I walk you through all of that on a call."
  That's the whole of the model on this page. **No splits, caps, tiers, stock, or rev-share numbers**
  (house rules #5). Compensation is explained on a private call, tailored (`02-prospect-targeting/19`).
- **Free vs paid, said straight** (`04-value-proposition/31`): what the member built that needs none of their
  time is free to agents who join; anything that needs their time or a team is discounted, with the price said
  up front — hidden costs after joining are how sponsors get a bad name. One honest line if it applies.

## The Organization section — what it's actually like inside (replaces "the local market")

The section that flips the page from "recruiting" to "real," and the strongest answer to "every sponsor
says that." From `offer.md` (what's included), `operations.md` (the weekly call, onboarding steps),
`proof.md` (organization today):
- **What we do together** — the recurring call, the group chat, the training, recognition. Only what exists
  today. A weekly script role-play call is a real thing to say; "a world-class ecosystem" is not.
- **Who's in it** — the kinds of agents by stage ("mostly agents in years one to five"); the count only if
  `proof.md` states it, with its date; never "fastest-growing" without a dated source.
- **Why that matters to you** — Mike's fifth pain: not doing it alone. "Getting agents is easy; keeping them
  is where the game is won" — the culture is the retention argument, and agents can feel it on a page.
- **Real photos** — the member adds photos of the call, events, the community at the design step. Real people only.

## The Proof section — let other agents do the convincing (dedicated block)

A standalone proof section beats proof sprinkled in passing — it's the biggest trust lever on the page. From
`identity/proof.md` (real only — never invent; consent on file):
- **2–4 short results** — first name or initials + situation + what happened ("Was two years in with one
  closing. Six months after we started working together, four under contract — Priya, Houston").
- **Hard numbers** only as `proof.md` states them — agents helped, organization size, years — each with its date.
- **Upline proof, labeled** — "our group has…" — only if `proof.md` holds it.
Tie it straight to the reader's pain: "agents who felt exactly what you're feeling — here's how it went." If
proof is thin, keep it small and honest; never pad with invented stories (house rules #5). The section stays;
it just gets smaller.

## The Socials section — their YouTube + socials (ONLY if they have any)

**Conditional.** Include this only if the member actually has channels (`identity/profile.md` → Social
handles). If they have none, **leave it out entirely** — never write an empty or placeholder socials block.
When they do have a presence, it's strong trust + authority (especially YouTube for a content-driven leader):
- **Their real channels** — handles/links only, from the Brain (never invented).
- **"Follow for more free value"** — frame it as proof they give value constantly; a real subscriber or
  follower count only if the member has stated one. The guide reads as a sample of much more.
- **Opt-in stays the ONE primary CTA.** Socials are a *secondary* trust signal — modest links, no Subscribe
  button, no autoplaying embed.

## The opt-in & CTA — make grabbing the guide a no-brainer

The opt-in flow is **button → pop-up → thank-you page** (house rules #4). Write all three — plus the
**mini-FAQ**: three one-line Q&As in the section, right before the button, dissolving the final objections an
agent has at the ask:
- *"Is this a recruiting pitch?"* → No — it's the guide, and it's fair to every model. If you ever want to
  talk, you know where I am.
- *"Will anyone know I downloaded this?"* → No — the list is private, nobody's contacted on your behalf, and
  you can unsubscribe from anything any time.
- *"I'm not planning to move — is this for me?"* → Then it's the thing to read before you ever decide. No
  commitment, just the honest picture. (Adapt the third line to the magnet's type of agent.)
One honest line each, in the member's voice. Objections dissolve best at the moment they occur — at the ask.

- **On the page:** name the magnet + a one-line promise, a **top-3 "what you'll get" recap** (the full stack is
  up in the Guide section) — these MUST match what the guide delivers (house rules #7) — and the CTA button.
- **The pop-up** (every CTA button opens it): a one-line headline restating the promise, **the form — first
  name, email, phone. Nothing else**, friction reducers right by the button, and the submit button.

**Pop-up micro-copy (the hardest moment on the page — write it, don't wing it):**
- **Labels:** `First name` · `Email` · `Phone`. Plain, no asterisks, no "required."
- **The contact line under Phone** — one honest sentence on what the member will actually do with it, built
  from `identity/operations.md`'s follow-up cadence: *"I'll text or call once to make sure you got it — no
  drip, no pressure."* Expectation set = trust earned; a phone field with no reason costs opt-ins.
- **Reassurance by the button:** *"Free. Instant. Private — nobody's contacted on your behalf. Unsubscribe anytime."*
- **Headline:** restate the promise, not the mechanics — *"Your free comparison guide — one step away."*
- **Button:** the canonical phrase, nothing else.
- **The thank-you page** (after submit): warm confirmation + **the direct link to the guide PDF as a big
  instant-download button right on the page** ("You're in — grab your guide below"), in the member's voice —
  then **the call, offered**: one warm, optional line and the booking link (*"Want to talk through where you're
  at? Book a call — no pitch, I'll just answer your questions."*). **Instant download first, NEVER email
  delivery — no "check your inbox" copy; the download never depends on booking** (house rules #4).

**CTA button copy** — ONE phrase, everywhere: **"Get the Free Guide."** It's this plugin's canonical CTA — the
same words on the hero, the mid-page repeat, the opt-in, and the pop-up submit; when the member writes a
link-in-bio button or a video/social CTA that points at this page, recommend the same phrase — so an agent
meets one consistent ask at every touchpoint. Don't improvise variants per page.
- ✅ "Get the Free Guide" — the ONLY label on any CTA button.
  A guide-named variant ("the comparison guide") is allowed ONLY in body copy — never on the button itself.
- ❌ "Submit" · "Sign up" · "Download" · "Join" · "Apply" · "Book a call" (on the opt-in page) — any button text
  other than the canonical phrase.
- ❌ "Send me the guide" or any send/email framing — delivery is an **instant download on the page**.
Repeat the CTA ~3× — hero, mid-page (the Guide), and the opt-in — same promise each time.

## Emails, DMs, and the newsletter (lm-delivery · lm-nurture · lm-partnerships)

Same principles, four extra rules:
- **Value first, every time** (`15-advanced-scaling/73`): Mike's whole nurture is "more value, more value,
  more value." An email that only sells is deleted; an email that teaches one thing is forwarded.
- **The call to action is warm and optional** — Mike's own: *"If you'd like to chat about partnering with
  me and getting my training, coaching, and mastermind calls for free, book a private call and we'll see if I
  can help. If not, that's okay."* Never a hard close, never "join [brokerage]."
- **Success stories of agents in the organization** are the strongest email there is (`/73`) — and they are
  real, consented, and in the agent's words.
- **Partners** (`/77`): position as *helping agents succeed*, not recruiting. *"I've been helping agents who
  are struggling with [pain]. If you run into one, feel free to send them my way — it's free, and I'd love to
  help."* Give them something to pass along (the guide, an event invite). Thank them for every introduction.
- **Subject lines** at fifth-grade level, specific, no clickbait, no emoji walls, no "[Brokerage] opportunity."

## Flow & structure — the page must move

The sections answer the agent's questions in the order they ask them, each building toward the opt-in (the
Socials section is optional — only if they have channels):

```
HERO            →  "What is this, is it for me?"          (promise + who + CTA)
THE PROBLEM     →  "Ugh, that's exactly my situation."     (name + agitate the pain)
THE GUIDE       →  "Oh — and look how useful it is."        (value-stack + mockup + mid CTA)
ABOUT (WHO)     →  "Can I trust this person?"             (the mirror + credibility + how they work)
WHY PARTNER     →  "What do they actually give?"          (the Partner Offer as outcomes — no comp)
THE ORGANIZATION→  "What's it like inside?"               (the call, the group, who's in it)
PROOF           →  "Has it worked for agents like me?"    (real agents helped + the strip)
SOCIALS *       →  "Are they legit + worth following?"    (YouTube + socials — * only if any)
OPT-IN          →  "Okay — what do I get and how?"        (the magnet + the email + CTA)
THANK-YOU       →  "Got it. What now?"                    (download first, then the call, offered)
```
Momentum rules: short sentences, short paragraphs (1–3 lines), one idea per block, sub-headers and bullets so
it's skimmable, generous white space, and ONE primary CTA repeated ~3×. Nobody reads a wall of text on a page.

## Voice-match checklist (run before finishing any copy)
- [ ] Does this sound like the member's real voice samples — not recruiter hype, not AI filler?
- [ ] Is it "you"-focused (except About), outcome-led, specific, fifth-grade reading level?
- [ ] Is every claim backed by real proof from the Brain (nothing invented; consent on testimonials)?
- [ ] Does the hero promise = the magnet's promise = the opt-in bullets = what's delivered?
- [ ] One job (the opt-in); the call only on the thank-you page and in the guide's close?
- [ ] No compensation numbers, no earnings talk, no ranking, no named-brokerage criticism, no former brokerage named?
- [ ] Skimmable — short lines, clear sections, white space?

---

## Hard "don'ts"
Hype words and fake urgency · clickbait that the magnet doesn't pay off · "me/we"-heavy bragging · vague
benefits · industry jargon · walls of text · invented testimonials, stats, or organization counts · invented or
empty socials · a socials "follow me" button that competes with the opt-in · splits, caps, tiers, stock,
rev-share, or income anywhere public · "best brokerage" / rankings / scored tables · anything negative about a
named brokerage, sponsor, or person · a former brokerage named in a story · targeting by a protected
characteristic · a "book a call" button on the opt-in page · "check your inbox" · rendering a design.
