# Funnel Guide — the opt-in page that converts an agent

One page, one job: **get the agent to opt into the lead magnet** (a pop-up form: first name, email, phone —
then a thank-you page with the instant download and, under it, the call offered). It's a **value-led
capture page** — it EARNS the opt-in with candour, a real leader's experience, real proof, and a real Partner
Offer, never hype and never a pitch for a brokerage. Trust-building, skimmable, focused on the opt-in as the
one action. (Read `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/shared/copywriting-kb.md` for how to write each line.)

## The flow (why this order works) — up to 9 sections + the thank-you
```
HERO             →  "What is this, is it for me?"             promise + who + CTA
THE PROBLEM      →  "Ugh — that's exactly my situation."      agitate the specific pain (before any offer)
THE GUIDE        →  "Oh — and look how useful it is."          value-stack + the magnet mockup (left/right) + CTA
ABOUT (WHO)      →  "Who's behind this — can I trust them?"   the mirror + credibility + how they work
WHY PARTNER      →  "What do they actually give?"            the PARTNER OFFER as outcomes — no compensation
THE ORGANIZATION →  "What's it like inside?"                 the call, the group, who's in it
PROOF            →  "Has it worked for agents like me?"      real agents helped + the photo strip
SOCIALS *        →  "Are they legit + worth following?"      YouTube + socials   (* ONLY if they have any)
OPT-IN           →  "Okay — what do I get, and how?"         the magnet + the pop-up (name/email/phone) + CTA
THANK-YOU        →  "Got it. What now?"                      download first, then the call, offered
```
Each section earns the next scroll, and the CTA repeats ~3× (hero, mid-page, opt-in). The Socials section is
**conditional** — included only when the member actually has channels. Keep momentum: short blocks, sub-headers,
bullets, white space. **A thin page reads like a recruiting deck; this depth is what makes it read like a leader.**

## The strategy underneath (lock this BEFORE writing — it's what makes copy persuade, not just inform)
The difference between an "okay" page and one that converts is strategy, not adjectives:
- **One big idea.** The page rides on a single sharp angle, not a feature list — for the comparison guide:
  *"the leader who explains every model straight, including the downsides of their own."* For a switching
  checklist: *"the person who makes sure your next move is the last one."* Every section reinforces that one idea.
- **Lead with the deepest desire + the most acute pain**, named early and plainly. From `avatars.md`
  ("biggest problem, in their words" · "what they'd need to hear to believe this member can help") and the
  magnet's framing page. Mike's five pains (`02-prospect-targeting/18`, `04-value-proposition/27`) are the
  vocabulary: financial uncertainty · lack of support, mentorship, training · technology gaps · limited growth
  · work-life balance and recognition. Pick the one this type of agent lives. **Comparison guide (the locked
  first campaign):** desire = to choose with the full picture and not get sold to; fear = picking on the one
  number that matters least, switching again for nothing, a sponsor who disappears after onboarding, being
  "just a number." The whole page moves them from the fear to the desire.
- **Dissolve the 3 real reasons an agent WON'T opt in:** (1) *"this is a recruiting pitch"* → candour, the
  trade-off of the member's own model on the page, zero model numbers, the mirror, the mini-FAQ's first line
  (Problem + About + Proof + the FAQ); (2) *"I can Google this / ask ChatGPT"* → a working leader's lived
  experience, the questions page, the honest trade-offs no deck prints (The Guide + The Organization);
  (3) *"I'm not looking to move"* / *"my broker will find out"* → the guide is FOR before you decide, the list
  is private, nobody is contacted on their behalf (The Guide + Opt-in + the mini-FAQ's second and third lines).
  Hit each one.
- **The emotional arc:** stuck / sold-to (hero + problem) → *"wait, somebody's actually explaining it"* (the
  guide) → *"and this person has been where I am"* (about + why partner + the organization) → *"agents like me
  have done this with them — and it's real, not a deck"* (proof + socials) → *"this is the easy, private,
  no-risk next step"* (opt-in) → *"and if I want to talk, I can"* (thank-you).
- **Specificity IS the proof.** Every claim carries a real routine, a real agent helped, or a real number —
  **real meaning it's in the Brain or a sourced check** (house rules #5). Where there is no number, be specific
  with facts, names, and steps instead; never estimate one. Vague = recruiting; specific = leader.

## SECTION 1 — HERO (90% of the result)
The first screen. If it's vague, they leave. Three parts — **phone-length** (most visitors tap in from a bio
link, a Reel caption, or a video description): headline ~6–10 words, subhead one to two short lines.
**Campaign one is always the comparison guide (house rules #11), so model it first:**
- **Headline** — the magnet's core promise, outcome-led, for this type of agent. Clear over clever.
  Comparison guide: *"The Honest Brokerage Comparison Guide."* · Campaign two+ (switching checklist example):
  *"Switch brokerages once — the checklist that makes it the last time."*
- **Subhead** (1–2 lines) — expand the promise, name who it's for by stage, remove doubt.
  Comparison guide: *"How cloud, franchise, flat-fee, and independent models actually work — the trade-offs of
  each (including mine), the questions to ask any sponsor, and nothing you can't check. Written by a working
  agent and leader, not a recruiting department."*
- **CTA button** — the one canonical phrase, exactly (copywriting KB): *"Get the Free Guide."*
The headline MUST equal the magnet's promise (alignment — house rules #7). No brokerage named in the hero
except the member's own where compliance requires it.

## SECTION 2 — THE PROBLEM (agitate before you offer anything)
The agent landed here because something's bugging them. Name it *before* you offer anything — they need to
feel "this page gets me." Take their **single most acute pain** (the strategy section says where it comes
from) and make it concrete:
- **Name the pain in their words** — the specific thing that goes wrong. Comparison guide: *"You're comparing
  brokerages on a spreadsheet at 11pm, and every sponsor you talk to says the same three things — and none of
  them will tell you the downside of their own model."* Switching checklist: *"You switched once already.
  Nothing changed except the logo on your signs."* (No figures unless real and sourced; no brokerage named;
  no protected characteristic — house rules #5.)
- **Agitate honestly** — the real cost of getting it wrong (another year of the same, a sponsor who vanishes
  after onboarding, choosing on the one number that matters least). Real stakes, never fake fear or doom.
- **Turn the corner** — one line that says *there's a better way to decide, and this guide is it* (bridges
  into The Guide).
Keep it tight — 3–5 lines. This section is empathy + stakes, not a pitch.

## SECTION 3 — THE GUIDE (what's inside + why it's worth it) — with the mockup beside it
This is the section that sells the magnet itself, so make the free thing feel genuinely valuable AND show it.
Pair the copy with the **guide mockup / cover image on the left or right** (note the placement — the design
step renders it). From the magnet's actual pages (alignment — house rules #7):
- **The mockup** — note in the copy that the guide cover/mockup sits **left or right** of this section: the
  visual proof the guide is real and substantial.
- **A "here's exactly what you'll get" stack** — 4–7 concrete bullets, each a real outcome from a page of the
  guide (comparison: *"the five parts every brokerage model is made of — so you can compare any of them"* ·
  *"how revenue share is actually funded, in plain English"* · *"the honest trade-off of my own model, in
  writing"* · *"the 14 questions to ask any sponsor — including me"*), never a vague tease.
- **Why it's worth more than free** — one line: it's fair to every model, including mine; nobody else will
  put that in writing.
- **Repeat the CTA** here (mid-page) — some readers are ready already.
Specificity + the visual is what earns the email.

## SECTION 4 — ABOUT THE LEADER (WHO they are — earns the trust to opt in)
**The welcome video slot (always written, always optional — the strongest trust element on the page).** A
**30–60s face-to-camera welcome clip** beside the About copy (left/right). Spec it structurally (*optional —
leave it out at the design step if there's no clip*) + write the 3-beat talking outline **for the ear, in
their spoken voice** (`voice-print.md`; `voice.md` if empty): **(1)** who I am + who I help, one breath ·
**(2)** what the guide gives you, in their words · **(3)** "grab it below" — warm, zero pitch. Rules: never
autoplays with sound; a play button, not a rival CTA; the opt-in stays the one action. Never ask the member
mid-build whether they have a video — the slot simply disappears at the design step if they never film it.
These members film for YouTube anyway — five minutes of existing muscle, and faces convert.
Agents don't give a stranger their email — and they've met a hundred sponsors. Three beats, every one framed
back to the reader:
- **The mirror** (`04-value-proposition/27`: you are most qualified to help the person you used to be) — one
  journey beat → the agent's present-day version. *"Three years in, I was paying for leads that didn't close,
  at a brokerage where nobody had sold a house in a decade. If that's you, this is the guide I wish someone
  had handed me."* The former brokerage is "a franchise" / "an independent" — **never named**.
- **Why they're qualified** — one credibility line from `proof.md` (agents helped, years, organization size
  with its date). Never invented. Thin proof → the upline's, labeled as the upline's ("the group I'm part of…").
- **How they work with an agent** — the first 30 days as a partner from `offer.md`, 3 steps max. Reassurance:
  there's a path, and the member has walked people down it.
Tone: humble-confident, human. (WHO they are; the next section is WHAT they give.)

## SECTION 5 — WHY PARTNER WITH [FIRST NAME] (the PARTNER OFFER — the section recruiting pages get wrong)
The real "what you get," straight from `offer.md` — NOT a feature list, and NOT the model. Make it substantive:
- **What's included** — each line an outcome (`04-value-proposition/32`: agents buy outcomes, not features).
  Not "weekly mastermind calls" → "every week you learn one thing from someone doing it right now, and you use
  it that week." Only what exists today (`/33`: a weekly script role-play call is a real offer; "a world-class
  ecosystem" is not).
- **The unique mechanism** — what the member does differently, tied to the pain (the thing that de-risks the move).
- **The transformation** — where the agent is now → where they could be, framed as what the member will SHOW,
  never what the agent will EARN.
- **The brokerage line** — *"and everything [Brokerage] provides — I walk you through all of that on a call."*
  That is the whole of the model on the page. **No splits, caps, tiers, stock, rev share, or income** (house
  rules #5; compensation is a private-call conversation, tailored — `02-prospect-targeting/19`).
- **Free vs paid, said straight** if it applies (`/31`): what the member built that needs none of their time is
  free to agents who join; anything that needs their time or a team is discounted, price said up front.
By the end the reader knows exactly what partnering means — the organization and the proof back it up next.

## SECTION 6 — THE ORGANIZATION (what it's actually like inside) — the un-fakeable section
This is what a recruiting deck can NEVER produce, and what flips the whole page from "pitch" to "real." From
`offer.md` (what's included), `operations.md` (the weekly call, onboarding steps), `proof.md` (organization
today):
- **What we do together** — the recurring call (day and what happens on it), the group chat, the training,
  recognition. Specific and small beats big and vague.
- **Who's in it** — the kinds of agents by stage ("mostly agents in years one to five, a few team leaders");
  the count only if `proof.md` states it, with its date; never "fastest-growing" without a dated source.
- **Why that matters to you** — Mike's fifth pain: not doing it alone. "Getting agents is easy; keeping them
  is where the game is won" (`02-prospect-targeting/18`) — culture is the retention argument, and an agent can
  feel it on a page.
- **Real photos** — the member adds photos of the call, events, the community at the design step. Real people
  only; agents' faces need their OK.
Pure value + reality — it's the strongest answer to "every sponsor says that," and it substantiates the
Partner Offer above.

## SECTION 7 — PROOF / RESULTS (let other agents do the convincing)
A *dedicated* proof block beats proof sprinkled in passing — it's a top trust lever on the page. From
`proof.md` (real only — never invent; consent on file):
- **2–4 short results** — first name or initials, the situation, what happened (*"Two years in, one closing.
  Six months after we started working together, four under contract — Priya, Houston"*). Proof from agents
  *like the reader* lands hardest (`04-value-proposition/32`).
- **Hard numbers** only as `proof.md` states them — agents helped, organization size, years — each dated.
- **Upline proof, labeled** — "our group has…" — only if `proof.md` holds it; never presented as the member's.
Tie it straight back to the reader's pain. If proof is thin — or `proof.md` is empty / still `[bracketed]` —
keep the block small and honest: one real result if there is one, otherwise experience only — **never pad**
(house rules #5). The section stays; it just gets smaller.

**The PROOF PHOTO STRIP (the moving part of the page).** Under the results, spec a **horizontal,
auto-scrolling strip of the member's real photos** — a slow, continuous glide across the section (a
marquee/carousel; the design step builds the motion). What goes in it, in the member's real photos:
- **the weekly call** — screenshots of the group on Zoom, the whiteboard, the training
- **events** — the meetup, the conference booth, the dinner
- **agents' wins** — a first closing celebrated, a cap announcement, a recognition post
- **the community** — the group chat moment, the handshake, the thank-you
Spec it structurally, not visually: *"photo strip: 8–12 real photos, auto-scrolling, slow"* + the **types**
above (the member picks the actual files at the design step — never ask mid-build, never invent a list).
**Real photos only, theirs to use — agents' faces need the agent's OK** (house rules #5; `compliance.md` →
testimonial consent). Fewer than ~6 good photos → skip the strip entirely (thin reads worse than none).
Why it works: motion draws the eye, and a wall of *real* moments proves "this organization actually exists"
in a way text never can — it's the un-fakeable section's visual twin.

## SECTION 8 — FOLLOW ALONG (their YouTube + socials) — ONLY if they have any
**Conditional. Include this section only if the member actually has channels** (`profile.md` → Social
handles). If they have none yet, **SKIP it entirely** — never render an empty or placeholder socials block.
When they do have a presence, it's a strong trust + authority signal — especially YouTube for a
content-driven leader:
- **Their channels** — YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Facebook. Real handles/links only, from the Brain.
- **Proof-of-value framing** — *"I put out free training for agents every week — here's where to follow
  along."* A real subscriber/follower count ONLY if the member has stated one — never "thousands" or "a
  growing community of…".
- **A taste of the content** — the kind of value they post, so this guide reads as a sample of much more.
- **Keep the opt-in as THE primary CTA.** Socials are a *secondary*, trust-building "follow for more" — modest
  links, NOT a big rival button, no Subscribe button, no autoplaying embed. The page has one job — the opt-in.

## SECTION 9 — THE OPT-IN (the ask) — button → pop-up → thank-you page
Make grabbing the guide a no-brainer. The flow is **two steps** (house rules #4): every CTA button on the
page opens the **opt-in pop-up**; submitting lands on the **thank-you page** with the download first and the
call offered under it. Write all three pieces:

**On the page (the section itself):**
- **Name the magnet** + a one-line promise.
- **A quick recap — top 3 "what you'll get" bullets** (the full stack is in §3 above; here just the highlights).
  These MUST match the guide's actual pages (alignment — house rules #7).
- **The mini-FAQ — 3 one-liners, right at the ask.** The last-moment objections an agent has, dissolved where
  they occur (not a separate section — three short Q&As in the member's voice):
  - *"Is this a recruiting pitch?"* → No — it's the guide, and it's fair to every model, including mine. If
    you ever want to talk, you know where I am.
  - *"Will anyone know I downloaded this?"* → No — the list is private, nobody's contacted on your behalf,
    and you can unsubscribe from anything any time.
  - *"I'm not planning to move — is this for me?"* → Then it's the thing to read before you ever decide. No
    commitment, just the honest picture. (Adapt the third line to the magnet's reader — a 90-day plan: *"I
    just joined somewhere — too late?"* → exactly when it helps most.)
  Keep each answer to one honest line, in the member's voice.
- **CTA button** — same promise as the hero: *"Get the Free Guide."*

**The OPT-IN POP-UP (every CTA button on the page opens this one modal):**
- **A one-line headline** restating the promise (*"Your free comparison guide — one step away"*).
- **The form** — labels `First name` · `Email` · `Phone`. Three fields, nothing else — never "brokerage,"
  never "production."
- **The contact line** under Phone — one honest sentence on what the member actually does with it, from
  `operations.md`'s follow-up cadence: *"I'll text or call once to make sure you got it — no drip, no
  pressure."* (Never collect a phone silently — house rules #4.)
- **Reassurance** by the button — *"Free. Instant. Private — nobody's contacted on your behalf. Unsubscribe anytime."*
- **The submit button** — the canonical phrase: *"Get the Free Guide."*

**The THANK-YOU PAGE (where submitting lands):**
- A warm confirmation in the member's voice (*"You're in — here's your guide"*).
- **The direct link to the guide PDF as a big instant-download button, first.** The email is never needed to
  deliver it. **Instant download ONLY, NEVER email delivery — no "check your inbox" copy, and never ask how to
  deliver it (house rules #4).**
- **The call, offered** — under the download, one warm, optional line + the booking link from `profile.md` /
  `operations.md`: *"Want to talk through where you're at? Book a call — no pitch, I'll just answer your
  questions. If not, that's okay."* (Mike's register, `15-advanced-scaling/73`.) The download never depends on it.
- One soft "here's where to find me" line (their social handles / website from the Brain).
- The same compliance stamp as the page footer (house rules #5).

## The static form rule (for the design step — carry "the rule" block into the funnel doc's appendix verbatim)
Netlify detects forms by parsing the **raw static HTML at deploy time** — it does not run JavaScript. A page
exported from a design canvas ships a JS bundle, so the form it draws is invisible to Netlify: nothing is ever
registered, and every submission is silently lost while the page looks perfect. Confirmed on a real funnel.

**The rule:** *"The deployed `index.html` must contain a real static `<form>` with a `name`, `method="POST"`,
`data-netlify="true"`, a honeypot (`netlify-honeypot="bot-field"` + a hidden `bot-field` input), and a matching
hidden static detection form in the markup with the SAME `name` and the SAME fields (first name, email,
phone) — present as plain HTML, never injected by JS. The submit handler must actually POST to `/` as
`application/x-www-form-urlencoded` with `form-name=<the exact form name>` plus every field BEFORE it reveals
the inline thank-you, and show a plain fallback with the member's phone and email if the POST fails. The
thank-you renders inline on the same page — never a second file or redirect. Verify before sending traffic:
the form appears in the site's Forms tab, the live page source contains no `{{` placeholder, and a test
submission shows the thank-you screen and lands in the Forms tab."* The Design Studio's `aa-funnel-design` ships
this wiring built in; the member never writes form code — their only job is to drop the folder in, turn on
Forms email notifications, and test-submit once.

## What converts vs. what kills it
**Converts:** one clear promise · a real pain named before any offer · a value-stack shown with the mockup ·
WHO + the PARTNER OFFER as distinct sections · **the trade-off of the member's own model, in writing** ·
**what the organization actually does each week** · **dedicated real proof with consent** · a real social /
YouTube presence shown as trust (when they have one) · outcome-led "you" copy · a tiny, reassured, private form ·
ONE primary CTA repeated ~3× · the call offered only after the download · short, skimmable, lots of white space.
**Kills it:** a vague headline · **a thin, all-pitch page that reads like a recruiting deck** · skipping the
problem · an empty/placeholder socials block · "me/we" bragging · hype and fake urgency · invented proof or
organization counts · splits, caps, tiers, stock, or income anywhere · a ranking or a named brokerage's
weakness · a former brokerage named · a long form · a "book a call" button on the opt-in page · a wall of
text · any claim the magnet doesn't pay off · a canvas export deployed without the static form.

## Compliance note
Every public line obeys house rules #5: the two cardinal rules, no ranking, compensation off the page, no
earnings claims, targeting by stage only, real proof with consent, recruiting scope respected, the Meta
"Employment" note if this ever becomes an ad. The gate is three-state: unset blocks the page; set applies the
stamp and reminds once; confirmed applies the stamp.
