SECTION 2 — YOUR STORY
════════════════════════════════════════

(illustrative — demo) Every count and figure in this section is fictional.

──── How it started ────

You got licensed in January 2021 after nine years teaching high-school history: eight months at a franchise office desk, a phone, a headshot and no syllabus. Two closings in that stretch, both from one former colleague. "I treated it like a job I could study for and found out there was no textbook."

──── The hardest stretch ────

Year two, 2022: you bought online leads because everyone around you had, and spent fourteen months chasing people who had clicked a photo once. The open houses were producing — eleven closings, most from a Saturday sign-in — but the lead bill ate the margin. Then an invoice arrived in a month with zero closings: "I'm paying rent on a pipeline I don't own."

──── The turning point — when it clicked ────

A Sunday in October 2022: you pulled every sign-in sheet, sat at the kitchen table, and wrote a five-step routine — every name gets one text by 6 pm Sunday, every text asks one question. Three of those names bought the next quarter, and you cancelled the leads in December. "It cost nothing — it cost a Sunday."

──── After — what the business looked like ────

By the end of 2023 the routine was the business: about twenty closings a year, six in ten from a Saturday sign-in, no lead bill. The strategy was never the open house — it was the Sunday after it. "The Saturday gets you the names. The Sunday gets you the clients."

The moment selling houses wasn't enough came in August 2026, when Priya, who had joined in June, texted that her third buyer contract in sixty days had come from the routine. "Watching it work for Priya felt bigger than any closing I've had."

Who you are doing this for: your family, and the teachers you shared a hallway with, who think the only way out of a salary that never moves is a second job. "I was one of them. If one of them gets a syllabus instead of a lead bill — that's the why."

>> "So that the agents who are where I was in 2022 get the routine instead of the lead bill — and so my own January stops starting at zero."

SECTION 3 — YOUR BROKERAGE STORY
════════════════════════════════════════

(illustrative — demo) Every count and figure in this section is fictional.

You joined Real Broker in March 2023. At your previous office the counter reset every January. A friend from licensing class had moved to Real the year before and kept describing a Tuesday call where agents taught each other what had worked that week. You ignored it for most of a year — "I assumed a cloud brokerage meant an office with no walls" — then went to one call as a guest, watched a four-year agent walk through her sign-in sheet, and thought: that is a classroom.

"I didn't move for a plan. I moved because the people on that call were teaching, and I'm a teacher."

>> "Nobody joins a brokerage. They join a Tuesday call. A cloud brokerage is a classroom you can take anywhere — most agents still picture an office with no walls, and they're looking at the wrong thing."

You chose Jordan as your sponsor because Jordan had built the kind of business you want to build: a group that teaches rather than a roster that counts. Jordan runs the Tuesday teaching call and joins your calls with agents who want to see how the brokerage works.

Why revenue share is the key to what you want, in your words: "Selling houses pays me once. Teaching the routine to agents who then teach it pays the group for as long as the group keeps closing — and nobody's counter resets in January. Other agents should care because the same thing is true for them: the first agent you help is the first month your income stops starting at zero." The brokerage is the vehicle; you are the reason.

The unique part is not that you host open houses — thousands of agents do. It is that the follow-up actually happens every Sunday, and that every sign-in answers two questions, so the Sunday text has something to say. Because you were a teacher first, the routine is five steps on a sheet another agent can run on their first Sunday: Host, Ask, Text, Track, Table.

What your upline — your sponsor and the leaders above them — provides today: Jordan's Tuesday teaching call for the whole group, and Jordan on a call with any agent who wants the brokerage explained properly. That is Jordan's, not yours, and an agent gets it on day one.

The biggest mistakes you see agents making right now, in your words: "They're paying for the names instead of following up the ones they already have. Every agent I know hosts open houses; almost none of them text the sheet on Sunday. The second mistake is trying five things at once — a lead subscription, a coaching program, a course — and finishing none of them. I'm the solution because I can show the exact Sunday that fixed it, and joining here means you're not learning it alone — there's a Tuesday table every week."

>> What you can teach that most agents can't: the Sunday after the open house — a five-step routine on one sheet that turns names you already have into buyers, with no lead bill.
