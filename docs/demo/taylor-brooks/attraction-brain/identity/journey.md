# Taylor — Journey & Why

## The three beats
### 1 · Where they started
Taylor got licensed in January 2021 after nine years teaching high-school history, and spent the first eight months at a desk in a franchise office with a phone, a headshot, and no syllabus. Two closings in that stretch — both from the same former teaching colleague, who sent a cousin and then a neighbour. "I treated it like a job I could study for and found out there was no textbook. Everyone said 'work your sphere' and nobody said what that meant on a Tuesday." (illustrative — demo)
- **Who relates to this:** a new agent (0–2 years) coming from salaried work — a teacher, a nurse, an office manager — with one or two closings from friends and no idea where number three comes from.

### 2 · The hardest stretch
Year two, 2022. Taylor signed up for online leads at about $1,100 a month (illustrative — demo) because that is what the agents at the next desks were doing, and spent fourteen months (illustrative — demo) chasing people who had clicked a photo once. The open houses were quietly producing — eleven closings that year, most of them traceable to a Saturday sign-in — but the lead bill ate the margin and the attention. The low point was an invoice that arrived in a month with zero closings. "I remember opening it at the kitchen table and thinking, I'm paying rent on a pipeline I don't own."
- **Who relates to this:** the agent two or three years behind Taylor on the same road — 2–5 years in, 4–10 deals a year (a profile, illustrative — demo), paying for leads that don't convert, with sign-in sheets in a drawer and no follow-up. **This line seeds the primary Agent Avatar.**

### 3 · The turning point
A Sunday in October 2022. Taylor pulled every open-house sign-in sheet from the year, sat at the kitchen table with a legal pad, and wrote a five-step follow-up routine: every name gets one text by 6 pm Sunday, every text asks one question, every answer goes on one sheet. The next quarter three of those names bought (illustrative — demo). The lead subscription was cancelled in December. The routine has run every Sunday since. "That was the first time it felt like a system instead of luck. And it cost nothing — it cost a Sunday."
- **Who relates to this:** the agent who already hosts open houses and already has the names — they are one Sunday away from a pipeline, and they don't know it yet.

## The leader moment
**When selling houses stopped being enough:** Tuesday, August 12, 2026 (illustrative — demo). Priya Nair, who had joined Taylor's group in June, texted a screenshot of her third executed buyer contract in sixty days with four words: "the routine works, Taylor." Taylor had taught the sheet and the five texts to one person and it had produced in someone else's hands. "I'd spent five years making the routine work for me. Watching it work for Priya felt bigger than any closing I've had. That was the day I knew what I was actually building."

## The WHY
**Who they are doing this for:** "My family, and the teachers I used to share a hallway with — the ones who think the only way out of a salary that never moves is a second job. I was one of them." · **The life it buys:** "Sundays that are mine, and an income that doesn't start at zero in January."
**The why-line (verbatim, one sentence):** "So that the agents who are where I was in 2022 get the routine instead of the lead bill — and so my own January stops starting at zero."

**Long version (bio, "about", the partner-call opener):** "I taught high-school history for nine years before I got my license in 2021. The first eight months I had two closings and both came from the same colleague, which told me something I didn't want to hear: I didn't have a business, I had a favour. One question per text — what's your timeline, what would make you move sooner. One sheet for the answers. One Tuesday to look at the sheet. One rule: it happens every Sunday whether or not I feel like it. The next quarter three of those names bought. I cancelled the leads in December and I have not paid for one since.

I moved to Real Broker in March 2023, and I want to be honest about why, because it wasn't a spreadsheet. I had capped twice at my old office and both times the only thing that happened was the counter reset in January. A friend from licensing class invited me to a Tuesday call where agents at Real were teaching each other what had worked that week, and I sat there watching a four-year agent walk through her sign-in sheet and thought: this is a classroom. That's what I'd been missing. People who teach.

Here is what I care about. There are agents in Austin right now who are exactly where I was in 2022 — two to five years in, paying for leads, hosting open houses with the names going into a drawer. They don't need another tool. They need the routine, and they need someone to sit next to them the first three Sundays. That is what I do. When Priya joined in June she brought her sign-in sheet to our first call, I gave her the five texts, and she had three buyers under contract in sixty days (illustrative — demo). Marcus came in September as a brand-new agent and texted every one of his first eleven sign-ins on his first Sunday.

When you partner with me you get Real Broker, and I'll walk you through how the model works on a call, not in a post. But what you get from me is the Tuesday table — a thirty-minute weekly call where one of us brings a real sign-in sheet and we run the five steps on it live — the sheet, the texts, the tracker, and me in your corner for the first thirty days. Where I'm taking this: a group of agents who all run the same routine, who teach it to the next person, and who stop starting from zero every January. If that sounds like the classroom you've been looking for, book a call and bring your sheet. I'd rather show you than tell you."
