## The year I paid for leads
- **Story:** "Year two I signed up for online leads because that's what the agents at the next desks were doing. About eleven hundred a month (illustrative — demo). I chased people who'd clicked a photo once for fourteen months. The month that broke me was the month the invoice showed up and I'd closed nothing. I opened it at the kitchen table and I remember the exact thought: I'm paying rent on a pipeline I don't own. I almost quit that week — I had a tab open with the teaching jobs. Then I noticed something on the sheet next to the invoice: my open-house sign-ins. Eleven closings that year and most of them had started on a Saturday. I'd been looking at the wrong column."
- **Lesson / point:** a lead bill isn't a system; the pipeline you own is the one you already have the names for
- **Persona it lands with:** experienced, low production (the 2–5 year agent paying for leads)
- **Pain it speaks to:** financial uncertainty
- **Use:** story reel · partner-call opener · objection answer ("I can't afford to lose my leads")
- **Emotional beat:** embarrassment → relief
- **Privacy:** Taylor's own — no one else named
- **Used-where:** story reel scripted 2026-10-04 (content-log)

## Two closings, one colleague
- **Story:** "My first eight months I closed two deals and both came from the same person — a teacher I used to share a hallway with. She sent her cousin and then her neighbour. I was grateful and I was terrified, because I knew that wasn't a business. That was a favour. Nobody in the office said what 'work your sphere' meant on a Tuesday morning, so I'd sit there with the phone and not know who to call. When Marcus joined in September that was the first thing I told him — your first two will come from somebody who already trusts you, and it still doesn't count as a system until it repeats."
- **Lesson / point:** your first system is the people who already trust you — and it isn't a system until it repeats
- **Persona it lands with:** new agent (the career-changer)
- **Pain it speaks to:** lack of support, mentorship, training
- **Use:** YouTube hook · onboarding (week one) · interview answer
- **Emotional beat:** gratitude
- **Privacy:** the colleague is anonymized ("a teacher I used to share a hallway with")
- **Used-where:** —

## The buyer I lost on a Tuesday
- **Story:** "Spring 2022. A couple came through an open house — relocating for a hospital job, clear timeline, they told me everything on the sign-in sheet. I meant to text them. I didn't. Three weeks later I saw the listing they bought, with another agent's name on it. I did the math on the commission (about nine thousand dollars, illustrative — demo) and then I did the math on how long the text would have taken: forty seconds. That's the deal that wrote the Sunday routine. Not a course. A couple I never texted."
- **Lesson / point:** the follow-up IS the business; the sign-in sheet with no next step is where the money leaks
- **Persona it lands with:** experienced, low production
- **Pain it speaks to:** financial uncertainty (and technology gaps — a sheet with no system)
- **Use:** objection answer ("I already do open houses") · story reel · partner call
- **Emotional beat:** embarrassment → vindication
- **Privacy:** the couple is anonymized; no address, no names
- **Used-where:** —

## The Sunday that changed it
- **Story:** "October 2022. I pulled every sign-in sheet from the year — they were in a drawer, honestly — and I sat at the kitchen table with a legal pad. I'm a teacher, so I wrote a lesson plan. Five steps. Every name gets one text by six o'clock Sunday. Every text asks one question. Every answer goes on one sheet. Tuesday I look at the sheet. And it happens every Sunday whether I feel like it or not. The next quarter three of those names bought (illustrative — demo). I cancelled the leads in December. It has run every Sunday since, including the ones I didn't feel like."
- **Lesson / point:** a routine you'll actually do beats a tool you'll pay for
- **Persona it lands with:** both avatars
- **Pain it speaks to:** financial uncertainty
- **Use:** YouTube hook · the "why join me" story · partner call · Module 1 intro
- **Emotional beat:** relief → pride
- **Privacy:** Taylor's own
- **Used-where:** YouTube "why I joined" outline, 2026-10-07 (content-log)

## The couple who thought they were priced out
- **Story:** "Two clients, both teachers, convinced they couldn't buy in Austin. They'd been told that by three people. I didn't argue. I put the whiteboard up and did the budget with them line by line — show, don't tell. They bought in Pflugerville in 2024 (illustrative — demo). What I learned is that the sheet does the convincing. I never had to persuade anyone of anything; I just had to make the math visible."
- **Lesson / point:** show, don't tell — the sheet does the convincing

## Priya's third contract
- **Story:** "Priya joined in June. Three years in, open houses every weekend, never once texted a sign-in on a Sunday. I gave her the sheet and the five texts on our first call and sat on Zoom with her for her first two Sundays. I haven't had a bigger day. That's when I knew what I was building wasn't a sales business."

## The two-question sign-in sheet
- **Story:** "Every open house I've hosted since 2022 asks two questions on the sign-in sheet. 'What's your timeline?' and 'What would make you move sooner?' Nobody minds — it takes ten seconds — and it means my Sunday text has something real to say. 'You said spring; want to see what spring looks like at this price?' Most agents I meet have a sheet with a name and a phone number and nothing to text about. The edge isn't a tool. It's a question."
- **Lesson / point:** the edge is a question, not a tool
- **Persona it lands with:** both avatars
- **Pain it speaks to:** technology gaps (paying for noise when the fix is a question)
- **Use:** authority reel · Module 1 · partner call
- **Emotional beat:** quiet pride
- **Privacy:** Taylor's own
- **Used-where:** authority reel scripted 2026-10-04 (content-log)
