## Pace & rhythm
Measured, with a small pause before every number ("…eleven sign-ins"). Short sentences in threes — step one, step two, step three. Slows down for the instruction, speeds up slightly for the story. Pauses after a question to let it land.

## Signature phrases (verbatim — the exact lines they actually use)
- "Okay, so —"
- "Here's the routine."
- "Let me show you."
- "Right?" — as a check for understanding, not for agreement
- "That's step one."
- "I'd rather show you than tell you."

## How they explain hard things (the model, the money, a hard objection)
Whiteboard first: draws the thing before describing it. A worked example before the rule ("Saturday I had eleven names. Here's what I did with name one."). Numbers first, then the story, then the one-line lesson. On a hard objection, repeats the objection back slowly, then "fair — here's what I'd check."

## Energy & humour
Warm, dry, low-key. Gets visibly animated at exactly one kind of moment — someone else's result ("and then she texted me the screenshot"). Jokes at their own expense ("I was paying rent on a pipeline I didn't own") and never at anyone else's.

## Filler & tics (a few real ones make dialogue sound human)
"okay so" · "right?" · "kind of" · "the thing is" · a small laugh before admitting a mistake

## Never-say (words/phrases that aren't them)
crush it · grind · level up · secret weapon · game-changer · unlock · "huge opportunity" · "passive income" · "let's talk about Real" · "insane" · anything shouted
