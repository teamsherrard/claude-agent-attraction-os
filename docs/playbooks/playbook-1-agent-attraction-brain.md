## 2. The Agent Attraction Framework

Taught upfront in Core video 1 and built over six weeks. The order matters: Brain and positioning first, then the content engines (short form, then long form), then conversation and conversion, then the organization. Content sits before conversion because an agent who has consumed your content arrives at the call most of the way to a decision.

**Positioning → Content → Conversation → Conversion → Retention → Duplication**

| Pillar | Weeks | The transformation |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | 1 and 2 | Know who you are and who you attract; give agents a compelling reason to pay attention to you |
| Content | 3 and 4 | Consistently get in front of agents; educate, pre-sell, generate inbound conversations |
| Conversation | 5 | Turn prospects and viewers into real conversations and booked calls |
| Conversion | 5 | Run the partner call, handle objections, follow up with a reason |
| Retention | 6 | Keep agents engaged and help them succeed |
| Duplication | 6 | Build an organization that grows beyond you |

### Pillar 1 — Positioning

Who you are as a leader, which type of agent you're built to help, and why an agent would partner with you over anyone else. Agents are sponsor shopping now, not just brokerage shopping; the magic position is when agents want to partner with you so badly the brokerage becomes irrelevant (Lesson: New Era of Cloud Based).

**Week 1 builds** your Agent Attraction Brain: who you are as a leader, your story bank, who your story speaks to (derived for you), your 12-month number and hours, your voice; then, in a second chat, your brand direction and the Design Package. **Week 2 builds** your positioning and Agent Partner Offer: the audit of what your brokerage and upline already provide, your value proposition ("I help [agent] achieve [outcome] through [mechanism]"), your Brokerage Model Expert, your bios, your first prospect list.

### Pillar 2 — Content

Getting in front of agents consistently, so they move from awareness to recognition to familiarity to trust to curiosity to a conversation. Short form gets you discovered; long form is where an agent decides you're someone they want to work with.

**Week 3 builds** your Short-Form Attraction Engine: content pillars, a 30-day calendar, a hook bank and CTA bank, your first Reels posted, the publishing routine. **Week 4 builds** your Long-Form Authority Engine: your YouTube strategy on the 8-video cycle, first outlines, an interview pipeline, thumbnails, and repurposing so every video becomes clips, posts, emails, and conversation starters.

### Pillar 3 — Conversation

Finding the right people and starting relevant, personalized, selfless conversations. Mike's principle: don't recruit, start conversations. Videos don't attract agents; agents attract agents (Lesson: Videos Don't Attract).

**Week 5 builds (first half)** your live pipeline with Prospect Radar, an Agent Intel report on each top prospect before you reach out, and personalized conversation starters.

### Pillar 4 — Conversion

The private one-on-one call: present the model tailored to the agent's pains and goals, blended with your value proposition; answer every question; handle every objection; if they're still unsure, a 3-way call with the best model explainer in your upline.

**Week 5 builds (second half)** the DM-to-call transition, the partner-call framework (Discovery → Diagnosis → Fit → Positioning → Questions → Next Step), your top five objections practiced with the Objection Handling Coach, your first real-call audit, your follow-up and re-engagement engine.

### Pillar 5 — Retention

Everything after an agent says yes. Getting agents eventually becomes easy; keeping them is where the game is won (Lesson: Understanding Movement Patterns).

**Week 6 builds (first half)** your 30-day onboarding path, support that scales so you're not everyone's assistant, your Organization Analysis, your recognition system.

### Pillar 6 — Duplication

Leaders create leaders. You don't tell your agents "go recruit"; you hand them the operating system you spent six weeks installing, and you move from producer to leader.

**Week 6 builds (second half)** your future leaders identified, your Duplication Playbook, version one of your digital product with the Value Vault, the Daily Debrief running and your Weekly Recruiting CEO Review a Friday habit ("run my CEO review"), your next 90-day growth plan.

## 3. The destination: 12-month and 90-day targets

### Realistic over heroic (Lesson: Setting Goals)

Setting goals is important; setting unrealistic expectations is what guarantees failure. Most people who fail at attraction want 100 to 300 agents in year one without consistency or discipline. Mike's rules:

1. **Commit "until it works out," not "if."** Three hard years, then the compounding does the work.
2. **Compare yourself only to who you were last week.** Not your chapter one to someone else's chapter twenty.
3. **Two kinds of goals.** Outcome goals you influence (Mike's worked example: 30 frontline agents in 12 months) and action goals you control (his: two long-form and five short-form videos a week, every week). Build the plan on the actions.
4. **Audit monthly, tangible and intangible.** Did you attract the number, convert the majority you spoke with, stay consistent? Then the intangibles: confidence on camera, explaining the model without notes, confidence on calls.
5. **Work in 12-week quarters.** Long enough to get something done, short enough to measure.

> **Mike:** "Your income is a direct reflection of your standards and habits."

### The WHY Worksheet (Lesson: Clarity on Your Why)

Mike's honest pre-frame: it will not be easy. You'll feel like quitting about once a year (he still does). People will talk badly about you and make things up. You'll have to execute when you don't feel like it and show up with energy when you're exhausted, because 100, 200, 300 people are watching how you show up.

So write down who you're doing this for, in specific scenes, and replay them on the hard days. Mike's were his parents' dream trip to Hawaii, retiring them, his mom's dream car, his dad's dream fishing trip, and financial freedom for the agents he helps. Every one has come true. The purpose has to be bigger than you.

### The residual-income conversation (Lessons: Power of Residual Income; 1 Agent Can Change Your Life)

Your motivation and your private-call material. The idea belongs in your content; the numbers never do.

| Active income (commission), as Mike frames it | Residual income (revenue share) |
|---|---|
| One and done: one deal, one paycheck | Compounds: January 1 starts where December 31 left off |
| Every January 1 you start from zero, whatever you achieved last year | Spread across markets and price points, so the market matters less |
| Trades time for money ("agents never retire, they just expire") | Massive leverage: one agent Mike attracted now has about 400 people in their group |
| Entirely dependent on you: money freedom without time freedom | Time freedom and money freedom, even when you're not the one closing |

Production is still the name of the game: your agents need to produce, or there's nothing to share. This is the second income you add to the first.

**Mike's own path (his numbers, quoted as his, illustrative, never a promise).** He ended year one with 357 agents and about $87,000 in revenue share; then, approximately, $450,000, $820,000, and $1.4 million in year four. He personally attracted more agents than his whole group did until he passed 157; then duplication started. One person's results at one brokerage: nothing here implies they're typical, and nothing you publish will state what any partner earns.

**One agent can change your life, with Mike's caveats.** Mike is roughly 70% of his own sponsor's organization. But strong leaders don't follow weak people (complainers, excuse-makers, the inconsistent); many agents watch for a year or more to see if you're legit; and most who join do less than you expect. So never spend every waking moment on one big prospect.

> **Mike:** "If you hit enough singles, not only will you win the game, but you will position yourself to hit more home runs."

## 4. Who are you

### From agent to leader (Lesson: From Agent to Leader)

> **Mike:** "The size of your business is the size of your leadership."

Production is about you and your clients. Attraction is real leadership: influence, serving others, developing talent, changing families' futures. Mike's core principles:

| Principle | In practice |
|---|---|
| **Compelling vision** | Paint a vision so big that everyone in your group can hit their goals inside it |
| **Absolute certainty** | Bring certainty to uncertain markets: never doom and gloom, never fear tactics, never complaining about the market |
| **Authentic congruence** | Walk the walk. Mike has never told his agents to do something he doesn't do himself at the highest level |
| **Courage and high standards** | Health, family, finances, fitness, faith. Raise agents' standards so they grow while they're with you; that's why they stay |
| **Trust, service, influence** | Maxwell's laws as Mike applies them: you are who you attract; trust is built through execution; leaders add value by serving; influence is built over time |
| **Empower others** | Pour into the ones willing to do the work; build a culture that would continue without you |

**Would you follow yourself?** Attraction has nothing to do with appearance and everything to do with mindset, perspective, work ethic, discipline, and results. Mike's test: would you be attracted to yourself, and would you join yourself? (Lesson: Understanding Movement Patterns)

**Leader self-check** (tick what's true today; the rest is your growth list, not a verdict)

- [ ] I show up with energy on every call, even exhausted
- [ ] I never talk negatively about the market, another brokerage, or another person
- [ ] I only ask agents to do what I already do myself
- [ ] I share what I know freely
- [ ] What I say I'll do, I do, every week
- [ ] I take ownership of results instead of pointing at the market, the broker, or the economy
- [ ] I'm investing in myself and passing it on
- [ ] I have a vision I can say out loud in one sentence

Say **"leadership audit"** any time for the full version: a readiness score and a fix-first list, never a judgment of the person.

**What agents are really asking (Lesson: New Era of Cloud Based).** From a sponsor they look at your track record, your reputation, training and support beyond the brokerage (strategy, mentorship, coaching, direction), culture and community, and a brand they're proud to represent. Two rules follow. Your value has to actually work and you have to actually deliver it: Mike hears weekly from agents who joined someone who promised everything and delivered nothing. And care about people, not the money: revenue share is a byproduct; selfish attraction plateaus every time.

#### Your voice — three questions, out loud

The biggest issue in any AI system is the voice. The only way to make yours accurate is to answer story questions in your own voice (Adam Gillespie's method, as Mike teaches it). Tap the microphone in the message box (or paste what your phone's voice-to-text wrote) and talk like you're on the phone with an agent you like — don't script it, don't make it perfect. Thirty seconds to two minutes each.

- Using voice dictation, talk about why you're excited about agent attraction.
- Using voice dictation, talk about your WHY — your purpose, who you're doing this for.
- Using voice dictation, answer this: why should someone partner with you?

Claude distils how you talk from these three answers — your pace, your phrases, how you explain things, the words you never say — and from how you typed every answer above. Everything the system writes for you from here on sounds like that.

## 5. Who should you attract

### Attraction versus recruiting (Lesson: Attraction vs. Recruiting)

| Recruiting | Attraction |
|---|---|
| Reaching out: cold calls, DMs, lunch-and-learns, paid ads | Agents come directly to you: long-form video (where conversion happens), short form (where awareness happens), a recognized personal brand, events |
| Linear: time in equals output out | Exponential: a slower burn, then an inflection point |
| "Constantly selling your soul" until you have a brand | They already know the value you bring |
| Pulls in personalities that don't match yours | You attract the type of person you are; culture and retention come easy |
| Constant objections, low show-up rates, burnout | Better conversion, better show-up rates, longer retention, more fun |

Mike has never reached out to a single agent since joining his brokerage, and he built the fastest-growing group there.

> **Mike:** "Your vibe attracts your tribe."

### Why agents move (Lesson: Understanding Movement Patterns)

Agents leave problems, not companies. Mike's top reasons, which he says come from data rather than his assumptions: **financial structure** (a 60/40 split, a team split) · **poor leadership** (no vision, mentorship, connection, or training) · **poor support** (no response, no care) · **poor tech** (old-school brokerages telling new agents to work a sphere they don't have) · **limited growth** (a ceiling, financial and personal) · **culture misalignment** (toxic, no recognition, no belonging).

**The five critical pain points** your value has to answer, in Mike's words: **financial uncertainty · lack of support, mentorship, and training (including outdated training) · technology gaps · limited growth · work-life balance**, plus limited recognition. Every avatar, offer, and story in your Brain is tagged against these five.

**What the model answers and what you answer.** The model answers superior training, multiple income streams, a long-term vision and exit, and leverage as their brand grows. You answer: role model through execution, proof of what's possible, training that fills the gaps in the brokerage's own training, something new always on the horizon, and culture. Two warnings: never create inner circles (an inner circle implies an outer one, and the outer one leaves), and reinvest in your agents the way you reinvested in your production business, or you plateau.

### Videos don't attract; agents attract agents (Lesson: Videos Don't Attract)

Sending the "model explained" video and waiting worked ten years ago, when there was one cloud brokerage and no competing sponsors. Now it's the lazy route: they don't watch it, or they watch it and have a hundred questions the video can't answer. The right way, which Week 5 turns into your call system:

1. Get them on a **private one-on-one Zoom call as quickly as possible.** Say as little as you have to in text; never explain the model by DM or email.
2. Ask what they're looking for, why they agreed to the call, and why they aren't hitting their goals. Then present the model **tailored to their pains and goals, blended with your value proposition.**
3. Answer every question, handle every objection. Still unsure? A **3-way call with the best model explainer in your upline**, not necessarily your sponsor.
4. Do the reps. Mike did one-hour calls with every agent for a year, 30-minute calls after about 300 because they'd binged his content first, and hosted a weekly "model explained plus my value" call every Tuesday for four years that his agents could invite prospects to.

### The mirror principle and the relatability test

You're most qualified to help the person you used to be. Your ideal agent is usually living your hardest stretch right now, two or three years behind you on the same road. If you're newer with little production history, you attract by shared vision and stage. The biggest mistake in attraction is trying to attract anyone with a license: one primary type, at most two secondary; a profile that fits everyone attracts no one.

**The relatability test, in order** (any "no" and that type is not your primary)

| Question | Yes / No |
|---|---|
| Would this agent recognize themselves in your journey? | |
| Would your "what worked" strategies solve this agent's biggest problem right now? | |
| Could you credibly help them: mentor if you're a few years ahead, build alongside if you're at the same stage? (Never "out-earn them.") | |

Mike's progression: new agents first (the volume, the reps, the foundation), then work up to top producers, team leaders, and brokers. Where you lack the proof or the seniority, lean on your upline and say "we, not me" (Lesson: New Agents).

### The two cardinal rules

Everything your Brain writes, and everything you say on a call, obeys two rules (Lesson: 2 Cardinal Rules, Week 2 vault, applied from day one):

1. **Never talk badly about another brokerage.** It looks unprofessional and desperate, and it means you don't know how to position your own value. Give credit, then say what you can do for them.
2. **Never talk badly about another sponsor or person.** Word gets around; agents repeat it. Even if you've heard negatives and have proof, don't acknowledge them; shift back to your value.

In practice: former brokerages are never named in your stories; research reports facts with sources, never verdicts; an objection about another sponsor never names a flaw, it asks whether they showed proof.
